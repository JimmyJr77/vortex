# Vortex progression outline: operating contract

**Stage 2 · Version 2.0 · Program design, not final exercise prescriptions.**

Read this contract with [the 60-day outline](PROGRESSION_OUTLINE.md), [the separate instructional map](instructional_on_ramp/INSTRUCTIONAL_MAP.md) and [the reviewed strategy](BLOCK_STRATEGY.md). The daily briefs are individually authored. The rendering script assigns their rotating weekdays and resolves links; it does not write workouts, select age-based loads or manufacture athlete results.

## Outcomes, periods and retained qualities

| Outcome | Block evidence sought | Retained exposure |
|---|---|---|
| O1: repeatable speed and explosive output | Comparable acceleration, branch-specific running, jump and throw observations; equal or better useful output with control and adequate recovery | Familiar speed/output tasks throughout all three periods |
| O2: a stronger full-body force foundation | Controlled work, appropriate load or useful range at a comparable effort; improved execution also counts | Knee/hip, push/pull and brace roles at every visit; unilateral practice across the offering |
| O3: controlled absorption, redirection and response | A reproducible approach, organized plant/landing and correct safe response to defined information | Predictable stopping and landing even when a cue or rebound is eligible |
| O4: usable range, balance and coordination | Appropriate range, rhythm, single-support and landing control within actual tasks | Standard preparation; controlled strength ranges; task-specific body organization |
| O5: sustainable participation and body-control continuity | Follow instructions, recover, report readiness and maintain separately observed body-control competencies | Enjoyable practice, appropriate workload and a documented separate tumbling handoff |

P1 (Weeks 1–4) retains and develops the athlete's current level. P2 (Weeks 5–8) gives force and purposeful redirection more attention. P3 (Weeks 9–12) applies, verifies and carries capacity forward. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 provide comparison and consolidation opportunities. These are Vortex organizational choices; neither periods nor review weeks override readiness or impose an automatic deload. All periods retain all outcomes. The eight tenets remain Strength, Explosiveness, Speed, Agility, Flexibility/Mobility, Balance, Coordination and Body Control.

## Five emphases, rotating across weekdays

| Family | Primary explosive job | Strength relationship | Priority outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACC | Short acceleration with a qualified start and stopping area | Knee-pattern lead; hip, push, pull and brace complement the visit | O1, O2; O3–O5 support |
| JUMP | Jump output with a qualified stick or rebound; familiar throwing may complement it | Hip-pattern lead; modest knee, push, pull and brace work | O1, O2, O4; O3/O5 support |
| BRAKE | Absorb and redirect from a known approach; add information only when qualified | Unilateral knee-pattern lead, hip support, push/pull and brace | O3, O2; O1/O4/O5 support |
| RUN | RUN-U: upright running in a verified runway; RUN-S: separately tracked short acceleration if the runway is unavailable | Balanced strength with modest knee lead and hip/push/pull/brace support | O1, O2; O3–O5 support |
| REACT | Respond to a defined cue or constrained partner with a known movement solution; familiar throws may complement it | Push/pull emphasis, small knee/hip roles and brace | O3, O1, O2; O4/O5 support |

Week 1 offers ACC/JUMP/BRAKE/RUN/REACT, then the order shifts one position each week. Every fixed weekday meets all five families within five weeks. This rotates the larger emphasis, not every exercise. It does not promise that once-weekly attendance gives the same adaptation as more frequent training. A coach reviews actual coverage and may replace a secondary task with a needed familiar anchor within the existing clock. Record that replacement and its exposure links; never add missed volume as debt.

Five families are not five isolated body-part days. Every day includes useful explosive practice and appropriately dosed lower-body, push, pull and trunk work. A unilateral knee exercise can fill the knee role; it does not require an additional bilateral knee exercise. A brace within a selected carry/support/lift can fill the trunk role without a mandatory extra circuit. All five strength patterns need not receive developmental loading every day.

RUN-U and RUN-S are distinct conditional branches, not interchangeable speed measurements. RUN-U requires verified buildup, upright zone, runoff, surface and supervision for the chosen task. A-march/A-skip can prepare rhythm but do not count as delivered upright speed. RUN-S changes the day to short acceleration; the coach records that change and its separate baseline. The facility branch remains unselected until verified. A recovered short-start alternative may be useful but does not satisfy an unperformed upright-speed outcome.

## Methods and selection boundaries

The plan uses descriptive method intents: fully recovered quality attempts; controlled straight strength sets; noncompeting pairs where they preserve recovery; concise technical practice; and repeatable observation replacing equivalent training work. These are planning references, not fabricated canonical method-card IDs. The [candidate register](ANCHOR_LIBRARY_CANDIDATES.md) records actual local evidence and missing approvals.

Order follows purpose: familiar preparation plus two demand drills, primary explosive task while fresh, complementary explosive work only when useful, then full-body strength with one lead emphasis. Pairing is conditional on different limiting tissues, rest and visibility. A row is not automatically recovery from a grip-limited hinge; a throw plus pressing may increase shoulder demand before tumbling. Choose a supported or less demanding alternative, separate the tasks, or remove the secondary work when needed. No conditioning circuit or repeat-sprint method silently replaces recovered speed.

Exact exercise choices, sets, age doses, loads, rest, equipment quantities and timed rotations are Stage 3/individual-session work. No numbers are inferred from a family label. Final selection requires the current library eligibility and a comparison of credible alternatives for the intended job. Age 12–14 is the reference only; each of the three age prescriptions will retain its own beginner/experienced and readiness route. No birthday automatically unlocks rebound, cue complexity, heavy loading or advanced tumbling.

## Attendance and recovery routes

Every offered day has both D (development eligible) and L (lighter practice/retention) routes. D does not mean every component or anchor progresses. L retains the day's movement intent using familiar, lower-demand practice, fewer demanding efforts/sets, no load/complexity progression, adequate recovery and an appropriate separate tumbling lane. The detailed day must quantify both routes; these qualitative labels alone are not final prescriptions. Pain, dizziness, distress or a restriction triggers the existing stop/referral procedure, not the L route as a clearance workaround.

| Actual attendance pattern | Starting route example, conditional on total sport load and readiness |
|---|---|
| One visit | D if ready, with broad full-body coverage. Select the athlete's established level and fill a meaningful coverage gap by replacement within time. Do not force L because the offered day is Tuesday. |
| Two separated visits | D/D may fit; compare actual work and sport demands before each. |
| Two consecutive visits | D/L is the initial model; retain the second day's intent with lighter physical demand. |
| Three nonconsecutive visits | D/D/D may fit if substantive loading is recoverable; individual anchors may still hold or reduce. |
| Three consecutive visits | D/L/D is a planning example, with the third D conditional on recovery. |
| Four visits | Use available gaps; D/L/D/L illustrates four consecutive visits. Do not manufacture a third hard day to match the offering. |
| Five visits | D/L/D/L/D is the initial weekday model, with any D downgraded or omitted for sport/recovery. The tumbling route must also match readiness. |

These route examples implement the reviewed strategy and youth-guidance distinction between an offering and substantive training frequency. They are not a guarantee that five visits are appropriate. Include weekend games, practice, school sport, other lifting and tumbling in the decision. Sometimes the solution is a day away or reduced attendance, not another lighter gym session. Do not infer recovery from a different exercise name or body-region tag.

For each visit: retrieve the athlete's most recent relevant **completed** productive work, the latest lighter/review work, symptoms/restrictions, attendance and upcoming sport. If ready, use the single justified change or retention decision. If partly ready, hold or reduce the limiting variable. If fatigued, use a suitable L route or reduce attendance. If an exposure was missed, use the last demonstrated level and only necessary calibration. If prerequisites are missing, assign the corresponding on-ramp module while preserving qualified work in other domains. None of these cases creates catch-up sets.

## Preparation, clocks and separate tumbling

The full familiar base is the sequence in the current philosophy: jog; backpedal; shuffle; carioca; walking and lateral lunges; inchworm/plank/bear-crawl/downward-dog sequence; snap-down to stick; mastered A/B/C march/skip variants; ankling; low pogos; concluding hinge and squat checks. The compact base follows the philosophy's reduced recognizable sequence, not a rushed full list. Stage 3 must expand every exercise and all three age doses.

Every daily entry names exactly two additional preparation purposes: (1) relevant position/mechanics rehearsal that also informs the strength demand; (2) progressive rehearsal of the primary explosive task. The base hinge/squat checks remain before these two tasks. These are demands to satisfy during selection, not extra drills to stack outside the clock.

| Component | Standard athletic booking | Compressed athletic booking |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare & Access | 0–15 minutes | 0–10 minutes |
| Explosiveness | 15–45 | 10–35 |
| Strength | 45–75 | 35–55 |
| Flexible capacity/competition/readiness window | 75–90 | 55–60 |
| Separate Body Control / Tumbling | 90–120; its own 30-minute booking | 60–90; its own 30-minute booking |

The final window provides recovery, low-fatigue coaching, readiness communication or suitable play. No day depends on extra sprint, jump, throw or strength volume hidden there. A scored explosive attempt belongs inside the explosive dose. Hard conditioning is not appended ahead of tumbling. Compressed-day selection protects the primary stimulus and its rest, core full-body strength and recognizable preparation; daily entries identify what secondary work loses priority. Final numeric deletions and timing calculations remain required later.

Use 15 athletes, three lanes and two qualified athletic coaches as **unverified initial planning assumptions**. Coaches must see all active tasks; unfamiliar tasks are serialized or simplified rather than assumed simultaneously supervisable. Running branches need measured travel/runoff; medicine balls need safe noncrossing throwing/retrieval space; weights need suitable implement sizes and enough stations to preserve recovery. Three lanes alone do not prove feasibility. Demonstration, queueing, returns, transitions and water remain inside every clock. Outline timing feasibility is conditional; no detailed 15-athlete timing PASS is claimed.

Each day communicates impact, leg, shoulder/wrist and attention demands to the separate tumbling coach. The tumbling coach selects the appropriate qualified lane and may retain foundations or defer unfamiliar high-consequence learning if fatigue interferes. **No precise approved 30-minute tumbling session has yet been linked.** Daily handoff descriptions are not completed tumbling prescriptions. Separate program identities, prerequisites, staffing and dose reconciliation remain a real later-stage dependency.

## Two ledgers and comparable exposure links

[The anchor ledger](anchor_progression_ledger.json) records every planned anchor exposure, prior and next offered exposure, and the prior/next matching role. Its dose and actual-response fields remain explicitly unresolved until detailed prescription and real delivery respectively. A small maintenance exposure is relevant history but does not replace the last productive lead exposure as the loading baseline. Matching a planning role is only a candidate comparison: the actual variation, dose and conditions must also match.

[The workload ledger](workload_exposure_ledger.json) separates running branches and effort categories, jump/landing contacts, throws, strength work, late capacity and separate tumbling. Planned numeric work is null until a detailed day exists; actual work is null because no athlete delivery record was supplied. Null means unknown, never zero. Do not sum unlike contacts or use a fabricated fatigue score. Warm-up and tumbling exposure must eventually be included without double counting a single landing under several tags.

Offering references are explicit Day numbers. They are not evidence that an athlete attended, and the preceding offering day is not always the prior relevant comparison. Day 1 anchors point to a prior-block record requirement; each final chain points to the next block. Current athlete data, if later supplied, gets a separate record with actual prescriptions and response. Changing the outline requires a revision-log entry and reconciliation of affected days, links and ledgers before delivery.

## Entry and assessment

The [separate map](instructional_on_ramp/INSTRUCTIONAL_MAP.md) links conduct/flow, preparation, sprint/landing/braking, strength participation, effort/recovery and body-control entry competencies to OR sessions and main families. An athlete may demonstrate an existing skill directly, take only missing modules and join the offering current when checks are met. Main entry grants the qualified variation, not every later-stage task or the group's calendar dose.

Two explicit alternative entry branches preserve qualified work when landing or traveling-response prerequisites are incomplete. **JUMP-T** uses a separately passed familiar stable throw instead of the main jump. **REACT-T** uses a separately passed early release/go-no-go throw instead of traveling response. Neither landing failure nor age grants throw or cue competence. Each relevant daily entry specifies the changed primary objective, quality target, two replacement preparation tasks, progression, full-body strength/tumbling implications and compressed priorities. Unqualified flight in the recognizable base is replaced with qualified non-flight practice; unaffected preparation stays familiar.

These branches replace both the regular primary explosive task and its complementary-throw allocation with **one** primary throwing allocation. They do not claim a jump, upright-speed or reactive-cut result. Their separate ledger chains inherit actual passed throw/cue evidence and link the next relevant offering. Selection stays null until the athlete is known. The later detailed session must resolve the exact approved throw/cue variant and age-specific dose; the planning label is not a canonical record or a new approval. If even this branch's prerequisites are absent, keep targeted instruction rather than declare main-family entry complete.

Comparable observations in Weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12 use the same task/branch, implement or load, distance/zone, surface, effort category, recovery and scoring method. Observations replace relevant training efforts/sets. Changed equipment or conditions produce a qualified/new comparison, not an invented gain. No expected percentage improvement, maximal strength test or universal fatigue cutoff is required. A missed review is rescheduled within a relevant normal visit without accumulated test volume.

The final handoff contains actual attendance; current variations/competencies; recent productive doses and latest review doses; comparable results and conditions; tolerance, restrictions and sport context; and one next retain/develop/reduce/remediate decision per relevant anchor. The strategy's hypothetical established-athlete example remains illustrative; it is not entered as actual ledger data.
