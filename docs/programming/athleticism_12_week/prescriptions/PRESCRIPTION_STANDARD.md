# Vortex session prescription standard

**Stage 3 · Version 1.1 · Written athletic exemplar design reviewed; current library release and facility facts remain unverified.**

The complete outline passed its planning gate. A detailed session now needs an independently readable coach export, structured doses and a session-specific timing/workload check. A candidate exercise is not presented as a currently approved facility record. The [mapping register](EXEMPLAR_LIBRARY_MAPPING.md) preserves exact local mechanics, source defaults, proposed contextual changes and remaining gates.

## Required order and fields

1. Session identity, outline version, primary stimulus brief, observable target and prior/next relevant exposures.
2. Audience/history assumptions; individual readiness and attendance routes; current restrictions and total sport/tumbling check.
3. Exact 90/60-minute athletic clocks plus the separate 30-minute tumbling status; equipment, space, groups and coaching assumptions.
4. Complete recognizable preparation, expanded from the standard record into every exported session; exactly two identified targeted tasks.
5. Each programmed exercise: component/order; exact variant and mapping; setup/execution; set purpose and daily contribution; three age prescriptions; dose/tempo/effort/rest; cues/errors; competency and stop/regress decision; prior → current → next relationship; selection/order/pairing rationale and metadata.
6. Explicit lighter and compressed prescriptions. A compressed session is a different stated allocation of work, not the same list performed faster.
7. Late-window activity/dose or explicit recovery-only choice; precise separate tumbling link when available, otherwise an honest missing-content status.
8. Calculated per-athlete and group timing, exposure accounting, equipment use and independent programming review. A correct clock sum alone is insufficient.

Use the existing card concepts—movement identity, requirements, taxonomy, phase profile, why layer, execution, dosage, scaling, pairing, safety and media. Documentation keys such as `GOBLET` are mapping references, never invented canonical IDs. The familiar backend fields `output` and `capacity-strength` may be source/profile names; the coach-facing components remain **Explosiveness** and **Strength**.

## Dose language

All planned doses are explicit for **9–11**, **12–14** and **15–18**. A number is not added merely to make an older band harder. Age prescriptions can share a task and dose when that is appropriate. Record load, support height, range and variant selected for the individual; a group age band does not determine one absolute weight.

- Count one unilateral repetition as one side's complete movement. A row set includes both sides plus the stated side-change time. A heel-tap repetition is one controlled heel contact and return; eight total means four each side. This is distinct from a source using a paired left/right cycle.
- Tempo is in seconds. `3–0–1–0` means three seconds lowering, no deliberate bottom pause, one second rising, no deliberate top pause. A stated handling allowance is additional to repetitions.
- Strength effort uses a coached reserve of good repetitions. A target of about three repetitions in reserve is an aid to selecting a controlled load, not a test requiring a child to fail. Novice estimates are corroborated by the coach; no forced reps or grinding.
- Sprint intent is task-specific quality, with adequate recovery and a qualified start/runoff. A percentage of perceived intent is a coaching estimate, not a measured percentage of maximum speed. Walking orientation and moderate technical starts are not counted as high-intent sprint output.
- Every set or group of identical sets has a job. Setup, calibration, practice, development, maintenance and comparison may differ. Repeated working sets can share the same purpose. Recovery and transitions are not disguised additional exercise sets.
- Null means not applicable or genuinely unknown as labeled; zero means deliberately no prescribed work. Planned work is never entered as actual completion.

## Continuing athletes and entry routes

The main exemplar assumes the selected variations are familiar and qualified. Its written doses are a concrete planning prescription, not fabricated prior-block data. Before delivery compare them with the athlete's actual last productive and review exposures. If those differ, record the exact current dose/variation decision and its timing effect; do not silently call a reduced dose a novice restart or add an unplanned set to restore volume.

D means development eligible, not “progress every exercise.” L means the explicit lighter prescription. A missing prerequisite may use that exercise's specified instructional alternative while other qualified components retain their assigned work. A symptom/restriction stop is not permission to continue through an L exercise. For frequent attendance use actual exposure and sport readiness; D/L/D/L/D is an initial five-visit model, not an attendance requirement or recovery guarantee.

The on-ramp exemplar uses short observed practice bouts, demonstrations and correction inside the same clock. It does not attempt to load a novice like a continuing athlete or certify every skill in one visit. An experienced newcomer demonstrates existing skills and uses only relevant instruction; elapsed attendance does not confer competence.

## Timing and progression rules

For every active set calculate repetitions × cadence, both sides, side changes, pickup/set-down and any travel/clearance. Then show actual time to the next relevant effort. A different station is not automatically recovery from shared grip, shoulder or trunk work. Stagger demanding supervised tasks where practical; low-demand familiar tasks may overlap only when both are visible and appropriately supervised.

Timetable times are **earliest releases**, not orders to rush. Wait for clearance and appropriate recovery. If a task takes longer than its model, remove the explicitly named optional/secondary work or reduce sets according to the session plan; do not reclaim time by shortening required recovery. If supervision/space still fails, the stated model is not deliverable and needs a revised route.

The next exposure uses actual response. Advance only the one justified variable after repeatable task quality at the intended effort and adequate recovery; hold partial readiness; reduce the limiting demand for fatigue; stop for a restriction or meaningful quality/symptom concern. Missing sessions create no debt. The last productive dose and the latest review/reduced dose both remain in the record.

## Status and publication boundary

`prescription_complete` means every planned task, dose, age route, set purpose and timing field is written and checked. `programming_review_pass` requires substantive independent review. `operational_release_verified` additionally requires actual facility eligibility, inventory/space, staffing and separate-program coordination. These are different claims. The current local database lacks the canonical/release/method/skill tables needed to prove current approved availability; production remains unknown.

No new card, method or media is published by writing these documents. Proposed session overrides are labeled. The full goal remains unfinished until all 60 main and 20 instructional plans and the required audits, mappings, separate tumbling status and coach assembly are complete.

The reviewed exemplars implement this distinction using `athletic_prescription_complete`, `programming_review_pass`, `operational_release_verified` and `separate_tumbling_prescription_complete`. The first two are true for their written athletic scope; the latter two remain false. See [EXEMPLAR_REVIEW.md](EXEMPLAR_REVIEW.md). Preparation snapshots include exact count units and resolved readiness pointers. Authored timing models support recalculation; the [ledger index](../LEDGER_INDEX.md) distinguishes current detailed dose supplements from the preserved outline-stage planning records.
