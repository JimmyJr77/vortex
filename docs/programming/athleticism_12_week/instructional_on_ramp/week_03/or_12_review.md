## Objective and selection

The primary objective is to teach return, honest readiness reporting, acceptance/request of recovery and coach-controlled release around an already owned running task. The primary OR08 lineage stays a 35 m build/rhythm/runoff corridor. Its actual easy or independently repeatable modest intent remains unchanged. OR10 standing-static acceleration alternatives retain different geometry, intent and recovery; a short acceleration result does not become an upright-running result.

Reporting is a new participation responsibility rather than a new exercise identity. First reporting instruction needs no prior independent reporting success. Coach reminders remain compatible with learning. The session does not induce fatigue, require a false concern, add a race or add an effort to manufacture a restart decision.

## Delivery and recovery decisions

The preparation base resolves in full. P1 uses 40 seconds; P2 uses 140, including the initial adjacent-bay gathering and explanation. The three preparation lanes require protected outside returns and actual observation/spacing fit. Longer real transitions or travel delay or omit work rather than force pace.

The main layout explicitly changes to two fixed coach/corridor assignments, with the third preparation lane closed as a buffer. Each corridor has a separate outside return and rear report/wait/staging area. Stationary athletes report from their own visible, conversationally audible personal bays without an extra walk or coach transfer. Actual facility dimensions, view, hearing and equipment remain conditions to verify.

Fifty-second global and 100-second same-lane release intervals provide room for the longest planned 45-second walk, 40-second return, 10-second routine report and five-second next-athlete staging. The opening 120 seconds owns final transfer, lane closure, report instruction and initial staging. Staging does not silently include equipment changes or coach relocation. A coach-to-coach HOLD/CLEAR acknowledgement confirms the previous main action actually ended before the next alternating release; a timer alone cannot provide that evidence.

The report allowance is not a cap on a concern. Actual longer action, return, report, staging or requested wait changes the release decision. Original slot IDs and route-specific recovery remain intact; slots that cannot fit or are unsuitable are omitted without replacement. Main recovery begins after the complete physical action and return, not from departure, report wording or empty space.

That recovery also carries into the first performed Strength action, including counted handling. An individual may start later only inside the original role/group observation window, preserving reset before a following group; otherwise that role is omitted. The remaining independently eligible roles keep their own checks. This addresses the concrete case where a late technical run leaves less than its required 180 seconds before the originally scheduled squat.

The compressed row rotation has its own 40-second familiar reminder/setup and group starts at 40/112/184 seconds. A 52-second row plus 20-second reset fits the 72-second separation; its last set ends by 236 seconds. Other compressed strength groups retain 60/120/180 starts. These are conditional planning allowances, not prescribed athlete pace or a claim that unverified equipment fits.
