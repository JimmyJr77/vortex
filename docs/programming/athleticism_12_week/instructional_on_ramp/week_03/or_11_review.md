## The intended change and its limits

The [reviewed stimulus brief](../../planning/OR11_STIMULUS_AND_SOURCE_REVIEW.md) identifies a specific progression: reduce an actual two-second stopped pause to one second before the ordinary announced walking exit. The athlete still stops bilaterally, then opens with the exit-side foot while the other foot lifts/replants and follows. Feet and body turn together. The two-metre exit remains ordinary walking at the retained 45-degree geometry, with a separate quiet two-second finish.

This is a proposed Vortex instructional context, not an immediate cutting prescription. Its purpose is to connect controlled components while preserving balance. Pause timing starts only after the actual stop. If stability takes longer, the athlete retains/restores a suitable held task; a one-second instruction is never a deadline to leave an unstable position.

First and retained work have different evidence paths. First one-second instruction requires actual repeatable corresponding two-second work and current suitability, without requiring a prior successful one-second attempt. Retained one-second work can use direct repeatable same-context one-second evidence without reconstructing an older two-second record. First full two-second exit instruction needs the actual corresponding controlled stop and comfortable ordinary walking/turning components; it does not require prior success at the complete exit. A first jogging-stop lesson remains terminal-only.

Each direction is independent. One side may retain one second while the other learns the full-pause exit, remains terminal or is omitted. A single changed attempt is recorded as an observation, not promoted to repeatability. The day therefore develops the appropriate actual branch without forcing established participants back through whole modules or treating an older newcomer as qualified.
