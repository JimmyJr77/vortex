## Daily stimulus brief

Teach one known release cue through a stable two-hand chest throw, then teach safe collective retrieval and organized push/pull stations. Preserve familiar knee, hip and brace practice at one set each. The main quality is controlled participation in a release-and-reset task; no reaction-time, distance or maximal-output result is promised. Prepare & Access rehearses position/handling; Explosiveness teaches the counted release; Strength prioritizes upper-body setup; the final window records what was actually learned and prepares the separate handoff.

**Success:** The athlete waits for their own clear release instruction, sends the implement forward within the inspected lane, stays behind the line until global all-clear, retrieves and racks without a return throw, and follows the selected push/pull setup. Record which actions were repeatable, emerging, modified or unobserved; do not infer a pass from attendance.

**Prior → current → next:** Actual relevant OR-01 conduct and OR-04 upper-body/strength response or direct equivalent → OR-05 known release plus retrieval at retained simple lower-body demand → OR-09 go/no-go only after stable throw and known release are repeatable, then OR-13 only the independently passed cue/movement branch. The five offered weekdays are choices, not five compulsory training exposures or prerequisite attendance.

### P2 — Hold, wait and put the ball down on instruction (Prepare & Access)

**Mapping:** BALL-HOLD-TEACH; unloaded/standing options are separate tasks **Set purpose / contribution:** One counted no-release handling set adds the actual implement and known instruction to a stable position. It teaches grip, waiting, comfortable floor pickup/set-down and a short controlled carry without hiding an extra throw or separate assessment inside preparation.

**Setup and execution:** Begin with the ball resting on a marked floor spot behind the throw line. After demonstration, use a comfortable bilateral hip/knee bend to pick it up to chest level over 5 s, hold comfortably for 5 s, walk 1 m out and 1 m back in the marked personal handling area over 4 s (2 s walking plus 2 s turning), set the ball back on its original floor spot over 6 s, then exit/reset over 4 s. The ball stays in two hands until set-down. This counted handling loop includes floor access and 2 m loaded carry; no throw, catch, loaded push-out, extra trial, or passing to a child. Do not force a floor reach that is unavailable or hurry the turn; stop the action and let the coach manage the implement if needed. Use both palms/finger pads on the rear-side surfaces of the ball, thumbs comfortably behind it and fingers spread enough for a secure grip; no forced thumb shape or joint angle. Keep wrists comfortable and elbows below shoulder height. Ball rests in the hands at chest level, not squeezed against the throat. Declare the individually fitting contact and retain it across P2/E1; a ball requiring painful wrist position or an insecure spread is unsuitable.

**Cues:** Reach the floor comfortably before taking the ball; two hands; stand still for the hold; walk and turn on instruction; return and set down gently. **Common errors:** Releasing on a setup cue, dropping the ball, prolonged unsupported waiting, forcing grip on a large implement, holding breath, or awarding a throwing/cue-speed pass.

**Selection / order / pairing:** This is the progressive rehearsal of task setup and permission. A complete release/retrieval cycle for all 15 new athletes does not fit the 180 s target budget. The first actual release is therefore the first counted E1 attempt after full demonstration. This detailed revision preserves the objective and adds no third preparation drill.

**Metadata:** Method: observed floor handling and waiting instruction. Tenets: Body Control / Strength; equipment conduct and attention integrated. Pattern: one floor pickup, chest hold, short walking carry/turn and floor set-down. Equipment: declared soft medicine ball, marked original floor spot and a separate unobstructed 1 m out-and-back path per lane; no shared crossing.

### E1 — Release once on the known cue, then retrieve together (Explosiveness)

**Mapping:** CHEST-PASS-TEACH; related to Medicine Ball Chest Pass, exact open-lane stationary profile unverified **Set purpose / contribution:** Each listed single-throw set has the same purpose: connect a controlled bilateral chest release to one clear early permission cue and then practice the shared retrieval rule. D uses 3 standard / 2 compressed opportunities; L uses 2 / 1, for all ages. This is a low-dose participation lesson in the Explosiveness window, not a maximal power assessment. The first attempt is the counted instructional observation.

**Setup and execution:** Use the same comfortable bilateral stance and declared ball, feet planted, ribs over pelvis. Ball begins at chest, elbows comfortable. Coach names the athlete, confirms the empty contained lane, then says “Ready … throw.” Project the ball forward with two hands in one smooth quick action; no squat/dip preload, step, jump, rotation or overhead action. Finish balanced at the line and wait. One athlete releases at a time across all lanes. When all three balls are stopped, the coach shuts down all releases and says “Retrieve.” Each owner walks to their own ball, picks it up, carries it back and racks it; no return throw or catch. Reset behind the queue line before any later release. Use both palms/finger pads on the rear-side surfaces of the ball, thumbs comfortably behind it and fingers spread enough for a secure grip; no forced thumb shape or joint angle. Keep wrists comfortable and elbows below shoulder height. Ball rests in the hands at chest level, not squeezed against the throat. Declare the individually fitting contact and retain it across P2/E1; a ball requiring painful wrist position or an insecure spread is unsuitable.

**Cues:** Your lane; wait for your name and throw cue; send it forward; stay at the line; retrieve only together; carry it back. **Common errors:** Throwing on another athlete’s cue, chasing a ball before all-clear, rapid partner exchange, adding a step/dip, throwing toward people, arching, choosing a heavier ball to reach a target, or treating a known permission cue as a measured reaction test.

**Selection / order / pairing:** Stationary two-hand projection isolates release discipline with little added lower-body impact after OR-02/03/04. A wall-return task changes rebound and preload requirements; an overhead or rotational throw changes mechanics; partner catching adds receiving skill. They are not silent substitutes. Single releases allow both coaches to observe. Long between-round rest protects attention and release quality; Strength then leads with matched push/pull teaching without extra sets.

**Metadata:** Method: repeated single observed teaching attempts with complete reset. Tenets: Strength / Body Control; coordination, perception and equipment conduct integrated.
