## Objective and exercise selection

[OR-04](or_04.md) teaches comfortable upright rhythm and the buildup, finish, runoff and return boundaries of a running corridor. Preparation contains exactly two tasks: the familiar basic in-place march and one short route rehearsal. A complete selected-corridor walk is a separately counted main-block task, E0. It is followed by recovered easy runs or independently eligible walking/short-start/stationary alternatives. Five familiar strength sets preserve knee, hip, push, pull and supported trunk control, with equal teaching time for each role.

The [OR-04 source register](../../prescriptions/OR04_LIBRARY_MAPPING.md) identifies why the lesson uses explicit teaching adaptations. Source127's stride-out is a maximal-intent legacy prescription with an archived incomplete segment/exit contract. Flying128 has a high-speed identity. Neither is silently presented as the easy lesson here. The basic march is stationary and has no flight; it does not claim source69's exact traveling A-March identity. Short starts retain the standing-static mechanics of ACC-STATIC with a declared low-intent contextual override, not its high-intent output profile. Walking orientation is distinct from source155's terminal braking task.

The two coaches need usable full-corridor sightlines; the plan does not require them to shadow a 35-metre walk and race back. P2 and E0 use a separate familiar/slow three-athlete wave policy. Mixed P2 routes can briefly overlap in a dedicated return; spacing, no catch-up and no shared merge are explicit conditions. Actual longer movement or transition times delay release and reduce later uncompleted work. Timing estimates never force a child's pace.

P1 is only a four-step or two-second standing preparation rehearsal, with an explicit 20-second minimum before repeating the same action. The earliest repeated stationary P2 action has 40 seconds after P1 ends. P2/E1 stationary practice retains its separate 60-second recovery. No earlier baseline or future block is silently shortened to create time.

Approach/running target metres, gradual runoff, walking orientation and outside returns remain separate quantities. The longer easy-running lesson deliberately changes route distance relative to short OR-01 starts; it does not claim unchanged total metres. Its small number of attempts and low intent serve the new teaching objective. Exact contacts and return metres remain actual observations. A faulty attempt still counts physically; a valid result is separate. There is no running finisher or invented tumbling dose.
