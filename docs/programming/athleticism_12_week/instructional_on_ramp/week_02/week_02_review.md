## What this week develops

Week 2 teaches the athlete to recognize and explain one justified change while retaining useful work elsewhere. That change may be a first small takeoff, first manageable hinge handling, an announced walking exit, modest running intent, a THROW/HOLD decision or a knee-resistance decision. It is conditional on the actual component prerequisites and current response. The five-day offering is not five required development sessions or an attendance-based promotion.

## Keep competence, completed dose and fatigue as distinct records

The athlete record must preserve three kinds of information. **Competence/current-control observations** describe the exact movement, setup, approach, direction or cue rule observed. **Completed compatible Strength doses** record actual sets, repetitions on each side, tempo, range, support, implement/load, handling and response. **Shared fatigue and outside workload** inform today's decision even when the earlier task has a different identity. Planned or attended work supplies none of these outcomes automatically.

This separation resolves several otherwise misleading week-to-week conclusions:

- The base and targeted preparation contain small hinge and partial-squat observations. They contribute exposure and current-control evidence, but do not automatically replace a compatible Strength-set repetition baseline because they happened later in the clock. An actual deliberate reduction of the training set does govern subsequent retention.
- OR-07's supported static stance is not a bilateral squat and cannot unload or overwrite a squat record. Its actually completed unloaded hip set can update the compatible hip record. Missing OR-07 produces neither event.
- First OR-06 DB handling does not establish familiar 10 s handling. It also does not erase the familiar unloaded hinge that permitted that instruction. Later appropriate bodyweight hinge work remains available without manufacturing familiar loaded handling.
- Familiar upper-body support does not qualify a knee load, and missing upper-body support does not remove independently suitable knee instruction. Landing competence does not establish jogging-stop control; long running does not establish the exact short-start route.
- Reintroduced knee resistance after a newer compatible bodyweight squat is an explicit new resistance decision with a current reason, renewed counted handling and the recent repetition ceiling. An older successful goblet record is not permission for silent restoration.

The one-set and smaller-count retention rules here apply to these five instructional prescriptions. They do not redefine an established athlete's productive capacity forever. A later authored development exposure may justify a change using its purpose, current readiness and actual response. The continuing-block strategy still distinguishes a temporary assessment dose from established productive work; neither automatic restoration nor an indefinite novice restart follows from this weekly audit.

## First instruction remains possible

Readiness gates should prevent unsupported progress without requiring a prior perfect performance of the very task being taught. OR-06 first flight can follow actual comfortable grounded descent/hold/rise and current counted preparation. P2 stays grounded; E1 contains the first flight attempts. A familiar grounded primary task can instead accompany eligible first hinge loading, with selected grip/support observed inside counted handling before repetitions.

OR-07 first walking exit uses the already controlled corresponding stop and comfortable ordinary stepping/turning. The prior whole exit need not already be mastered. P2 can establish a walking stop; it cannot establish a jogging stop. First gentle-jog stopping therefore stays terminal-only until its own stop evidence supports the additional exit. Supported stance grants neither permission.

OR-08's first modest long-route effort needs actual repeatable easy work on that route. An athlete with direct repeatable modest-route evidence may retain that work without reconstructing an older easy record. E0 is a walk, so it cannot establish running repeatability. OR-09 first known-cue throwing uses its component handling/conduct gates; a first or emerging E0 throw cannot simultaneously supply the prior repeatability needed for first choice instruction. OR-10 first goblet uses repeatable compatible bodyweight control, then the actual fitting implement's pickup/chest support inside the counted set; failed handling can consume the set before any squat occurs.

Unknown historical dose and unfamiliar current movement are different. OR-06–09 permit specific small counted bodyweight or high-support teaching observations when independently suitable and actually supervised. They are not all familiar-only lessons. Their at-most-two or otherwise reduced packet is a dose bound, not evidence that a new setup is suitable. Extra individual instruction uses real time and may require a later omission. An unfamiliar bench/DB row does not become eligible through fewer repetitions: OR-09 retains independently qualified pull handling or defers that role. Push, pull and brace suitability have separate records. OR-10's secondary hip, push and pull clocks are stricter: use familiar current setups, retain eligible other roles and arrange missing instruction separately when those setups are unfamiliar. The heel-tap/breathing choice always keeps its own floor/support and movement gates.

All age bands use their complete explicit prescriptions. Ages 12–14 are the reference, not beginners; an experienced younger athlete and an older newcomer can take different independently eligible routes. No birthday, elapsed week or mode label creates competency.

## Recovery and usefulness across the offering

If all five complete Strength menus are performed, they contain five one-set roles per visit: 25 nominal station sets across the week. This is a count of roles, not equivalent muscular work or proof of recovery; supported holds, breathing, pressing and loaded squats differ. Partial instruction, failed handling, reductions and omissions must remain in the actual record. Recurring base floor support, hinge/squat observations, upper-body handling and the separate program add demand outside that nominal set count.

OR-06 → OR-07 → OR-08 concentrates landing/grounded control, braking/stance and running/runoff over three adjacent offerings. Their different primary labels do not eliminate overlapping lower-body demand. Before each visit, inspect current movement/control, recent discomfort or unusual fatigue, sport/tumbling commitments and the actually completed earlier work. A row after hinge or ball handling is not grip rest. OR-10 returns to a knee-resistance/handling decision while retaining starts. It therefore needs current lower-body, grip and attentional readiness, not merely enough elapsed minutes since the prior set. Long inter-attempt recovery and a smaller menu support the teaching aim; they do not demonstrate that five visits suit every athlete's outside schedule.

One or two weekly visits should remain useful instructional exposures with retained work in mastered domains. They should not automatically inherit the smallest dose intended for a fatigued frequent attendee, nor be filled with a week's missed work. Three, four or five visits require a deliberate mixture of development and reduced practice based on actual response. The ledger's D/L sequences describe available assignments; a mathematical all-D path is not a recommendation to attend every day at development demand.

The coach records the exact branch and response after the visit and uses those observations at the next relevant exposure. If the surrounding sport calendar leaves inadequate recovery, adjust scheduling or attendance as well as the selected gym dose. The block strategy's source-backed recovery principles remain the basis; this audit establishes no universal weekly contact, tonnage or fatigue threshold.
