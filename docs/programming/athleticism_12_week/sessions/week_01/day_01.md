**SP-02, ages 9-11, 12-14, 15-18:** Two backward and two forward steps, then settle (3 × 6 s). Tempo: About 1 second per step plus 2-second settle each cycle. Effort: Easy and conversational; no maximal speed, height, stretch or fatigue target. Entry: Comfortable action, clear personal bay and ability to stop on cue. Contacts: No intentional flight; ordinary support/steps are not scored plyometric landings.

**SP-03, ages 9-11, 12-14, 15-18:** 1 m right then 1 m left (2 × 8 s). Tempo: 4 seconds per traversal; settled direction change, no hard plant. Effort: Easy and conversational; no maximal speed, height, stretch or fatigue target. Entry: Comfortable action, clear personal bay and ability to stop on cue. ### S5 — Low-load moving-limb brace practice (Strength)

**Mapping:** HEEL-TAP:source66 arms-fixed heel-tap candidate; placement in Strength is a proposed contextual adaptation, not a verified Strength profile **Set purpose / contribution:** Each set rehearses trunk organization during controlled alternating leg motion at low fatigue. It fills the explicit brace role without claiming it adds another heavy strength stimulus.

**Setup and execution:** Lie on a stable mat, arms vertical and still, hips/knees in comfortable tabletop. Lower one bent leg to a declared light heel target, return to tabletop, then alternate. Use comfortable breathing and a shorter target range before forcing floor contact.

**Cues:** Arms still; light heel; quiet trunk; return before switching. **Common errors:** Moving the opposite arm, sliding the heel, arching, holding the breath, confusing eight total taps with eight pairs or forcing a low target.

**Selection / order / pairing:** This small moving-limb control task adds little grip demand after the hinge and helps preserve shoulder/wrist capacity for tumbling. A loaded carry would add grip and travel constraints already present elsewhere; it is not required to fill the brace role today.
