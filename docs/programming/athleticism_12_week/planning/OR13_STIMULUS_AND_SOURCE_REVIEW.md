## Intended stimulus and continuity

**OR-07/OR-11 controlled stopped exit + OR-09 cue/response evidence → OR-13 early choice at unchanged movement demand → OR-17 retained-rule observation and OR-20 individual domain handoff.** These are relevant opportunities, not mandatory attendance. An OR-09 throw result does not establish travel or a new left/right rule; an OR-11 stopped exit does not establish throwing, withholding or a reactive cut. OR-12 adds useful report/wait behavior only where actually observed; attendance provides no release authority or movement pass.

The proposed authoring direction is to resolve the directional information **before the athlete starts the approach**, then retain the known controlled stop, actual positive pause, ordinary opening-step exit and finish. Declare cue modality, meanings, number of choices, timing relative to a separate coach release, and what happens when recognition is uncertain. No response deadline or faster movement is needed for this objective. A clear acknowledgement may occur in the allocated instruction/cue time; any athlete step, rehearsal or false start is physical work and must be counted.

Only early choice changes. Do not simultaneously introduce the first whole stopped exit, first jog stop, shorter pause, faster approach, unfamiliar direction, smaller braking zone, new throw load or new strength handling. Retain each direction’s actual compatible pause and approach rather than choosing the shorter OR-11 option because it was offered. If different directions have different actual contexts, later authoring must either resolve that asymmetry explicitly within the allowed choice set or retain a known-direction task; a nominal left/right choice cannot hide an unqualified direction.
