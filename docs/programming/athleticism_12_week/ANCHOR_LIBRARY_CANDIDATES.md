## Evidence status and identity rules

- **C — authored canonical candidate:** a local contract or migration explicitly authors a candidate definition/variant. Its recorded review state is not publication, a live facility identity, or permission to deploy it.
- **R — research packet:** a local research assessment contains identity findings, alternatives and/or snapshots. It is useful for resolving synonyms but is not itself a live card.
- **L — legacy authored card:** actual seed-card content contains a stable source slug, instructions, dose defaults and metadata. A later canonical consolidation may have changed its owner or selectable variants.
- **F — existing format/taxonomy:** schema or taxonomy supports a programming structure. This does not establish a separately approved programming-method card.

No live canonical UUID or programming-method numeric ID is claimed here. Numeric source IDs explicitly identified below are legacy source identities from local contracts. Candidate variant keys are copied from local content, not invented. Candidate dose fields can inform later review; none is adopted as a prescription in this register.

## Constraints that affect the outline now

1. **RUN requires a real speed corridor.** Flying Sprint needs a declared buildup, measured fly zone and safe run-out. Three short teaching lanes do not establish that corridor. If it cannot be verified, prescribe a separately named acceleration exposure and retain upright-rhythm practice at its actual intensity. Record “upright high-speed exposure not delivered” and keep the fallback on its own progression ledger.
2. **A march or skip is not a sprint exposure.** No-flight A-March and step-hop A-Skip are different tasks; neither proves that maximal velocity was trained. In the ACC strength label, “knee” means knee-dominant resistance work. A knee-lift rhythm drill is preparation, not its replacement.
3. **Stick, rebound, measured jump and turn-and-exit are different contracts.** Their names cannot be interchanged to preserve an apparent anchor streak. Hold/measurement, contact counts, landing action, direction and cue must be recorded.
4. **REACT must include an actual unpredictable cue.** A preannounced cone route is planned movement. An auditory go signal tests simple initiation; a directional visual choice adds decision demand. Change the ledger and success criterion when that demand changes.
5. **Time includes retrieval, side changes and recovery.** Ball throws need a controlled return/retrieval lane; unilateral lower work requires both sides; carry lanes cannot cross running lanes. A second station is recovery only if it does not compromise the first task.
6. **Method names need semantic checking.** Several locally available methods that sound suitable for quality practice actually target repeatability under fatigue. Do not use their names to give recovered speed, technical learning or strength an inaccurate library mapping.

## Exact gates before Stage 3 final mapping

1. Retrieve a current, authorized read-only facility export or equivalent records with definition ID/slug/version, exact variant, delivery profile, lifecycle status and current approval evidence. Resolve legacy consolidation and archived/quarantined owners; do not promote sources or infer approval from migration literals.
2. Verify the exact instruction/media match, eligibility/prerequisites, reviewed profile/taxonomy and progression/substitution relationships. Candidate media URLs and metadata do not close these gates.
3. Match the chosen organization to an actual programming-method record when one exists. Otherwise preserve an accurately labeled existing format/proposed organization and explicitly resolve the method-authoring/review need before claiming final canonical coverage. Do not fabricate method IDs or remap fatigue methods.
4. Confirm facility dimensions, run-out, floor/throw separation, equipment type/size/quantity, anchored supports, group size and coaching coverage. Model demonstrations, turns, side changes, returns, resets, recovery and transitions inside each component.
5. Use each athlete's completed variant/dose/results/readiness and recent sport/tumbling load to choose the starting exposure. Missing histories remain unknown, not zeros or novice status.
6. Finalize all three requested age-band prescriptions plus within-band readiness alternatives, with set purpose, actual effort/rest, success/stop rules and prior/current/next linkage. One controlled change can be a retained variant/dose; novelty is not required.
7. Verify the separate managed tumbling session, prerequisites and load interface. A handoff or locally seeded skill is not a completed approved 30-minute session.

**Stage 2 conclusion:** tangible local source candidates exist for all five focus families and the full-body strength requirements. Geometry, current approval and exact method-card coverage remain conditional. The outline can use these families and honestly named formats now; this document does not make the later sessions coach-ready.
