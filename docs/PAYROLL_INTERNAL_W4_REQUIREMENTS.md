# Internal 2026 W-4 completion

The current employee onboarding UI requires an uploaded signed W-4 or a secure-provider receipt. That is not yet an internal form-completion journey. The native flow must capture employee answers, present the official information, retain the final signature and exact submitted content, produce a readable hard copy, and feed reviewed tax elections without administrator retyping.

Sources checked September 12, 2026: [IRS Publication 15-T, electronic and substitute W-4 requirements](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15t), [official 2026 W-4 and instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw4.pdf). The retained template is `backend/payroll/forms/irs-w4-2026.pdf`, five pages, SHA-256 `92444d8856ce55d9e25dca8b6d1420634fc68b11e1ab1f760916ea29ddd312b2`. This pins the reviewed edition; a mutable remote URL must not silently change what employees sign.

Implementation requirements:

- Preserve the required face-of-form wording and all choices from Step 1(c) through Step 4(c), including the exemption section. Required wording must not be hidden by default or only shown in hover/pop-up content. Provide the complete official instructions/worksheets and the official link.
- Keep all three filing-status choices, Step 2(c), unrestricted whole-cent Step 3 totals (not only multiples of dependent credits), Steps 4(a)–4(c), the exemption checkbox/conditions and a nonresident-alien field. The 2026 form uses $2,200 in Step 3(a); do not carry older year wording into this form.
- For an exempt submission, the official instructions require personal information and signature only; contradictory completed steps need correction rather than silent deletion. Capture must not imply that an exempt or nonresident submission is automatically supported by every payroll calculation path.
- Preserve employee identity, answers, exact perjury wording, form/template revision, authenticated session, final signature and submission timestamp. Signature is the final entry; editing answers afterward requires a new review/signing step. Record submission accesses and retain evidence of the named employee's submission.
- Encrypt sensitive identity/form data with the existing scoped document vault. Do not put SSNs or complete forms in onboarding response JSON, ordinary audit messages, URLs, browser storage, or logs. A missing production vault key is a configuration issue, not permission to store plaintext.
- Produce and verify a hard-copy PDF containing the submitted information. Retain all five official pages. Validate both canonical AcroForm values and page widget appearances; inspect rendered output for clipping. Preserve the untouched source template.
- Bind a review intent to the exact payload and employee/onboarding cycle. Revalidate the employee session under the employer lock at signing, make retries idempotent, and reject stale intents. Signing, encrypted document creation, submission audit and task transition must be atomic.
- Link the submitted form to administrator review and downstream federal elections without fabricating a signature or treating an administrator's edit as the employee's response. Changed answers require an employee amendment. Existing uploaded forms remain usable during the transition.
- Test the entire browser journey, return/reload, amendment, response loss, concurrent signing, revoked session, cross-employee access, encrypted storage, PDF contents, and calculation-source changes. Verify the new form capture against the actual full onboarding journey.

This is groundwork only: no native form screen, signed PDF, storage/submission transaction or automatic tax-election integration has been implemented yet.
