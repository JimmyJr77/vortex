# Vortex 12-Week Athletic Development Program Library

**Version:** 1.0  
**Audience:** Qualified coaches delivering small-group or individual athletic development  
**Model:** Three progressive four-week blocks; clear session themes; measurable outcomes; readiness-based advancement  
**Program length:** 12 weeks, normally 3 nonconsecutive sessions per week

This is an original Vortex coaching system informed by long-term athletic-development principles and the clear, outcome-led packaging used by athletic performance brands such as Overtime Athletes. It is not a reproduction of another provider's program.

---

## 1. Use, safety, and scope

These are general performance-training templates, not medical care or return-to-play plans. A qualified coach must screen the athlete, select loads, supervise youth, and substitute around pain, disability, space, equipment, sport practice, and competition. An athlete with pain, dizziness, altered movement, a recent injury, or a relevant medical condition needs appropriate medical clearance.

### Non-negotiable rules

1. **Pain rule:** Stop any drill that causes sharp, escalating, radiating, or joint pain. Do not “train through” altered mechanics.
2. **Quality rule:** End a set when posture, landing, bar path, sprint mechanics, or rep speed clearly deteriorates.
3. **Jump rule:** Land quietly with controlled knee/hip alignment. Stop the jump block after two poor landings or a visible drop in jump height.
4. **Sprint rule:** Give full recovery for speed work. Stop after two repetitions slower than approximately 95% of the athlete's best quality rep, or when mechanics deteriorate.
5. **Loading rule:** Finish most strength sets with **2–4 good repetitions in reserve (RIR)**. No missed reps in routine training.
6. **Progression rule:** Change only one primary variable at a time: load, volume, range, speed, complexity, or density.
7. **Youth rule:** Athletes under 15 use technique-first resistance training. No 1RM testing, forced reps, ballistic maximal lifting, or unsupervised loaded work.
8. **Recovery rule:** Keep at least 48 hours between demanding exposures to the same quality. Reduce this plan when sport practices or games already supply sprint and jump volume.
9. **Power rule:** Power work happens early, while fresh. Conditioning never turns maximal-speed, high-skill, or high-impact work into fatigued repetitions.
10. **Readiness rule:** Age brackets are guardrails, not automatic levels. A technically inexperienced 17-year-old starts with foundation prescriptions.

### Session readiness check (60 seconds)

Ask for sleep, soreness, pain, stress, and desire to train on a 1–5 scale; observe three bodyweight squats, two snap-downs, and a 10 m build-up.

- **Green:** normal movement, no pain, readiness near baseline → planned session.
- **Yellow:** poor sleep, unusual soreness, or reduced coordination → remove 1 set from loaded and impact work; cap at RPE 6.
- **Red:** pain, illness, dizziness, or materially altered movement → no performance session; refer appropriately.

## 2. Age and readiness brackets

| Bracket | Typical session | Strength prescription | Jump contacts / session | Sprint volume / session | Coaching emphasis |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Foundation: 8–11** | 45–55 min | 1–3 × 8–12, bodyweight/light external load, RPE 5–6 | 20–35 low/moderate | 80–160 m | Play, landing, rhythm, positions, broad movement vocabulary |
| **Development: 12–14** | 55–65 min | 2–4 × 6–12, RPE 6–7, 3+ RIR | 25–45 | 120–240 m | Technique, deceleration, bilateral/unilateral balance |
| **Performance: 15–18** | 60–75 min | 3–5 × 3–10, RPE 6–8, 2–4 RIR | 30–55 | 160–320 m | Force, velocity, reactive ability, individualized deficits |
| **Adult: 19+** | 60–80 min | 3–6 × 2–12, normally RPE 6–8 | 25–60 based on history | 160–400 m | Goal specificity, load management, tissue tolerance |

**Masters or deconditioned adults:** use the Development volume column for the first four weeks, favor low-amplitude jumps, and add load only after recovery is predictable.

### Advancement gate

An athlete advances an exercise only after two sessions with:

- all prescribed reps completed;
- stable posture and full intended range;
- controlled landings/decelerations;
- no pain during or the following day;
- target RIR/RPE maintained; and
- understanding of the coach's key cue without constant correction.

If the gate is not met, repeat the variation or regress it. The calendar does not override competency.

## 3. Universal 12-week progression

Every program below uses the same three-block spine. The exercise-specific tables say what changes.

| Weeks | Block | Intent | Prescription |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | Foundation | Learn positions; establish repeatable baseline | W1 conservative baseline; W2 add 1–2 reps or one low-volume set; W3 add 2.5–5% load or modest complexity only if the advancement gate is met |
| **4** | Consolidate | Absorb, refine, and recheck | Reduce main volume 30–40%; keep quality high; repeat the program KPI without maximal fatigue |
| **5–7** | Build | Increase usable force or velocity | W5 introduce the Block 2 variation; W6 add a set or small load; W7 highest quality volume, never grinding |
| **8** | Consolidate | Reduce fatigue, preserve speed | Reduce volume 35–45%; retain moderate intensity; submaximal KPI check |
| **9–10** | Express | Convert capacity to faster or more specific output | Lower repetitions; increase intent, speed, or specificity; use full recovery |
| **11** | Peak quality | Best outputs with low fatigue | Reduce accessory volume 20–30%; stop well before failure |
| **12** | Taper and assess | Demonstrate progress | Two light exposures early in week; assess 48–72 hours after last hard session |

### Weekly placement

| Day | Emphasis | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **Session A** | Acceleration / jump + primary strength | Readiness → dynamic warm-up → neural/power → main lift → assistance → core → mobility |
| **Session B** | Agility / rotation + unilateral strength | Readiness → dynamic warm-up → movement skill → rotation → strength → isometric → mobility |
| **Session C** | Max velocity / elastic power + total body | Readiness → dynamic warm-up → speed/plyometric → total-body strength → carries/core → recovery |

For a **single-goal plan**, perform that goal's A/B/C sessions. For a **complete-athlete plan**, use Section 7.

### Standard warm-up (8–12 minutes)

1. Raise: skip, bike, or easy game — 2 minutes.
2. Mobilize: ankle rocks, adductor rock-back, half-kneeling hip shift, open book — 5 each side.
3. Activate: glute bridge, dead bug, scapular push-up — 6–8 each.
4. Potentiate: march → skip → two build-ups or snap-down → pogo — 2 rounds.

## 4. Goal programs

Notation: `sets × reps`; distances are per repetition; unilateral prescriptions are per side. Rest 45–75 seconds for teaching/accessories, 90–150 seconds for strength, and 2–4 minutes for maximal jump/sprint work.

### G01 — Rotational Strength

**KPI:** standing medicine-ball scoop toss distance or velocity, both sides.  
**Equipment:** medicine ball, cable/band, landmine optional.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Hip-to-hand force** | Split-stance band lift 3×8; med-ball scoop toss 4×3; goblet split squat 3×8; half-kneeling Pallof press 3×8; suitcase carry 3×20 m |
| **B — Decelerate rotation** | Step-behind med-ball shot-put 4×3; cable/band chop 3×8; lateral lunge 3×8; tall-kneeling anti-rotation hold 3×20 s; dead bug pullover 3×6 |
| **C — Transfer and express** | Rotational wall toss 5×3; landmine rotation or band pivot press 3×6; single-leg RDL 3×8; side plank row 3×8; cross-body carry 3×20 m |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Tall/split stance; pause at catch; 70–80% throw intent; own ribcage-over-pelvis position |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Add step, pivot, or lateral transfer; 80–90% intent; slightly heavier strength work |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Reactive partner cue or sport stance; light ball at maximal safe velocity; reduce throw reps in W11–12 |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 uses 1–2 kg ball and band patterns; 12–14 uses 2–3 kg; 15–18 typically 2–4 kg; adults 2–5 kg. Ball speed and clean sequencing matter more than ball mass.

### G02 — Jump Power and Height

**KPI:** countermovement jump (same device and arm rule each test) plus standing broad jump.  
**Do not chase contacts:** every repetition should look like an attempt, not conditioning.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Vertical force** | Snap-down 3×3; countermovement jump 5×2; squat 4×6; rear-foot-elevated split squat 3×6; calf isometric 3×25 s |
| **B — Horizontal force** | Pogo 3×10; broad jump with stick 5×2; Romanian deadlift 4×6; step-up 3×8; dead bug 3×8 |
| **C — Approach and elastic** | Low hurdle hop 4×3; approach jump 5×2; hip thrust 4×6; lateral lunge 3×6; soleus raise 3×12 |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Stick landings, low amplitude, 3–5 seconds reset between reps |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Repeated pogo/hurdle rhythm; light trap-bar or dumbbell jump only for competent 15+ athletes; keep loads light |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Depth drop to jump only after landing gate; approach/cued jumps; low volume and full rest |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 performs 3–4 jump sets and no loaded jumps; 12–14 may use a very light countermovement load only with expert coaching; 15–18/adults use the table volume. Depth is earned—start from 15–30 cm, not an arbitrary high box.

### G03 — General Strength

**KPI:** technically consistent 6RM estimate or submaximal rep test in squat, push, hinge, and pull patterns. No youth 1RM.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Squat + horizontal push/pull** | Goblet/front squat 4×6–8; push-up/DB bench 4×6–10; 1-arm row 4×8; split squat 3×8; farmer carry 3×25 m |
| **B — Hinge + vertical push/pull** | KB/trap-bar deadlift 4×6; half-kneeling press 3×8; assisted pull-up 4×6–8; step-up 3×8; Pallof press 3×8 |
| **C — Total-body unilateral** | Front-foot-elevated split squat 4×6; hip thrust 4×8; incline press 3×8; chest-supported row 3×10; suitcase carry 3×20 m |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | 8–12 reps, 3–4 RIR, controlled eccentric; master range |
| **Weeks 5–8** | 6–8 reps, 2–4 RIR; add load in 2.5–5% steps after all reps are clean |
| **Weeks 9–12** | 4–6 reps for competent 15+/adults; youth remain 6–10; W12 uses submaximal rep-quality assessment |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 uses 1–3 sets and bodyweight/goblet loading; 12–14 uses 2–4 sets; 15–18/adults use full prescription. Never progress load and range/complexity together.

### G04 — Explosiveness

**KPI:** 10 m sprint plus medicine-ball chest throw or broad jump.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Lower-body rate of force** | Wall switch 3×5; 10 m sprint 6 reps; broad jump 4×2; squat 4×5; sled push 5×10 m |
| **B — Upper-body projection** | Med-ball chest pass 5×3; overhead backward throw 4×3; push press pattern 4×5; pull-up/row 4×6; split squat 3×6 |
| **C — Total-body contrast** | Jump 3×2 paired with squat 3×4; scoop toss 4×3 paired with landmine press 3×5; 15 m sprint 5 reps; carry 3×20 m |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Separate skill and strength; long resets; submaximal starts |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Add resisted sprint or light ballistic implement; pair one strength movement with one power movement |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Contrast work for competent athletes; maximal intent, minimal fatigue; halve accessory volume in W11 |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 uses throws, jumps, games, and no complex loaded ballistic lift; 12–14 may use light push press; Olympic-lift derivatives are reserved for coached, competent 15+ athletes.

### G05 — Speed

**KPI:** electronic or consistently hand-timed 10 m and flying 10 m. Record surface, shoes, start, and wind.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Acceleration** | Wall march/switch 2×5; falling start 4×10 m; 3-point start 4×15 m; sled march/push 4×10 m; split squat 3×6 |
| **B — Max velocity** | A-skip 2×15 m; dribble run 3×15 m; build-up + fly 10 m × 5; wicket run 4 passes; hamstring bridge 3×8 |
| **C — Speed transfer** | Pursuit or chase start 6×10–15 m; curved sprint 4×20 m; bounds 3×10 m; RDL 3×6; calf/soleus work 3×10 |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Posture, projection, stop mechanics; 80–90% build-ups |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Longer acceleration (15–25 m), fly 10s, light sled resistance that preserves shape |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Competitive/cued starts, fly 10–20 based on space; reduce total sprint volume 25% in W11 |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 uses races and 10–15 m reps with 60–90 s rest; 12–14 uses 15–25 m with 90–150 s; 15–18/adults use 20–40 m and 2–4 min rest. Never use speed sessions as conditioning.

### G06 — Agility

**KPI:** 5-10-5 shuttle plus coach-scored deceleration (trunk, knee/foot alignment, steps to stop).

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Deceleration** | Snap-down 3×3; run-to-stick 5×5 m; lateral shuffle-to-stick 4×side; lateral lunge 3×8; Copenhagen plank regression 3×15 s |
| **B — Planned change of direction** | 45° cut 4×side; 90° cut 4×side; 5-10-5 technique 4 reps; split squat 3×6; calf isometric 3×25 s |
| **C — Reactive agility** | Mirror drill 5×8 s; color/point call 6×5–10 m; partner gate race 5 reps; single-leg RDL 3×6; side plank 3×20 s |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Planned stops and cuts at 60–80%; clear center of mass control |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Faster entry, sharper angles, shuffle-to-sprint and crossover |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Unplanned visual cue, partner/opponent, sport-relevant space; cap work bouts at 3–8 seconds |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 uses tag/mirror games and wide angles; 12–14 blends planned and reactive; 15–18/adults receive more sport-specific perception demands. The cone is not the stimulus—an external cue is.

### G07 — Core Strength

**KPI:** quality side-plank time capped at 60 s plus controlled loaded carry distance. Avoid exhaustive sit-up tests.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Anti-extension** | Dead bug 3×6; body saw or long-lever plank 3×15–30 s; bear crawl 3×10 m; goblet squat 3×8; farmer carry 3×25 m |
| **B — Anti-rotation/lateral** | Pallof press 3×8; side plank 3×20–40 s; suitcase carry 4×20 m; split squat 3×8; 1-arm row 3×8 |
| **C — Integrated trunk** | Half-kneeling chop/lift 3×8; bird dog row 3×6; cross-body march 3×10; push-up 3×8; overhead carry 3×15 m |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Short levers and static control; nasal exhale without losing position |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Longer lever, unilateral load, crawling, and marching |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Dynamic limb action around a stable trunk and asymmetrical carries; never trade spinal position for load |

**Age scaling:** use the same exercises; change lever length, load, and set duration. Stop static holds at 45–60 seconds and progress difficulty instead of chasing endurance records.

Foundation 8–11 athletes use 15–25 second holds and light carries; Development 12–14 uses 20–35 seconds; Performance 15–18 and adults use 20–45 seconds with harder levers or loads. Position—not survival time—determines the set.

### G08 — Plyometric

**KPI:** reactive strength index when equipment is available; otherwise repeated pogo consistency plus broad jump.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Landing and vertical elastic** | Snap-down 3×3; drop landing 4×2; pogo 4×10; squat jump 4×2; calf isometric 3×25 s |
| **B — Horizontal/lateral** | Broad jump stick 4×2; skater bound stick 4×3/side; line hop 3×8/side; split squat 3×6; adductor plank 3×15 s |
| **C — Reactive rhythm** | Low hurdle hop 5×3; jump-rope rhythm 4×20 s; skip for height 3×15 m; hip hinge 3×8; soleus raise 3×12 |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Landing → pause → reset; low-amplitude extensive contacts |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Short combinations of 2–3 contacts; bilateral to controlled unilateral |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Fast ground contacts and low box depth jump only after competency; reduce contacts 30–50% before test |

**Age scaling:** Foundation 20–35 contacts; Development 25–45; Performance/adult 30–55. Jump rope and skips count as extensive contacts but not one-for-one with maximal jumps. Do not add height when landing quality is marginal.

### G09 — Isometric Strength

**KPI:** standardized overcoming isometric pull on force plate when available; otherwise quality split-squat and calf hold at a fixed joint angle.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Lower yielding** | Split-squat hold 4×20–30 s; wall sit 3×30 s; calf hold 3×25 s; push-up 3×8; farmer carry 3×20 m |
| **B — Upper/trunk yielding** | Push-up midpoint hold 4×10–20 s; flexed-arm hang 4×10–20 s; side plank 3×20 s; RDL 3×8; suitcase carry 3×20 m |
| **C — Overcoming/angle specific** | Mid-thigh pull against pins 5×3–5 s; isometric split squat against immovable pins 4×3–5 s; Pallof hold 3×20 s; row 3×8; mobility reset |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Yielding holds at mid-range, 15–30 s, smooth breathing |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Longer lever or load; 20–40 s; introduce submaximal overcoming efforts |
| **Weeks 9–12** | 3–6 s high-intent overcoming work for competent 15+/adults; use multiple relevant joint angles |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 and 12–14 stay mainly with yielding holds and games; 15–18/adults may use supervised overcoming isometrics. Avoid prolonged breath-holding.

### G10 — Neural Training

Here “neural” means low-fatigue coordination, reaction, rhythm, stiffness regulation, and rapid force intent—not vague brain hacks.

**KPI:** reaction drill accuracy plus first-step time, balance errors, or rhythm-task completion.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Reaction and first step** | Ball-drop reaction 6 reps; partner point start 6×5 m; rapid switch 3×5/side; low pogo 3×10; split squat 3×6 |
| **B — Rhythm and coordination** | A-march/A-skip 3×15 m; ladder or floor-line rhythm 6 patterns; cross-crawl 3×20 s; med-ball catch/toss 4×5; carry 3×20 m |
| **C — Perception/action** | Mirror drill 5×6 s; numbered gate call 6 reps; single-leg balance with toss 3×20 s; reactive jump 4×2; row/push-up 3×8 |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | One cue and one response; predictable rhythm; high success |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Two-choice reactions, faster rhythm, mild dual-task |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Uncertain cue timing, opponent information, sport-relevant choice; keep bouts under 10 s |

**Age scaling:** 8–11 receives game-heavy sessions; 12–14 adds choice; 15–18/adults add sport-specific information. Stop when errors rise—fatigue changes the target.

### G11 — Mobility

**KPI:** knee-to-wall ankle distance, active straight-leg raise, shoulder flexion wall test, and pain-free squat depth. Mobility is usable range with control.

| Session | Foundation movement menu |
|---|---|
| **A — Ankle/hip** | Knee-to-wall 2×8; calf eccentric 3×8; 90/90 hip switch 3×6; adductor rock-back 2×8; goblet squat pry 3×20 s |
| **B — Thoracic/shoulder** | Open book 2×6; quadruped rotation 2×6; wall slide 3×8; scapular push-up 3×8; half-kneeling pulldown 3×8 |
| **C — Integrated control** | Cossack squat 3×6; controlled articular hip circle 2×3; split-squat ankle drive 3×6; overhead squat reach 3×6; loaded carry 3×20 m |

| Block | Exercise progression |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Find pain-free range; slow exhale; low load |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Add end-range isometric 10–20 s and light load |
| **Weeks 9–12** | Integrate new range into squat, lunge, reach, crawl, and overhead patterns |

**Age scaling:** all ages use 5–8 controlled reps. Younger athletes need movement variety more than long passive stretching. Never force range or stretch into nerve-like symptoms.

Foundation 8–11 sessions favor crawling, reaching, squatting, and games through varied ranges; Development 12–14 adds brief end-range control; Performance 15–18 and adults may add light loaded mobility after demonstrating pain-free active range.

|---|---|

## 7. Complete-athlete 12-week schedule

This option trains all requested qualities without trying to run eleven full programs at once.

| Day | Main elements | Goal coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Linear output** | Acceleration; vertical jump; squat; push/pull; anti-extension; ankle mobility | Speed, jump height, strength, explosiveness, plyometric, core, mobility |
| **B — Multidirectional output** | Neural reaction; agility; rotational throw; unilateral strength; split-squat isometric; hip mobility | Neural, agility, rotational strength, strength, isometric, mobility |

### Complete-athlete sessions

**Session A**

1. Falling start: 4–6×10–20 m.
2. Countermovement jump: 4–5×2.
3. Squat variation: 3–4×6–8.
4. DB bench or push-up: 3×8.
5. Row/pull-up: 3×8.
6. Dead bug: 3×6/side.
7. Knee-to-wall: 2×8/side.

**Session B**

1. Ball-drop or point reaction: 6 reps.
2. 45°/90° cut: 3–4×side.
3. Med-ball scoop toss: 4×3/side.
4. Split squat: 3–4×6/side.
5. 1-arm row: 3×8/side.
6. Split-squat isometric: 3×20–30 s/side.
7. 90/90 hip switch: 2×6.

**Session C**

1. Build-up + fly 10: 4–6 reps.
2. Broad jump or low hurdle hop: 4×2–3.
3. RDL/deadlift variation: 3–4×6.
4. Med-ball chest pass or light push press: 4×3–5.
5. Step-up: 3×8/side.
6. Suitcase carry: 3×20 m/side.
7. Open book + wall slide: 2×6 each.

Apply the Universal 12-week progression and age-bracket volume. Choose **one** equipment track for the strength slots; do not stack a full equipment program on top.

## 8. Assessment and tracking

Test in Week 1 after familiarization, recheck submaximally in Weeks 4 and 8, and formally reassess in Week 12. Use the same protocol, surface, equipment, warm-up, time of day when practical, and number of attempts.

| Quality | Primary measure | Secondary coach measure |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation | Scoop toss distance/velocity L/R | Side-to-side difference and sequencing |
| Jump | Countermovement jump | Broad jump and landing score |
| Strength | Submaximal load × clean reps | RIR and technique score |
| Explosiveness | 10 m + chest throw | Rep velocity if available |
| Speed | 10 m and fly 10 | Video mechanics |
| Agility | 5-10-5 | Deceleration score |
| Core | Carry load/distance | Side-plank quality |
| Plyometric | RSI or pogo consistency | Contact/landing quality |
| Isometric | Force-time measure or fixed hold | Joint-angle position |
| Neural | Reaction accuracy/time | Error rate under choice |
| Mobility | Knee-to-wall, ASLR, shoulder flexion | Pain-free squat/reach |

### Session log

Record:

- date, session, athlete, readiness color;
- exercise variation, sets, reps, load, RPE/RIR;
- sprint time or jump/throw result;
- pain or technique stop;
- next-session progression/regression decision; and
- sport-practice/game load that may affect recovery.

An improvement is meaningful only when the protocol and technique remain comparable.

## 9. Coach delivery checklist

Before the cycle:

- obtain health/injury history and required clearance;
- identify training age, sport schedule, growth-spurt symptoms, and equipment access;
- choose one primary outcome and no more than two secondary outcomes;
- perform technique familiarization before baseline testing;
- select regressions in advance.

Every session:

- run readiness check and inspect the space;
- state the day's quality target;
- demonstrate and rehearse before loading;
- place speed/power before fatigue;
- document stops and substitutions;
- finish with a low-intensity restore block.

After each four-week block:

- compare KPI, technique, attendance, and recovery;
- progress only athletes who pass the advancement gate;
- change the minimum effective number of variables;
- coordinate volume with team practices and competition.

## 10. Evidence basis

The design follows these current principles:

- The American Academy of Pediatrics emphasizes developmental appropriateness, qualified supervision, dynamic warm-up/cool-down, broad fitness integration, technique, and evaluation of injury or overuse symptoms.
- ACSM youth guidance supports approximately 1–3 sets of 6–15 repetitions, 2–3 nonconsecutive days per week, with gradual progression based on technical competency.
- The NSCA long-term athletic-development model prioritizes physical literacy, motor skill, foundational strength, safe progression, and avoidance of overtraining and premature specialization.
- AAP public guidance cautions against maximal lifting and unsupervised powerlifting-style work before skeletal maturity. This library therefore separates powerlifting-inspired adult programming from youth technique substitutions.
