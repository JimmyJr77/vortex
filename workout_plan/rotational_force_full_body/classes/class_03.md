### E1 — Split-stance explosive hip pivot — grounded, proposed exercise

**Connection and coaching:** Support → output: Carry foot-led initiation from the pivot into the paused scoop without an extra wind-up. Isolate the support-foot release and hip-turn initiation before adding a swinging leg. Mirror the stance for the opposite direction. Keep knees soft and following toes; reduce turn size if a foot sticks, knee twists or trunk leads the turn. Stop if the surface will not permit controlled pivoting.

### E2 — Med Ball Rotational Scoop Toss — paused standing start; floor-target adaptation

**Connection and coaching:** Support → output: Carry foot-led initiation from the pivot into the paused scoop without an extra wind-up. Primary anchor: generate rotational projection from a settled load. Stand side-on, feet at a comfortable athletic width; hold the ball near the rear hip and pause 2 seconds. Pivot the rear foot, turn the hips/trunk, and sweep both hands through the target. No preparatory bounce or step. Finish balanced for 2 seconds. Reduce ball mass or turn range if the arms lead or the athlete spins out.

**Source:** [Upper body · Class 1 · E1](../../rotational_explosiveness_upper_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E3 — Standing low roundhouse shadow kick, proposed exercise

**Connection and coaching:** Accelerate → control: Keep the same controlled support-foot release when the output changes from kicking leg to pushing hand. Integrate stance-leg rotation with free-leg acceleration and recovery. Accelerate the hip/thigh briskly; keep a soft knee at extension and brake the leg actively. No target, impact, partner contact, head-height kick, jump or spinning kick. Use a shorter arc and less terminal extension instead if recoil or balance is lost. Never snap an unsupported knee into lockout.

**Source:** [Lower body · Class 1 · E2](../../rotational_explosiveness_lower_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E4 — Med Ball Shot-Put Throw — rotational, floor-target adaptation

**Connection and coaching:** Accelerate → control: Keep the same controlled support-foot release when the output changes from kicking leg to pushing hand. Finish the turn with a unilateral pushing release. Stagger the feet with the nonthrowing foot forward. Support the light ball at the rear shoulder with the other hand, turn through the rear foot/hip, then drive the throwing hand forward; the support hand guides and releases. Do not wind the arm behind the shoulder or turn it into an overhead baseball throw. Use a smaller/lighter grippable ball if wrist position or speed is lost.

**Source:** [Upper body · Class 1 · E4](../../rotational_explosiveness_upper_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E5 — 90-Degree Hop to Stick — two-foot countermovement execution

**Connection and coaching:** Recover → redirect: Use the jump to observe lower-body orientation and the kneeling throw to observe trunk control when leg drive is reduced. Express rapid leg-driven rotation using a countermovement, with a repeatable two-foot base. Land feet, knees and pelvis facing the same direction. Use a smaller 45° turn instead, with the same dose, if 90° cannot be completed before landing; record the changed angle. Do not screw the feet around after contact.

**Source:** [Lower body · Class 1 · E3](../../rotational_explosiveness_lower_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E6 — Half-Kneeling Rotational Med Ball Throw — floor-target adaptation

**Connection and coaching:** Recover → redirect: Use the jump to observe lower-body orientation and the kneeling throw to observe trunk control when leg drive is reduced. Express trunk-to-hand acceleration with leg propulsion reduced. Target-side foot forward, opposite knee comfortably supported on the floor; ball begins beside the rear hip. Use a small comfortable thoracic turn and fast two-hand release; do not force the pelvis to remain rigid while twisting farther. Keep the front foot planted and pelvis quiet. Shorten the turn if the knee shifts or back arches; use a seated bench rotational throw instead of this slot if kneeling contact is intolerable.

## Setup and delivery

Use the existing separate 10 m lanes. Keep waiting athletes outside the swept-leg, landing and ball paths. For released balls, verify a light grippable implement, permitted low floor target about 2–3 m beyond release, and containment of the entire flight/rebound/roll; use a throwing wall only after verification. Use a non-rebounding slam ball for slams. Clear the area between the leg drill and hand drill of each pair. Pairing is a coaching connection, not simultaneous stations or a throw immediately after a jump. Secure band/rope anchors and the landmine base. Kicks stay below hip height with a soft terminal knee, active recoil and no contact or spinning kick. Handoffs are controlled: secure the grip and wait for the feeder to release and step clear before turning. No new airborne-feed progression is added.
