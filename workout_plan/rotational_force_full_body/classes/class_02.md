### E1 — 90-Degree Hop to Stick — two-foot countermovement execution

**Connection and coaching:** Load → rotational output: Compare the single dip of the turning jump with the single compact preload of the ball throw; each load flows into one action. Express rapid leg-driven rotation using a countermovement, with a repeatable two-foot base. Land feet, knees and pelvis facing the same direction. Use a smaller 45° turn instead, with the same dose, if 90° cannot be completed before landing; record the changed angle. Do not screw the feet around after contact.

### E2 — Med Ball Countermovement Rotational Throw — floor-target adaptation

**Connection and coaching:** Load → rotational output: Compare the single dip of the turning jump with the single compact preload of the ball throw; each load flows into one action. Change a brief preload immediately into the same projection. Standing side-on, make one small, smooth wind-back and reverse promptly into the throw; no pause, repeated rocking or extra step. This differs from source E1's settled start. Keep the wind-back small enough that the trunk stays controlled. Reduce its amplitude if the athlete overtwists or delays the reversal.

**Source:** [Upper body · Class 1 · E3](../../rotational_explosiveness_upper_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E3 — Lateral Bound to Rotational Stick

**Connection and coaching:** Absorb → diagonal force: Own the rotational bound landing, then use a settled base to direct the side slam. The landing and slam are separate efforts with full recovery. Load the single-leg lateral plant that must accept the body before a hip turn. The turn occurs during flight; no twisting on touchdown. Choose distance inside a cleared 2 m travel segment and permit a separate clear 2 m recovery area. Shorten the bound/turn for pelvic drop or a recovery hop. If turn clearance/control is insufficient, use instead a short lateral bound to stick without the turn, same dose; retain a clear landing/recovery area.

**Source:** [Lower body · Class 1 · E4](../../rotational_explosiveness_lower_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E4 — Med Ball Overhead to Side Slam — non-rebounding slam-ball execution

**Connection and coaching:** Absorb → diagonal force: Own the rotational bound landing, then use a settled base to direct the side slam. The landing and slam are separate efforts with full recovery. Accelerate the hands diagonally downward through a turn and arrest the body's follow-through. From a comfortable overhead position, turn through the feet/hips and drive the ball to the floor slightly forward and outside the target-side foot, clear of toes. Stay long through the trunk; do not fold and twist at the low back. Lift/reset deliberately after the ball stops. Use a shoulder-height diagonal side slam instead of the overhead version if overhead range requires rib flare; same dose.

**Source:** [Upper body · Class 1 · E6](../../rotational_explosiveness_upper_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E5 — Lateral step-in to rotational plant — proposed grounded entry drill

**Connection and coaching:** Entry → usable plant: Match the lateral entry and step-behind throw by uncrossing and settling the support foot before the pelvis and hands turn. Convert arriving bodyweight into a grounded hip turn. This adds an entry/weight-acceptance demand to source E1's in-place pivot and differs from E3's airborne reorientation. Mirror the step/turn for the other receiving leg. Shorten step/angle for overstriding, an extra shuffle or a trapped knee; feet must turn freely with the pelvis.

**Source:** [Lower body · Class 1 · E5](../../rotational_explosiveness_lower_body/classes/class_01.md). Source class labels describe the original lesson, not today’s ordering.

### E6 — Med Ball Step-Behind Rotational Throw — floor-target adaptation

**Connection and coaching:** Entry → usable plant: Match the lateral entry and step-behind throw by uncrossing and settling the support foot before the pelvis and hands turn. Transfer a short weight shift into upper-body rotation without continued travel. From side-on, make one small step behind, replant the lead foot and deliver a scoop release. Keep total approach travel within 1 m, both feet grounded at release and the finish inside the start bay. No running, hop or jump. Use a simple step-in rotational throw instead of the crossover version if foot placement is unreliable; retain 1 × 2 per direction and record the replacement.

## Setup and delivery

Use the existing separate 10 m lanes. Keep waiting athletes outside the swept-leg, landing and ball paths. For released balls, verify a light grippable implement, permitted low floor target about 2–3 m beyond release, and containment of the entire flight/rebound/roll; use a throwing wall only after verification. Use a non-rebounding slam ball for slams. Clear the area between the leg drill and hand drill of each pair. Pairing is a coaching connection, not simultaneous stations or a throw immediately after a jump. Secure band/rope anchors and the landmine base. Kicks stay below hip height with a soft terminal knee, active recoil and no contact or spinning kick. Handoffs are controlled: secure the grip and wait for the feeder to release and step clear before turning. No new airborne-feed progression is added.
