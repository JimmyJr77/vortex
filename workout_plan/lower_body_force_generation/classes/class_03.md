# Lower Body Force Generation — Class 3 of 12

**Week 3: One lead leg contributes** · One coached class this week.

**Primary effort:** Apply force through either lead leg while preserving a forward line and controlled support.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and individual load selection. Reviewed Class 2 and its audit/workload, current ledgers and available athlete feedback. No actual performance, completion, symptoms, load tolerance or recovery was supplied. This is a conditional prescription, not an earned progression.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The split-start broad jump keeps two-foot takeoff/reception but biases initiation toward one lead leg. Single-leg hip and ankle tasks isolate contributors; grounded step-up coordination and a small scoop connect them. Two short starts provide limited acceleration practice, not maximum-speed development.

**Quality marker:** Within E1 record controlled split-start projections separately for each lead leg: front foot loaded, no twist, feet parallel at reception and a quiet 2 s hold. Record all four attempts per lead; no weak side is inferred before actual data exists. Use the listed attempts only; a miss or invalid attempt consumes the dose. Record actual attempts, setup and any substitutions; no bonus tests or comparison across unlike tasks.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use light resistance or bodyweight that permits visibly rapid force with control. Begin with E1 while fresh. Each row has a distinct job; supporting work stays small. Rest may be extended to 2–3 minutes when speed, control or attention has not returned. Stop for pain, loss of alignment, stiff/asymmetric landing or clear slowing; rest and regress remaining work only if control returns, otherwise record stopped work. Never make up failed attempts. No drop landings or rotational throws are prescribed.

| 2 | **Glute Bridge — single-leg rapid rise with level pelvis** | **1 × 2 per leg** | Express hip extension on each side without the second leg hiding pelvic control. **Delivery:** Inspect the split takeoff and receiving corridor. The two 2 m starts reserve the first 1 m for the starting stance, accelerate from 1 m to 3 m and need the final 7 m clear for deceleration; if the actual athlete cannot stop within it, use the listed grounded replacement. Running foot strikes are not counted as jump landings; record actual running contacts only if measured. Inspect stable supports, minimum loads, surface grip and all active/waiting/return paths. Keep one athlete active per lane under suitable supervision; no group size or station count is assumed. Three separate 10 m lanes are not a 30 m runway. Ball releases need separately verified flight/bounce/roll containment. Replacements occupy the original slot; missing readiness or equipment can change the practice exposure and actual totals.

Light controlled strengthening, at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Do not chase burn, shaking or pre-fatigue. Reduce load/range when control fades; stop for symptoms.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Working sets are challenging at about 2–3 technical reps/cycles in reserve. Select load/lever from demonstrated ability, not age or calendar week. Keep the stated controlled lowering and complete range; no forced repetitions, maximum testing or conditioning circuit. Check late-set and late-session quality; record reductions instead of forcing nominal completion.

**Progression note:** Week 2 used seven bilateral primary bouts. Here Split Squat becomes the priority at 2 × 5 per leg; RDL reduces to 1 × 6, Goblet Squat stays 1 × 6 and the bridge slot yields to the returning Split Squat while both calf-angle and hamstring jobs are retained. Advance only after both sides meet the stated technique/reserve and actual recovery is satisfactory.
