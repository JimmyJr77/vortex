# Lower Body Force Generation — Class 5 of 12

**Week 5: Preload into projection** · One coached class this week.

**Primary effort:** Connect a controlled preload or reception to the next sagittal push without losing foot and pelvic alignment.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and individual load selection. Reviewed Class 4 and its audit/workload, current ledgers and available athlete feedback. No actual performance, completion, symptoms, load tolerance or recovery was supplied. This is a conditional prescription, not an earned progression.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Two short broad-jump pairs anchor the session. Fixed-arm vertical force, a staggered hip reversal, flexed-knee stance-ankle output, leg-led upward release and supported single-leg hinge give the other slots specific roles. Strength volume falls while receiving-to-push coordination is introduced.

**Quality marker:** In E1 record four prescribed landings and whether the first landing of each pair permits a controlled second takeoff. If it does not, stop that pair and use separated attempts for remaining work. Do not force a rapid rebound time or longer distance. Use the listed attempts only; a miss or invalid attempt consumes the dose. Record actual attempts, setup and any substitutions; no bonus tests or comparison across unlike tasks.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use light resistance or bodyweight that permits visibly rapid force with control. Begin with E1 while fresh. Each row has a distinct job; supporting work stays small. Rest may be extended to 2–3 minutes when speed, control or attention has not returned. Stop for pain, loss of alignment, stiff/asymmetric landing or clear slowing; rest and regress remaining work only if control returns, otherwise record stopped work. Never make up failed attempts. No drop landings or rotational throws are prescribed.
