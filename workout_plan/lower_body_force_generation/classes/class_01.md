# Lower Body Force Generation — Class 1 of 12

**Week 1: Settled force initiation** · One coached class this week.

**Primary effort:** Produce a decisive knee, hip and ankle push from organized positions; express it upward and forward.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and individual load selection. Reviewed the existing neighboring curricula, current ledgers and available athlete feedback. No actual performance, completion, symptoms, load tolerance or recovery was supplied. This is a conditional prescription, not an earned progression.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The still-start squat jump is the main task. Separate hip-driven implement motion, grounded ankle finish, forward projection, one-lead-leg rise and a small leg-driven release. Six direct strength jobs supply knee/hip extension, lead-leg contribution, knee-flexor leverage and both knee-angle plantar-flexion demands.

**Quality marker:** Within E1 record valid still-start takeoffs out of six attempts: no second dip, clear upward push, both feet receive together and the 2 s landing hold is controlled. Record arm position, depth, surface and any measured output only with a verified method. Use the listed attempts only; a miss or invalid attempt consumes the dose. Record actual attempts, setup and any substitutions; no bonus tests or comparison across unlike tasks.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use light resistance or bodyweight that permits visibly rapid force with control. Begin with E1 while fresh. Each row has a distinct job; supporting work stays small. Rest may be extended to 2–3 minutes when speed, control or attention has not returned. Stop for pain, loss of alignment, stiff/asymmetric landing or clear slowing; rest and regress remaining work only if control returns, otherwise record stopped work. Never make up failed attempts. No drop landings or rotational throws are prescribed.

**Delivery:** Use one inspected takeoff lane, one low step and stable hand support; no jumping down. Kettlebell and throw tasks require separate clearance and coach attention. If the minimum bell or ball cannot move quickly with control, use the listed same-slot version. Inspect stable supports, minimum loads, surface grip and all active/waiting/return paths. Keep one athlete active per lane under suitable supervision; no group size or station count is assumed. Three separate 10 m lanes are not a 30 m runway. Ball releases need separately verified flight/bounce/roll containment. Replacements occupy the original slot; missing readiness or equipment can change the practice exposure and actual totals.
