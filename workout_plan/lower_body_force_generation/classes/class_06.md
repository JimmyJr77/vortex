# Lower Body Force Generation — Class 6 of 12

**Week 6: Midpoint consolidation** · One coached class this week.

**Primary effort:** Keep crisp knee, hip and ankle force while reducing volume and coordination demands.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and individual load selection. Reviewed Class 5 and its audit/workload, current ledgers and available athlete feedback. No actual performance, completion, symptoms, load tolerance or recovery was supplied. This is a conditional prescription, not an earned progression.

**Daily stimulus brief:** A short familiar still-start jump leads. Retained grounded hip, ankle and step-up tasks plus two isolated countermovement jumps maintain six separate jobs with fewer repetitions. All drills deliberately repeat for consolidation; no new test or skill is introduced.

**Quality marker:** Use E1’s three attempts to record settled-start control and a quiet held reception, under the same setup as Week 1 if comparison is intended. Three attempts are a reduced sample, not a matched six-attempt performance test. Use the listed attempts only; a miss or invalid attempt consumes the dose. Record actual attempts, setup and any substitutions; no bonus tests or comparison across unlike tasks.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use light resistance or bodyweight that permits visibly rapid force with control. Begin with E1 while fresh. Each row has a distinct job; supporting work stays small. Rest may be extended to 2–3 minutes when speed, control or attention has not returned. Stop for pain, loss of alignment, stiff/asymmetric landing or clear slowing; rest and regress remaining work only if control returns, otherwise record stopped work. Never make up failed attempts. No drop landings or rotational throws are prescribed.
