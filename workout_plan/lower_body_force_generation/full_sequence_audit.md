# Full twelve-week sequence audit

**Result:** Twelve complete source classes, twelve workload records and twelve individual audits. Every class was developed after the previous class was audited and saved. The saved map, workload/progression records, drill/strength history and available feedback were reviewed before each next design; original review hashes remain in each workload. Final revisions and current-file verification are separate from those historical hashes. Athlete completion and response remain unknown.

## Framework, direct relevance and scope

The program title maps to Jumps > Vertical / Horizontal with limited straight-line acceleration transfer inside the unchanged Sprinting subcategory. The exact seven categories/subcategories are reproduced without reorganization. Existing Access & Prepare 1 is referenced only. Each class has four ordered sections and exactly 6/2/6 nonzero exercise entries; no additional test, finisher, tumbling block or conditioning circuit is prescribed.

Daily effort progresses from settled force to hip drive, lead-leg contribution and knee drive; small preload/reception connections precede a reduced midpoint. Later classes address independent stance-leg force, hip-to-ankle sequencing, controlled entry and finishing force, followed by modest integration and matched review. This is a conditional teaching sequence, not a fixed adaptation timetable or an automatic load progression.

Primary strength directly assigns knee/hip drive, lengthened-hip force, lead-leg contribution or near-finish hip force, straight-knee ankle force, bent-knee ankle force and knee-flexor leverage. When bridge replaces split squat, or lead-leg emphasis replaces bilateral squat, the day's progression states the tradeoff. Lower-leg angle variants have different knee constraints; walkout cycles supply knee-flexor leverage rather than another hinge. No upper-body lift or generic abdominal slot is inserted to fill counts.

Six explosive tasks are not six maximal jump workouts. Grounded rapid force tasks and small support doses preserve room for the main anchor and six challenging primary jobs. Continuous swing, light deadlift and bodyweight rise tasks are declared as rapid force practice, not equivalent jump contacts. Leg-led ball releases use long arms and grounded feet to avoid turning this into upper-body force work.

## Recovery, timing and delivery

Working loads/levers follow current demonstrated control at about 2–3 technical RIR; light exercises retain at least five. Side/set recovery and same-lift preparation are explicit. Full resets apply to isolated efforts, with clearly counted exceptions for continuous swings, bridges and linked jump/ankle pairs. Replacements change actual exposure and may add reset time; they never create a bonus exercise.

Post-preparation estimates range **44–71 minutes** across the course. Workload records calculate active work, prescribed gaps, side/set rests, seven strength preparation rounds in most weeks (six at consolidation), phase/section transitions and 10–16 additional instruction/setup minutes. Explicit still-start/landing holds, step-up re-stance and box step-down receive appropriate allowances after revision. Longer rest and actual queues extend delivery; Access & Prepare 1 and total booking duration remain unknown.

No staff count or station quantity is assumed. Mark the full setup, action and receiving/stopping envelope inside each separate 10 m lane. Week 3 reserves 1 m for the split start, 2 m for acceleration and up to 7 m for slowing; the three lanes are not a 30 m runway. Ball flight/bounce/roll containment and support/load dimensions require actual inspection. Box jumps reset by stepping down; no drop or rotational throw is inserted.

One weekly coached class follows the user's requested twelve-week format. Overall strength training frequency, sports and tumbling remain outside this document; one weekly focus exposure alone is not claimed to optimize youth strength. Review the actual broader schedule, initially often allowing 48–72 hours between demanding leg exposures and adjusting to response. Do not combine this with neighboring jump/sprint/rotation programs merely because they are saved.

## Audit revisions and remaining decisions

Independent review corrected underallocated movement time, the lane's starting-stance space, non-existent between-rep rest at one-rep bouts, incomplete central parent-source paths, stale hard-coded cue/replacement counts after reduced prescriptions, same-class replacement collisions, and missing numeric replacement resets. These changes are documented in [revision_log.md](revision_log.md). Week 7 E1 now has a distinct bilateral settled-jump fallback; when several substitutions would duplicate slots, the class explicitly records modified/incomplete delivery instead of padding the count. Nominal working doses were not changed by these text/timing corrections.

Mechanical roles are intended support relationships, not demonstrated outcomes from these exact sessions. OTA-style power-before-strength ordering is inspiration; official youth guidance supports supervised, individualized quality work without validating this whole course. Device capabilities, actual preparation, outside workload, athlete response, staffing and booking fit remain unresolved. The sequence deliberately does not provide maximal sprinting, extensive same-leg receiving, agility, endurance or a separate body-control program.

**Handoff:** The authored plan is complete. Review actual attendance, technique, loads/levers, valid attempts, substitutions, symptoms and recovery before each delivery and before selecting the next cycle. Missing response calls for an assessment within prescribed attempts, a hold or a revision—not a claim that progression was earned.
