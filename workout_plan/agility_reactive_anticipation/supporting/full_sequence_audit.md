**Substantive uniqueness correction:** Class 7’s proposed crossover bound was too close to an existing skater-bound task and too ambiguous to justify a new slot. It was replaced with a defined single-leg vertical hop. No exercise was retained merely under a new spelling, colour or load. The set contains related variants with different jobs; it does not claim 72 unique movement families. Strength/stabilization repetitions are intentional under the user’s clarification.

## Progression, overlap and recovery

The sequence moves from commitment reading to inhibition, angle control, retreat/recovery, closeout, pre-release inference, a second decision, timing, route choice, attention transfer and integrated intent. It is not a weekly schedule or an automatic increase in difficulty. Each new task has a clearer/slower/shorter entry version when readiness is unknown. Feedback remains absent; no athlete is said to have earned a progression.

Stable strength anchors provide continuity while drills change. The [strength trace](strength_progression_trace.md) makes the previous written exposure → current dose → rationale → next condition explicit. Load changes require actual owned range, technical reserve and recovery. A set allocation can shift toward the day’s main mechanical demand without being called a strength gain. Recurring light work stays easy and purposeful; primary work remains challenging rather than unstable balance or conditioning.

Audit changes preserved the important tasks:

- Reduced Class 4’s initial eight unilateral landing allocation to four, appropriate to its consolidation role.
- Reduced Class 11 from seventeen locomotor efforts to twelve, preserving its four main attention-shift trials and reducing late support fatigue.
- Kept primary set rounds generally modest, particularly in Classes 9–12; the final workout is not a maximal test or cram session.
- Kept no-go trials inside a meaningful go/no-go task. They are legitimate inhibition practice, not zero-dose exercise rows.
- Added the missing Class 1 mirror terminal-stop allowance: up to 56 m locomotor travel including stopping, not 50 m.
- Corrected a 45-degree cut count from “reversal” to “direction change”; direction changes are not all 180-degree reversals.
- Corrected strength fallbacks that could duplicate an existing slot: manual curl now has a seated band-curl replacement; step-up has a split-squat replacement only in classes without another split-squat slot. Hold-to-dynamic replacements have explicit workload conversion.
- Expanded light-strength timing allowances where holds, relaxation and side changes required it. Estimates are now 70–95 minutes outside Access & Prepare 1, depending on class, for one closely coached athlete without queues.

The [workload table](workload_summary.md) distinguishes bilateral/unilateral landing types, throws, other ballistic work, locomotor attempts, decisions, primary bouts, preparation and holds. It is not a common “stress score.” Actual false starts, extra contacts, other sport and separate tumbling may change the real load. Stop criteria and contextual recovery guidance are in every class.

## Space, equipment, staffing and timing

The supplied specification confirms three separate 10 m lanes; another facility file states 12 m. This plan conservatively stays within 10 m longitudinally. Neither source gives usable width, quantities, lane-sharing or group size. The first angular layouts understated the width needed by complete stopping branches, so the full-sequence revision made full-route width explicit (up to 7 m for some tasks) and added quantified compact same-drill versions for a verified 4 m-wide bay. Do not combine full-route and compact distances or infer width from painted lane count. Check the complete athlete body envelope and both routes, plus coach positions and queues.

Class 11 requires a qualified coach and a second non-training cue provider. Other live tasks also require the coach to stay out of the athlete route. Athlete leaders would create extra actual workload and are not prescribed. Ball drills require appropriate light loads, clear trajectories and retrieval, with no assumption that wall-ball availability proves a safe wall. Low hurdles, boxes, bench height, knee/elbow contact surface, hand supports, bands and kettlebell fit require on-site qualification. Shortening a movement within the same drill is a replacement, not another exercise.

The class time estimates include actual prescribed work, rest, side changes, same-lift preparation, setup and coaching reserve. They do not establish a 60- or 90-minute full-class booking once Access & Prepare 1 and group queues are added. Larger groups need a real athlete/staff/equipment flow; three lanes do not prove three simultaneously supervised stations. Required recovery must not be shortened to disguise an overrun.

None validates this exact 12-workout protocol or guarantees game transfer. Many exact short-space/live-cue variants are proposed additions; local names/slugs were checked, and no live-card approval was fabricated.

No sport/position, actual preparation sequence, athlete response, equipment fit, full usable geometry or group booking has been supplied. The generic opponent work leaves sport-specific tactical and ball-flight coverage incomplete. Because drills are not repeated, there is no common pre/post agility protocol; do not report a reaction-time improvement from unlike tasks. These are complete conditional prescriptions, not certification that a particular group can execute all tasks today.
