# Class 1 design, workload and delivery audit

**Audit scope:** the complete [Class 1](../classes/class_01.md), ledger, provisional map and state against the supplied specification. This is an original conditional prescription for competent ages 12–14. No delivery, tolerance or performance gain is asserted.

## Relevance and consequential choices

| Decision | Finding and final action |
|---|---|
| Live body cues versus colour/number commands | Chose a live coach with genuine preparatory movement so the main task includes information available from an opponent. Verbal directions and light-pod reaction times would change the targeted read. No device capability assumed. |
| Primary task placement | Put E2 after only eight low pogo contacts. Static jumps, unilateral bounds and strength cannot pre-fatigue the principal decision practice. Six bouts provide the largest perceptual exposure, with no extra test attempts. |
| Mirror versus pursuit overlap | E2 is a shuffle task with a live mid-movement reversal. E6 is a single read followed by hip opening and running acceleration, without another reversal. Different locomotor solutions justify both; a second mirror slot with a renamed cue was rejected. |
| Six explosive slots | E1 small bilateral elastic return; E2 live decision/reversal; E3 paused bilateral lateral drive; E4 opposite-leg force acceptance; E5 rapid entry into a known brake; E6 live pursuit. E5 is explicitly a proposed rapid-entry variant rather than misclassifying the legacy slow resilience prescription. |
| Lateral jumping overlap | Paused bilateral jumps isolate force from stillness with shared support; skater bounds isolate one-leg propulsion/contralateral landing. Limited each to four attempts rather than full multi-set plyometric blocks. No extra hurdle/drop contacts. |
| Main strength priority | Lateral lunge receives two challenging sets/leg for direct step/plant/return loading. Split squat adds stable lead-leg extension and RDL adds a hip hinge. A generic upper-body press/pull or extra squat would consume recovery without a more direct job. |
| Adductor/calf overlap | Copenhagen adds direct adductor/pelvis load, limited to one hold/side. Straight-knee and bent-knee calf work support different knee positions; straight-knee work is limited to one set/leg. Both calf patterns do not receive three sets. |
| Stabilization placement | Step-down and short-foot work stay at one easy round each. Short-foot keeps both feet supported; a demanding balance task or hard adductor hold in this block could interfere with primary strength. |
| Loading and progression | Dynamic primary work uses 2–3 technical RIR; the adductor hold uses time reserve. No fixed kilograms, max tests, assumed previous loads or automatic escalation. Original Class 2 repeat-drill advice is superseded: the continuation request calls for unique drills, with recurring strength lifts. |

**Revisions made during design review:** removed extra partner-lead exposure by specifying a coach as cue provider; excluded the OTA example's spin; constrained all travel/stopping to 10 m; limited support jumps to four per exercise and adductor/straight-knee calf work to one round; added explicit preparation doses and proposed-variant labels; separated planned metres from actual foot contacts; made all substitutions replace existing slots. These are prescription edits, not observations of athlete response.

## Workload and recovery findings

The ledger accounts for **16 landing events** by laterality/type, **14 locomotor efforts**, up to **16 live decisions**, **6 mid-bout reversals** and **14 terminal decelerations**. There are 12 m of pursuit acceleration; up to 56 m of combined mirror/shuffle/pursuit travel includes stopping allowances and is not a continuous sprint prescription. Jump distance and foot contacts during locomotion are unknown until delivered. Preparation-sequence impacts may add materially to the session and are excluded because its content was not supplied.

Primary strength has 16 separately performed bouts: 10 compound bouts, two adductor holds and four calf bouts. This is a substantial lower-body class despite low jump numbers. RIR, preserved ranges and recovery between sides keep the strength work challenging without six maximal compound lifts. If preceding sport or tumbling load compromises quality, use reduced demand in listed slots and record actual omissions; do not preserve a paper count by forcing fatigued work. Reassess the whole day rather than calling it safe because contacts are below a numerical cap.

Estimates include the exit recovery written in each row; do not count that same recovery again as transition time. Instruction/setup may occupy resting time where practical. The ranges allow ordinary load adjustment and feedback, not unspecified large-group waits.

No booking was established for this focus, so this is a disclosed delivery unknown, not a hidden overrun.

## Group and equipment audit

- Three lanes do not imply three simultaneous live-coach stations. The baseline uses two reserved parallel paths, one moving athlete and one coach, with verified ≥2 m lateral separation; the third lane is not automatically an approved queue. The coach never crosses the athlete's route.
- For a group, demonstrate common technique once but give each athlete the full follower dose. Serial coach-led E2 time grows with athlete count; unused time may supply rest only if the athlete's actual schedule meets each required interval. Six athletes do not each complete six bouts in the time budget for one athlete. No automatic role rotation disguises extra explosive work.
- Strength can proceed in supervised serial sets or duplicate stations only when qualified staff and actual equipment quantities permit it. This document does not assume six matched dumbbell sets or enough benches for a circuit. Group size, staff, station inventory and actual session window must be recorded before declaring delivery feasible.
- Low step fit, stable bench, suitable elbow contact surface and fixed hand support are required; their dimensions are not confirmed. Bodyweight/light substitutions can preserve slots when they still meet the intended effort. A comfortable support surface is a requirement, not a claim that mats are in inventory.
- The 10-versus-12 m source conflict is handled conservatively by using 10 m. Separate path width/clearance remains unverified. No throwing lane or continuous 30 m runway is assumed.
