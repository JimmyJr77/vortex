# Reactive Agility and Anticipation — Class 12 of 12

**Primary effort:** Recognize opponent intent, select the appropriate movement and adapt when a route closes.

**Framework:** Agility > Reactive agility and anticipation. **Baseline:** ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and current readiness. Prior classes are prescribed, not known to have been performed.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Integrate approach, retreat and lateral response without trying to make the final class the hardest. E1 uses three opponent intentions and a short response distance. A fore–aft-to-lateral rebound, parallel-to-split acceptance, moving lateral takeoff, ballistic hip extension and a late route closure test different execution requirements. End with review of actual evidence, not a repeated drill or a manufactured before/after score.

**Quality marker, inside E1:** E1: report six actual intention choices and whether the selected movement, foot placement and stop were controlled. Keep approach, retreat and lateral outcomes separate. Scores from the 12 different drills cannot establish an improvement in reaction time. Strength evidence may be compared only under matched execution/load/range/rest conditions, if actual records exist.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 is the primary performance task and comes first while fresh. Coaches provide cues; athletes do not add leader rounds or extra tests. Physical support drills are not themselves evidence of anticipation gains.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Three-Intent Opponent Read** | **1 × 6 trials: 2 advance, 2 retreat, 2 lateral**. Each selected response travels 1 m and stops within 1 m. Coach gives one clear intention per trial; lateral openings include one each way. Rest 75 s between trials and 90 s before E2. Keep intentions obvious and response speed controlled if accuracy is uncertain. | Main integration: choose whether to give ground, close space or shift laterally from the opponent’s intention. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Forward-to-Lateral Rebound Hop** | **1 × 1 pair/leg = 4 landings**. Hop a short distance forward, rebound outward once on the same leg, then hold 2 s; right leg exits right, left leg exits left. Rest 75 s between legs and 60 s before E3. Shorten both distances if the second contact loses alignment. | Redirect a small forward landing into an outward lateral push on the same leg, rather than reversing back along the same line. |
| 3 | **Forward Jump to Split-Stance Landing** | **1 × 1 per landing lead = 2 jumps**. Jump a short distance forward from parallel feet and land split, both feet contacting with control; one each lead leg, hold 2 s. Do not switch again or add a second jump. Rest 45 s between jumps and 60 s before E4. Shorten travel/stance for a heavy front-foot landing. | Accept a two-foot forward impulse in an asymmetric ready base, preparing either leg to own the next step. |
| 4 | **Shuffle-to-Bound Release** | **1 × 1 each direction = 2 bounds**. Take two controlled shuffle steps over ≤1 m, push off the trailing-side leg into one small opposite-leg lateral bound and hold 2 s. One each direction. Rest 75 s between trials and 60 s before E5. Slow/shorten the shuffle and bound for reaching or collapse. | Convert incoming lateral travel into one unilateral flight, unlike the stationary skater push or grounded shuffle stop. |
| 5 | **Kettlebell Swing — two-hand** | **1 × 6 swings**. Use a manageable bell, secure two-hand grip, hinge then snap hips so the bell floats no higher than chest height. Arms guide, spine stays stacked; no overhead swing or squat-lift. First two scheduled swings stay small. Rest 90 s before E6. Stop for rounding, lumbar lean-back, grip loss or an arm-dominated pattern; do not replace failed swings with extra reps. | Rapid hip extension from a grounded hinge supports repeated acceleration impulses without extra jump landings. |
| 6 | **Late Gate-Closure Escape** | **1 × 2 trials, one initial route each side**. Coach on protected path leaves a gate open; athlete starts toward it. After the first step, coach visibly covers that route; athlete brakes, selects the other exit and stops. Total movement ≤2.5 m plus ≤1.5 m stopping. Rest 90 s between trials and 90 s before strength. Default is an early, obvious closure and a deliberate braking step; no last-instant planted-knee twist. | Re-plan an offensive route when the initially available exit is closed after departure. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Light controlled strengthening only; finish ready for primary strength, not fatigued.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lateral Step-Down — light dumbbell** | **1 × 4/leg**. Lower for 3 s from a suitably low stable platform, free heel lightly taps; keep standing heel down. Use bodyweight or reduce range for pelvic/knee drift. If no platform fits, use a shallow supported single-leg squat instead, same dose. Rest 30 s between sides/holds and 30 s before the next exercise. Stay at RPE 3–4/10, at least five clean reps or ample hold time in reserve. | Final low-fatigue control check over a planted leg, without turning it into a balance test. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill — both feet supported** | **1 × 3 holds × 5 s/foot**. Keep both feet supported and work one target foot at a time: maintain heel and metatarsal contact while gently raising the arch, with long toes. Relax 5 s between holds; use seated support instead if toes curl. Rest 30 s between sides/holds and 30 s before the next exercise. Stay at RPE 3–4/10, at least five clean reps or ample hold time in reserve. | Maintain the contact base used across the three response types. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Working doses below. Dynamic sets: **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; isometric primary holds: about **5 s clean hold reserve**. Use controlled lowering (about 2 s unless specified), purposeful ascent and normal breathing. No failure or maximal tests; choose loads through demonstrated technique, not age. These are serial strength sets, not a circuit.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lateral Lunge — goblet dumbbell** | **1 × 5/side**. Step sideways from standing, sit into the working hip with whole-foot pressure, then return to the starting stance. Reduce depth/load for groin discomfort, knee collapse or twisting. Rest 90 s between sides; 120 s before the next exercise. | Retain the original outside-plant/return strength anchor at a smaller final-session dose. |
| 2 | **Split Squat — dumbbells at sides** | **1 × 6/side**. Keep both feet on the floor in a stationary staggered stance; front heel down, controlled descent, drive through the lead leg. Reduce depth/load for pelvic rotation or rear-leg jumping. Rest 90 s between sides; 120 s before the next exercise. | Retain stable lead-leg extension for whichever response is selected. |
| 3 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6 bilateral**. Hinge from standing with soft knees; keep weights close and stop descent where the pelvis/spine remain controlled. Finish tall without leaning back. Reduce range/load for rounding. Rest 120 s before the next exercise. | Retain hip-hinge strength, with only one set after the new swing exposure. |
| 4 | **Copenhagen Plank — Short Lever** | **1 × 15 s/side**. Upper knee bent about 90 degrees on a stable bench, forearm under shoulder; lift the lower leg and keep pelvis level. Select a short lever with about 5 seconds clean hold reserve. Stop for groin, shoulder or knee pain. If bench/support is unsuitable, use side-lying hip adduction instead, 1 × 8/side at 2–3 RIR; record changed hold/repetition totals. For that replacement, prepare with 1 × 4 easy reps/side instead of the two 5 s preparation holds; retain side rest. Rest 60 s between sides; 60 s before the next exercise. | Maintain adductor/pelvis force for an unexpected route closure. |
| 5 | **Single-Leg Calf Raise — supported standing, dumbbell** | **1 × 8/side**. Stand on flat floor with a nearly straight knee and light hand support; raise the heel, pause briefly and lower for 2 seconds. No bounce or ankle roll. Reduce load when heel height declines. Rest 60 s between sides; 60 s before the next exercise. | Maintain single-leg ankle push without a fatigue test or maximal load. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise — bilateral, bent-knee** | **1 × 10 bilateral**. Use a stable bench, knees near right angles, secure dumbbells on distal thighs above knees. Lower heels for 2 seconds to the floor, pause, then rise. Reduce load for bouncing or shortened range. Rest 60 s before the next exercise. | Maintain low-stance ankle strength through controlled repetitions. |

**Anchor delivery:** Athlete starts in the centre of a marked response bay. Coach presents six natural body commitments in hidden order: two advances (athlete gives ground), two retreats (athlete closes), and two lateral openings (athlete shifts the same way, one each side). Each athlete response is only 1 m, with 1 m stopping space. Coach remains in a protected front observation box, not the athlete route. Give obvious movements with no feints when readiness is unknown. Classify the intention first, then move; no extra trial is added to improve the score.

**Space and delivery:** For E1 use a verified response bay with ≥5 m usable width, athlete centred at longitudinal 4 m; forward/back endpoints including stopping lie at 2/6 m, lateral endpoints are ±2 m from centre. Coach has a protected box near the 9–10 m end; verify ≥2 m body clearance from the entire athlete envelope. E2/E4 use short jump travel in a clear strip. E5 needs a manageable kettlebell and overhead-free swing space; no person in front. E6 needs a separately verified branching bay and a coach position outside both exits; its ≤2.5 m travel plus ≤1.5 m stopping fits longitudinally within 10 m. If width or clearance is unavailable, reconfigure the task footprint before delivery, rather than assuming three lanes supply it. Full-route width is a requirement, not confirmed inventory: branch stopping extensions can exceed a 4 m bay. Compact same-drill option for E1: keep forward/back responses at 1 m plus 1 m stopping but shorten each lateral response to 0.5 m plus 0.75 m stopping. Two responses of each intent give 5 m movement plus up to 5.5 m stopping. E6 uses a first step of 0.5 m, then a 1 m redirected route and 1 m stopping, with early closure; two trials give 3 m movement plus up to 2 m stopping. Verify all paths in a 4 m-wide bay and keep the coach outside the buffers. No additional trials or exercises. At setup, mark complete body/landing envelopes, both possible routes, run-off and the coach position; a floor-line diagram alone does not establish clearance. If even the compact footprint is unavailable, resolve the layout before delivery.

**Readiness and stopping:** First scheduled attempts, at controlled demand, count toward the listed dose and establish today’s execution. Do not add make-up reps or force the paper count. Record actual omissions and modified rules. Landings are controlled; no maximal jump-distance chase.
