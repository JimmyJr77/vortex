# Reactive Agility and Anticipation — Class 9 of 12

**Primary effort:** Read an opponent’s rhythm change and use the brief moment when movement becomes available.

**Framework:** Agility > Reactive agility and anticipation. **Baseline:** ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and current readiness. Prior classes are prescribed, not known to have been performed.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Consolidate Class 8’s decision load by limiting direction choices and emphasizing timing. E1 starts with both people already moving so the athlete reads a change of pace, not another stationary go cue. Low unilateral ankle return, short scissor impulse, controlled reburst, catch-to-release reversal and an offensive timing window supply distinct support jobs.

**Quality marker, inside E1:** E1: correct response to both pace changes, preserved gap and a controlled stop. Record the coach’s sequence and speed description; no claim of speed improvement from a different drill or different cue rhythm.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 is the primary performance task and comes first while fresh. Coaches provide cues; athletes do not add leader rounds or extra tests. Physical support drills are not themselves evidence of anticipation gains.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Moving Opponent Tempo Match** | **1 × 4 bouts, ≤4 s each**. Begin at a slow shared pace, respond to one pace increase and one stop/easing, travel ≤4 m, stop in ≤2 m. Rest 75 s between bouts and 90 s before E2. Widen the pace contrast if either event is missed. | Main rhythm task: detect acceleration and easing from an already-moving opponent while preserving a useful gap. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Pogo in Place** | **1 × 3 contacts/leg = 6 landings**. Three low hops in place on one foot, stop after the third; repeat opposite after 60 s. Rest 60 s before E3. Soft knee, tall torso; reduce amplitude for heel slap or lateral drift. | Practice small repeated ankle return on one support leg for an unpredictable next step. |
| 3 | **Straight-Leg Bounds — low amplitude** | **1 × 2 linked contacts, one landing/leg**. Take two short forward straight-leg-style bounds with softly unlocked knees, alternating feet; low height and no reaching for distance. End controlled. Rest 90 s before E4. Shorten stride for hamstring tension or braking on a locked leg. | Create a quick alternating hip-driven impulse with little knee lift or flight height, complementing ankle-only pogos. |
| 4 | **Build–Float–Reburst Run** | **1 × 3 repetitions of 2 m build + 1 m float + 1 m reburst**. Use marked segments at controlled speed, then stop within 2 m. Float is reduced drive, not walking or an abrupt brake. Rest 75 s between reps and 60 s before E5. Reduce overall speed if the reburst causes overstriding. | Physically rehearse changing force output while already moving without adding a directional choice. |
| 5 | **Soft Medicine-Ball Catch-to-Scoop Return** | **1 × 4 catch-return releases**. Coach sends a soft low underhand pass; receive with two hands near the waist, yield slightly through hips/knees and immediately return underhand without a jump. No hard throw or deep squat. Rest 30 s between releases and 60 s before E6. Reduce load/delivery for collapsing posture or slow arm-only returns. | Accept a small incoming load through the hips/trunk and reverse it promptly into a forward release, supporting tempo changes in force expression. |
| 6 | **Bait-and-Go Timing Window** | **1 × 4 bouts, ≤4 s each**. Coach guards a corresponding gate on a separate parallel path, then makes one visible weight commitment away from it. Athlete may make a small nontravelling shoulder feint, but bursts 2 m only when the window opens; stop within 2 m. Rest 75 s between bouts, 90 s before strength. Make the opening longer for premature or missed departures. | Read when a defender’s weight commitment creates time to leave, introducing an offensive timing problem. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Light controlled strengthening only; finish ready for primary strength, not fatigued.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill — both feet supported** | **1 × 3 holds × 5 s/foot**. Keep both feet supported and work one target foot at a time: maintain heel and metatarsal contact while gently raising the arch, with long toes. Relax 5 s between holds; use seated support instead if toes curl. Rest 30 s between sides/holds and 30 s before the next exercise. Stay at RPE 3–4/10, at least five clean reps or ample hold time in reserve. | Low-fatigue foot control after unilateral ankle contacts. |
| 2 | **Heel-Dig Bridge Hold — light bilateral** | **2 × 10 s**. On a comfortable floor surface, heels down and knees bent; lift hips only to a stacked trunk and gently pull heels toward the hips without moving them. Keep easy and breathe; lower height for cramp or back extension. Rest 30 s between sides/holds and 30 s before the next exercise. Stay at RPE 3–4/10, at least five clean reps or ample hold time in reserve. | Easy heel-driven posterior-chain tension before a reduced-volume strength block. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Working doses below. Dynamic sets: **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; isometric primary holds: about **5 s clean hold reserve**. Use controlled lowering (about 2 s unless specified), purposeful ascent and normal breathing. No failure or maximal tests; choose loads through demonstrated technique, not age. These are serial strength sets, not a circuit.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Goblet Squat** | **1 × 6 bilateral**. Hold one dumbbell at the chest; sit between the hips with whole-foot pressure and rise smoothly. Use an owned depth; reduce load/range for heel lift or trunk collapse. Rest 120 s before the next exercise. | Retain knee/hip force with one quality set during timing consolidation. |
| 2 | **Reverse Lunge — dumbbells** | **1 × 6/side**. Step one foot backward, lower under control, then use the front leg to return to standing. Keep the front heel down; shorten the step/load for wobble or push-off from the rear toes. Rest 90 s between sides; 120 s before the next exercise. | Single-leg acceptance and return from the ready stance without adding another compound round. |
| 3 | **Hip Thrust — sandbag** | **1 × 8 bilateral**. Support upper back on a stable bench, secure the sandbag across the hip crease, drive through both feet and finish with ribs stacked. No lumbar arch; reduce load for pelvic loss. If bench fit fails, use a floor glute bridge instead, same dose, recording the shorter range. Rest 120 s before the next exercise. | Hip extension for the reburst after a period of reduced drive. |
| 4 | **Partner Manual-Resistance Hamstring Curl** | **1 × 8/side**. Lie prone on a comfortable floor surface; coach applies smooth resistance above the heel while the athlete bends and straightens the knee through an owned range. Keep hips down; no sudden pushes. Match resistance to 2–3 RIR, not a contest. If prone support/contact is unsuitable, use Seated Band Hamstring Curl instead, same sets/reps per side: sit on a stable bench, secure a light band at the heel with a coach holding steady forward tension, then pull the heel beneath the bench without moving the thigh. Qualify band/bench fit; no sudden release. This replaces only this slot and preserves totals. Rest 60 s between sides; 60 s before the next exercise. | Controlled knee-flexor force during alternating running contacts. |
| 5 | **Single-Leg Calf Raise — supported standing, dumbbell** | **1 × 8/side**. Stand on flat floor with a nearly straight knee and light hand support; raise the heel, pause briefly and lower for 2 seconds. No bounce or ankle roll. Reduce load when heel height declines. Rest 60 s between sides; 60 s before the next exercise. | Maintain ankle push capacity with unchanged support and heel range. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise — bilateral, bent-knee** | **1 × 10 bilateral**. Use a stable bench, knees near right angles, secure dumbbells on distal thighs above knees. Lower heels for 2 seconds to the floor, pause, then rise. Reduce load for bouncing or shortened range. Rest 60 s before the next exercise. | Maintain bent-knee force for the braking response to an easing opponent. |

**Anchor delivery:** Coach and athlete move slowly forward on parallel paths, athlete about half a step behind, both within a 2–6 m work strip. During a ≤4 s bout the coach makes one clear acceleration then one clear easing/stopping action, with no direction change. Athlete matches the pace change without closing the marked longitudinal gap, stops within 2 m beyond the strip. Four bouts. If response history is unknown, make the pace change broad and slow; do not increase duration. Record whether acceleration and braking were read separately.

**Space and delivery:** Use parallel 10 m lanes with verified ≥2 m lateral separation. E1 total athlete travel ≤4 m per bout plus ≤2 m stopping. E4 starts at 1 m, builds for 2 m, floats for 1 m, rebursts for 1 m and stops by 7 m. E5 coach and athlete face each other about 2 m apart in an isolated, verified catch zone; use a ball light enough for soft underhand delivery and controlled fast return, not a hard chest-height projectile. E6 uses protected parallel attacker/defender paths and a marked 2 m athlete gate route; there is no body contact.

**Readiness and stopping:** First scheduled attempts, at controlled demand, count toward the listed dose and establish today’s execution. Hold the simpler stated version when recent execution/recovery is unknown. Stop the affected exercise for pain, slipping or unsafe landing/stopping; after two consecutive technical misses, reduce the stated distance, speed or cue complexity for the remaining attempts, or end it. Do not add make-up reps or force the paper count. Record actual omissions and modified rules. Landings are controlled; no maximal jump-distance chase.
