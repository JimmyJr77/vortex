# Agility: Directional — Class 5 of 12

**Primary effort:** Brake before a square corner and project cleanly into a planned 90-degree exit.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 is the running corner task. Low quarter-turn landings teach controlled reorientation, a shuffle-to-backpedal corner changes gait, same-leg lateral hops address outside-leg acceptance, a bound-to-run exit links lateral force to forward travel, and a three-point start isolates diagonal projection.

**Performance marker within E2:** In E2, record whether the athlete lowers before the corner, redirects through 90 degrees without a fixed-knee twist and keeps the first two steps inside the exit corridor. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** Confirm quarter-turn landing and same-leg lateral control at low height before increasing intent. Prior class was prescribed as consolidation; no actual recovery has been established. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Quarter-Turn Jump to Stick — bilateral — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each turn direction**. Low two-foot jump, turn the whole body 90 degrees in flight and land on both feet for a 2 s hold. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Reorient the feet and hips together rather than twisting the knee after contact. **Regress:** Reduce turn angle and height within the same task if feet land misaligned; log the changed angle. |
| E2 | **90-Degree Speed Cut** | **2 × 1 per plant leg**. Run in 1.5 m at controlled speed, brake before the cone, redirect 90 degrees and accelerate 2 m; stop over 2.5 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main braking-to-perpendicular-running exit practice. **Regress:** Reduce incoming speed if the athlete reaches for the plant or rounds the corner uncontrollably. |
| E3 | **Shuffle-to-Backpedal Corner — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each shuffle direction**. Shuffle 1 m, brake, then backpedal 1.5 m without rotating to run; stop over a further 1.5 m. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Find backward force after a lateral plant while preserving the facing needed for the next task. **Regress:** Shorten backward steps if the athlete leans back or crosses the feet. |
| E4 | **Single-Leg Lateral Hop to Stick** | **1 × 1 per leg**. Hop laterally away from the free leg no more than 0.3 m, land on the same leg and hold 2 s. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Accept lateral momentum when the outside leg must remain the support leg. **Regress:** Shorten flight for pelvic drop, inward knee movement or free-foot touch. |
| E5 | **Lateral Bound to Forward Sprint — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each initial bound direction**. Make one short lateral bound within 0.4 m, land on the opposite leg and immediately run 2 m forward; stop over 2.5 m. No second hop. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Use lateral landing force to create a perpendicular running exit. **Regress:** Reduce bound distance and exit intent if the landing requires a recovery hop. |
| E6 | **Three-Point Diagonal Start — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each exit direction**. Use one hand on the floor and a stable staggered stance aimed down a diagonal lane; push forward into 2 m acceleration, then stop over 3 m. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Direct force from a stable low position along the chosen exit without an approach or a cut. **Regress:** Use a less crouched start if the hand or trunk position is uncomfortable; stop if hand support is unsuitable. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** A clear 3 m × 3 m landing/fall area; no height or distance target. Exposure per attempt: 1 bilateral landing; body rotation is not a locomotor cut.
- **E2:** Mark the incoming line, corner and outgoing line. Keep 2 m beyond the approach cone clear for an aborted turn and the full 4.5 m exit/stop clear. Exposure per attempt: 1 cut and 2 locomotor braking episodes.
- **E3:** An L route in the bay; short lateral incoming leg, backward exit along the long dimension, mirror sides separately. Exposure per attempt: 1 travel-direction change and 2 braking episodes.
- **E4:** Face across the lane so flight follows its length; leave 2 m beyond the target clear. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing per leg.
- **E5:** Place the bound at the corner of the bay; outgoing run and stop follow its long dimension. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing, 1 flight-to-run redirection and 1 terminal locomotor stop.
- **E6:** Orient the whole start/run/stop down a cleared 10 m lane; the diagonal is relative to initial torso orientation, not a demand to cross occupied lanes. Exposure per attempt: 1 start and terminal stop; no incoming-momentum cut.

**Space and flow:** E2/E3/E5 require a measured 8 m × 5 m clear bay including straight-ahead abort and exit-stopping corridors. Orient the 2 m exit plus 2.5 m stop along its long dimension. Jump/short-start tasks can be oriented along the 10 m lane; actual side clearance still needs verification. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Hip Airplane — supported** | **1 × 4 per stance leg**. Bodyweight, hand support and slight hip hinge. Slowly open/close pelvis through a small owned arc without rolling the stance foot. | Control pelvis reorientation over the planted leg at the square corner. Reduce arc or use more hand support if knee/foot rotates uncontrollably. |
| S2 | **Seated Band Ankle Eversion — self-anchored (proposed)** | **1 × 6 per foot**. Sit securely, use the other foot to hold a light loop; turn the working forefoot slightly outward against tension, 2 s return, keep knee still. | Maintain foot-edge control under a lateral component of the 90-degree plant. Use less tension/range for toe gripping or whole-leg rotation. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |

**Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 1 × 3 per side light; P2: 2 × 3 per leg, light then intermediate; P3: 1 × 5 light; P4: 1 × 8 s easy; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** Use instead of the band, gentle hand resistance at the outer forefoot with the same easy reps if a loop cannot be secured. If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.

**Workload:** 10 locomotor attempts; 27 m declared movement paths and up to 24 m additional stopping (some approach paths already contain braking). 8 changes of travel direction; 16 locomotor braking episodes, which are **not foot-contact counts**. 2 bilateral and 4 unilateral flight landings; 0 throws. Keep intensity/flight types separate as described above. Light work: 2 rounds/4 side bouts, 20 reps plus 0 s holds. Primary: **6 rounds/9 bouts**, 38 reps plus 50 s holds. Preparation adds 11 bouts, 28 reps plus 18 s holds. Bilateral bouts count once; per-side work counts both sides. Access & Prepare 1, walking resets and outside training remain unquantified, not zero.

**Timing:** **64–76 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, before extra group queues: E1–E6 approximately 4, 8, 4, 4, 5, 4 minutes; light block 6; primary block 29. This includes attempts, reset/rest, demonstrations, ordinary setup and the stated preparation sets; the upper allowance covers load changes/extended recovery. Full duration remains unknown. Alternate athletes only when actual intervals supply their prescribed rest and the route clears. A coach must model the real class size, equipment quantities and booking; do not compress rests, delete slots or call a partial class complete to fit an assumed window.

**Scheduling:** This loads the legs through plants, landings and lifting. Roughly 48–72 hours before comparably demanding lower-body work is a starting planning interval, adjusted for actual recovery and the full sport/gym/tumbling schedule. A new drill does not mean recovered tissues. No observed response is yet available.

**Progression note:** The new corner and flight-to-run task increase directional complexity, so approach stays short and support strength retains the reduced Class 4 round count. A sharper angle is not permission for higher entry speed. Repeated strength anchors: Keep the Class 4 reduced round counts and target reserve. Shift exercise order toward the lateral lunge without increasing sets or load. Advance only one justified demand after reported control on both sides, intended effort and acceptable recovery; hold for unknown response, regress for loss of control or overload. Unique drills permit comparison of coaching criteria, not claims of improved test scores between different routes.
