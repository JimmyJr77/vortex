# Agility: Directional — Class 10 of 12

**Primary effort:** Keep the body organized while switching between running, shuffling and backpedaling on a known route.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 follows a short box with three gait corners. A backward W prepares diagonal backward force, a crossing route challenges obtuse plants, a crossover reversal changes lateral gait direction, and two small single-leg landing tasks isolate backward and rotational acceptance.

**Performance marker within E2:** In E2, record control at each of the three corners and whether the athlete maintains the declared facing through the run–shuffle–backpedal–shuffle sequence; a completed route with crossed feet is not automatically a valid attempt. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** Verify backward and same-leg rotational landing competence independently during the scheduled small attempts. Keep route pace controlled; the second/third corner must not become a fatigue test. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

## 1. Access & Prepare 1

Complete the **existing predetermined Access & Prepare 1**. Its content and duration are not supplied; neither is created or modified here. Include its actual exposures in the coach's full-session review.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **W Drill Backpedal Cuts — short route** | **1 × 1 complete route**. Face forward throughout and backpedal through three 1 m alternating rear-diagonal legs, then stop over 1.5 m. Keep feet low and do not spin to run. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Prepare backward pushes toward alternating diagonals while preserving facing. **Regress:** Reduce speed/step length if the athlete crosses heels or falls behind the base. |
| E2 | **Box Drill — Sprint, Shuffle, Backpedal** | **1 × 1 clockwise and 1 counterclockwise route**. Use a 1.5 m square: run forward, shuffle sideways, backpedal, then shuffle back to the starting corner; stop over 1.5 m on the last heading. Chest keeps its initial facing. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main planned gait switching while keeping a useful base at each corner. **Regress:** Lower course pace if the athlete changes facing or crosses feet to recover. |
| E3 | **X Crossing Route — two obtuse plants — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each mirrored route**. On a 1.5 m square, run lower-left to upper-right, across to upper-left, then diagonally to lower-right; stop over 2 m on that diagonal. Mirror the route for the other side. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Prepare obtuse plants while the next exit crosses the earlier travel line. **Regress:** Reduce entry speed at both far corners if the athlete loops around instead of controlling the plant. |
| E4 | **Crossover Run Reversal — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each initial direction**. Crossover-run 2 m along the lane, brake before the cone and return 2 m with crossover running in the opposite direction; stop over 2 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Redirect a crossing gait without the feet tangling at the turn. **Regress:** Lower entry speed if the athlete needs an uncontrolled spin or extra hop. |
| E5 | **Single-Leg Backward Hop to Stick — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 per leg**. Hop backward a very short distance, no more than 0.2 m, landing on the same leg for a 2 s hold. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Own backward force acceptance when a single leg remains the support. **Regress:** Make the hop nearly in place if the pelvis falls behind the heel; stop if single-leg control remains absent. |
| E6 | **Quarter-Turn Single-Leg Hop to Stick — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 per leg, mirrored turn directions**. Make a very low hop, rotate up to 90 degrees away from the free leg and land on the same leg; hold 2 s. No travel target. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Coordinate the pelvis and support foot before receiving rotational force. **Regress:** Reduce rotation and height if the foot and knee land misaligned; do not force 90 degrees. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** Mark three short rear-diagonal legs and clear the final 1.5 m backward stopping continuation; the route contains both left and right changes. Exposure per attempt: 2 cuts and 3 braking episodes.
- **E2:** 6 m route travel plus 1.5 m final stop. The three intermediate corners are cuts; the start/finish is a terminal stop. Clear the final extension outside the square. Exposure per attempt: 3 cuts and 4 braking episodes per route.
- **E3:** Legs are about 2.12, 1.5 and 2.12 m, total 5.74 m. One athlete owns the crossing and final 2 m stop; no cone touch or extra return. Exposure per attempt: 2 cuts and 3 braking episodes per route.
- **E4:** Start at 4, turn at 6, return to 4 and stop by 2; mirror 6 → 4 → 6 → 8. Keep the chest obliquely oriented rather than using a forward sprint turn. Exposure per attempt: 1 reversal and 2 braking episodes.
- **E5:** Inspect the empty landing space before starting; use the middle of the lane with clear fall margins. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing per leg.
- **E6:** 3 m × 3 m clear landing area; no approach or rebound. Record actual turn angle. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing per leg; body rotation is not a running cut.

**Space and flow:** Use a measured 9 m × 6 m bay including each final stopping continuation. Mark route order and direction clearly before each attempt. No partner calls, moving targets or overlapping courses are used. The full bay width is still a delivery requirement, not confirmed inventory. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Hip Airplane — supported** | **1 × 4 per stance leg**. Bodyweight, hand support and slight hip hinge. Slowly open/close pelvis through a small owned arc without rolling the stance foot. | Maintain pelvis-over-foot control when facing and travel direction differ. Reduce arc or use more hand support if knee/foot rotates uncontrollably. |
| S2 | **Seated Band Ankle Eversion — self-anchored (proposed)** | **1 × 6 per foot**. Sit securely, use the other foot to hold a light loop; turn the working forefoot slightly outward against tension, 2 s return, keep knee still. | Support foot-edge control at crossing-gait and backward corners. Use less tension/range for toe gripping or whole-leg rotation. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Reverse Lunge — two dumbbells** | **1 × 5 per returning front leg**. Step backward under control, lower 2 s and drive through the original front foot to return. Keep knee tracking with the foot. Rest 60 s between legs, 120 s between rounds. | Load a controlled backward step and forward return relevant to the gait changes. Reduce step length/load for an uncontrolled backward reach or front-heel lift. |
| P2 | **Hip Thrust — dumbbell** | **1 × 8**. Upper back on a stable bench, dumbbell secured over pelvis; rise to hip extension without arching the back, lower 2 s. Rest 90 s before next exercise. | Maintain hip-extension force for running exits after backward travel. Reduce load/range for lumbar extension or hamstring cramp. |
| P3 | **Lateral Lunge — goblet dumbbell** | **1 × 6 per lunging leg**. step out, lower **2 s**, push back to the original stance; 2–3 RIR. Rest **60 s between legs**. | Maintain frontal-plane force for the box's lateral legs. Keep the lunging foot grounded and knee aligned; sit into that hip without rotating away. Reduce width, depth or load for loss of control. |
| P4 | **Glute Bridge Iso Hold — long-lever hamstring variant** | **1 × 20 s**, bilateral. Heels farther from the hips than a standard bridge, knees still bent; bodyweight or a securely hand-held sandbag over the pelvis to reach the holding-effort target. | Maintain knee-flexor braking support without extra dynamic volume. Try to pull the heels toward the body without sliding them; keep ribs down. Bring heels closer or remove load for cramp/lost height; stop for symptoms. |
| P5 | **Copenhagen Side Plank — short lever** | **1 × 15 s per supported top leg**. top knee/lower thigh on a stable bench, lower leg clear. Rest **60 s between sides**. Use bodyweight; if already controlled and too easy, secure a small dumbbell at the upper hip with the upper hand to reach the holding-effort target. | Maintain adductor support across the lateral corners. Keep shoulder comfortable and pelvis level. Use light lower-foot assistance if needed; stop for groin/shoulder pain or loss of position. |
| P6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **1 × 8**, bilateral; knees near 90°, forefeet on floor, **1 s at top, 2 s lowering, quiet bottom reset**; 2–3 RIR. Rest **90 s between sets**. | Maintain bent-knee ankle force for low gait transitions. Secure the weights above the knees, maintain knee angle and repeatable heel rise. Reduce load if heel height falls or bouncing replaces muscle force. |

**Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 2 × 3 per side, light then intermediate; P2: 1 × 5 light; P3: 1 × 3 per side light; P4: 1 × 8 s easy; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** Use instead of the band, gentle hand resistance at the outer forefoot with the same easy reps if a loop cannot be secured. If bench geometry is unsuitable, use instead a loaded dynamic floor glute bridge at the same rep/effort and preparation dose. If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.

**Workload:** 7 locomotor attempts; 34.48 m declared movement paths and up to 12.5 m additional stopping (some approach paths already contain braking). 14 changes of travel direction; 21 locomotor braking episodes, which are **not foot-contact counts**. 0 bilateral and 4 unilateral flight landings; 0 throws. Keep intensity/flight types separate as described above. Light work: 2 rounds/4 side bouts, 20 reps plus 0 s holds. Primary: **6 rounds/9 bouts**, 38 reps plus 50 s holds. Preparation adds 11 bouts, 28 reps plus 18 s holds. Bilateral bouts count once; per-side work counts both sides. Access & Prepare 1, walking resets and outside training remain unquantified, not zero.

**Timing:** **60–72 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, before extra group queues: E1–E6 approximately 3, 5, 5, 5, 4, 4 minutes; light block 6; primary block 28. This includes attempts, reset/rest, demonstrations, ordinary setup and the stated preparation sets; the upper allowance covers load changes/extended recovery. Full duration remains unknown. Alternate athletes only when actual intervals supply their prescribed rest and the route clears. A coach must model the real class size, equipment quantities and booking; do not compress rests, delete slots or call a partial class complete to fit an assumed window.

**Scheduling:** This loads the legs through plants, landings and lifting. Roughly 48–72 hours before comparably demanding lower-body work is a starting planning interval, adjusted for actual recovery and the full sport/gym/tumbling schedule. A new drill does not mean recovered tissues. No observed response is yet available.

**Progression note:** Apply a known multi-gait route with short 1–1.5 m entry legs. The added corner count is balanced by one W route, two box routes and low support doses; no speed increase is prescribed. If actual linked-corner control is not established, lower pace and record incomplete work where necessary. Repeated strength anchors: Keep all lifts at one working round and target reserve unchanged. Reuse the Class 6 reverse lunge/hip thrust rather than introducing a new heavy lift while gait complexity changes; soleus replaces straight-knee calf emphasis for the lowered transition posture. Advance only one justified demand after reported control on both sides, intended effort and acceptable recovery; hold for unknown response, regress for loss of control or overload. Unique drills permit comparison of coaching criteria, not claims of improved test scores between different routes.

The two unilateral landing drills have different force directions and remain one small attempt per leg. Whole routes stop after the prescribed traversal.
