# Agility: Directional — Class 11 of 12

**Primary effort:** Reorganize quickly after a small false step while preserving a high-quality exit.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 uses a known lateral jab followed by a crossover exit. A two-foot-to-one-foot lateral landing controls an asymmetric receiving base, a short V shuttle links different corner types, two forward rebounds provide brief elastic force, a forward bound becomes a running exit, and a half-kneeling start isolates the initial lateral drive.

**Performance marker within E2:** For E2, record whether the jab leg accepts weight under control and the next crossover sends the body into the intended exit without an extra gather step; score both jab directions separately. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** The jab is planned and small, not a reactive dodge against a defender. Confirm single-leg landing and comfortable floor-knee support; use conservative flight distances. Review actual fatigue from the linked routes rather than assuming that a new class number means recovery. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

## 1. Access & Prepare 1

Complete the **existing predetermined Access & Prepare 1**. Its content and duration are not supplied; neither is created or modified here. Include its actual exposures in the coach's full-session review.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Two-Foot Lateral Jump to Single-Leg Landing — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 per landing leg**. Push from both feet laterally within 0.3 m, land on the outside leg and hold 2 s; the free foot stays clear. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Receive lateral force on one leg after a shared push, keeping the next foot free for an exit. **Regress:** Shorten the flight if the pelvis drops or the free foot must touch down. |
| E2 | **Planned Lateral Jab-to-Crossover Exit — proposed addition** | **2 × 1 each jab direction**. From athletic stance, make a 0.5 m lateral jab, accept weight, then cross into the opposite forward diagonal for 2 m; stop over 2.5 m. The coach declares the whole route beforehand. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main force reversal after a small deceptive entry while avoiding extra foot gathering. **Regress:** Reduce jab width and speed if the athlete reaches outside the base or delays the exit with a hop. |
| E3 | **V Shuttle to Opposite Diagonal — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each mirrored starting diagonal**. Run 1 m along one forward diagonal, reverse back 1 m to the start, then cut 90 degrees into the other diagonal for 1 m and stop over 2 m. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Prepare a different second corner after a return rather than repeating one angle throughout a course. **Regress:** Lower speed on the return leg if the junction cut is late. |
| E4 | **Two Forward Rebound Jumps — proposed addition** | **1 × 2 linked jumps**. Make two low forward jumps with an immediate controlled rebound after the first; hold the second landing 2 s. Each flight no more than 0.4 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Express a second forward push after accepting force without a long pause. **Regress:** Shorten both flights if the first landing sinks or the second requires a setup step. |
| E5 | **Single Forward Bound to Running Exit — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 per first landing leg**. Make one forward bound within 0.4 m onto the opposite leg, then run 1.5 m along the same heading; stop over 2.5 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Let the first accepted contact feed the next forward running step without an extra jump. **Regress:** Shorten flight and exit speed if the athlete needs a recovery hop. |
| E6 | **Half-Kneeling Lateral Start — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each exit side**. Start half-kneeling with the front foot aimed toward the exit; drive through it, bring the trailing leg through and accelerate 2 m laterally relative to the initial torso. Stop over 3 m. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Create the initial exit force from the supported low leg position without relying on a preceding run. **Regress:** Use more upright torso and slower first step; defer the drill if comfortable knee contact or controlled rise cannot be established. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** Clear 3 m × 3 m around the landing; mirror directions/landing legs, no approach. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing per leg.
- **E2:** Mark the jab footprint and opposite diagonal exit. Keep the 4.5 m exit/stop and an empty escape area beyond the jab; mirror one route at a time. Exposure per attempt: 1 travel redirection, jab braking and terminal stopping.
- **E3:** Two 1 m diagonals form a right-angle V. Reserve final stop beyond the second arm and a clear abort corridor beyond the first cone; no extra return after the finish. Exposure per attempt: 2 cuts and 3 braking episodes.
- **E4:** Use two floor targets along the lane. Small hurdles may mark the same targets only if low enough for this flight; hurdle use does not create a new exercise. Clear 2 m beyond the final landing. Exposure per attempt: 2 bilateral landings, no additional hops.
- **E5:** Start near 2 m; bound, run 1.5 m and stop over 2.5 m, all within the lane. Flight distance is separate from the 1.5 m run ledger. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing and 1 terminal locomotor stop; no change of travel heading.
- **E6:** Orient the front foot/run down the cleared lane, start near 2 m and finish stopping by 7. Choose the initial torso angle so the exit remains in the lane; floor-knee contact must be comfortable. Exposure per attempt: 1 running initiation and terminal stop; no prescribed jump.

**Space and flow:** Use a measured 8 m × 6 m clear bay for the jab/diagonal exit and V route, including final stopping. Rebound and bound-to-run tasks use a single 10 m lane. Half-kneeling work needs a comfortable stable floor contact; do not require kneeling through discomfort. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Lateral Step-Down — low support, light** | **1 × 4 per stance leg**. Bodyweight first; a light dumbbell only if still easy. Lower **3 s**, lightly touch the free heel, return under control. Rest **30 s between legs; 45 s before S2**. | Keep the receiving leg controlled during asymmetric landings and the small jab. Rehearse the plant leg controlling knee travel and pelvic position during weight acceptance. Use the lowest stable support that permits a level pelvis and whole stance-foot contact; use hand support if needed. Reduce step height/load for knee collapse or pelvic drop. |
| S2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported standing hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot**. **5 s relaxed reset** between holds, **30 s between feet**, **60 s before P1**. Stand with most weight on the working foot and fingertip support. | Maintain active foot pressure while the jab changes the base. Strengthen active arch control while maintaining heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact for planting. Keep toes long; do not roll onto the outside edge. Use bilateral weight support instead if arch control needs toe-clawing or ankle rolling. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Goblet Squat** | **1 × 6**. Dumbbell held close; lower 3 s through owned range and stand decisively with both feet grounded. Rest 120 s between sets. | Maintain shared knee/hip force with a stable base as route density is reduced. Reduce load/depth if the trunk folds or heels lift. |
| P2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6**, bilateral; **2 s down**, purposeful hip extension; 2–3 RIR. Rest **120 s between working sets**. | Retain hip-hinge strength for forward exit projection. Maintain a softly bent, nearly constant knee angle, weights close and a controlled back position; stop lowering at the owned range. |
| P3 | **Lateral Lunge — goblet dumbbell** | **1 × 6 per lunging leg**. step out, lower **2 s**, push back to the original stance; 2–3 RIR. Rest **60 s between legs**. | Retain lateral acceptance and push-back strength for the jab. Keep the lunging foot grounded and knee aligned; sit into that hip without rotating away. Reduce width, depth or load for loss of control. |
| P4 | **Hip Thrust — dumbbell** | **1 × 8**. Upper back on a stable bench, dumbbell secured over pelvis; rise to hip extension without arching the back, lower 2 s. Rest 90 s before next exercise. | Support finishing hip extension in the short running exits. Reduce load/range for lumbar extension or hamstring cramp. |
| P5 | **Copenhagen Side Plank — short lever** | **1 × 15 s per supported top leg**. top knee/lower thigh on a stable bench, lower leg clear. Rest **60 s between sides**. Use bodyweight; if already controlled and too easy, secure a small dumbbell at the upper hip with the upper hand to reach the holding-effort target. | Maintain adductor control while the jab shifts force sideways. Keep shoulder comfortable and pelvis level. Use light lower-foot assistance if needed; stop for groin/shoulder pain or loss of position. |
| P6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **1 × 8**, bilateral; knees near 90°, forefeet on floor, **1 s at top, 2 s lowering, quiet bottom reset**; 2–3 RIR. Rest **90 s between sets**. | Maintain bent-knee ankle force without extra calf volume. Secure the weights above the knees, maintain knee angle and repeatable heel rise. Reduce load if heel height falls or bouncing replaces muscle force. |

**Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 2 × 4 light then intermediate; P2: 1 × 5 light; P3: 1 × 3 per side light; P4: 1 × 5 light; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** If the step is unsuitable, use instead a hand-supported shallow single-leg squat, the same 1 × 4 per leg and easy effort. If bench geometry is unsuitable, use instead a loaded dynamic floor glute bridge at the same rep/effort and preparation dose. If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.

**Workload:** 10 locomotor attempts; 23 m declared movement paths and up to 25 m additional stopping (some approach paths already contain braking). 8 changes of travel direction; 18 locomotor braking episodes, which are **not foot-contact counts**. 2 bilateral and 4 unilateral flight landings; 0 throws. Keep intensity/flight types separate as described above. Light work: 2 rounds/4 side bouts, 8 reps plus 30 s holds. Primary: **6 rounds/8 bouts**, 40 reps plus 30 s holds. Preparation adds 9 bouts, 29 reps plus 10 s holds. Bilateral bouts count once; per-side work counts both sides. Access & Prepare 1, walking resets and outside training remain unquantified, not zero.

**Timing:** **61–73 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, before extra group queues: E1–E6 approximately 4, 8, 5, 3, 5, 4 minutes; light block 6; primary block 26. This includes attempts, reset/rest, demonstrations, ordinary setup and the stated preparation sets; the upper allowance covers load changes/extended recovery. Full duration remains unknown. Alternate athletes only when actual intervals supply their prescribed rest and the route clears. A coach must model the real class size, equipment quantities and booking; do not compress rests, delete slots or call a partial class complete to fit an assumed window.

**Scheduling:** This loads the legs through plants, landings and lifting. Roughly 48–72 hours before comparably demanding lower-body work is a starting planning interval, adjusted for actual recovery and the full sport/gym/tumbling schedule. A new drill does not mean recovered tissues. No observed response is yet available.

**Progression note:** Reduce discrete cuts to eight and keep each flight small. The new false-step and mixed-corner tasks apply prior qualities but do not provide a matched test of previous drills. Repeated strength anchors: Keep one round per lift, familiar technical reserve and no automatic load increase. Use stable goblet squat and the already introduced hip thrust; omit the separate long-lever bridge this day to avoid unnecessary posterior-chain overlap. Advance only one justified demand after reported control on both sides, intended effort and acceptable recovery; hold for unknown response, regress for loss of control or overload. Unique drills permit comparison of coaching criteria, not claims of improved test scores between different routes.
