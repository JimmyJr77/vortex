# Agility: Directional — Class 4 of 12

**Primary effort:** Consolidate controlled braking and a clean restart while reducing sharp-cut exposure.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 trains braking followed by a same-heading restart. Small forward/back rebounds, a falling initiation, a running-to-shuffle transition and two different forward landing bases support sagittal control without repeating the earlier stopping drills.

**Performance marker within E2:** For E2, record whether the athlete stops inside the braking zone, holds balanced for one second and accelerates again without a false backward step or upright jump. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** Treat this as consolidation after the planned diagonal class, not a harder day. Review actual soreness and coordination; keep the hinge and knee-loading dose below a demanding developmental session when recovery is uncertain. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

Complete the **existing predetermined Access & Prepare 1**. Its content and duration are not supplied; neither is created or modified here. Include its actual exposures in the coach's full-session review.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Forward-Back Line Hops** | **1 × 6 small continuous hops**. Both feet hop a few centimetres forward/back over the line, staying low; finish after six landings. Rest **45 s between attempts**; **60 s before the next exercise**. | Small sagittal ankle-force reversals support stop-and-go rhythm with little approach momentum. **Regress:** Reduce excursion/cadence for lost rhythm or stiff noisy landings. |
| E2 | **Deceleration Re-Acceleration Sprint** | **2 × 2 attempts**. Accelerate 2 m, brake within 1.5 m, hold 1 s, accelerate another 1.5 m and stop within 2 m. Alternate starting lead foot. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main force renewal after a full stop without adding a sharp turn. **Regress:** Lower both acceleration intents if the first stop drifts or the restart needs a false step. |
| E3 | **Falling Start Acceleration — short exit** | **1 × 2 attempts**. Lean the whole body forward from the ankles until a step is needed, then accelerate 2 m and stop over 3 m; alternate first step. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Direct the body forward on the restart instead of stepping backward to find force. **Regress:** Use a smaller lean if the athlete folds at the waist or reaches far ahead. |
| E4 | **Forward Run to Lateral Shuffle — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each exit direction**. Run 1.5 m, brake before the corner, keep the chest's original facing and shuffle sideways 1 m; stop over the next 1.5 m. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Retain a low controllable stance while reducing forward force and transferring into lateral travel. **Regress:** Lower entry speed if feet cross or the torso spins into a sprint. |
| E5 | **Forward Hop to Stick — bilateral** | **1 × 2 jumps**. Jump forward no more than 0.5 m from both feet, land on both and hold 2 s. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Share sagittal force acceptance through both legs after a forceful forward push. **Regress:** Shorten flight for extra steps or knee/trunk collapse. |
| E6 | **Single-Leg Forward Hop to Stick** | **1 × 1 per leg**. Hop straight forward within 0.3 m from one leg to the same leg; hold 2 s without free-foot contact. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Isolate sagittal single-leg acceptance after reducing diagonal and cutting complexity. **Regress:** Shorten the hop for loss of foot/knee control. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** Middle of the lane, toes facing along its length; no obstacle or maximal height. Exposure per attempt: 6 low bilateral landings.
- **E2:** Start at 1 m, accelerate to 3, stop by 4.5, restart to 6 and stop by 8. Leave 8–10 empty. Exposure per attempt: 2 braking episodes, 0 direction changes, 5 m movement before the final 2 m allowance.
- **E3:** Start at 2 m, accelerate to 4 and stop by 7. This is a 2 m delivery variant, not a ten-yard sprint. Exposure per attempt: 1 running initiation and terminal stop.
- **E4:** Set a short L route inside the stated bay, with 2 m straight-ahead abort clearance at the corner. Mirror one side at a time. Exposure per attempt: 1 direction change and 2 braking episodes.
- **E5:** Centre of lane with 2 m clear beyond landing; no run-up or height target. Exposure per attempt: 1 bilateral landing.
- **E6:** Along the lane centreline, with 2 m clear ahead and beside the landing area as actually measured. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral landing per leg.

**Space and flow:** E1/E2/E3/E5/E6 fit the long axis of a 10 m lane with the stated margins. E4 needs a measured 7 m × 4 m bay for an orthogonal gait transition and stopping. The width remains unconfirmed. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Lateral Step-Down — low support, light** | **1 × 4 per stance leg**. Bodyweight first; a light dumbbell only if still easy. Lower **3 s**, lightly touch the free heel, return under control. Rest **30 s between legs; 45 s before S2**. | Retain low-fatigue knee and pelvis control while the main session reduces cutting demand. Rehearse the plant leg controlling knee travel and pelvic position during weight acceptance. Use the lowest stable support that permits a level pelvis and whole stance-foot contact; use hand support if needed. Reduce step height/load for knee collapse or pelvic drop. |
| S2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported standing hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot**. **5 s relaxed reset** between holds, **30 s between feet**, **60 s before P1**. Stand with most weight on the working foot and fingertip support. | Keep foot pressure controlled in the held stop before restarting. Strengthen active arch control while maintaining heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact for planting. Keep toes long; do not roll onto the outside edge. Use bilateral weight support instead if arch control needs toe-clawing or ankle rolling. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells, both feet on floor** | **1 × 6 per lead leg**. **3 s down**, smooth forceful rise; 2–3 RIR. Rest **60 s between legs, 120 s between rounds**. | Maintain lead-leg force at a reduced round count for consolidation. Keep full front-foot pressure and knee travel aligned with the foot. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee drift or trunk collapse. |
| P2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6**, bilateral; **2 s down**, purposeful hip extension; 2–3 RIR. Rest **120 s between working sets**. | Maintain hip-hinge strength without a second demanding work set. Maintain a softly bent, nearly constant knee angle, weights close and a controlled back position; stop lowering at the owned range. |
| P3 | **Lateral Lunge — goblet dumbbell** | **1 × 6 per lunging leg**. step out, lower **2 s**, push back to the original stance; 2–3 RIR. Rest **60 s between legs**. | Retain the frontal force base despite fewer lateral cuts today. Keep the lunging foot grounded and knee aligned; sit into that hip without rotating away. Reduce width, depth or load for loss of control. |
| P4 | **Glute Bridge Iso Hold — long-lever hamstring variant** | **1 × 20 s**, bilateral. Heels farther from the hips than a standard bridge, knees still bent; bodyweight or a securely hand-held sandbag over the pelvis to reach the holding-effort target. | Maintain heel-dig support for sagittal braking. Try to pull the heels toward the body without sliding them; keep ribs down. Bring heels closer or remove load for cramp/lost height; stop for symptoms. |
| P5 | **Copenhagen Side Plank — short lever** | **1 × 15 s per supported top leg**. top knee/lower thigh on a stable bench, lower leg clear. Rest **60 s between sides**. Use bodyweight; if already controlled and too easy, secure a small dumbbell at the upper hip with the upper hand to reach the holding-effort target. | Maintain adductor/pelvic support with one short round. Keep shoulder comfortable and pelvis level. Use light lower-foot assistance if needed; stop for groin/shoulder pain or loss of position. |
| P6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **1 × 8**, bilateral; knees near 90°, forefeet on floor, **1 s at top, 2 s lowering, quiet bottom reset**; 2–3 RIR. Rest **90 s between sets**. | Keep bent-knee ankle force while trimming total lower-leg work. Secure the weights above the knees, maintain knee angle and repeatable heel rise. Reduce load if heel height falls or bouncing replaces muscle force. |

**Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 2 × 3 per leg, light then intermediate; P2: 1 × 5 light; P3: 1 × 3 per side light; P4: 1 × 8 s easy; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** If the step is unsuitable, use instead a hand-supported shallow single-leg squat, the same 1 × 4 per leg and easy effort. If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.

**Workload:** 8 locomotor attempts; 29 m declared movement paths and up to 17 m additional stopping (some approach paths already contain braking). 2 changes of travel direction; 14 locomotor braking episodes, which are **not foot-contact counts**. 8 bilateral and 2 unilateral flight landings; 0 throws. Keep intensity/flight types separate as described above. Light work: 2 rounds/4 side bouts, 8 reps plus 30 s holds. Primary: **6 rounds/9 bouts**, 38 reps plus 50 s holds. Preparation adds 11 bouts, 28 reps plus 18 s holds. Bilateral bouts count once; per-side work counts both sides. Access & Prepare 1, walking resets and outside training remain unquantified, not zero.

**Timing:** **62–74 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, before extra group queues: E1–E6 approximately 3, 8, 4, 4, 4, 4 minutes; light block 6; primary block 29. This includes attempts, reset/rest, demonstrations, ordinary setup and the stated preparation sets; the upper allowance covers load changes/extended recovery. Full duration remains unknown. Alternate athletes only when actual intervals supply their prescribed rest and the route clears. A coach must model the real class size, equipment quantities and booking; do not compress rests, delete slots or call a partial class complete to fit an assumed window.

**Scheduling:** This loads the legs through plants, landings and lifting. Roughly 48–72 hours before comparably demanding lower-body work is a starting planning interval, adjusted for actual recovery and the full sport/gym/tumbling schedule. A new drill does not mean recovered tissues. No observed response is yet available.

**Progression note:** Consolidate the common stop-and-drive quality through different tasks, with only two direction changes. Small line hops are tracked separately from unilateral landings. This is not automatic overload. Repeated strength anchors: Hold split squat at one round, and reduce RDL and soleus to one round each. Retain the same execution and 2–3 RIR targets; do not raise loads to compensate for fewer sets. Advance only one justified demand after reported control on both sides, intended effort and acceptable recovery; hold for unknown response, regress for loss of control or overload. Unique drills permit comparison of coaching criteria, not claims of improved test scores between different routes.

Kept the complete stop-restart path inside 10 m and reduced strength rounds while preserving six useful lifts.
