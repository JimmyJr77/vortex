# Agility: Directional — Class 12 of 12

**Primary effort:** Apply early braking and purposeful exits across new, fully planned multidirectional routes.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 is the final application task: an M-shaped route with three known corners. A gathered stop-to-diagonal restart, compass route, three-hop pattern, crossover-to-backward transition and single-leg push to a shared landing sample complementary force-control demands. This is a new application, not a repeated pre/post test.

**Performance marker within E2:** For E2, record each of the three corners separately: early braking, controlled foot/knee/pelvis position and two exit steps toward the next target. Report valid corners and routes by mirrored direction; never compare its time directly with another drill's time. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** Review all available actual attendance, loading and response records before delivery. None is currently supplied. Keep the route entry intent conservative; link three hops only at a spacing the athlete can control. Do not use the final class as a maximum test or a reason to finish poor-quality work. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Gathered Stop-to-Diagonal Restart — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each exit direction**. Run 1.5 m, brake over 1 m while taking two controlled reorienting steps into a staggered base, hold 1 s, then accelerate 1.5 m along the known 45-degree exit and stop over 2.5 m. No sliding hockey stop. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Rebuild a usable base before the next exit when an immediate single-plant cut is unsuitable. **Regress:** Lower entry speed and shorten reorientation steps if the athlete twists over a fixed foot. |
| E2 | **M-Drill Multidirectional Cut — compact course** | **1 × 1 each mirrored course**. Run the four marked legs of the M route with controlled braking at its three intermediate cones, then stop over 2 m on the last heading. No cone touching or extra return. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main integration: prepare each next plant while producing an effective exit from the current one. **Regress:** Reduce speed over all legs if any later corner becomes a rushed pivot; do not add a faster retry. |
| E3 | **Five-Cone Compass Route — known three-arm sequence** | **1 × 1 complete N–centre–E–centre–S route**. From the centre, run 1 m north, return to centre, run 1 m east, return to centre, then run 1 m south and stop over 1.5 m. All directions are known; west cone marks unused space, not a reaction target. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Choose the next force direction at a shared junction after two different returns. **Regress:** Lower speed returning to centre if the next arm cannot be entered without an extra gathering hop. |
| E4 | **Three-Hop Directional Pattern — same leg — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 three-hop pattern per leg**. On one leg, hop forward 0.2 m, sideways away from the free leg 0.2 m, then diagonally back toward the starting point; hold the third landing 2 s. Mirror on the other leg. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Accept and redirect small forces repeatedly while one leg remains the support. **Regress:** Shrink the target triangle while keeping its sequence; stop if the first/second landing needs free-foot assistance or a setup hop. |
| E5 | **Crossover Run to Backpedal Exit — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each crossover entry direction**. Crossover-run laterally 1 m, brake and reorganize into a 1.5 m backward exit relative to the original torso facing; stop over 1.5 m. No spin into a forward sprint. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Produce backward force after a crossing lateral entry with the feet untangled. **Regress:** Slow the crossover and shorten steps if the athlete cannot find the backward gait without crossing heels. |
| E6 | **Single-Leg Lateral Push Jump to Two-Foot Landing — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 per takeoff leg**. Hold a shallow single-leg stance for 1 s, push laterally within 0.3 m and land on both feet for a 2 s hold. Rest **75 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Express each plant leg's lateral force while sharing the receiving load at the end of the session. **Regress:** Shorten flight if the free leg must touch down before takeoff or the shared landing is uncontrolled. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** Mark the entry/braking strip and separate diagonal exit/stop. Feet unload before turning; clear a straight abort path beyond the braking strip. Exposure per attempt: 1 travel redirection and 2 braking episodes; the 1 m initial braking strip is included in path distance.
- **E2:** Local metre coordinates: A(0,0), B(1,1.5), C(2,0.5), D(3,1.5), E(4,0). Legs total about 6.43 m. Translate the pattern into the bay with margins; final stop extends 2 m beyond E along D→E. Mirror across the long axis for the second route; keep tangent abort paths empty. Exposure per attempt: 3 cuts and 4 braking episodes per course. Corners are approximately 101, 90 and 101 degrees, not mislabeled as three identical 45-degree cuts.
- **E3:** Four outer cones sit 1 m from centre. Travel totals 5 m plus the southward 1.5 m stop. Coach declares a right-foot turn at N and left-foot turn at E; observe actual feet rather than assuming them. Keep centre crossing and all abort paths empty. Exposure per attempt: 4 cuts at N, centre, E and centre, plus 1 terminal stop: 5 braking episodes. Both lateral plant directions occur within the course.
- **E4:** Three close floor targets in a 3 m × 3 m clear area; no height target, no fourth hop. Final diagonal is about 0.28 m, not 0.2 m. Exposure per attempt: 3 unilateral landings per leg; do not add these to the discrete running-cut count.
- **E5:** Use a short L inside the bay; the backward exit and stop run along its long dimension, with 2 m beyond the incoming corner clear. Exposure per attempt: 1 direction change and 2 braking episodes.
- **E6:** Clear a 3 m × 3 m landing area; mirror takeoff legs and directions. The free foot joins only for landing. Exposure per attempt: 1 unilateral takeoff and 1 bilateral landing per attempt.

**Space and flow:** The M course and compass exits require a measured 9 m × 7 m clear bay, including abort and stopping corridors. This is the widest requirement in the sequence and is not confirmed by three 10 m lanes. Measure it before delivery; if unavailable, the coach must adapt/defer those drills and record the uncompleted directional coverage rather than imply all twelve were delivered. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

Slowly open/close pelvis through a small owned arc without rolling the stance foot. | Maintain controlled pelvis orientation over each plant leg in the integrated routes. Reduce arc or use more hand support if knee/foot rotates uncontrollably. |
| S2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported standing hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot**. **5 s relaxed reset** between holds, **30 s between feet**, **60 s before P1**. Use bilateral weight support instead if arch control needs toe-clawing or ankle rolling. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

**Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 2 × 4 light then intermediate; P2: 1 × 5 light; P3: 1 × 3 per side light; P4: 1 × 5 light; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** If bench geometry is unsuitable, use instead a loaded dynamic floor glute bridge at the same rep/effort and preparation dose. If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.
