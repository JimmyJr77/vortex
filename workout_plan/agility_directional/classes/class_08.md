# Agility: Directional — Class 8 of 12

**Primary effort:** Carry controlled curved momentum into a clean exit while consolidating strength load.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision and demonstrated readiness for the selected movements. This is a conditional prescription; no athlete completion or improvement has been reported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E2 combines a curved entry with one planned plant. Lateral skips supply rhythmic side force, a figure-eight changes curvature continuously, carioca rehearses alternating hip crossings, a two-vector bound changes landing direction, and an arcing shuffle exit avoids forcing every transition into a sharp corner.

**Performance marker within E2:** In E2, record whether the athlete controls lean on the arc, brakes before the declared plant and exits along the tangent-relative 90-degree line without a recovery hop. Record attempts actually performed and valid attempts by side, entry intent and faults. Use scheduled attempts only. This drill is unique in the curriculum; its results are not a like-for-like time/distance comparison with a different class.

**Before delivery:** Keep arc speed modest; this class is not a maximum-speed curved sprint test. Verify comfortable carioca foot placement and each bound landing before linking the two flights. Previous written doses are not evidence of recovery. Review recent sport, gym, other Vortex classes and separately programmed tumbling. Missing feedback never proves tolerance. Use conservative entry speed and familiar controllable loading; do not raise approach speed, cut demand and strength load together.

## 2. Explosiveness

Use E1–E6 in order. Routes/directions are known before starting; this is directional practice, not reactive anticipation. E2 receives the main practice allocation. All takeoffs, pushes and exits have fast intent within demonstrated control; no maximum entry speed, maximal-distance landings or conditioning races. A continuous route or flight-to-exit is one drill, not a circuit of additional exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Lateral Skip — two cycles — proposed addition** | **1 × 2 skip cycles each direction**. Step laterally then make a small hop on that leading leg, gather and repeat once; no maximal distance. Travel no more than 1 m per two-cycle attempt. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Produce repeatable lateral push rhythm without a long approach or sharp plant. **Regress:** Reduce step/hop size if rhythm needs crossed feet or trunk sway. |
| E2 | **Curved Run to Cut** | **2 × 1 each curve/plant direction**. Run a quarter arc of radius 1.25 m, brake before its end, plant into a 90-degree change relative to the incoming tangent, accelerate 1.5 m and stop over 2.5 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Main curved-entry braking and force redirection, with the turn point declared in advance. **Regress:** Lower arc speed if leaning persists through the plant or the athlete cannot find the exit line. |
| E3 | **Figure-8 Cone Run** | **1 × 1 each initial loop direction**. Run one smooth figure-eight around two cones, one full loop each, then leave on the marked tangent and stop over 2.5 m. Controlled speed, no cone touches. Rest **120 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Change the direction of body lean smoothly between left and right curves. **Regress:** Slow the loops if the athlete cuts across the circles or loses posture at the crossing. |
| E4 | **Carioca Quick Hips** | **1 × 1 pass each direction**. Travel 2 m using alternating front and behind crossover contacts, then decelerate over 1.5 m. Keep the hips moving smoothly and the feet low. Rest **60 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Reorganize hip crossing without stopping the lateral travel each step. **Regress:** Reduce cadence and step width if feet tangle; retain a crisp push without chasing speed. |
| E5 | **Diagonal-to-Lateral Bound Pattern — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 two-bound pattern each mirrored side**. Bound diagonally forward to the opposite leg, then redirect laterally onto the original leg; hold the second landing 2 s. Each flight stays within 0.4 m. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Accept one vector and send the next push along a different vector without an extra setup hop. **Regress:** Shorten both flights if the first landing cannot support the second push. |
| E6 | **Shuffle Arc to Running Exit — proposed addition** | **1 × 1 each arcing direction**. Shuffle 1 m, smoothly open into a quarter arc of radius 1 m, then run 1.5 m along its tangent and stop over 2.5 m. No abrupt planted corner. Rest **90 s between attempts**; **90 s before the next exercise**. | Let the hips and force direction change progressively when a rounded exit is appropriate. **Regress:** Reduce entry speed if the arc becomes an uncontrolled pivot or cross-step stumble. |

**Exact setup and counting:**

- **E1:** Along the lane axis while facing across it; finish after two step-hop cycles and settle in place. Exposure per attempt: 2 unilateral hop landings per direction, plus two ordinary entry steps; not sprint metres.
- **E2:** Arc length about 1.96 m; mark tangent and perpendicular exit so the cut angle is unambiguous. Clear a 2 m tangent abort corridor. Exposure per attempt: 1 planted cut and 2 locomotor braking episodes.
- **E3:** Use two tangent circles of radius 0.75 m, centres 1.5 m apart; total curved travel about 9.42 m. The footprint is compact even though route travel exceeds lane length. Clear the crossing and final tangent stop. Exposure per attempt: Continuous curving with 1 terminal stop; 0 discrete planted cuts counted.
- **E4:** Move along the long axis of the lane, facing across it; start near 2, travel to 4 and stop by 5.5, then mirror. Exposure per attempt: 1 lateral-gait pass and terminal stop; no prescribed flight landings.
- **E5:** Mark the two small landing targets in a 3 m × 3 m clear area. One continuous two-contact bounding pattern, not two separate sets. Exposure per attempt: 2 unilateral landings, one each leg, per pattern.
- **E6:** The declared path is 1 + 1.57 + 1.5 m, with a separate 2.5 m stop. Mark the arc so it stays inside the measured bay. Exposure per attempt: 0 discrete planted cuts; 1 terminal braking episode.

**Space and flow:** Use a measured 9 m × 6 m clear bay for arcs and the figure-eight, including each final 2.5 m stopping path. Mark radii rather than using random cone spacing. Only one route is active at a time; the three separate lanes do not confirm this shared width. Lengths include final stopping; do not connect separate lanes into a longer runway. Verify lane width and side clearance on site. One athlete uses the entire active route at a time; close intersecting/neighboring paths as needed. Coach stands outside the plant and exit corridors. Returns occur only after the active attempt finishes, with the next athlete held until the route is clear. For throws, clear the entire flight/impact/retrieval area, throw away from people, let the ball settle and retrieve on the coach's release; do not catch the rebound. If that clearance is unavailable, use the stated replacement within the same slot. Stationary tasks require a clear landing/fall area; smaller travel never proves an unknown width is adequate.

**Quality decisions:** First scheduled attempts calibrate entry intent and count in the dose. After a fault, recover and reduce speed, flight distance or range in the **same** drill on the next scheduled attempt; no make-up attempts. Do not rename a previous drill as a regression to satisfy the unique-drill rule. Stop the task for repeated overrun, loss of knee/foot/pelvis control despite regression, or immediately for pain, dizziness, slipping or an uncontrolled plant. If a required skill/space cannot be established, defer that drill and record incomplete actual work; do not invent successful completion or force the written counts.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

Light/bodyweight strengthening, **at least 5 clean reps in reserve** or an equally easy hold. No shaking or burning; these sets must leave the athlete ready to lift. Rest 30 s between sides, 45 s between these exercises, then 60 s before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Hip Airplane — supported** | **1 × 4 per stance leg**. Bodyweight, hand support and slight hip hinge. Slowly open/close pelvis through a small owned arc without rolling the stance foot. | Control pelvis orientation over one leg while the travel direction curves. Reduce arc or use more hand support if knee/foot rotates uncontrollably. |
| S2 | **Mini-Band Lateral Walk** | **1 × 4 steps each way**. Very light loop above knees; small quiet steps, toes forward and knees aligned. Both directions are one set round. | Maintain hip support against drifting toward the outside of the curve. Use a lighter band/smaller step if pelvis sways or the task burns. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

Use challenging, technically controlled working loads with **2–3 good repetitions in reserve**. Isometrics finish with about **5 s of clean holding capacity remaining**, without testing a maximum. Choose load from demonstrated ability and today's preparation sets, not age or a prior written load. Repeated lifts retain their technique/effort standard; recurring work is intentional strength progression, not a repeated explosive drill.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift — supported dumbbell** | **1 × 6 per stance leg**. Light fingertip support; hold dumbbell in opposite hand, keep pelvis level and hinge 2 s down through controlled range. Rest 60 s between legs. | Load hip force with single-leg pelvic control relevant to curved stance contacts. Reduce range/load when pelvis opens; support should remove balance as the load limiter. |
| P2 | **Lateral Lunge — goblet dumbbell** | **1 × 6 per lunging leg**. step out, lower **2 s**, push back to the original stance; 2–3 RIR. Rest **60 s between legs, 120 s between rounds**. | Retain lead-leg knee/hip force through the exit step. Keep full front-foot pressure and knee travel aligned with the foot. | P4 | **Glute Bridge Iso Hold — long-lever hamstring variant** | **1 × 20 s**, bilateral. **Preparation sets, separate from work:** P1: 1 × 3 per leg light; P2: 1 × 3 per side light; P3: 2 × 3 per leg, light then intermediate; P4: 1 × 8 s easy; P5: 1 × 5 s per side assisted; P6: 1 × 5 light. These use the same listed exercises; they are not new slots. Allow 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s between preparation rounds, 90 s before the first compound working bout and 60 s for other lifts. Rest at least 90 s between primary exercises. Additional ramps must be logged and extend time; never rush the lift to fit the clock.

**Equipment-fit replacements:** If no suitable stable Copenhagen support fits, use instead floor side-lying hip adduction, upper foot on the floor in front, 1 × 8 per lower leg at 2–3 RIR with 60 s between sides; add a small hand-secured dumbbell above the inner knee only if needed and comfortable. Its preparation is 1 × 4 easy reps per leg instead of the hold ramps. This proposed replacement adds 16 dynamic working reps and removes 30 s of working holds; preparation adds 8 reps and removes 10 s of holds. Bouts are unchanged; allow up to 2 extra minutes. For seated calf work, use a securely held sandbag instead of dumbbells when its shape and load fit better. All replacements **use instead of** the original slot, retain its working/preparation dose unless stated otherwise, and must meet the same technical effort target. Record changed implements/support before comparing a strength anchor. No unlisted rack, cable stack, sled, machine or throwing wall is assumed.

**Workload:** 10 locomotor attempts; 44.82 m declared movement paths and up to 23 m additional stopping (some approach paths already contain braking). 4 changes of travel direction; 14 locomotor braking episodes, which are **not foot-contact counts**. 0 bilateral and 8 unilateral flight landings; 0 throws. Keep intensity/flight types separate as described above. Light work: 2 rounds/4 side bouts, 16 reps plus 0 s holds. Primary: **6 rounds/10 bouts**, 44 reps plus 50 s holds. Preparation adds 12 bouts, 29 reps plus 18 s holds. Bilateral bouts count once; per-side work counts both sides. Access & Prepare 1, walking resets and outside training remain unquantified, not zero.

**Timing:** **68–80 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, before extra group queues: E1–E6 approximately 4, 8, 5, 4, 5, 5 minutes; light block 6; primary block 31. This includes attempts, reset/rest, demonstrations, ordinary setup and the stated preparation sets; the upper allowance covers load changes/extended recovery. Full duration remains unknown. Alternate athletes only when actual intervals supply their prescribed rest and the route clears. A coach must model the real class size, equipment quantities and booking; do not compress rests, delete slots or call a partial class complete to fit an assumed window.

**Scheduling:** This loads the legs through plants, landings and lifting. Roughly 48–72 hours before comparably demanding lower-body work is a starting planning interval, adjusted for actual recovery and the full sport/gym/tumbling schedule. A new drill does not mean recovered tissues. No observed response is yet available.

**Progression note:** New curved and crossing patterns consolidate directional control at modest speed; only E2 contains a discrete running cut. Longer figure-eight path is paced as short skill practice with 120 s recovery, not conditioning. Repeated strength anchors: Keep one working round in all six slots. Replace the bilateral hinge with one supported single-leg hinge round for pelvis control; load is newly calibrated and not compared directly with a bilateral RDL load. Other anchors hold their reduced doses. Advance only one justified demand after reported control on both sides, intended effort and acceptable recovery; hold for unknown response, regress for loss of control or overload. Unique drills permit comparison of coaching criteria, not claims of improved test scores between different routes.
