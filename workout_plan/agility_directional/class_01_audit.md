## Selection findings and revisions

| Consequential choice | Final decision and reason |
|---|---|
| Main direction-change task | Select the short 180° turn over a full 5-0-5 or 5-10-5. The latter routes cannot be assumed to fit three separate 10 m lanes. The turn is a substantial braking task even with a short approach: control entry speed, retain full recovery and give it six attempts before lateral support work. |
| Six explosive jobs | Forward stop, running reversal, unilateral lateral landing, lateral stop, lateral reversal, crossover running exit. Each is a discrete movement with a distinct terminal action or force demand. No generic vertical-jump/throw filler, unloaded mobility row, or endurance interval. |
| Stop versus reversal overlap | E1 prepares forward braking before E2 adds reorientation and drive; E4 rehearses facing-preserving lateral stopping before E5 adds immediate lateral return. The two stop-only drills receive just two attempts each. Related tasks earn separate slots because their terminal control versus force-reversal jobs differ. |
| Lateral bounds | Four opposite-leg landings suffice as a small supporting dose. Reject depth drops and repeated skater rebounds for this initial prescription: they add impact or elastic demands without an observed need. E3 is explosive push plus controlled absorption, not a maximal-distance test. |
| Sixth explosive slot | Prefer the crossover running exit over a pogo or ball throw: it directly trains the first exit step with hip reorientation. Preserve crossover-first entry; a drop step or a bound would change the task. |
| Light strengthening | Low step-down plus active foot control both address plant mechanics. Easy effort, eight step-down reps and short holds limit pre-fatigue. No generic abdominal or unrelated shoulder circuit. |
| Primary allocation | Split squat gets two rounds per side; RDL gets two bilateral rounds. Lateral lunge, bridge and Copenhagen receive one round each. Two calf rounds retain direct bent-knee ankle loading. This is 13 actual working bouts, not nine identical-load sets. All six must meet their challenging technical effort targets. |
| Primary overlap | Split squat = lead-leg knee/hip force; RDL = loaded hip hinge; lateral lunge = frontal acceptance/push; bridge = fixed-position heel-dig/knee-flexor support; Copenhagen = isolated adductor/pelvic support; soleus = bent-knee ankle force. Limit the latter support work instead of prescribing six large compound workouts. |
| Library revisions | Follow consolidated slugs for the turn, lateral landing, split squat, lateral lunge, bridge, Copenhagen and soleus. E5 remains an honestly proposed addition; do not attach a convenient but incorrect reactive-drill ID. |
| Loading/alternatives | Specify reserve targets for both dynamic and isometric strength. Add assistance or modest secure loading to hit the intended demand, not maximal tests. Give explicit equipment-fit replacements and count P5's replacement preparation/dynamic reps separately. |
| Progression | Retain the new reference protocol for the next class rather than auto-increasing distance, angle and load. Unknown athlete response remains unknown. New diagonal work on the map requires measured space; it is not assumed available. |

## Time audit

Estimates are for one athlete with equipment ready, not a confirmed group timetable. Work, walking resets, rest, instruction and load changes are included. Transition/demonstration time may occupy a prescribed recovery interval when the athlete is resting; it is not counted twice. Extra ramp sets or prolonged coaching increase the estimate.

|---|---|---|
| E1 | 4 | Two brief travel/hold attempts; 60 s between, 90 s transfer/recovery; initial explanation and return. |
| E2 | 10 | Six brief turns/exits/stops, five 90 s recoveries and 90 s to next task. Coach records during recovery; preceding transfer supports demonstration. |
| E3 | 4 | Four bounds/holds, three 30 s resets, 60 s transfer, stance/landing instruction. |
| E4 | 4 | Two shuffle/stop/hold attempts, 60 s recovery and 90 s transfer; setup/face-direction explanation. |
| E5 | 7 | Four reversals/exits/stops, three 90 s recoveries and 90 s transfer. |
| E6 | 5 | Four short exits/stops, three 60 s recoveries and 90 s transfer. |
| S1–S2 | 6 | Eight slow step-down reps; six short-foot holds, relaxed resets and side recovery; support setup and transfer. |
| P1 | 12 | Four working side bouts plus four ramp bouts; 60 s working side rest/120 s between rounds; preparation rests and outgoing recovery. |
| P2 | 7 | One ramp and two working bouts; 90 s to first work, 120 s between working sets, outgoing recovery/loading allowance. |
| P3 | 6 | Two ramp side bouts plus two working side bouts; side/working recovery, load choice and outgoing recovery. |
| P4 | 3 | One easy 8 s hold, 60 s recovery, one 20 s working hold and 90 s outgoing recovery. |
| P5 | 5 | Two easy 5 s holds, side rest and 60 s to work; two 15 s working holds with 60 s between; outgoing recovery. |

| **Subtotal / usable range** | **78 / 78–90** | 34 explosive + 6 light + 38 primary; up to 12 more minutes for individual load setup or extended recovery. Access & Prepare 1 excluded, not assigned zero duration. |

**Group constraint:** Unknown athlete/staff counts, load quantities and support dimensions prevent a complete group clock. Use serial supervised work, not simultaneous unsupervised heavy lifting. A three-athlete example is a capacity calculation, not a confirmed class: E2 alone requires 18 attempts. If the lane is clear within 25 s, a 35 s launch interval gives each athlete 105 s between starts, supporting at least 90 s recovery when their effort lasts at most 15 s. Eighteen launches span 595 s before the last effort and outgoing recovery, around 12 minutes with that recovery. Pause launches if the lane/return is occupied or individual rest is insufficient. Larger groups or slower clearing increase time. Do not infer full-session fit from this one-drill example.

A coach must establish actual group size, equipment allocation, clearing times, supervision and booking before using a group timetable. If a booking is shorter than the fully modeled class, revise meaningful doses/flow while retaining 6/2/6, or resolve the booking; do not silently rush recovery or delete work. Full session time remains unresolved, as permitted by the specification when Access & Prepare 1 duration is unavailable.

## Workload, recovery and limits

The [ledger](progression_ledger.md) distinguishes 10 direction changes, 28 braking episodes, four unilateral bound landings, 18 locomotor attempts and up to 120 m total locomotor path. An episode is not a foot contact. Main strength is 9 rounds/13 bouts, 64 dynamic reps plus 50 s holding; ramps are additional. No equivalence score makes these loads interchangeable.

Primary RIR, isometric reserve and easy stabilization limits have practical stop/regress instructions. The coach must check recent training and movement competence. Recent actual sport/gym/tumbling and response to the separate Vertical Jumps plan are unknown. The small plyometric count does not demonstrate recovery from cutting, adductor or lower-body lifting load. Scheduling guidance is contextual.
