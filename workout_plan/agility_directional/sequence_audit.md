## Mechanical duplicate review

The [72-drill index](drill_index.md) records a concrete distinction for every entry; the per-class audits contain exact movement identities and comparison notes. Names alone were not used to decide uniqueness. The curriculum necessarily reuses movement primitives and several meaningful directional variants; it does not claim 72 unrelated biomechanical families.

- **Stopping versus exiting:** terminal stops, paused restarts, immediate reversals and flight-to-running exits have different terminal actions. A standing crossover start, a crossover running stop and a crossover running reversal are explicitly different contact sequences.
- **Gait:** forward running, square shuffling, crossover running and backpedaling change incoming support and the first exit action. Class 6 separates a backpedal reversal with facing unchanged from a same-heading open turn with facing changed.
- **Flight support:** two-foot to two-foot, one-foot to same-foot, one-foot to opposite-foot and two-foot to one-foot landings are separately declared. Body turns in flight differ from fixed-foot pivots. Terminal holds differ from continuous rebound or flight-to-run tasks.
- **Direction:** forward/backward, lateral and diagonal force demands have specific jobs. The 45°, 90° and 135° running cuts are intentional angle-specific prescriptions, not three names for the same route. Directional landing/pogo variants are described transparently as related variants.
- **Linked routes:** W, Z, triangle, U, figure-eight, X, box, V, M and compass courses have explicit different leg orders, gait orders or topology. Reusing a turn within a longer route is not falsely presented as a new fundamental movement. Whole courses, rather than every constituent turn, are the drill entries.
- **Rejected duplicates:** a closeout-style linear stop, another pure curved acceleration-to-stop and another lateral rebound-return pair were removed from consideration because they would largely duplicate earlier work. New names or altered distances alone were insufficient.

Strength/light exercises may recur under the clarified instruction. No identical exercise is counted in two sections of one default class. Replacements occupy the original slot; they are not extra circuits, finishers or testing blocks. The two no-ball substitutes are unused default drills elsewhere.

## Workload, timing and delivery review

The [summary](workload_summary.md) and individual ledgers distinguish locomotor routes, discrete changes, braking phases, bilateral/unilateral flight landings, throws, strength side bouts, holds and preparation sets. They do not equate low ankle hops with unilateral bounds or cutting contacts. Failed attempts and reductions must enter actual records when reported; no make-up attempts are prescribed.

The main direction task appears early. Most new supporting tasks receive only one attempt per side; complex routes generally receive one complete route per mirrored direction. Later primary work uses one challenging round per lift rather than six large compound workouts. Light work is explicitly easy and limited in volume. RIR and hold-reserve targets, recovery intervals, same-exercise ramps and quality-based stopping are stated in every class.

Class estimates range from **58–90 minutes after the existing preparation**, depending on class and individual setup/recovery allowance. These are individually paced work estimates, not guaranteed group bookings. Access & Prepare 1 duration remains unknown. Actual staffing, class size, equipment quantities and route-clearance times are also unknown; a real group timetable must include those constraints. Necessary recovery cannot be removed to make a shorter booking appear feasible.

**Material space limitation:** The owner-confirmed spec establishes three separate **10 m lanes**, but no combined transverse width. Several angle, curve and course drills require a measured two-dimensional bay; the widest is Class 12's **9 m × 7 m** requirement. These are stated requirements, not asserted available space. No route uses a supposed continuous 30 m runway. Before delivery, verify the complete footprint, escape paths, stopping, coach position and queue/return areas. If unavailable, adapt/relocate or defer the affected work and record the coverage gap; the written prescriptions do not prove the facility can deliver every route unchanged.

All equipment categories come from the supplied inventory. No rack, sled, cable stack, machine or throwing wall is assumed. Supports, usable loads, ball/floor suitability and body-size fit still need checking. Bodyweight and specified same-slot substitutions address equipment-fit problems where feasible; no improvisation of unstable supports is required.
