## Selection, audit findings and revisions

| Question | Final decision and rationale |
|---|---|
| How to consolidate starts without another arbitrary stance? | Use the locally named split-stance auditory start. It retains a known two-point movement but adds a meaningful externally controlled onset. One non-startling verbal cue means straight forward; no feints, multiple choices or directional cuts. This is a task variant, not novel start kinematics. |
| Does the main task get enough quality practice? | Four opportunities, balanced front legs, full 75/120 s recovery and first-pair stopping qualification. Rejected a draft accepted-rep rule that could inflate attempts after guesses/misses. An early launch consumes an opportunity and uses controlled slowing; a missed cue is logged as no start. No forced abrupt stop or penalty repetition. |
| Is the new unilateral reception justified? | A modest isolated same-leg broad receiver links unilateral forward push/acceptance. Earlier unilateral forward jumps ended on both feet or the opposite foot; Class 10’s unilateral receivers were vertical. It is a new demand, not earned progression. Keep four isolated attempts with a shared-receiver replacement. Rejected a linked double broad because repeating the same-leg receiver would increase demand without response data. |
| Why the split scoop toss? | A forward external release loads coordinated leg/hip force while retaining the stagger; this differs from body projection and Class 8's parallel toss. Four throws avoid another jump block. No added catch, step, rotational target or heavier-ball progression. Actual containment determines feasibility. |
| Why isolated A-switches? | Four short flight exchanges allow a coach to observe the support position after each rapid exchange without adding another eight-to-twelve-contact running bout. This is a related resettable force/coordination task, not a new movement because of a lower rep count. Full holds/resets distinguish the task. |
| Why two recurring drill families? | Class 3’s full-cycle drill retains complete free-leg placement with no support exchange; Class 4’s standing-arm contingency retains quick opposing arms with no foot flight. A more complex airborne cycle or another jump/throw was less defensible for this consolidation. Declare both recurrences instead of renaming them. |
| Are E4/E5 redundant? | E4 transfers body support with one rapid thigh exchange; E5 holds the support foot down while the other leg completes recovery, release and placement. If E4 becomes a nonflight step/hold, it still transfers support. E5’s simplified downstroke removes the full cycle but still keeps the other foot planted. |
| Does six-strength architecture overload the finish? | Keep P1–P5 and preparation at Class 10 doses, retaining 2–3 technical RIR. Reduce P6 from two working sets to one: this removes eight dynamic reps and 130 modeled seconds while keeping a challenging flexed-knee ankle contribution. No claim of a completed deload or lower observed fatigue. |
| Are claims and comparisons matched? | Single-cue onset is observed qualitatively. No ordinary gate measurement is treated as reaction time. Same-leg broad distances are separate from bilateral/opposite-leg/vertical variants. Unknown loads, recovery and actual previous technique remain unknown. |
