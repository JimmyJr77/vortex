# Sprinting — drill history and uniqueness review

**User preference added during Class 1 development:** “Try to force unique drills; while strength lifts may recur as needed for progressions.” Apply this across the curriculum's explosive drills. Relevance, room, athlete competence and meaningful dosage still govern selection.

Track written drill identity separately from actual execution. A different label, cue, side, load, set count or arbitrary obstacle arrangement does not establish a new drill. A useful distinction changes the start/task constraint or force-expression demand and earns a specific job. Do not fill the later curriculum in advance just to reserve unique names.

A scheduled alternative becomes actual history only when reported used. Avoid advertising it as a brand-new concept merely because its earlier use is unknown.

**Next-class rule:** Review all previous prescribed and actually used drills; prefer a distinct performance task and complementary drills directly relevant to the day’s start or upright-support emphasis. Reuse a drill only when the alternatives are less defensible for the primary effort, available room or athlete readiness, and state that reason. Strength and light resilience exercises may recur for a traceable reason. Keep any new explosive drill at an appropriate entry demand; uniqueness is not progression evidence.

**Measurement rule:** Different start types/jump variants are separate tests. Preserve their own conditions for any future repeat and compare strength anchors only under matched conditions. Coach observations of a shared quality can guide decisions across drills, but do not report a numerical performance improvement from changing the drill.
