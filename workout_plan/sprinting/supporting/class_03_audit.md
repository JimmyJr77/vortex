## Choice and revision findings

| Concern | Decision and reason |
|---|---|
| Three-point versus another start | One grounded hand adds a release/leg-drive coordination demand that neither standing two-point nor half-kneeling starts shares. Prone get-ups would add more upper-body movement; a falling start would shift the task toward catching forward momentum. Three-point best fits coordinated launch from support today. It does not isolate either leg or simulate blocks exactly. |
| Step-up flight | A launch from the box would add landing height and expose unknown platform dimensions. The chosen rapid nonflight step-up still requires high acceleration of body mass through the working leg, with a controlled return and six purposeful reps. It is not slow stabilization work mislabeled as power. |
| Opposite-leg bound | Changes the receiving task from Class 1's two-foot landing to controlled support on the opposite leg. It remains one short bound at a time, two per takeoff leg, with no rebound progression. Fewer landings do not prove lower joint/tendon stress; the two-foot fallback remains available. |
| Dynamic broad jump | The prior broad jump removed countermovement and arm contribution. Today's continuous dip/arm action has an integration job and is a substantive variant, consistent with the spec's paused-versus-dynamic distinction. It is not a renamed load/repetition change, and its distance cannot be treated as improvement over the paused variant. |
| Upright drill choice | A longer-lever traveling straight-leg bound/run was considered but adds travel/landing demands unnecessary to this day. A planted-leg cycle-and-strike isolates return/placement without a new runway or hopping sequence. It differs from low-dribble alternating running exchanges and wall-supported reciprocal switches. It is a constrained drill, not a complete stride at top speed. |
| Banded knee drive | The setup was revised so the nonmoving leg stays long with its heel on the floor: stretching the loop must oppose the target knee drive. Starting with the opposite thigh raised could instead assist the returning leg and would not serve the intended job. The final task combines a rapid drive and controlled lowering as one cycle, distinct from heavy seated hip flexion. If traction, loop fit or resistance direction cannot be secured, use the unresisted version and label it. |
| Posterior-chain overlap | Step-ups, forward bounds and broad jumps introduce leg-specific extension/receiving demands while actual readiness is unknown. Reduce RDL from **2 × 6 to 1 × 6** and walkout from **4 to 3 cycles**. Keep the remaining working sets challenging through qualified technique/reserve, not heavy fatigue. This is a programming allocation, not an assertion that athletes were fatigued. |
| Retained strength | P1 remains the main comparable strength prescription. P3 still addresses loaded thigh lift, P5/P6 ankle force with different knee positions. Light short-foot and abduction still address the foot base and pelvic direction. No unrelated pushing/pulling lifts are inserted. |
| Exact counts and uniqueness | Six default explosive tasks have real jobs; two have rapid force intent without flight. Eight strengthening exercises remain. Default drill distinctions are documented in history; equipment/readiness fallbacks explicitly reuse prior tasks when appropriate rather than manufacturing novelty. |
| Space and equipment | Default class needs a protected start/jump floor, suitable low box, DBs, fitted seated support and a suitable light closed band. No throwing area, rack, sled or wall is presumed. The current supplied 10 m footprint governs despite the separate 12 m note. Bound distance is modest and individualized, not a measured performance target. |
