## Daily stimulus brief, before exercise selection

1. **Result:** Better-organized projection into the first two start steps, supported by relevant hip, knee and ankle force capacity.
2. **Demand:** Generate forward impulse from stillness while coordinating the free thigh, arms and a connected trunk/pelvis. Introduce upright support mechanics in a small supplemental dose; the start and upright tasks have different body orientations.
3. **Anchor:** E1's six short starts, observed for the push, first contacts and controlled stopping. No top-speed measurement or extra testing block.
4. **Other jobs:** Bilateral static horizontal impulse; unilateral horizontal impulse with simpler landing; isolated ankle recoil; upright step-hop/thigh coordination; externally loaded rapid hip extension. Then easy foot-base and lateral-hip control; finally split-stance extension, flexed-hip hinge strength, hip flexion, knee-flexor support, straight-knee and bent-knee ankle force.
5. **Marker and constraints:** Record E1 quality within scheduled attempts. Use a protected 10 m lane with early termination of driving, ages 12–14 baseline competence, unknown individual readiness and unknown preparation/booking/group size. Exact 6/2/6; gym supplementation beyond starts.

## Consequential choices and audit revisions

| Question | Finding and final decision |
|---|---|
| Which main task most directly answers the request? | Two-point starts give direct opening-step practice. A 10 m sprint would consume the entire known lane and leave unverified run-off; flying sprints and a full acceleration-to-upright transition do not fit. Limit driving to two strikes/3 m of drive from line 1 to line 4, then progressively stop by 9 m; first prescribed attempts establish feasibility at controlled intent. |
| Six explosive rows without six full workouts? | Put six attempts into the main start. Limit paused bilateral and single-leg horizontal jumps to four each; pogo to ten and skip to six hop landings. Keep the swing at ten repetitions. This preserves useful work while avoiding a repeated-bound circuit. |
| Are the horizontal jumps redundant? | E2 removes the countermovement/arms to isolate a still-start bilateral push. E3 requires one leg to supply takeoff impulse with bilateral reception. Different takeoff demands justify separate small doses; neither distance is used as a sprint-time surrogate. |
| Are pogo and skip the same? | E4 isolates bilateral ankle recoil at small amplitude. E5 integrates unilateral step-hop support with opposite thigh and arm recovery in an upright stance. Their contact types are separately counted. Neither approximates an actual maximum-speed stride's full forces or velocity. |
| Why swing instead of a throw or repeated maximal jump? | The externally loaded rapid hinge uses available KBs and adds no planned impacts. A throw would need unconfirmed containment; more repeated jumps would add calf/landing demand. A qualified swing is required; the stated CMJ replacement is one-for-one and changes the exposure record. |
| Why these two light exercises? | Short-foot addresses the foot base with no added loaded calf set. Side-lying abduction addresses lateral hip control without a tiring single-leg stance test before split squats. Doses stay easy and short. |
| Why six strength slots rather than six compound lifts? | A split squat gives the primary split-stance knee/hip extension task; the RDL adds a hip-dominant pattern. A third squat/lunge or hip-thrust slot would overlap heavily. Direct seated hip flexion, a small long-lever knee-flexor task, and two knee-angle ankle tasks fill distinct gaps instead. No upper-body fillers. |
| Posterior-chain overlap? | Starts, both horizontal jumps, swings, RDLs and walkouts all involve the posterior chain. The walkout is one challenging set, and only two hinge working sets are prescribed. Its job is knee-flexor support as the knee opens/returns, not another hip-extension lift or maximal eccentric Nordic dose. |
| Calf overlap? | Starts, pogos and skipping overlap with P5/P6. Three primary calf bouts are enough for this initial prescription; no extra ankle-hop drill or calf endurance finisher. Straight versus bent knees is a purposeful joint-position distinction, not a claim of exclusive muscle isolation. |
| Equipment requirements? | Default class uses confirmed equipment categories and no wall anchor/rack/sled. Bench height, DB/KB loads, grip, landing surface and sweep clearance still need checking. Stable box substitution preserves the seated movement. Unsupported bilateral P5 avoids assuming a hand-support structure; its supported unilateral replacement requires an actually verified support. |
| Missing feedback? | Other local plans and modeled workload scenarios do not demonstrate athlete attendance or tolerance. Start at Class 1; keep loads conditional and observation fields unknown. Later novel drills must not smuggle in an automatic impact/intensity increase. |
| User's drill-uniqueness update? | Updated map, ledger and handoff to prioritize distinct explosive drills across classes. Repeated strength lifts carry matched progression. Track actual exercise identity and job in drill history; a renamed or differently loaded drill is not unique. Any necessary recurrence needs a documented feasibility/relevance reason. |

The [ledger](../progression_ledger.md) records all 14 rows, both sides, preparation sets and replacements. Default totals are **6 starts, 24 jump/hop landing events plus 6 skip transfer contacts, 10 swings, 2 light rounds/4 bouts and 9 primary rounds/12 bouts**. Primary work is **70 dynamic reps plus 4 complete walkout cycles**; load-preparation adds **32 dynamic reps plus 1 cycle**, in **9 bouts**. There are no throws or maximum-velocity runs. Unknown preparation means these are not complete-session totals.

Quality-limited starts may have fewer than two driving contacts before the distance boundary. Failed attempts and stopped work stay in actuals. A final document can prescribe 14 meaningful entries while allowing an athlete to stop when conditions require; omitted actual work is not retroactively treated as a full-dose class.

## Group delivery feasibility

- **Start example only, not confirmed staffing/capacity:** a pod of three takes successive scheduled starts 30 s apart. If each start/ease-down takes about 10 s, that athlete receives about 80 s recovery before its next start, meeting the 75 s minimum. After each athlete's second start, delay that athlete's next start until at least 120 s after its finish. Every returning athlete must be clear before the next one moves. Longer clearances or stops lengthen the schedule.
- The first controlled pair still applies individually. Do not race side by side, launch through a returning athlete, or replace walking recovery with extra drills. Coach must be able to observe each start.
- For strength, the modeled time assumes prompt access to each individual's qualified loads and support. Shared dumbbells/bench, differing loads and side-specific sets can create queues; the fact that one pair rests does not establish another can lift safely in the same area. Use actual cohort/equipment counts before promising a group duration.
- Initial qualified coaching coverage is required for starts, unilateral jumps, swings and loaded lifting. If one coach cannot observe concurrent tasks, serialize them and extend time. Do not assume three lanes equal three staffed stations.
