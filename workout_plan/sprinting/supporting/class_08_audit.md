## Daily stimulus brief

1. **Result:** Release a stable crouched start into consecutive forward pushes, recovering the free leg without losing the support line.
2. **Demand:** Coordinate the change from two-hand support to alternating steps; accept and reapply force when the next contact follows without a long reset.
3. **Anchor:** Four four-point starts, observing hand release, first two steps and stopping within the protected lane. These scheduled attempts supply the quality record.
4. **Supporting jobs:** A small bilateral forward rebound; forward projection into a split receiver; two linked supported leg exchanges; rapid knee-fold recovery; hip-led forward implement release. Keep the eight strengthening roles and doses because they still serve these demands.
5. **Constraints:** Baseline ages 12–14 with established competence and qualified coaching; exact 6/2/6; existing Access & Prepare 1; ten-metre footprint; unknown actual response, wall/ball behavior and group clock. Prefer meaningful drill distinctions without inventing progress.

## Dosage, overlap and progression

- **E1:** Four starts, two/front-leg choice. At most eight driving strikes and 12 m of driving, contained within four × eight metres = **32 m maximum outward travel** from the 1 m front-toe origin to the 9 m stop line. Four stopping episodes; actual slowing/return contacts unknown.
- **E2:** Two bouts × two consecutive jumps = **four bilateral landings**, including two intermediate rebound receivers and two final held receivers.
- **E3:** Four bilateral takeoffs into **four split bilateral landings**, two with each leg forward. Total E2/E3 = **eight bilateral landing events / 16 landing foot impacts**. The split receiver and rebound receiver are different exposures despite the same foot count.
- **E4:** Four double-switch pairs = **eight supported foot contacts**, four/foot, plus **four controlled set-downs**, two/foot. E5 = **eight upright running contacts**, four/foot, plus **one controlled finish set-down**. Keep fast contacts, set-downs and jump landings separate. The audit added these deliberate set-downs explicitly rather than hiding them in incidental exit work.
- **E6:** **Four forward throws**, no prescribed athlete flight, catch or follow-up sprint. Ball ground contacts and walking retrieval are not jump repetitions. No swings or wall pulses in the default path. No actual maximum-velocity running.
- **Light work:** Two rounds / four bouts / 12 dynamic reps plus 30 foot-seconds. **Primary:** Seven rounds / nine bouts / 54 dynamic reps plus three walkout cycles (24 heel steps). **Preparation:** Seven rounds / nine bouts / 32 dynamic reps plus one short-lever cycle (eight heel steps). Loads and Access & Prepare 1 exposure remain unknown.

Class 7 also had four starts and eight bilateral jump receivers, but Class 8 introduces two immediate rebound receivers, eight wall contacts, eight upright contacts and four releases instead of pulses, controlled wall returns and six swings. It is a redistribution with different coordination/impact demands, not equal workload or evidence of improved tolerance. Limit jumps to modest distance, keep linked efforts brief and hold reduced primary volume. First scheduled attempts establish control; remaining attempts can be regressed or stopped.

## Replacement effects

- E1 standing retains the 1 m front-toe origin, four starts and the same drive/stop bounds. One-step versions used throughout cap driving strikes at four; actual path is still observed.
- E2 isolated broad jumps retain up to four bilateral landings but remove rebound receivers; this repeats Class 3's family. If a first landing fails, omitting its second jump reduces actual exposure rather than generating a replacement trial.
- E3 parallel reception retains four bilateral landings but removes the split receiver and repeats an earlier family. E2 and E3 replacements would otherwise become the same isolated broad jump: if both require replacement, use **four same-lead Split Squat Jumps to Stick in E3 instead**, as defined in the class. This is an explicit Class 2 recurrence with vertical projection.
- E4 isolated wall switches keep eight fast supported contacts but give eight controlled set-downs instead of four, because the lean is released after every switch. This repeats Class 2. Without a wall, high-plank isolated switches give eight fast extending-foot contacts, with the forward foot hovering and knees lowered for recovery; setup/knee placements are recorded separately. This repeats the earlier contingency with different support. Four pairs are not added afterward. Allow about one extra minute for repeated floor setup if needed.
- E5 nonflight heel-recovery march keeps eight target-leg cycles but replaces eight airborne running contacts with eight controlled foot placements; no equivalence claim.
- E6 parked swings give four floats instead of four throws, no athlete flight. If those are unsuitable, four rapid bilateral bridges supply nonflight hip drive with different loading. Both fallback families are disclosed in earlier history.
- Supported unilateral P5 yields **10 primary bouts / 56 dynamic reps + 3 cycles**, and **10 preparation bouts / 33 dynamic reps + 1 cycle**, about one additional minute. Other setup/range adjustments are recorded with actual identity and conditions.
