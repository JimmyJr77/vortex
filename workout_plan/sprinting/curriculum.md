The user's request is a category-wide **Sprinting** curriculum covering starts and gym support for maximum-running-velocity mechanics. It maps to `Sprinting > Acceleration (straight-line and multidirectional)` and `Sprinting > Maximum velocity`. ## Exact seven-category framework

### Protracted running
- Endurance
- sustained pace
- pacing control

### Sprinting
- Acceleration (straight-line and multidirectional)
- Maximum velocity

### Jumps
- Vertical
- Horizontal
- Lateral
- Force absorption
- Elastic rebound

### Agility
- Deceleration
- Change of direction/reacceleration
- Reactive agility and anticipation

### Object control & receiving
- Tracking
- Catching/receiving
- Controlling/manipulating objects

### Upper-body force generation
- Pushing
- Pulling
- Straight-line projection (such as chest passes)

### Rotational explosiveness
- Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings)
- Lower-body emphasis (rotational kicks)

Strength, stability, explosiveness and resilience are supporting qualities, not additional categories. Jumps used here support Sprinting and do not change the curriculum target. Body control/tumbling stays separate. Each class references the existing **Access & Prepare 1** and then uses exactly **6 Explosiveness / 2 Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) / 6 Strength (Primary)** entries in that order.

## Drill uniqueness and strength continuity

Prioritize a new, purposefully distinct set of explosive drills for each class. Check the [drill history](supporting/drill_history.md) before selecting them. A change in load, reps, cue, name or side alone does not create a new drill. A different start condition, support demand or force-expression task must have its own relevant job and be feasible in the same gym. Do not promise 72 unique drills before reviewing feasibility and athlete response; the user said to try, and relevance remains a requirement. If recurrence is necessary, document why the proposed alternatives are less suitable rather than quietly repeating or adding filler.

Strength exercises, including light resilience work, may recur when they support progression. Keep previous prescription → current dose → reason → next criterion traceable. For new explosive drills, keep mechanical quality observations and workloads traceable but establish a new measurement baseline: changing a drill does not establish performance improvement. No actual response still means unknown response, even when a new drill is selected.

## Evidence, style and limits

The design takes inspiration from Overtime Athletes' combination of start practice, directional plyometrics and upright mechanics, keeping each exercise tied to a named task. Its published examples include two-point starts, single-leg broad jumps and A-skips. Local distances, doses, replacements and six-strength-exercise architecture are authored for this request, not copied from an OTA program. [OTA speed-drill guidance](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/soccer-speed-drills/), [OTA two-point-start example](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/strength-training-for-basketball/).

Ground-force application and hip/ankle function provide mechanical reasons for these support jobs. They do not demonstrate that this exact collection of exercises improves sprint times in these athletes. Upright skipping is a constrained coordination drill, not top-speed running; force capacity and technique learned in the gym still require actual running practice for sport-specific integration when appropriate space is available. [Weyand et al., 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11053354/), [Dorn, Schache and Pandy, 2012](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/11/1944/10883/Muscular-strategy-shift-in-human-running).

Youth resistance guidance supports supervised, individually appropriate technique, resistance and recovery. It does not validate this exact 14-entry session or establish readiness from age alone. The user's 2–3 technical RIR target and existing competence assumption guide working loads. [NSCA youth resistance training position statement](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf).
