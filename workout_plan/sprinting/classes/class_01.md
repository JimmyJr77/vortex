# Sprinting — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Project out of a two-point start through the first two steps with a connected trunk and pelvis. Build the hip and ankle capacities that support those steps, with a small introduction to upright contact and thigh-recovery mechanics.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified coaching. Individual readiness, recent training, restrictions and actual exercise competence remain unknown. This is a finalized, conditional prescription; no athlete completion is recorded.

**Scope:** `Sprinting > Acceleration (straight-line and multidirectional)` and `Sprinting > Maximum velocity`. Today emphasizes straight-line starts. Everything beyond the very short start is supplemental gym work. There is **no actual maximum-velocity running** in this class and no claim that gym drills replace it.

**Quality marker:** During E1's six scheduled attempts, record whether the athlete pushes into the first two steps without a backward rock, waist fold, conspicuous reaching foot or immediate upward jump, and then stops inside the boundary. Record each criterion separately and valid attempts out of attempts actually made; never add attempts to improve a score. This is a coaching observation, not a sprint-time or force test.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 gets the greatest practice and recovery investment. Complete exercises in order. Jumps use bodyweight; speed and clean execution take priority over height, distance or fatigue.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Two-point start — two-step projection** | **3 × 2 starts**; one with each foot forward per pair. Front toes at **1 m**; fit the entire starting body inside **0–2 m**. At most **2 driving strikes or 3 m of drive**, ending by **4 m**; progressively slow and stop by **9 m**. **75 s between starts; 120 s between pairs.** | **Primary start anchor:** coordinate the first push, reciprocal arm action and the first two ground contacts. Set a comfortable stagger; hold still briefly, then self-start. Push the floor behind you without deliberately lengthening the stride. First pair uses controlled fast intent to establish the route; remaining pairs use the fastest clean intent that preserves stopping room. |
| 2 | **Paused Broad Jump to Stick** | **2 × 2**. Hold a comfortable shallow squat **2 s**, hands on hips; jump forward without re-dipping. **20 s between reps; 90 s between sets.** | Express horizontal impulse from stillness without a running approach or arm contribution. Use an energetic push to a coach-selected distance that permits a two-foot, two-second landing hold. Shorten distance if the athlete reaches with the feet or cannot hold. |
| 3 | **Single-leg Broad Jump to Two-foot Landing** | **1 × 2 per takeoff leg**. Natural arms, modest dip, controlled distance. **20 s between same-leg reps; 45 s before changing legs.** | Load each leg's forward push separately while keeping the receiving task bilateral. Push from one foot, then land on both; hold **2 s**. Reduce distance for twisting, uncontrolled takeoff or a staggered landing; do not add reps to a visually weaker side. |
| 4 | **Ankle Pogo in Place** | **1 × 10 rebounds**, low amplitude, bodyweight. | Isolate quick ankle recoil in a tall bilateral stance before integrating alternating contacts. Use small knee motion, brief contacts and quiet rhythm; do not chase jump height. Stop the set when contacts become heavy, the knees deepen or rhythm breaks. |
| 5 | **Stationary A-Skip** | **1 × 3 left–right pairs** = 6 step-hop actions: **3 per side**. Low amplitude, continuous rhythm, approximately 8–12 s; reps govern. | Link upright support with reciprocal thigh recovery and opposite-arm timing. Step onto the left foot and hop on it as the right thigh rises; step onto the right and hop as the left rises. Stay in place, ribs over pelvis, contact close beneath the body. Reduce thigh height/amplitude for leaning or reaching; this must retain a crisp hop, not become a slow march. |
| 6 | **Kettlebell Swing — two hands, chest-height ceiling** | **2 × 5**, light enough for a crisp hip-driven float. **90 s between sets.** Five full outward swings, then park under control. | Express rapid hip extension against an external load without further landing impacts. Hinge, hike, snap the hips and let the bell float; keep feet planted and finish tall without leaning back or lifting with the arms. Lower load for a slowing float or lost hinge. |

**Recovery between exercises:** After the last effort of E1, E2 and E3, allow **90 s** before the next exercise; after E4, E5 and E6 allow **60 s**. Instruction/setup may occupy that recovery; it is not an extra clock to count twice. Extend rest if breathing, concentration or force expression has not recovered. No conditioning rotations.

**Start layout and limits:** Use **one protected 10 m lane**: front toes at 1 m, whole starting body inside 0–2 m; stop driving after two contacts or at line 4 m (3 m of drive), whichever comes first; progressively ease down to a stop no later than 9 m; leave 9–10 m clear as reserve. These are planning boundaries, not proof that every athlete can stop there. Never sprint the full lane, aim for a hard stop at line 4 m, or concatenate the three lanes. One athlete moves at a time; the lane remains closed while that athlete walks back. Waiting athletes and equipment need a separate verified space. Coach from the side outside the path. If the first controlled attempts reveal insufficient room, **use a one-step start instead of the two-step start** for remaining scheduled attempts, lowering intent as needed; record the change. If that still cannot be controlled, stop E1 and record incomplete work.

**Other space and replacement details:** E2/E3 each use a clear part of the same lane with the intended landing inside its first 3 m and further empty space beyond; distance is individualized and never a target to chase. E4/E5 need a clear impact-appropriate floor; E6 needs unobstructed bell sweep, grip and overhead clearance. If the athlete lacks a qualified swing or no suitable bell fits, **use hands-on-hips Countermovement Jump to Stick instead of E6**, **2 × 3**, 20 s between jumps and 90 s between sets. Use a shallow continuous dip, energetic vertical takeoff and a two-second two-foot finish. This changes the job to unloaded dynamic vertical extension and adds six bilateral landing events; log it as a different exercise, not ten swings.

**Quality rules:** A faulty attempt still counts as exposure. Cue or reduce demand before the next scheduled effort; repeated loss of the same criterion despite adjustment ends that exercise. Pain, altered gait, a slip or loss of control ends the affected task immediately. Do not fill stopped work with unlisted exercises or replacement attempts.

**Final sequence correction:** All E1 defaults and standing/one-step replacements use the 1 m front-toe origin and entire starting footprint inside 0–2 m. Drive at most two strikes/3 m to line 4, progressively stop by line 9 and keep the final metre clear. Maximum outward travel is 8 m/start; this is a boundary from the toe line, not an exact body trajectory. The revised layout requires fresh stopping qualification; prior 0 m-layout scores are not matched comparisons. Counts/rests hold; additional setup or recovery extends the allowance.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep these easy: approximately **RPE 3–4/10**, well short of fatigue. Bodyweight is sufficient. These are controlled strengthening doses, not mobility or balance tests.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill** | **1 × 3 holds × 5 s per foot**; both feet remain supported. Release **5 s between holds; 20 s between feet.** | Practice maintaining the heel, big-toe base and little-toe base while gently raising the working arch. This provides a controllable foot base for the start push and ankle loading without another calf set. Keep toes long; reduce effort or **use seated foot doming instead of standing** if toes claw or the foot rolls. |
| 2 | **Side-Lying Hip Abduction** | **1 × 6 per side**; bodyweight, **2 s up / 1 s hold / 2 s down**. **20 s between sides.** | Give the lateral hip a small, isolated strengthening dose for pelvic control over the stance leg. Bottom knee bent, top leg straight and in line with the torso; lift only while the pelvis stays stacked and the toes face forward. Reduce range if the pelvis rolls or the thigh drifts forward. |

Allow **45 s between S1 and S2**, and **60 s after S2** before primary-strength preparation sets. Neither exercise should create a burn that changes the first lifting set. Stop for pain or persistent compensation.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Choose working loads or leverage that leave approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve (RIR)** at the prescribed endpoint. A clean but very easy set is a load-selection observation, not a reason to add repetitions. Do not use maximal tests, forced reps or grinding. Lower under control and lift deliberately; the fast-intent strength lifts need not move at ballistic speed.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **2 × 5 per leg**, 2–3 RIR. Lower **2 s**, rise strongly. **60 s between legs; 120 s between rounds.** | **Main strength anchor:** strengthen lead-leg knee and hip extension in a stable split base for the initial sprint push. Both feet stay on the floor; use a modest whole-trunk forward inclination without rounding. Reduce load/depth for rear-leg hopping, knee collapse or pelvic rotation. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **2 × 6**, 2–3 RIR. **2 s down**, controlled strong ascent; **120 s between sets.** | Load hip extension with the hips flexed and hamstrings lengthened, complementing the knee-dominant component of P1 and the rapid hinge in E6. Soft knees, bells close, stop depth before the pelvis or spine compensates. Reduce range/load if control is lost. |
| 3 | **Seated Dumbbell Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 per leg**, 2–3 RIR. **1 s lift / 1 s hold / 2 s lower**; **45 s between legs.** | Directly strengthen thigh lifting while the trunk stays still, supporting swing-leg recovery rather than adding another squat. Sit near the front of a stable bench, starting with the foot supported and knee near a right angle. Secure a light DB across the distal thigh above the knee with both hands; lift the knee a few centimetres without hand assistance or leaning back. Return the foot gently. Reduce load/range for tipping, pinching or a shifting implement. |
| 4 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 complete cycles**, choose heel-step length for **2–3 clean cycles in reserve**. Each cycle = **4 alternating small heel steps out + 4 back**, about **8–10 s**. | Strengthen knee-flexor support as the knee opens while the pelvis stays raised. This adds a knee-extension/return demand to P2's hip hinge; it does not reproduce sprint swing speed. Start in a bilateral bridge, walk out only to a controlled soft-knee position, return to the original heel positions. Shorten steps for sagging or cramp; stop if control cannot be restored. |
| 5 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — bilateral, floor range** | **1 × 10**, 2–3 RIR. **1 s rise / 1 s top / 2 s lower**, heels return to floor. | Strengthen plantarflexion with the knees nearly straight for ankle force capacity, complementing the low-load recoil work. Hold DBs at sides; rise through both forefeet without rolling outward, bouncing or locking knees. Reduce load for lost heel height. If balance limits force, **use a single-leg calf raise with one DB and light contact on a verified stable support instead of this version**, **1 × 6 per leg**, same tempo, **30 s between legs**; log the changed laterality. |
| 6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, bilateral DB loading** | **2 × 8**, 2–3 RIR. **1 s rise / 1 s top / 2 s lower / 1 s bottom**; **90 s between sets.** | Load plantarflexion with flexed knees, giving the knee-flexed ankle-support task its own small strength dose. Keep knees near a right angle, weights secured on thighs above the knees, forefeet planted and heel travel repeatable. Reduce load if knees straighten, feet roll or the athlete bounces. |

**Load-preparation work — not working sets:** In the already listed movements, use P1 **1 × 4/leg** bodyweight or light (30 s between sides; 60 s before working); P2 **1 × 5 easy then 1 × 3 moderate** (60 s between; 90 s before working); P3 **1 × 3/leg light** (30 s between; 45 s before working); P4 **1 short-lever cycle** (45 s before working); P5 **1 × 5 easy** (45 s before working); P6 **1 × 5 easy** (60 s before working). These do not replace Access & Prepare 1. Add a small preparation set of the same movement only if needed to establish an appropriate load; record its work and added time. P5's single-leg replacement uses **1 × 3/leg easy** preparation, 20 s between sides and 45 s before working.

**Between primary exercises:** Allow **120 s after P1 and P2**, and **90 s after P3, P4 and P5**, before the next exercise's preparation set; equipment changes and instruction can occupy that time. No additional mandatory rest is assigned after the last set of P6. Keep all six productive by preserving these rests and reducing an inappropriate load rather than rushing. If bench height is unsuitable for P3/P6, **use a suitably sized stable box instead of the bench** for the same seated exercise; equipment fit and load range must be checked.

**Timing and delivery:** Budget approximately **75–85 minutes after Access & Prepare 1** for an athlete with prompt equipment access: roughly 30 min explosiveness, 6 min stabilization, 39 min primary work, including the listed rests, preparation sets, instruction and transitions; the upper end allows additional routine adjustment. Extra coaching, added ramps and group queues can extend this. The full session duration is unresolved because preparation duration and the actual booking/group/staff are unknown. A 60-minute total booking does not fit this prescription; a 90-minute total booking is not yet confirmed to fit. See the [timing audit](../supporting/class_01_audit.md).

**Recovery and scheduling:** Treat this as a demanding lower-body exposure despite the short running distance. Review actual sprinting, jumps, sport, gym and separately programmed tumbling before scheduling; other written Vortex classes are not evidence of completed work. A nonconsecutive demanding lower-body exposure, often around 48–72 hours later, is a planning starting point only. Adjust to actual fatigue, symptoms, soreness, movement quality and upcoming sport; elapsed time alone does not establish readiness. Do not add this session on top of another full lower-body plan without reconciling their actual doses.

**Progression note:** This establishes the first Sprinting prescription, not an earned increase from prior training. Per the updated user preference, prioritize distinct explosive drills across classes; retain useful strength lifts such as P1 for traceable progression. Class 2 should address the next opening-push demand through a meaningfully different drill, selected after reviewing E1 observations and the drill history. A new drill is not an earned intensity increase: with missing feedback, use a conditional entry dose and do not raise the total workload by default. Compare numerical results only within the same exercise and protocol. Any explosive-drill recurrence needs a specific relevance or feasibility justification, not convenience; do not invent cosmetic variants just to claim uniqueness.
