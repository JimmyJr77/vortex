# Sources, assumptions and exercise identities

Reviewed 2026-09-13. Sources support style and general programming principles. None validates the exact 14-entry session, its 40 releases, proposed adaptations, recovery gates or individual suitability. Exercise jobs describe intended mechanical contributions, not demonstrated sport outcomes.

## External sources checked

1. [Overtime Athletes — 3 Best Exercises to Build Rotational Power for Athletes](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/3-best-exercises-to-build-rotational-power-for-athletes/). Public style source describing stationary/dynamic side tosses, unilateral shot-put work and resisted rotational chops. Used for purposeful projection and resisted turning; the present doses, floor-target adaptations and sequence are original coaching decisions. Its broad transfer, injury and vertical-jump claims are not adopted as established outcomes.
2. [Faigenbaum et al. — Youth Resistance Training: Updated Position Statement Paper from the NSCA (2009)](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf). Official specialist guidance for qualified supervision, technique-led progression, short quality power sets, individualized resistance and recovery. Table 1 and pages S71–S72 inform those principles. The specification supplies the 2–3 technical RIR target; neither that exact target nor this whole 6/2/6 prescription is claimed to be validated by the paper. Its general nonconsecutive-session guidance informs the contextual recovery note, not automatic permission to train after a timer expires.

No precise threshold for improved rotational force, injury reduction, guaranteed bat speed or medicine-ball-to-sport transfer is claimed. No commercial paid program is reproduced.
