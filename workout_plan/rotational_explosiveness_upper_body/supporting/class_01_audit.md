# Class 1 audit — upper-body rotational explosiveness

**Status:** Finalized prescription, conditional on athlete readiness and verified delivery setup. No athlete execution is established. Review date: 2026-09-13.

## Relevance and choice review

The daily task is rotational projection into the hands with a controlled finish. The upper-body emphasis retains foot/hip participation; isolating trunk twisting was rejected as a poor interpretation of the requested effort.

| Consequential choice | Selected decision and rejected alternative |
|---|---|
| Primary anchor | Settled standing scoop toss: repeatable start, meaningful ground-to-hand contribution. A step-behind anchor adds entry variability before any actual reference exists. The moving variant remains four supporting releases. |
| Six explosive jobs | Settled initiation; reduced leg drive; immediate preload reversal; unilateral push release; step-to-turn transfer; downward diagonal finish. These are execution constraints and force paths, not six loads or renamed duplicates of one throw. |
| Compound/slam combination | Excluded Med Ball Slam to Rotational Throw: it combines two releases/actions in one slot. A standalone side slam earns a distinct downward finish job. |
| Rebound catching | Excluded from Class 1: it adds receiving/braking load and depends on a suitable wall/rebound setup. Its omission preserves low-volume projection quality without creating another training category. |
| Main strength | Landmine rotation and rotational press receive two sets because they load the turning arc and turn-to-arm force expression directly. A general squat/deadlift/pull-up block would spend the six slots on less direct contributors to today's upper-body target. |
| Pulling and diagonal strength | The rotational row trains the pulling contribution and controlled return; chop and lift oppose different diagonal resistance paths. These share tissue demand, so each receives only one working set per side. Bands replace unconfirmed cable equipment, with proposed identities labeled where necessary. |
| Sixth primary slot | Unilateral floor press supplies controlled horizontal release-side pressing after the turn. It overlaps P2's pressing muscles, but removes the leg turn and angled landmine path to load terminal horizontal force. One set per side limits its marginal cost. A heavy side plank would duplicate the postural emphasis already addressed by S1 more than it would add release force. |
| Light work | Pallof control and side-lying external rotation provide distinct trunk/shoulder loading jobs at light effort. Generic balance and mobility filler were rejected. |

## Dosage revisions before finalization

The equal-volume explosive option, 2 × 3 per side for all six entries, would prescribe 72 releases and dilute the anchor's priority. The finalized allocation is **40**: E1 12, E2/E3/E4/E6 6 each, E5 4. This is a deliberate reduction, not a finding that 40 is a universally safe cap. E1 still precedes all supporting work.

The main strength anchors receive two sets; remaining contributors receive one challenging set per side. This avoids six equally large strength workouts after the throws. P6 is limited specifically because it adds to P2's pressing demand. S1/S2 remain one light set per side and must not become pre-fatigue tests.

The library's default throwing-wall assumption was replaced with an explicitly labeled floor-target adaptation using the listed ball inventory and 10 m planning constraint. No wall, catch, flight distance, rebound containment or floor suitability is asserted as confirmed. The coach must establish containment; otherwise the ball block requires an approved area and a revised delivery audit before execution.

## Workload and recovery findings

- 40 explosive releases: **34 throws + 6 slams**, 20 per side/direction. The 14 side bouts include varied upper-body, reversal, entry and overhead demands; these are not equivalent mechanical loads.
- Light work: **26 reps, 20 seconds of included holds, 4 side bouts**; at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve.
- Primary work: **88 reps, 14 physical bouts**; approximately 2–3 technical repetitions in reserve. The 36 minimum preparation reps/11 bouts are separate, easy work and included in time. Additional preparation adds documented workload/time.
- P2/P6 add **36 challenging pressing reps**, alongside six shot-put releases and the other throws. Shoulder/trunk readiness must therefore be reviewed jointly, including actual preparation and sport/tumbling, not separately by section.
- Zero prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or rebound catches outside the unknown preparation. This does not mean the whole day's actual impact or sport load is zero.
- Recovery is part of the dose: 10–15 seconds/reset between releases and 90 seconds between explosive side bouts; primary rest is 90 seconds between sides/exercises, with 2–3 minutes for P1 sets and 2 minutes between P2 rounds. No circuit or finisher.

Each additional athlete needs their own actual throw and lift work. A queue is not automatically free: calculate elapsed time to the same athlete's next bout and use that as rest, rather than adding full nominal rest again. One active throwing lane does not support an unlimited group in this estimate. ## Drill-uniqueness review after user update

Reviewed the finalized Class 1 drills for duplicate tasks. E1 and E3 are the closest pair: a settled-start throw versus immediate reversal of a wind-back. They remain because force initiation and reversal are distinct jobs under the existing specification, with different doses. E2 reduces leg propulsion; E4 uses a unilateral pushing release; E5 adds grounded entry transfer; E6 changes to a downward diagonal finish. These are related rotational-ball tasks, not six unrelated movement families. S1 and S2 load different trunk/shoulder control demands.

No exercise/dose was changed in this preference update. The substantive change removes automatic future recurrence of E1/E3/E5 from the map, ledger, class handoff and machine-readable resume state. A usage register now makes all eight drill selections visible to the next run, including close families and conditional replacements. New names, loads, rep counts or target surfaces cannot be counted as new drills. Primary strength recurrence remains permitted for a documented progression purpose; light-strength recurrence also needs a stated purpose while retaining its light role.

At the time of this preference update, Class 2 was unwritten. Subsequent audits must explicitly compare every proposed drill with the register, state the relevant task difference and identify any necessary recurrence. Selection should preserve direct rotational relevance and appropriate demands; no unobserved performance or tolerance is inferred.
