## Continuity and progression review

Classes 1–3 move from settled projection to the hand-force link and immediate preload reversal. Classes 4–5 connect deliberate entry to release. Class 6 lowers volume and uses familiar tasks openly to consolidate control. Class 7 introduces controlled external supply at low strength volume; airborne feeds are conditional replacements, not the baseline. Classes 8–10 refine diagonal, entry/hand-path and unilateral contributions. Class 11 consolidates supplied-ball organization without incoming flight. Class 12 integrates one new task and deliberately revisits selected references.

Primary working reps are **88, 94, 76, 82, 76, 70, 70, 82, 76, 82, 70, 88**. The changing allocation follows the day's force task rather than a monotonic “harder each class” pattern. Sets and loads are still conditional on actual readiness. The increase to 94 in Class 2 shifts emphasis to pressing as ball releases fall; those different stresses are not asserted to cancel numerically. The lower-volume Classes 6/7/11 provide planned consolidation opportunities without claiming actual fatigue or recovery.

The finalized sequence prescribes **252 ball releases** and **162 other fast actions** across all twelve documents, not across a fixed week. Baseline external supply is **40 handoffs and zero airborne catches**. Class 7 alone offers up to 20 gentle airborne receptions instead of its 20 handoffs, only after demonstrated readiness. No optional receptions are added on top. These totals describe the documents; they are not cumulative actual athlete exposure or a safe-dose limit.

## Uniqueness judgment and limits

Task introductions are most concentrated where a new performance constraint is useful. Related families remain visible: scoops, pressing releases, backhand arcs, diagonal slams, band impulses and rope impulses. A pause versus immediate reversal, released versus retained load, reduced leg propulsion, entry arrest, unilateral contribution or external supply can be meaningful, but changing a name, side, weight or target alone is not credited.

Class 6 intentionally repeats four fast tasks to consolidate at a smaller dose. Class 12 intentionally repeats five fast tasks for reference observations and low-load support. Other recurrences limit unfamiliar loading or provide a contrasting force-path reference. Their rationale is recorded in the class audits instead of claiming every row is unprecedented. This is the defensible interpretation of “try” to force uniqueness while preserving direct relevance and manageable demands.

Strength recurrence is permitted rather than mandatory: set allocation changes with the day's priority, and supported rowing replaces rotating rowing when needed to prevent same-session duplication. The curriculum does not promise actual strength progression without reported loads and reserve.

## Final reference conditions

Class 12 E1 repeats the original paused scoop prescription and observation conditions. Selected other releases and the two original loaded anchors are also revisited. The session as a whole differs from Class 1, so it is not an identical whole-session test. Grip/ball, stance, target, pause, side order, rest, load, range, prior fatigue and observation method must be recorded before making any actual comparison. Missing earlier results mean a first observed reference, not demonstrated improvement. No radar, force-plate feature or athlete test result is invented.
