# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 3 of 12

**Primary effort:** Reverse a compact preload into rotational hand speed without a second wind-up.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 is the countermovement rotational throw, now the main reversal task rather than a small support. The limitation is delay or excessive wind-back before force reaches the hands. Supporting jobs are pushing reversal, pulling acceleration, diagonal impulse, an outward backhand release and an upward short-arm finish. Strength emphasizes turning and an anchored pulling platform; light control protects rib position and shoulder support.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 note whether one small wind-back flows directly into the release, without a pause, second rock, added step or exaggerated lumbar turn. Count balanced two-second finishes on each side within scheduled attempts. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 2, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 2 prescribed 12 ball releases plus 30 other fast actions and 94 strength reps. No execution was reported. This class changes the distribution to 24 releases and 18 other fast actions; it does not claim those stresses are interchangeable. E1 is an explicitly justified repeat from Class 1, not relabeled as new. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** The reversal anchor receives 2 × 3 per side; other tasks are 1 × 3 per side. Maintain a small preload; do not chase a larger range. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Med Ball Countermovement Rotational Throw — floor target** | **2 × 3 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Primary: turn one compact preload immediately into release.** Stand side-on with a light ball near the rear hip; make one small wind-back then reverse into a two-hand scoop release. Feet may pivot; no entry step or repeated rocking. Hold the finish for 2 seconds. Reduce wind-back range if the athlete pauses, overtwists or loses direction. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Countermovement rotational shot-put throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Apply prompt reversal to a unilateral pressing release.** Ball supported beside the rear shoulder; allow one small backward body turn, then pivot forward and push toward the low target with the rear hand. Keep the elbow below shoulder height and guide hand relaxed. Reduce the preload or ball mass if the wrist collapses or the arm winds behind the torso. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Rotational band row speed drive** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Accelerate the pulling side of the turn without jerking the shoulder.** Light band anchored ahead at lower-chest height. From a controlled reach, pivot/turn and draw the elbow toward lower ribs quickly; stop cleanly and return in 3 seconds. One pull-return is one rep. Reduce tension/reach if the shoulder hikes or the trunk yanks backward. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Two-hand diagonal battle-rope chop pulse** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Reverse an elevated preload into a brief downward rotational impulse.** With a light manageable rope pair, start hands near one shoulder; pivot and drive both ends diagonally toward the opposite hip. Settle the rope completely and reset deliberately before another pulse. Use the stated diagonal band-pull replacement if rope weight or clearance prevents clean speed. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Two-hand medicine-ball backhand throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Express outward transverse force without relying on elbow extension.** Begin hands across the front of the body near the opposite lower ribs, elbows softly bent. Turn toward the cleared lane and sweep both hands outward at waist-to-chest height into the low target. Reset without receiving a rebound. Shorten the sweep if the ball drags the shoulder behind the torso. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Rotational shadow uppercut** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Link a compact turn to an upward bent-arm hand path.** No target or implement. With elbow bent, move the fist from lower-rib height toward chin height as the rear foot/hip turns; stop before the shoulder shrugs. No deep squat or overhead punch. Shorten the upward path if the low back extends or the arm separates from the turn. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Bear Position Shoulder Tap** | **1 × 4 per side/direction**; 8 reps/actions | **Maintain trunk position while support briefly shifts to one arm.** Hands below shoulders, knees just clear of the floor. Slowly lift one hand to touch the opposite shoulder, replace it, then alternate; pelvis stays level. Take about 3 seconds each rep. Use knees on the floor instead if the pelvis sways; keep the same slow tap dose.  |
| S2 | **Band-Resisted Dead Bug Pullover** | **1 × 6 total**; 6 reps/actions | **Keep ribs controlled while the arms lengthen into the next preload.** Lie supine with knees bent and feet supported; light band anchored securely beyond the head. Move both hands from above the face toward the ribs, then return slowly without arching. This is one arm-pullover task; do not add alternating leg extensions. Reduce overhead reach/tension if the ribs lift.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Landmine Rotation** | **2 × 6 alternating total**; 12 reps/actions | **Strengthen the turning arc that must reverse under control.** Use both hands and softly bent elbows. Turn feet/hips with a modest side-to-side arc; keep ribs over pelvis. One one-way traverse is one rep and the reverse is the next rep; six alternating traverses contain three per direction. Take about 2 seconds per traverse and settle briefly at the endpoint. Reduce arc/load if the knee twists, trunk leans or endpoint runs away.  |
| P2 | **One-Arm Dumbbell Row — supported** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Load scapular pulling force while the trunk supplies a stable platform.** Support the free hand on a stable bench, feet staggered. Row the dumbbell toward the same-side hip without turning the torso; lower in 3 seconds. Use less load if the shoulder hikes or the trunk rotates.  |
| P3 | **Landmine Rotational Press** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Preserve force through the unilateral release-side press.** Rear hand holds the sleeve at the rear shoulder in a modest staggered stance. Pivot the rear foot and turn into a forward/up press without a dip-drive or release. Return in 3 seconds, wrist stacked and ribs level. Use less load for rib flare or elbow/wrist collapse; if minimum bar load is unsuitable, use a rotational band press instead at this dose.  |
| P4 | **Cable / Band Chop — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Load the downward diagonal and its controlled return.** Secure a high band anchor to the side; opposite foot forward. Both hands travel from above that shoulder toward the opposite front pocket, with a small foot/hip turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3. Reduce range/tension if sideways crunching or band recoil replaces control.  |
| P5 | **Cable / Band Lift — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Load the opposite diagonal that establishes the preload.** Secure a low band anchor to the rear side. Move both hands from the rear hip across to opposite upper-chest height, allowing feet/hips to turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3; stop below rib flare. Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |
| P6 | **Single-arm Dumbbell Floor Press** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Maintain terminal pressing strength with limited added volume.** Lie supine with feet comfortably planted and one dumbbell over the chest. Lower in 3 seconds until the upper arm gently meets the floor, briefly pause and press. Keep ribs/pelvis level; no bridge or roll. Use less load if the elbow lands hard or the trunk rolls.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** Rope diagonal pulses require a compatible anchored rope and a clear diagonal sweep; use a light two-hand diagonal band speed pull instead if the rope setup is unsuitable, at the same dose and recorded as a replacement. Speed rows use an approved front anchor and controllable light band. Strict dumbbell rows need a stable bench/support; benches are in the confirmed inventory but dimensions/quantity are unconfirmed.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

**Timing and group flow:** About **75–85 minutes for this prescribed work**, excluding existing Access & Prepare 1 and additional group queues. Model: explosive 30 min including resets/bout rest and ~7 min coaching/setup; light strength 4 min; primary strength 39 min including listed preparation, work, recovery and ~7 min loading/instruction; ~4 min section changes/logging. This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. **Scheduling:** Review actual throwing/batting/racquet/swinging/contact-sport work, upper-body lifting and separately programmed tumbling before this exposure. ### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 24 ball releases (24 throws + 0 slams), separately **18 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 14 reps/holds, including 0 seconds held, in 3 bouts. Primary: **76 working reps in 12 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** The countermovement throw from Class 1 changes from one to two sets per side because reversal is today’s primary instructional task; this is a conditional allocation, not an earned escalation. Landmine rotation returns from Class 2 one set to two; rotational press/floor press each fall from two to one. Replace the rotational band strength row with a strict supported dumbbell row so the fast rotational row is not duplicated in primary strength. Retain one set each of loaded diagonals. The resulting 76 strength reps are below Class 2’s 94. Advance reversal speed or load only with actual immediate, controlled reversals and normal recovery; otherwise keep the small range and lower resistance. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
