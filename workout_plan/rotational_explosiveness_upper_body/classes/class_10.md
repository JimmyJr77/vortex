# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 10 of 12

**Primary effort:** Express fast rotational hand force from a stable base with a short, controlled arm path.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 is a one-arm rotational hook throw: the bent-arm arc must stay coupled to the body turn rather than becoming a long shoulder swing. Unilateral transverse/downward rope impulses expose retained-load control; a settled scoop and two-hand push retain useful release references. An unloaded backhand rehearsal finishes the explosive block without more object load.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record elbow angle staying approximately constant through the compact arc, the hand remaining in front of the trunk, a directed release and a balanced finish. Record actual ball/grip suitability and any substitution; do not infer speed from the size of the wind-up. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 9, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 9 prescribed twenty ball releases, eight other fast actions and 76 primary reps. This class keeps twenty releases, removes entries and adds four small retained/unloaded actions. A stable base does not establish readiness for a one-hand grip; the coach must verify the implement match. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** E1 uses 2 × 3 per side; all five supports use 1 × 2. No overspeed claim, heavier-ball prescription or high-speed entry. A fast action remains controlled within the athlete’s comfortable range. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Single-arm rotational medicine-ball hook throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **2 × 3 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Primary: couple a bent-arm transverse release to the trunk turn.** Hold a securely grippable light ball at the rear side of the chest, elbow bent near 90 degrees. Pivot and turn while sweeping the bent forearm through a short forward/transverse arc into the low target. Avoid a long backswing or forceful elbow lock. Use the two-hand backhand throw instead if one-hand grip or shoulder position is insecure. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Single-arm transverse battle-rope pulse** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Produce and brake a unilateral horizontal rotational impulse.** Hold one manageable rope end beside the working hip; pivot and sweep the hand across a short waist-to-chest-height arc, then settle the rope. Keep the unused end away from feet. Use an unloaded one-arm transverse sweep instead if rope mass or clearance is unsuitable. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Med Ball Rotational Scoop Toss — paused floor-target start** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Retain a settled-start whole-chain release reference.** Stand side-on with both hands holding the ball near the rear hip for 2 seconds. Pivot, turn and sweep toward the low target; no entry or countermovement. Hold the finish for 2 seconds. Reduce ball mass/turn range if arms lead or balance is lost. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Single-arm diagonal battle-rope downstroke** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Control a unilateral downward rotational impulse.** With one manageable rope end near upper-chest height, pivot and drive the working hand toward the opposite front pocket. Keep a soft elbow, long trunk and short path; settle completely before resetting. Use an unloaded diagonal hand drive instead if rope load prevents clean control. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Standing rotational two-hand chest push throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Preserve a simultaneous pushing-release reference after the one-arm tasks.** Ball near the rear side of the chest in both hands. Turn the feet/hips and extend both arms through the low target; do not scoop from the hip. Use less ball mass if one arm dominates or the athlete side-bends. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Unloaded two-hand rotational backhand sweep** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Rehearse connected outward hand travel without extra ball or rope load.** Palms close together across the lower ribs, elbows soft. Pivot and sweep both hands outward in front of the body, stop within the comfortable arc, then reset slowly. Shorten the arc if shoulders trail behind the torso. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Light-band straight-arm shoulder extension** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Control the shoulder as the arm returns toward the hip after release.** Band anchored ahead near shoulder height. With elbow softly straight, pull the hand down/back to beside the hip without extending the spine; return in 3 seconds. Keep the motion small and shoulder low. Use less tension/range if the elbow bends or back arches.  |
| S2 | **Side Plank Reach-Through Control — short lever** | **1 × 4 per side/direction**; 8 reps/actions | **Maintain shoulder support while allowing a small controlled trunk turn.** From forearm and bent-knee support, hips comfortably raised, move the top hand a short distance under the chest with a small thoracic turn; return to stacked shoulders in 3 seconds. Do not force end-range spinal rotation. Keep hips lightly supported instead if the shoulder shrugs or the turn becomes strenuous.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Landmine Rotational Press** | **2 × 6 per side/direction**; 24 reps/actions | **Main strength: keep body turn connected to the release-side arm.** Rear hand holds the sleeve at the rear shoulder in a modest staggered stance. Pivot the rear foot and turn into a forward/up press without a dip-drive or release. Return in 3 seconds, wrist stacked and ribs level. Use less load for rib flare or elbow/wrist collapse; if minimum bar load is unsuitable, use a rotational band press instead at this dose.  |
| P2 | **Landmine Rotation** | **1 × 6 alternating total**; 6 reps/actions | **Maintain force and control through a compact turning arc.** Use both hands and softly bent elbows. Turn feet/hips with a modest side-to-side arc; keep ribs over pelvis. One one-way traverse is one rep and the reverse is the next rep; six alternating traverses contain three per direction. Take about 2 seconds per traverse and settle briefly at the endpoint. Reduce arc/load if the knee twists, trunk leans or endpoint runs away.  |
| P3 | **Standing rotational band row** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Strengthen the opposing pulling contribution to the hand path.** Band fixed in front at lower-chest height, opposite foot forward. Turn feet/hips/trunk modestly while drawing the elbow toward the same-side lower ribs; return in 3 seconds. Keep the shoulder down and trunk long. Reduce band tension/turn range if the shoulder hikes or the athlete yanks from the low back.  |
| P4 | **Cable / Band Chop — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Support the single-arm downward path through loaded diagonal control.** Secure a high band anchor to the side; opposite foot forward. Both hands travel from above that shoulder toward the opposite front pocket, with a small foot/hip turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3. Reduce range/tension if sideways crunching or band recoil replaces control.  |
| P5 | **Cable / Band Lift — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Maintain the rising preload into the arm platform.** Secure a low band anchor to the rear side. Move both hands from the rear hip across to opposite upper-chest height, allowing feet/hips to turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3; stop below rib flare. Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |
| P6 | **Single-arm Dumbbell Floor Press** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Retain terminal pressing strength without extra unfamiliar lifts.** Lie supine with feet comfortably planted and one dumbbell over the chest. Lower in 3 seconds until the upper arm gently meets the floor, briefly pause and press. Keep ribs/pelvis level; no bridge or roll. Use less load if the elbow lands hard or the trunk rolls.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** The hook throw requires a ball light and small enough for a secure one-hand grip. If not, use the two-hand backhand throw instead at the same dose, openly recording recurrence. Single-end rope tasks require manageable mass, a safe fixed anchor and the unused end clear of the feet; use the matching unloaded short arm arc instead if those conditions fail. No punching target or person is struck.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 20 ball releases (20 throws + 0 slams), separately **12 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 24 reps/holds, including 0 seconds held, in 4 bouts. Primary: **82 working reps in 13 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** Landmine Rotation changes from Class 9 two sets to one; rotational press changes from one to two, giving 82 primary reps. The other lifts hold one set per side. This moves strength investment toward the fast hand-force link without prescribing heavier loads. Support explosive sets remain at two reps per side to contain the new single-arm demands. Progress only after actual connected arm arcs, secure grip and recovered shoulder response; absent those observations, keep the replacement/tension/range conservative and do not escalate speed by widening the arc. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
