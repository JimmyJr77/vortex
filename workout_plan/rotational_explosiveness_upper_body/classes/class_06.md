# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 6 of 12

**Primary effort:** Keep the turning impulse compact and repeatable while reducing accumulated release and strength volume.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** This is a planned consolidation opportunity, not a claim that athletes are fatigued or have completed five sessions. E1 is a low-to-high rotational rope pulse: accelerate briefly and own the endpoint. Supporting tasks revisit outward release, constrained unilateral push, a new downward unloaded hand path, retained-ball braking and pulling acceleration. Each retains a specific force-path job at a small dose.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record a visible single rope impulse without a second swing, stacked ribs at the hand endpoint, and a controlled return before the next rep. Score each direction within the eight scheduled pulses; rope behavior is not a force measurement. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 5, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 5 prescribed 16 releases and 16 retained/unloaded actions, plus 76 strength reps. This consolidation reduces fast-action count and uses one working set for each primary lift. No actual fatigue, completion or successful progression is assumed. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** All support tasks use just 1 × 2 per side; E1 uses 2 × 2. Make each pulse deliberate and fast, then stop fully. Four familiar tasks recur openly to reduce teaching demand while reviewing control. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Two-hand rotational battle-rope lift pulse** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **2 × 2 per side/direction**; 8 reps/actions | **Primary: create a compact upward rotational impulse and stop it cleanly.** Start settled rope ends beside one hip. Pivot feet/hips and drive both hands across toward opposite upper-chest height, producing one brief pulse. Keep elbows soft and ribs down, then settle the rope before resetting. Use the stated low-band speed-lift replacement if rope mass or clearance prevents clean acceleration. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Two-hand medicine-ball backhand throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Review an outward released hand path at low volume.** Ball begins across the lower ribs, elbows softly bent. Turn toward the lane and sweep both hands outward toward the low floor target, then hold the body finish for 2 seconds. Shorten the arc or lighten the ball if the shoulder is dragged backward. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Half-Kneeling Medicine Ball Shot-Put Throw — floor target** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Review unilateral pressing without an entry step.** Target-side foot forward and rear knee comfortably down. Support the ball near the rear shoulder, make a small turn and push to the low target with the rear hand; remain stacked. Use the standing version instead if kneeling contact is intolerable. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Unloaded rotational diagonal hand drive** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Link a short body turn to a downward single-arm finish.** No target or implement. Start the open hand near the opposite upper-chest line; pivot and drive it diagonally toward the same-side front pocket with a softly bent elbow. Stop clear of the thigh and reset slowly. Shorten the path if the shoulder drops or the trunk bends sideways. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Close-held medicine-ball rotational snap-to-stop** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Review active braking of body and implement together.** Hold a very light ball at the sternum. Make a short fast pivot/turn and stop the ball and body together for 2 seconds; reset without a release. Use an unloaded palms-together turn instead if inertia overwhelms the endpoint. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Rotational band row speed drive** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Review a fast pulling contribution with controlled return.** Light band ahead at lower-chest height. From a small reach, pivot and draw the elbow toward lower ribs quickly, then return in 3 seconds without shrugging. Reduce tension/reach if the trunk yanks or shoulder hikes. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Light-band shoulder internal rotation at side** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Control the inward shoulder-rotation component of hand acceleration.** Stand with the working elbow at the ribs, bent 90 degrees, and a light band anchored to the working side. Turn the forearm across the abdomen in 2 seconds and return in 3; keep the elbow and trunk still. Reduce tension/range if the elbow drifts or shoulder rolls forward.  |
| S2 | **Supported light-dumbbell forearm rotation** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Control the forearm orientation needed for a stable ball/punch wrist.** Seated with forearm supported on the thigh and elbow bent, hold a very light dumbbell securely with the wrist straight. Rotate slowly from a comfortable palm-up to palm-down orientation and back; one complete out-and-back is one rep. Use less load or a smaller rotation if the wrist bends or grip strains.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Landmine Rotation** | **1 × 6 alternating total**; 6 reps/actions | **Retain controlled turning strength with one set.** Use both hands and softly bent elbows. Turn feet/hips with a modest side-to-side arc; keep ribs over pelvis. One one-way traverse is one rep and the reverse is the next rep; six alternating traverses contain three per direction. Take about 2 seconds per traverse and settle briefly at the endpoint. Reduce arc/load if the knee twists, trunk leans or endpoint runs away.  |
| P2 | **Landmine Rotational Press** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Retain the turn-to-arm press link.** Rear hand holds the sleeve at the rear shoulder in a modest staggered stance. Pivot the rear foot and turn into a forward/up press without a dip-drive or release. Return in 3 seconds, wrist stacked and ribs level. Use less load for rib flare or elbow/wrist collapse; if minimum bar load is unsuitable, use a rotational band press instead at this dose.  |
| P3 | **One-Arm Dumbbell Row — supported** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Maintain pulling strength with the trunk supported after fast rows.** Free hand on a stable bench, feet staggered. Row toward the same-side hip with a quiet torso and lower in 3 seconds. Use less load if the shoulder hikes or trunk turns.  |
| P4 | **Cable / Band Chop — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Retain strength along the downward hand path.** Secure a high band anchor to the side; opposite foot forward. Both hands travel from above that shoulder toward the opposite front pocket, with a small foot/hip turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3. Reduce range/tension if sideways crunching or band recoil replaces control.  |
| P5 | **Cable / Band Lift — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Support the rising rotational path without more explosive pulses.** Secure a low band anchor to the rear side. Move both hands from the rear hip across to opposite upper-chest height, allowing feet/hips to turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3; stop below rib flare. Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |
| P6 | **Single-arm Dumbbell Floor Press** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Maintain terminal hand-force capacity with the trunk supported.** Lie supine with feet comfortably planted and one dumbbell over the chest. Lower in 3 seconds until the upper arm gently meets the floor, briefly pause and press. Keep ribs/pelvis level; no bridge or roll. Use less load if the elbow lands hard or the trunk rolls.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** For the rising rope pulse, check an approved fixed anchor and enough clearance for a modest hip-to-upper-chest sweep; use a low-anchored light two-hand rotational band speed lift instead at the same dose if rope fit fails. The kneeling throw needs tolerable floor contact; use the standing shot-put instead at the same dose if kneeling is not tolerated. Both replacements are documented recurrences, not new slots.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. **Progression decision:** Class 5’s two rotation sets become one; the other five primary lifts hold one working set each, totaling 70 reps. Explosive work decreases to 28 actions, with eight ball releases and twenty retained/unloaded actions. Familiar backhand, kneeling press, close-ball stop and speed row tasks recur to examine execution under a smaller prescription rather than introducing four more near-duplicate throws. Returning the heavier rotational band row to primary would duplicate E6, so today’s primary row is a supported dumbbell row at the same one-set/eight-rep dose. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
