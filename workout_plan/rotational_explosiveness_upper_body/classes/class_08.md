# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 8 of 12

**Primary effort:** Send rotational force along a diagonal without substituting side bending or back extension.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 is a tall-kneeling diagonal side slam, constraining lower-body drive so trunk-to-hand delivery stays visible. Supporting jobs are unilateral upward impulse, a low one-arm release, an outward descending backhand, a familiar downward rope pulse and low-load upward hand rehearsal. Strength puts extra investment into the loaded chop; the opposite lift, turn, press and pull retain supporting roles.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record a small comfortable turn, hands driving to the declared forward-side floor zone, ribs remaining over the pelvis and the kneeling base staying quiet. Count attempts that finish without folding sideways; do not measure force from ball sound. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 7, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 7 prescribed 20 releases plus eight other fast actions and 70 primary reps, with controlled handoffs by default. No reception results exist. This class removes incoming ball work and keeps twenty releases, changing their plane and unilateral demand rather than claiming recovered tolerance. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** Use a non-rebounding compatible ball for the kneeling slam and comfortable overhead range; the turn stays small. There is no entry step, incoming ball or repeated slam cycle. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. ### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** Tall kneeling requires comfortable floor contact and stable knee spacing. If unavailable, use the standing overhead-to-side slam instead at the same dose, recorded as recurrence. Single-arm scoop release requires a light ball the athlete can securely hold in one hand; use a two-hand scoop instead if grip size is unsuitable. Rope uppercuts require an approved anchor and a manageable single rope end; the unused end lies clear, not across the feet. Use an unloaded rotational uppercut instead if rope minimum load is excessive.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

**Timing and group flow:** About **75–85 minutes for this prescribed work**, excluding existing Access & Prepare 1 and additional group queues. Model: explosive 28 min including resets/bout rest and ~7 min coaching/setup; light strength 4 min; primary strength 41 min including listed preparation, work, recovery and ~7 min loading/instruction; ~4 min section changes/logging. This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. Athlete count, equipment quantities and qualified coach coverage are unknown, so verify actual station throughput. A fixed 60-minute total booking is not supported; complete session length remains unresolved.

**Scheduling:** Review actual throwing/batting/racquet/swinging/contact-sport work, upper-body lifting and separately programmed tumbling before this exposure. Roughly 48–72 h between comparable demanding rotational/upper-body exposures is a planning reference, adjusted for actual workload, symptoms and recovery; no calendar is imposed. Unknown prior work is not zero. Technique deterioration despite recovery calls for lower load/range or ending the slot; do not finish a numerical quota at the expense of control.

### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 20 ball releases (12 throws + 8 slams), separately **14 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 10 reps/holds, including 20 seconds held, in 3 bouts. Primary: **82 working reps in 13 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** Compared with Class 7, only the primary band chop changes from one to two sets per side; all other lifts hold one, totaling 82 primary reps. This prioritizes the diagonal strength task while incoming-ball work is removed. No load increase is prescribed as earned. New unilateral/diagonal tasks use small ranges and one support set. The additional chop round remains subject to actual clean first-set execution and readiness; stop/reduce if technique or reserve fails. Advance range or resistance only from observed stacked diagonal work and acceptable recovery. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
