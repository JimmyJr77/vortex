# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 7 of 12

**Primary effort:** Receive a predictable external ball delivery and turn it into a controlled rotational return.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 is an externally supplied rotational scoop return. The performance problem is accepting the implement without reaching or losing posture, then accelerating it through the turn. E2–E4 apply that input to pushing, downward and backhand paths. Retained-ball stopping and a band sweep support the endpoint without further receptions. Strength holds the lower consolidation volume so a new receiving demand is not paired with heavier lifting.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record hands meeting the ball near the rear hip without a trunk lunge, a controlled transition into the turn, release into the same target and a balanced finish. Log whether the ball was handed over or softly fed; compare only the same input condition. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 6, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 6 prescribed eight releases, twenty other fast actions and 70 primary reps. No catching competence or completed work has been reported. Therefore the baseline uses a controlled hand-to-hand ball supply; a gentle airborne feed is an optional readiness-dependent replacement, not presumed earned. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** For E1–E4 the baseline is a coach handing the ball into the stated start position, fully letting go before the athlete turns. The athlete establishes grip and then accelerates. Only with directly demonstrated comfortable reception may a gentle predictable underhand feed replace the handoff at the same dose. One reception-to-return is one exercise rep, containing one reception and one release; no separate catching sets. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Externally supplied rotational scoop return** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **2 × 2 per side/direction**; 8 reps/actions | **Primary: organize a supplied ball into a balanced rotational release.** Coach hands a light ball into both hands near the rear hip. After the coach lets go, establish grip and turn feet/hips/trunk into a low forward scoop release. Under the optional gentle-feed condition, cushion the ball near the same hip before turning. Use a self-held paused scoop instead if the athlete reaches, fumbles or loses posture; keep the same dose. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Externally supplied rotational shot-put return** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Transfer reception at the chest into unilateral pressing.** Receive a handoff in both hands near the rear chest. Once supported securely, guide with the front hand, pivot and push with the rear hand toward the low target. A verified gentle feed replaces the handoff only under the stated gate. Use the self-held standing shot-put instead if grip or wrist alignment fails. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Externally supplied diagonal side-slam return** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Redirect supplied load into a downward rotational finish.** Receive the handoff at lower-chest height in both hands. Turn and drive the light non-rebounding ball down slightly forward/outside the lead foot; no overhead lift or second throw. Use a self-held shoulder-height side slam instead if reception causes a lunge. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Externally supplied two-hand backhand return** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Accept the implement across the body and release along an outward arc.** Receive a handoff in both hands near the opposite lower ribs. Once the feeder releases, turn toward the lane and sweep the ball outward into the low target; keep the hands in front of the body. Use the self-held backhand throw instead if the shoulder is pulled behind the torso. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Close-held medicine-ball rotational snap-to-stop** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Check body-and-ball endpoint control without another incoming ball.** Hold the light ball close to the sternum, make a compact fast pivot/turn, and hold the endpoint for 2 seconds. Reset slowly. Use an unloaded turn instead if the load cannot be stopped cleanly. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Two-hand band backhand speed sweep** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Apply a predictable resisted sweep after the externally supplied tasks.** Band anchored at chest height to the start side. Sweep both hands across a small transverse arc as feet/hips turn, stopping cleanly and returning slowly. Reduce tension/arc if the body leans or the band recoils. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Tall-Kneeling Anti-Rotation Pulldown — light band** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Hold rib/pelvis alignment while an offset arm force acts after reception.** Kneel tall comfortably with light band anchored high and slightly to one side. With both hands, pull toward the upper chest without turning the trunk; return in 3 seconds. Mirror anchor sides. Use standing split stance instead if knee contact is intolerable; reduce tension if the ribs flare.  |
| S2 | **Prone light-dumbbell horizontal arm raise** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 8 total**; 8 reps/actions | **Control the posterior shoulder during the return from a released arm path.** Lie chest-supported on a stable bench with light dumbbells hanging. Raise both arms outward only to a comfortable line below shoulder height, elbows soft; lower in 3 seconds without lifting the chest. Use less load/range if the neck tightens or trunk extends.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|

Lower in 3 seconds until the upper arm gently meets the floor, briefly pause and press. Keep ribs/pelvis level; no bridge or roll. Use less load if the elbow lands hard or the trunk rolls.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** A qualified feeder must stand beside/behind the athlete outside the release corridor and never become the throw target. The feeder supplies one light ball after the lane is clear, releases hands before the athlete turns, and waits for the outgoing ball to settle. If handoff/feeder positioning cannot be arranged, use the equivalent self-held return instead at the same dose and record it as a regression/recurrence. No blind, hard, overhead incoming or rebound feeds. Baseline: 20 handoffs, zero airborne catches; if all four receiving rows use verified gentle feeds instead: 20 airborne catches, not 20 additional reps. Extend setup/retrieval time for the actual feeder flow.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

**Timing and group flow:** About **70–85 minutes for this prescribed work**, excluding existing Access & Prepare 1 and additional group queues. Model: explosive 29 min including resets/bout rest and ~7 min coaching/setup; light strength 4 min; primary strength 37 min including listed preparation, work, recovery and ~7 min loading/instruction; ~4 min section changes/logging. This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. Athlete count, equipment quantities and qualified coach coverage are unknown, so verify actual station throughput. A fixed 60-minute total booking is not supported; complete session length remains unresolved.

**Scheduling:** Review actual throwing/batting/racquet/swinging/contact-sport work, upper-body lifting and separately programmed tumbling before this exposure. Roughly 48–72 h between comparable demanding rotational/upper-body exposures is a planning reference, adjusted for actual workload, symptoms and recovery; no calendar is imposed. Unknown prior work is not zero. Technique deterioration despite recovery calls for lower load/range or ending the slot; do not finish a numerical quota at the expense of control.

### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 20 ball releases (16 throws + 4 slams), separately **8 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 20 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 20 reps/holds, including 0 seconds held, in 3 bouts. Primary: **70 working reps in 11 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** All six primary lifts remain one working set each, retaining Class 6’s 70-rep total; the rotational band row returns because no speed row is prescribed here. There is no load increase. The external-input tasks total 20 receptions and 20 releases; the other eight fast actions have no reception. Incoming momentum is new loading if a feed is selected, so the baseline handoff is mandatory without demonstrated catching readiness. Advance only the input condition after clean repeated receiving/turning at the actual session; do not simultaneously increase ball mass, feed speed or reps. Hold the handoff or self-start version when uncertain. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
