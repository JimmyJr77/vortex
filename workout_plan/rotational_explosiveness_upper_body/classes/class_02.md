# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 2 of 12

**Primary effort:** Turn a stable base into a fast pushing release, then control the arm finish.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Use a short rotational punch as the primary task. The limiting demand is turning the pelvis/trunk before the hand accelerates, while avoiding elbow snap or torso drift. E1 is the light-band rotational cross. The five support jobs are short-arc rotational hand speed, pulling-side sweep, constrained-base ball pressing, two-hand release and retained rope impulse. Strength prioritizes the integrated press and terminal press; shoulder/trunk control remains light.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record whether the rear-foot pivot/hip turn precedes the hand, the wrist stays straight, the elbow remains softly extended and the athlete returns to the same stance without a recovery step on each scheduled attempt. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 1, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 1 prescribed 40 releases and 88 primary working reps, with no completed work reported. This class changes the fast tasks and reduces ball-release exposure; retained resistance and unloaded punches are logged separately, not treated as equivalent ball throws. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** E1 receives two sets; other drills receive one small set per side. No target is struck and no partner contact is permitted. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Light-band rotational cross** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **2 × 3 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Primary: transmit the turn into a fast forward punch.** From a modest staggered stance, anchor behind the rear shoulder; turn the rear foot/hip then drive the hand forward at shoulder height. Stop short of locked elbow and return under control; never release the band. Reduce tension or reach if the wrist folds or arm leads the turn. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Rotational shadow hook** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Coordinate a short transverse hand arc with the trunk turn.** With no implement or target, keep elbow bent near 90 degrees and fist between chest and shoulder height; pivot and swing the forearm as a compact unit, stopping near the midline. Reset fully. Shorten the arc if the fist outruns the trunk or shoulder rolls forward. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Two-hand band backhand speed sweep** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Accelerate the transverse pulling/sweeping path while controlling its endpoint.** Anchor at chest height on the starting side. Both hands begin near that side of the chest; pivot and sweep across toward the opposite side with softly bent elbows, stopping within a comfortable arc. Return slowly. Reduce tension/arc for side bending or a jerky return. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Half-Kneeling Medicine Ball Shot-Put Throw — floor target** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Express release-side pressing with leg drive reduced.** Target-side foot forward, rear knee comfortably grounded. Support the ball beside the rear shoulder; use a small comfortable trunk turn then push it toward the low forward target with the rear hand. Guide hand releases first. Use a lighter grippable ball or smaller turn if the knee shifts or back arches. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Standing rotational two-hand chest push throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Deliver the turn through simultaneous horizontal arm extension.** Hold a light ball near the rear side of the chest with both hands. Turn the feet/hips and press both hands through the verified low forward target; do not scoop from the hip. Reduce ball mass if the athlete bends sideways or lets one hand take over. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Two-hand transverse battle-rope whip** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Apply a brief rotational impulse against rope inertia, then brake.** Facing the rope anchor in an athletic stance, hold one rope end in each hand; shift both hands from beside one hip across to the other with a small foot/hip turn. One directed sweep is one rep; settle the rope and walk hands back slowly before the next. Use the stated band-sweep replacement if rope mass or anchor fit prevents a fast controlled impulse. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** For band punches, anchor behind the working shoulder to an approved fixed point; inspect band wear and keep people out of its line. A rope whip requires an approved anchor, manageable rope and a clear rope sweep fully inside the available lane. If its anchor/rope minimum load is unsuitable, use a two-hand band horizontal speed sweep instead at the same dose, recorded as a proposed replacement. Keep hands outside the rope path. Ball throws use the verified floor-target setup.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

**Timing and group flow:** About **80–90 minutes for this prescribed work**, excluding existing Access & Prepare 1 and additional group queues. Model: explosive 30 min including resets/bout rest and ~7 min coaching/setup; light strength 4 min; primary strength 45 min including listed preparation, work, recovery and ~7 min loading/instruction; ~4 min section changes/logging. This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. Athlete count, equipment quantities and qualified coach coverage are unknown, so verify actual station throughput. A fixed 60-minute total booking is not supported; complete session length remains unresolved.

**Scheduling:** Review actual throwing/batting/racquet/swinging/contact-sport work, upper-body lifting and separately programmed tumbling before this exposure. Roughly 48–72 h between comparable demanding rotational/upper-body exposures is a planning reference, adjusted for actual workload, symptoms and recovery; no calendar is imposed. Unknown prior work is not zero. Technique deterioration despite recovery calls for lower load/range or ending the slot; do not finish a numerical quota at the expense of control.

### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 12 ball releases (12 throws + 0 slams), separately **30 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 10 reps/holds, including 30 seconds held, in 3 bouts. Primary: **94 working reps in 15 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** Class 1 main turning/pressing emphasis becomes two sets each of rotational press and unilateral floor press; rotation drops to one set and other supporting lifts retain one. Primary volume changes from 88 to 94 reps because the priority shifts to pressing, not because athletes earned more load. Ball releases drop from 40 to 12; 30 other fast actions introduce different stresses. Match appropriate effort rather than equating these units. Without actual readiness for retained resistance, reduce band tension/range and preserve controlled endpoints. The next advancement requires an observed clean turn-to-hand sequence and recovered shoulder response. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
