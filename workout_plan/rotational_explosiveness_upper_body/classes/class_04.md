# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 4 of 12

**Primary effort:** Deliver rotational force through the hand after a short deliberate step, without losing the release line.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The main task is a step-in rotational shot-put: arriving at a planted base must feed the hand rather than create a falling throw. Support jobs are an upward retained punch, guided transverse arm arc, lead-hand release, overhead rotational projection and compact hand-path rehearsal. Strength concentrates on the turn-to-press link while preserving a controlled pulling and diagonal base.

**Integrated quality marker:** For E1 record a planted lead foot before release, a rear-foot pivot rather than knee twisting, a straight wrist and a balanced two-second finish inside the start bay. Record each scheduled attempt by side. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 3, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 3 prescribed 24 releases, 18 other fast actions and 76 working strength reps. This class holds those explosive action totals but changes their mechanical demands; no athlete tolerance is assumed. Moving-entry pressing requires first-attempt coaching rather than assumed mastery from earlier documents. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** E1 has the largest allocation. An approach is one deliberate step within 1 m, not a run-up. No jump release or rebound catching. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Step-in rotational medicine-ball shot-put** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **2 × 3 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Primary: turn a deliberate plant into a directed pushing release.** Start staggered with ball at the rear shoulder; take one small lead-foot step toward the low forward target, settle that foot, pivot the rear hip and press the ball with the rear hand. Hold the finish. Use the standing rotational shot-put instead at the same dose if a step causes falling or knee twist. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Light-band rotational uppercut** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Apply the turn to an upward hand path against retained resistance.** Band secured low behind the working side, hand near the hip. With bent elbow, pivot the rear foot and drive the fist toward upper-chest height; stop below shoulder shrug and return slowly. Reduce tension/height if the trunk arches or band pulls the wrist out of line. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **One-Arm Landmine Arc Press — light fast arc** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Control the release-side shoulder through a rapid guided transverse arc.** Start sleeve in one hand in front of the same-side upper chest, elbow softly bent. Sweep a short arc toward the opposite upper chest with a small body turn, maintaining safe bar clearance; return deliberately. No release or overhead circle. Use the stated unloaded transverse sweep replacement if the bar is too heavy for speed/control. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Lead-hand rotational medicine-ball push throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Express force through the arm nearest the target without a large rear-arm wind-up.** Use a very light ball held at the lead-side chest with the other hand assisting its support. Make a small turn toward the lane and extend the lead arm into the low target; the supporting hand releases. Keep both feet planted/pivoting. Use a smaller grippable ball or shorten the turn if the lead shoulder rolls forward. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Two-hand rotational overhead medicine-ball throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Transfer the turn through a comfortable elevated arm position.** Hold a light ball above the forehead, not behind the neck. Turn feet/hips toward the lane and throw forward/down through the cleared target while keeping ribs stacked. One release, full reset. Use the stated rotational chest-push replacement if overhead position requires arching. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Rotational shadow hook** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Rehearse a compact connected hand arc without additional implement stress.** No target or implement. Pivot the rear foot and sweep the bent forearm at chest height as one compact unit with the trunk; stop near midline, then reset. Shorten the arc if the hand leads the body or shoulder rolls forward. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|

Take one short step away, pause 2 seconds without turning, then return. One out-and-back is one rep; mirror sides. Use a smaller step or less tension if the hips shift or breath is held.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** The arc press requires a securely anchored landmine whose minimum bar load can be moved briskly without shrugging; use an unloaded two-hand transverse shadow sweep instead if that minimum load is unsuitable, same dose and logged. Overhead projection requires overhead clearance and a compatible ball/target; use a two-hand rotational chest push instead if comfortable overhead positioning is unavailable, rather than forcing range. These are replacements, not extra drills.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

**Timing and group flow:** About **80–90 minutes for this prescribed work**, excluding existing Access & Prepare 1 and additional group queues. Model: explosive 30 min including resets/bout rest and ~7 min coaching/setup; light strength 5 min; primary strength 41 min including listed preparation, work, recovery and ~7 min loading/instruction; ~4 min section changes/logging. This is an individual/small-wave estimate, not a confirmed booking. One active throwing area at a time; clear it before lifting. **Scheduling:** Review actual throwing/batting/racquet/swinging/contact-sport work, upper-body lifting and separately programmed tumbling before this exposure. ### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 24 ball releases (24 throws + 0 slams), separately **18 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 22 reps/holds, including 12 seconds held, in 4 bouts. Primary: **82 working reps in 13 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** Compared with Class 3, Landmine Rotation drops from two sets to one and Landmine Rotational Press rises from one to two; the other four strength jobs use one set per side. The rotational band row returns because today’s explosive block has no speed row. This is an emphasis change with 82 primary reps, not evidence for increasing resistance. Hold all loads at clean technical reserve until actual wrist, plant and follow-through control and recovered shoulder response support progression. Entry size remains fixed; do not simultaneously increase step speed and ball load. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
