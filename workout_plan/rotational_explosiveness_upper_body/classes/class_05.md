# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 5 of 12

**Primary effort:** Convert a small lateral arrival into an upper-body rotational impulse and a controlled finish.

**Framework:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). Existing seven-category framework in the curriculum remains unchanged. Foundational athletes: ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 is a short shuffle-to-rotation throw. The limiting task is stopping unwanted travel while sending force through the hands. Other jobs are unloading/reloading the rear hip, a planted retained punch, a moving-entry rope impulse, a downward moving-entry release and active braking of a retained ball. The strength block loads the turn and its return, while light shoulder/trunk work supports the finish.

**Integrated quality marker:** During E1, record whether the feet remain uncrossed in the shuffle, the lead foot accepts the arrival before the hands sweep, and the athlete remains inside the start bay after release. Score the two-second balanced finish on both sides. Observe during the scheduled anchor attempts, count all attempted work, and do not add testing reps. A different drill is not a comparable performance test or evidence of improvement.

**Review before design:** Read the saved curriculum/map, preceding Class 4, workload/progression ledger, drill usage and feedback; source hashes are retained with this class record. Class 4 used 24 releases and 18 other fast actions, with 82 primary reps. The short-entry theme warrants smaller explosive sets here: only two reps per side for the moving ball/rope tasks. Actual entry competence and recovery remain unknown. **Prescribed is not observed:** no athlete execution/response has been supplied; this is a conditional prescription.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly six exercises.** Movement is deliberately small, with total travel no more than 1 m. The upper-body rotational impulse is the task; this is not a sprint or agility conditioning session. Use light implements and fast coordinated intent; a retained-load drill ends under control rather than being released. Both sides receive the written dose.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Med Ball Shuffle-to-Rotation Throw — short floor-target version** | **2 × 2 per side/direction**; 8 reps/actions | **Primary: accept lateral arrival and send the turn through the hands.** Start side-on; take one short uncrossed shuffle toward the target direction, plant the lead foot and make a two-hand rotational scoop release. Stay grounded and finish within the marked bay. Use a single side-step into the throw instead if uncrossed shuffle placement is unreliable; same dose. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E2 | **Drop-step rotational medicine-ball scoop throw** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Reload the rear hip before delivering the turn.** From side-on, move the lead foot slightly away/back to open the stance and settle toward the rear hip; re-establish pressure, pivot and sweep the ball through the low target. Keep the whole action inside 1 m. Reduce the step to a small stance opening if the knee twists or balance is lost. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E3 | **Step-plant rotational band cross** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Connect a plant to a retained forward hand impulse.** With a light band secured behind the rear shoulder, take one small lead-foot step, plant, pivot and punch forward without locking the elbow. Return the hand and step back deliberately. Use the stationary band cross instead at this dose if the plant or band line becomes unsafe. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E4 | **Lateral-step transverse battle-rope pulse** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Transfer a small lateral arrival into a retained inertial sweep.** With rope settled, step laterally a short distance, plant and sweep both rope ends toward the opposite hip using a small body turn. Settle completely before returning to the start. Use the stationary transverse whip instead if entry clearance or placement is inadequate. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E5 | **Step-Behind Rotational Slam Ball Slam** | **1 × 2 per side/direction**; 4 reps/actions | **Direct entry momentum into a downward diagonal hand finish.** Use one small step-behind and lead-foot replant, then turn and slam a light non-rebounding ball slightly forward/outside the lead foot. No overhead windmill or jump; wait until the ball settles. Use the stationary side slam instead at the same dose if crossing placement is unreliable. Rest **90 s between side bouts/sets**. |
| E6 | **Close-held medicine-ball rotational snap-to-stop** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 3 per side/direction**; 6 reps/actions | **Brake the trunk-and-hands impulse without a release or catch.** Hold a very light ball close to the sternum. Make a short fast foot/hip/trunk turn, then stop the ball and body together within a comfortable arc for 2 seconds. Reset slowly; do not whip the ball at long arm length. Use an unloaded palms-together turn instead if ball inertia prevents a clean stop. Rest **60 s between side bouts/sets**. |

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly two light strengthening exercises.** Preserve at least 4 technical repetitions in reserve, or comfortably stop a hold with about 5 seconds of sound position available. They must not pre-fatigue primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Side Plank Row — short-lever light band** | **1 × 5 per side/direction**; 10 reps/actions | **Maintain lateral trunk support while the pulling arm moves.** From a bent-knee side plank on the forearm, pull a very light band toward the upper-side ribs without rotating the pelvis; return in 3 seconds. Anchor is in front and outside the face line. Use hips lightly supported on the floor instead if holding them up makes the row strenuous.  |
| S2 | **Side-lying dumbbell external rotation** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Revisit controlled shoulder rotation after entry-driven releases.** Lie on the nonworking side with elbow at the ribs and bent 90 degrees. Rotate the forearm upward in 2 seconds and lower in 3, keeping the trunk still. Use less load/range if the elbow leaves the ribs or the torso rolls.  |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly six challenging lifts.** Working doses below exclude the easy load-preparation sets described afterward. About 2–3 technical repetitions remain; no failure or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Landmine Rotation** | **2 × 6 alternating total**; 12 reps/actions | **Load the turning arc and endpoint braking after arrival.** Use both hands and softly bent elbows. Turn feet/hips with a modest side-to-side arc; keep ribs over pelvis. One one-way traverse is one rep and the reverse is the next rep; six alternating traverses contain three per direction. Take about 2 seconds per traverse and settle briefly at the endpoint. Reduce arc/load if the knee twists, trunk leans or endpoint runs away.  |
| P2 | **Landmine Rotational Press** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Keep force linked from the plant through the release-side arm.** Rear hand holds the sleeve at the rear shoulder in a modest staggered stance. Pivot the rear foot and turn into a forward/up press without a dip-drive or release. Return in 3 seconds, wrist stacked and ribs level. Use less load for rib flare or elbow/wrist collapse; if minimum bar load is unsuitable, use a rotational band press instead at this dose.  |
| P3 | **Standing rotational band row** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 8 per side/direction**; 16 reps/actions | **Strengthen the pulling side as the upper body redirects.** Band fixed in front at lower-chest height, opposite foot forward. Turn feet/hips/trunk modestly while drawing the elbow toward the same-side lower ribs; return in 3 seconds. Keep the shoulder down and trunk long. Reduce band tension/turn range if the shoulder hikes or the athlete yanks from the low back.  |
| P4 | **Cable / Band Chop — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Load the downward hand path used after a planted entry.** Secure a high band anchor to the side; opposite foot forward. Both hands travel from above that shoulder toward the opposite front pocket, with a small foot/hip turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3. Reduce range/tension if sideways crunching or band recoil replaces control.  |
| P5 | **Cable / Band Lift — standing band execution** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Strengthen the rising preload from the rear hip.** Secure a low band anchor to the rear side. Move both hands from the rear hip across to opposite upper-chest height, allowing feet/hips to turn. Drive in 1–2 seconds and return in 3; stop below rib flare. Reduce range/tension if leaning back supplies the lift.  |
| P6 | **Single-arm Dumbbell Floor Press** **[Proposed addition/variant]** | **1 × 6 per side/direction**; 12 reps/actions | **Maintain terminal pressing force without more entry volume.** Lie supine with feet comfortably planted and one dumbbell over the chest. Lower in 3 seconds until the upper arm gently meets the floor, briefly pause and press. Keep ribs/pelvis level; no bridge or roll. Use less load if the elbow lands hard or the trunk rolls.  |

### Delivery, loading and stop rules

**Athlete and readiness:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique; rotational and implement-specific competence still needs coach observation. Earlier class files are prescribed work only. Actual attendance, technique, loads, soreness and recovery remain unknown. Do not presume the class number grants readiness. On the first scheduled repetitions, use manageable range/resistance and apply the listed regression; these attempts count, not extra tests. Pain, uncontrolled rotation or unsafe equipment/ball paths stop the affected work; record incomplete delivery rather than making up attempts.

**Ball/station setup:** Use the same conditional floor-target approach documented in Class 1: a low forward ball release into a target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, compatible low-rebound ball and impact-permitted floor, with the entire flight/rebound/roll contained inside an excluded 10 m lane. Verify width and side clearance. A suitable throwing wall may be used instead only after verification, with no rebound catches unless a row explicitly prescribes receiving. Do not force a heavy ball to shorten flight. For entering drills, all footwork stays within a 1 m start bay and there is no hop, jump or sprint. Three lanes are not one 30 m runway. If neither floor nor approved wall containment works, arrange an approved area and re-audit before delivery.

**Additional setup for this class:** Mark the entire entry and final stance within a 1 m start bay before clearing the ball corridor. Rope entry work requires the same approved anchor and sweep clearance as earlier rope tasks, plus room to step without tripping on the rope; use a stationary transverse rope whip instead if entry clearance is insufficient, same dose and a declared regression. The retained-ball turn uses a light ball held close, never a heavy long-lever stop.

**Recovery/loading:** Ball releases and other fast actions are discrete efforts with 10–15 s between repetitions for controlled reset; no conditioning circuits. Use the row's between-bout recovery (60 s for small supports, 90 s for the anchor/higher-demand work), and at least 60 s between explosive exercises; extend it to preserve speed/control. Left and right are separate bouts. Do not trade away rest to fit a clock. Light strength stays at least 4 clean repetitions in reserve, with 30–45 s between sides and 45–60 s between its two exercises. Primary resistance is challenging at about 2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve; keep the indicated tempo and choose each side's appropriate load. Rest 90 s between primary side bouts/exercises, 2 min between rounds of a two-set lift and 2–3 min between Landmine Rotation sets. Equipment changes/queues can supply that rest only when elapsed time actually suffices; count time once.

**Load-preparation sets:** Before each listed primary lift, perform one easy set of 3 per side using lighter resistance; for alternating Landmine Rotation use 4 traverses total instead. These are the same six exercises, not additional slots. Allow 30–45 s to change sides and 60–90 s before working. Minimum preparation is **34 easy reps in 11 bouts**, separate from working work. If another preparation set is needed, repeat that lift's easy dose, log it and add the time. No max tests or forced reps. Secure band anchors and landmine; if the minimum bar is unsuitable, use the same-dose standing two-hand band rotation or one-arm rotational band press instead of the relevant landmine lift, recording the proposed replacement.

### Workload and progression

**Prescribed workload:** 16 ball releases (12 throws + 4 slams), separately **16 retained-load/unloaded fast actions**, and 0 prescribed ball receptions. Explosive work comprises 14 physical side bouts. These are different loading types, not equivalent contact units. No prescribed human jumps, hops, bounds, sprints or tumbling outside the unknown preparation. Light work: 22 reps/holds, including 0 seconds held, in 4 bouts. Primary: **76 working reps in 12 bouts**, plus 34 easy reps. Two-sided doses are fully counted; failed attempts still count as actual exposure. The accompanying record separates side counts and action types.

**Progression decision:** Landmine Rotation changes from Class 4 one set to two to prioritize the turning/braking arc; rotational press decreases from two to one. Other primary lifts keep one set per side, giving 76 working reps. Moving-entry ball and rope work is reduced to two reps per side, while simple retained actions use three. None of this demonstrates tolerance. Before increasing entry speed, actual reports must show repeated planted releases and controlled finishes in the marked bay, with no shoulder/trunk recovery issue. Keep range and loads unchanged when such feedback is absent. New tasks do not establish readiness or justify heavier loads. Hold or regress when actual feedback is absent or control/recovery is insufficient. For any recurring lift, retain a controllable load until clean work at the intended reserve and acceptable recovery are actually reported; then change one appropriate variable, not load, range and volume together.
