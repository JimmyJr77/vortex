# Rotational Explosiveness — Upper Body — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Accelerate a ball through a controlled body turn: feet and hips initiate, the trunk transmits the turn, and the hands finish the projection without an uncontrolled spin or side bend.

**Focus:** Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis (rotational throws, punches, and swings). **Baseline:** ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, qualified supervision, and individually demonstrated readiness for these movements. This is the first prescription in this curriculum; athlete completion, loads and responses are unknown.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Establish repeatable rotational projection from a stable start. The primary anchor is E1, the paused standing rotational scoop toss. Its limiting demand is coordinating the turn and release while retaining balance. Supporting jobs are upper-body contribution with less leg drive, immediate reversal of a small wind-up, a unilateral pushing release, transfer of a short step into the turn, and a downward rotational finish. Strength then loads the turning arc, rotational press and pull, opposing diagonal paths and terminal horizontal press. Light trunk and shoulder work must leave the athlete fresh for those lifts.

**Quality marker:** During E1's twelve scheduled attempts, record each side's valid attempts: controlled rear-foot pivot and hip initiation, hands following the turn, release into the declared target zone, and a balanced two-second finish without an extra recovery step. Record the specific fault on unsuccessful attempts; they still count as exposure. This is a coaching observation, not a measurement of force or an established pass-rate test. Do not add attempts for assessment.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly 6 exercises.** Use light, grippable balls that permit a visibly fast release with controlled posture. Select by demonstrated execution, not an age-based kilogram prescription. Each attempt is one release followed by a full reset; no rebound catches, repeated slams or return throws. The first scheduled attempt may establish an appropriate ball and range; it is included in the dose.

**Throwing setup applies to E1–E5:** Use the floor-target adaptation described below: a low forward release into a cone-marked floor target, initially about 2–3 m downrange, using an impact-compatible ball with verified low rebound. This is rotational projection practice, not maximum-distance testing. Mirror the stance while keeping every release down the same cleared lane. A throwing wall is unconfirmed. Do not reinterpret a listed ball as proof that a safe throwing surface or suitable rebound behavior exists.

**Recovery for every row:** Allow **10–15 seconds between releases** for retrieval/reset, longer if needed; rest **90 seconds after each side's set before the next side/set/exercise**. A round means complete one side, rest, complete the other side. For E1, rest again before the second round. Retrieval and transition can occur within rest; count elapsed time once. Stand clear until the ball stops and the coach releases the area.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Med Ball Rotational Scoop Toss — paused standing start; floor-target adaptation** | **2 × 3 per direction**; 12 releases | **Primary anchor: generate rotational projection from a settled load.** Stand side-on, feet at a comfortable athletic width; hold the ball near the rear hip and pause 2 seconds. Pivot the rear foot, turn the hips/trunk, and sweep both hands through the target. No preparatory bounce or step. Finish balanced for 2 seconds. Reduce ball mass or turn range if the arms lead or the athlete spins out. |
| E2 | **Half-Kneeling Rotational Med Ball Throw — floor-target adaptation** | **1 × 3 per direction**; 6 releases | **Express trunk-to-hand acceleration with leg propulsion reduced.** Target-side foot forward, opposite knee comfortably supported on the floor; ball begins beside the rear hip. Use a small comfortable thoracic turn and fast two-hand release; do not force the pelvis to remain rigid while twisting farther. Keep the front foot planted and pelvis quiet. | E3 | **Med Ball Countermovement Rotational Throw — floor-target adaptation** | **1 × 3 per direction**; 6 releases | **Change a brief preload immediately into the same projection.** Standing side-on, make one small, smooth wind-back and reverse promptly into the throw; no pause, repeated rocking or extra step. | E5 | **Med Ball Step-Behind Rotational Throw — floor-target adaptation** | **1 × 2 per direction**; 4 releases | **Transfer a short weight shift into upper-body rotation without continued travel.** From side-on, make one small step behind, replant the lead foot and deliver a scoop release. Keep total approach travel within 1 m, both feet grounded at release and the finish inside the start bay. No running, hop or jump. Use a simple step-in rotational throw instead of the crossover version if foot placement is unreliable; retain 1 × 2 per direction and record the replacement. |
| E6 | **Med Ball Overhead to Side Slam — non-rebounding slam-ball execution** | **1 × 3 to each side**; 6 slams | **Accelerate the hands diagonally downward through a turn and arrest the body's follow-through.** From a comfortable overhead position, turn through the feet/hips and drive the ball to the floor slightly forward and outside the target-side foot, clear of toes. Stay long through the trunk; do not fold and twist at the low back. Lift/reset deliberately after the ball stops. Use a shoulder-height diagonal side slam instead of the overhead version if overhead range requires rib flare; same dose. |

**Quality rules:** High intent means fast coordinated release, not a larger wind-up or heavier ball. On a technical miss, stop the set, recover and adjust the remaining scheduled attempts; do not replace used attempts. If the same fault persists after one adjustment, or release speed visibly deteriorates despite full rest, end that slot and record incomplete delivery. Stop immediately for pain, an unsafe ball path or loss of control. Never catch a returning ball to save time.

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly 6 exercises.** Working resistance should leave approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** at the stated tempo. Coach and athlete judge that reserve from clean repetitions; no failure tests or maximal load tests. Choose each side's load separately if necessary, with equal prescribed reps. Use resistance that challenges the intended action, rather than balance, grip failure or breathlessness.

**Preparation sets, additional to working sets but within the same exercise entries:** P1: 1 × 4 alternating traverses with a lighter load. P2: 1 × 4 per arm lighter. P3–P6: 1 × 3 per side lighter at that exercise's working tempo. Allow 30–45 seconds to change sides during these easy sets, then 60–90 seconds before the first working set. If another preparation set is necessary for P1 or P2, repeat its stated easy dose, log it and allow extra time. Preparation sets must not become challenging work. These are load checks for the listed lifts, not a replacement preparation sequence.

### Delivery, timing and recovery notes

- **Station requirement, not a confirmed floor plan:** Use one active ball area at a time. Within a 10 m lane, reserve roughly 0–2 m for the athlete/approach, place the floor target roughly 2–3 m beyond the release point, and keep the remaining downrange area empty for ball travel. Verify lane width, side clearance, floor impact permission and full rebound/roll containment with the actual implement before athlete releases. Use adjacent lanes as exclusion space if needed; do not run lifters alongside a live ball path. No one waits downrange. The three lanes are separate, not a 30 m corridor.
- **Containment fallback:** A compatible impact-rated wall may be used instead of the floor target only after staff confirms its suitability and rebound clearance. Keep the movement doses and no-catching rule; record the changed target, because that anchor setup is not directly comparable with the floor-target version. If neither floor containment nor a suitable wall is available, the release block is not deliverable as written; arrange an approved area and re-audit it before running the class. Do not use a heavier ball, suppress intent or merge the throw slots to conceal this limitation.
- **Strength setup:** Verify a secure landmine with a bar whose minimum load is manageable, safe grips, and approved band anchors at the required heights. Cable machines are not assumed. For an unsuitable landmine minimum load, use a standing two-hand band rotation instead of P1, or a standing one-arm rotational band press instead of P2, with the same working/preparation doses and tempo; both are proposed replacements. Where a band anchor cannot be verified, resolve that station before delivery. Do not improvise attachment to a movable bench.
- **Timing:** Allow approximately **80–90 minutes for the prescribed work**, including the listed load-preparation sets, recovery, demonstrations and equipment changes, under the individual/small supervised-wave assumptions in the audit. Add the actual Access & Prepare 1 duration and any group queues. This is not a verified whole-class booking. A 60-minute total booking does not fit; a 90-minute total booking is also unconfirmed until preparation and group flow are known. Do not shorten recovery to force a fit.
- **Group flow:** All athletes complete the explosive section before moving through light resilience and then primary strength. Preserve each athlete's order. Clear the ball area before setting up lifts. Equipment counts, cohort size and staffing are unknown; one landmine or one ball may require serial waves and more time. A coach must be able to observe each active throw/lift and manage the exclusion area.
- **Scheduling:** Treat this as a demanding rotational trunk/shoulder exposure. Review actual batting, throwing, racquet swings, contact sport, upper-body lifting and separate tumbling/hand-support work before delivery and before the next demanding exposure. Roughly 48–72 hours between comparable demanding sessions is an initial planning reference, adjusted for the athlete's schedule, symptoms and recovery; elapsed time alone does not establish readiness. Persistent pain or altered movement warrants stopping and appropriate assessment.
