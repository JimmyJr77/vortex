## Required architecture and focus

All twelve use the exact seven-category framework in curriculum.md, with the selected mapping **Rotational explosiveness > Lower-body emphasis (rotational kicks)**. Each contains exactly four ordered sections: existing Access & Prepare 1 reference, **six explosive entries**, **two light stabilization/resilience entries**, **six challenging primary-strength entries**. No extra conditioning, reaction block, finisher, testing block, tumbling or additional performance category was added. All repeated hops and integrated entry/kick/recoil phases are explicitly counted, not concealed as exercise circuits.

All exercise jobs are directed toward support-foot/hip rotation, free-thigh acceleration/recovery and the lower-body force needed to receive, redirect or initiate that task. The small seated/supported speed drills are rapid force-expression tasks, not maximal-amplitude ballistic joint rotations or mobility entries falsely counted as power. If a task can only be performed slowly, the class instructs the coach to record technique-only exposure. Intended mechanical roles are not demonstrated training outcomes.

## Continuity and justified progression

- Classes 1–3 distinguish a prepared rear-leg task, front-leg unloading and force from a held start. They do not assume the athlete attended or mastered the earlier classes.
- Class 4 emphasizes the free thigh and active return. Class 5 adds short lateral arrival. The change in task is explicit rather than an unexplained harder workout.
- Class 6 reduces landing count and primary strength volume; it consolidates workload and recurring strength patterns without claiming all its different task constraints are already learned.
- Classes 7–9 introduce conditional coupling and planned forward/backward/lateral entries with explicit shorter or separated versions. The new demands need their own readiness check; the existence of Class 6 is not evidence of recovery.
- Class 10 uses early cue-based variants of known movement families and long uniform inter-attempt recovery. Class 11 removes cue uncertainty and reduces primary volume while changing precision constraints.
- Class 12 uses a matched Class 1 prefix and a few short application probes. Its extra six application kicks are disclosed as extra leg-swing exposure, not balanced away by an equivalent-contact calculation. All strength lifts remain at the lower six-round allocation.

The progression is a sequence of intended learning/force demands with conditional loading, not a claim of linear athlete improvement. Actual feedback can require holding, reducing or revising any class. Class number is not clearance.
