# Lower-Body Rotational Explosiveness — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Produce a quick support-foot pivot and pelvic turn into a low roundhouse kick, then recover the leg and stance under control. Framework: **Rotational explosiveness > Lower-body emphasis (rotational kicks)**.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Kicking experience, current restrictions, recent training and recovery are unknown. Basic lifting competence does not establish kicking competence: a coach able to teach the pivot and kick must qualify the first scheduled attempts. This is a finalized conditional prescription, not a record of training performed.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The result sought is a fast, repeatable hip-led kick from a stationary stance. The demand is turning over a usable support foot while accelerating the free thigh without twisting a fixed knee or losing the pelvis. **E2 is this class's performance anchor.** Supporting jobs are grounded initiation, countermovement rotational takeoff, lateral plant acceptance, arriving into a grounded rotational plant, and rapid chamber lift; strength supplies the stance-leg push, hinge, lateral hip force, hip-rotation torque, chamber strength and ankle drive. Judge **valid E2 attempts out of six per kicking leg**, using the same stance, height and execution within this class. No speed or impact-force measurement is claimed.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly 6 exercises.** Bodyweight unless a band is specified. Fast intent within a controllable range; no conditioning pace. E1 is four brief explosive initiations, not a mobility sequence. Each rep has a complete reset. Clockwise/counterclockwise counts are viewed from above.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split-stance explosive hip pivot** — grounded, proposed exercise | **1 × 2 per direction = 4 attempts.** Start in a short split stance; rapidly shift toward the lead leg and turn feet and pelvis together through a comfortable approximately 45° arc. Heels may release; both forefeet remain grounded. No kick or jump. Finish still for **2 s**. Rest **20 s between attempts**, then **60 s** before E2. | Isolate the support-foot release and hip-turn initiation before adding a swinging leg. Mirror the stance for the opposite direction. Keep knees soft and following toes; reduce turn size if a foot sticks, knee twists or trunk leads the turn. Stop if the surface will not permit controlled pivoting. |
| 2 | **Standing low roundhouse shadow kick** — **primary anchor**, proposed exercise | **3 × 2 per kicking leg = 12 attempts, 6/leg.** From the same short fighting stance with the kicking leg initially at the rear, turn the support foot with the pelvis, lift and sweep the bent thigh, then extend only through a controllable low arc, recoil and return to the starting stance; hold **2 s**. Foot stays below hip height. Rest **20 s between the two reps**, **60 s between legs**, **90 s between rounds**, and **90 s after the final round**. | Integrate stance-leg rotation with free-leg acceleration and recovery. Accelerate the hip/thigh briskly; keep a soft knee at extension and brake the leg actively. No target, impact, partner contact, head-height kick, jump or spinning kick. Use a shorter arc and less terminal extension instead if recoil or balance is lost. Never snap an unsupported knee into lockout. |
| 3 | **90-Degree Hop to Stick** — two-foot countermovement execution | **2 × 2 per direction = 8 jumps.** Two-foot takeoff and landing. Use one natural dip and an immediate explosive turn; prioritize orientation over height. Finish rotation in the air and hold the landing **2 s**. Rest **20 s between reps**, **60 s between directions**, **90 s between rounds**, and **90 s before E4**. | Express rapid leg-driven rotation using a countermovement, with a repeatable two-foot base. Land feet, knees and pelvis facing the same direction. Use a smaller **45° turn instead**, with the same dose, if 90° cannot be completed before landing; record the changed angle. Do not screw the feet around after contact. |
| 4 | **Lateral Bound to Rotational Stick** | **1 × 2 each travel direction = 4 bounds.** Right-to-left takeoff/landing twice and left-to-right twice. Make a short lateral flight with a small, preselected turn, **no more than 45°**, toward the landing side; land on the opposite leg and hold **2 s**. Rest **25 s between bounds**, then **90 s** before E5. | Load the single-leg lateral plant that must accept the body before a hip turn. The turn occurs during flight; no twisting on touchdown. Choose distance inside a cleared **2 m travel segment** and permit a separate clear **2 m recovery area**. Shorten the bound/turn for pelvic drop or a recovery hop. If turn clearance/control is insufficient, **use instead a short lateral bound to stick without the turn**, same dose; retain a clear landing/recovery area. |
| 5 | **Lateral step-in to rotational plant** — proposed grounded entry drill | **1 × 2 per receiving leg = 4 efforts.** From a parallel athletic stance, make one brisk, short lateral step onto the receiving foot, then redirect the pelvis through a comfortable turn of up to **45°** into a split kick stance. Let the receiving heel release and the other foot realign; finish still for **2 s**. No kick, run-up or flight. Keep step/finish within **2 m**, with another **2 m clear for recovery**. Rest **25 s between efforts**, then **90 s** before E6. | Convert arriving bodyweight into a grounded hip turn. This adds an entry/weight-acceptance demand to E1's in-place pivot and differs from E4's airborne reorientation. Mirror the step/turn for the other receiving leg. Shorten step/angle for overstriding, an extra shuffle or a trapped knee; feet must turn freely with the pelvis. |
| 6 | **Split-Stance Band-Resisted Knee Drive** — light band | **1 × 3 per driving leg = 6 efforts.** A light loop links the forefeet, with the supporting foot securing its end. Begin in a short split stance with the driving toes resting behind; drive that bent knee briskly forward/up to a controlled low chamber, then lower/reset without recoil from the band. No hop or forced turn against the loop. Rest **15 s between reps**, **60 s between legs**, then **90 s** before Section 3. | Give the free thigh a small resisted acceleration demand, isolating chamber initiation from E2's full kick. Keep the pelvis quiet and support foot secure. Reduce band tension for slow movement, toe snagging or trunk lean. If a suitable secure loop is unavailable, **use instead an unresisted split-stance knee drive at the same dose**; record the resistance change. |

**Anchor scoring and decisions:** In E2, a valid attempt has a coordinated support-foot/hip turn, no visibly trapped knee or lumbar wrench, a controlled low extension/recoil, and a **2 s finish without an extra step**. Record attempted and valid reps separately for each leg, plus the actual fault, stance, height and cue. A visibly slower attempt is a reason to extend recovery, not a measured percentage loss. The first round supplies the initial observation; the remaining rounds practice one coach-selected correction. Do not add attempts to improve a score.

**Quality rule for all six:** After a technical miss, pause, cue and reduce the relevant range/load for the remaining scheduled attempts. If control still fails after recovery, stop that exercise and record unperformed work. Pain, joint pinching, dizziness or an uncontrolled landing ends the task immediately. The exact-count document does not require an athlete to complete unsuitable work. If E2 can only be rehearsed slowly, record a technique-only exposure; do not label it explosive completion or assume readiness for the next class.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly 2 exercises.** Light bodyweight strengthening, approximately **RPE 3–4/10**, with at least five clean repetitions' reserve or comparable ease. These should not tire the legs before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Standing Hip Airplane Kick Prep** — hand-supported, small-range strengthening execution | **1 × 4 per stance leg = 8 reps.** Light fingertip support on a stable bench; shallow single-leg hinge. Slowly open the pelvis a small amount over the stance hip and return to square: **3 s out, 3 s return** is one rep. Foot stays planted and knee tracks toes. Rest **30 s between sides**, then **30 s**. | Apply low-load eccentric/concentric control around the support hip, limiting pelvic spill during the kick. Hand support removes balance as the limiting task. Shrink the arc or use light trailing-toe support instead if knee alignment or foot pressure changes; no forced end-range rotation. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill** — standing, both feet supported | **1 × 3 holds × 5 s per target foot = 6 holds / 30 foot-seconds.** Both feet remain supported; gently draw the target forefoot toward the heel without moving either contact or curling toes. Relax **5 s between holds**, allow **15 s to switch feet**, then **60 s** before Section 4. | Strengthen low-level arch control for a stable forefoot base as the support heel releases during the pivot. This is active foot-muscle work, not a calf raise. Reduce effort or **use the seated version instead**, same holds, if toes claw or the ankle rolls. |

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly 6 exercises.** Choose loads that make the final prescribed reps challenging while leaving **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**. No maximal testing, forced reps or grinding. Reps use roughly **2 s lowering, 1 s controlled lifting and 1 s to organize the next rep**, except P4's stated rotation tempo. Rest is mandatory; perform each exercise's work before moving on.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dumbbell Split Squat** — stationary, both feet on floor | **2 × 6 per lead leg = 24 working reps.** Dumbbells at sides; front foot flat, rear forefoot supported. Descend through a repeatable comfortable range and drive up through the lead leg. Rest **90 s between legs**, **120 s between rounds**, then **120 s**. | Main strength investment: stance-leg knee/hip extension for the planted push initiating the turn. The loaded rep stays square; do not twist the knee to imitate a kick. Reduce load/depth if pelvis rotates, front heel lifts or rear-leg push dominates. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift** — dumbbell, fingertip support | **1 × 6 per stance leg = 12 working reps.** One hand on a stable bench; hold the dumbbell in the other, weight near the stance leg. Soft knee, hips back, pelvis square; finish tall. Rest **90 s between legs**, then **120 s**. | Challenge stance-hip extension and posterior-chain control while the opposite leg moves. This loads a hinge rather than repeating P1's knee-dominant pattern. Stop the descent before the pelvis opens or back rounds; reduce load/range. **Use a kickstand RDL instead**, same working-leg dose, if suitable hand support is unavailable. |
| 3 | **Loaded Cossack Squat** — goblet dumbbell, qualified depth | **1 × 6 per loading side = 12 working reps.** Wide stance; sit toward the working hip while the opposite leg lengthens, then push back to center. Complete one side before switching. Rest **90 s between sides**, then **120 s**. | Supply lateral hip/adductor force through a controlled range for the plant and sweeping-leg recovery. Distinct from the fore–aft split squat; one set per side limits overlap with E4. Keep the loaded foot supported and knee aligned; reduce depth/load for groin pulling or pelvic collapse. Deep range is not mandatory. |
| 4 | **Seated band hip internal rotation** — proposed strength exercise | **1 × 8 per hip = 16 working reps.** Sit on a stable bench, hips/knees near 90°, thighs supported. A loop around the lower shins resists the working foot moving outward, lightly clear of the floor, while that knee stays in place; the other foot is firmly planted. **2 s outward rotation, 2 s controlled return.** Rest **60 s between sides**, then **60 s**. | Apply direct, controlled internal-rotation torque at the hip, complementing the gross leg push. Outward foot motion here represents inward thigh rotation. Use modest travel and enough band resistance for 2–3 clean RIR; keep pelvis still and avoid knee/ankle twisting. If the loop cannot be fitted, **use instead self-resisted seated hip internal rotation**, same reps/tempo, the opposite hand resisting the outside of the lower shin. Log manual resistance as a different, less reproducible load. |
| 5 | **Standing Cable or Band Hip Flexion** — band-only strength execution | **1 × 8 per lifting leg = 16 working reps.** Stable upright stance; loop at the forefeet secured by the support foot, optional fingertip bench support. Raise the bent knee to a comfortable chamber and lower without swinging. Rest **60 s between sides**, then **60 s**. | Challenge active chamber-lifting strength. E6 uses light resistance and rapid initiation; this slot uses greater relative resistance, controlled range and no momentum. Reduce tension/height if the pelvis tips or foot slips. If the loop cannot be secured, **use instead a self-resisted seated bent-knee hip-flexion lift**, same reps/tempo, pressing down on the distal thigh with the hands while it lifts. Log the substitute and technical effort. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise** — bilateral, bent knees | **2 × 8 = 16 bilateral working reps.** Stable bench, feet on floor and knees near 90°. Secure dumbbells on the distal thighs above the knees; raise heels without rolling outward and lower fully with a brief floor pause. Rest **90 s between sets**. | Load plantar-flexion force with a flexed knee for the support-foot rise and push accompanying the hip turn. Seated support allows challenging calf loading without making balance the limiter. Reduce load for bouncing or shortened heel travel. If the bench fit is unsuitable, **use instead a supported standing bent-knee calf raise with a secured dumbbell**, same bilateral dose; record the changed position. |

**Load-preparation sets, separate from working sets:** P1: **1 × 4/leg**; P2: **1 × 3/leg**; P3: **1 × 3/side**; P4: **1 × 4/side**; P5: **1 × 3/leg**; P6: **1 × 5 bilateral**. Use bodyweight or lighter resistance in the already listed movement. For P1–P3 allow **30 s between preparation sides and 60 s before working work**; for P4–P5 **20 s between preparation sides and 45 s before working work**; P6 **60 s before working work**. Add another small ramp in that same exercise only when necessary to select a qualified load, and log its work/time. These sets do not replace Access & Prepare 1.

**Strength decisions:** If the last reps lose the defined position or leave fewer than two clean reps, reduce the next working load or range. If the set is too easy, use only a small controllable adjustment within the remaining planned work; do not add repetitions or test failure. For P4/P5 manual replacements, resistance must stay repeatable through each rep and permit a credible reserve estimate. Pain or inability to find a technically sound challenging version ends that slot; record the incomplete prescription.

**Delivery and timing:** Allow approximately **83–90 minutes for this prescribed work**, including the modeled rests, stated load-preparation sets, demonstrations, equipment adjustment and recording, with prompt equipment access. Access & Prepare 1, extra ramps, longer recovery and group queues are additional. The full session duration is unresolved. A 60-minute total booking cannot fit this version; a 90-minute total booking is not established as feasible. Use a confirmed cleared section within a 10 m lane, with separate waiting space outside the sweep/landing area. Lane widths, bench fit, band loops, class size and staffing must be checked; one lane being long enough does not establish group capacity. See the [delivery audit](../supporting/class_01_audit.md).

**Scheduling:** This is a demanding lower-body exposure, including adductors, hip flexors, calves and unilateral lifting. Review actual sport/kicking, gym, other focus classes and separate tumbling before placement. Approximately **48–72 hours** before another comparably demanding lower-body session is a starting consideration, adjusted for observed recovery and the actual schedule. Different curriculum names do not justify stacking their prescriptions. No calendar week or completed attendance is implied.
