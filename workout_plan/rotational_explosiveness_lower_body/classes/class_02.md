# Lower-Body Rotational Explosiveness — Class 2 of 12

**Primary effort:** Shift support onto the rear leg and launch a low lead-leg roundhouse without an extra preparatory step. Exact framework: **Rotational explosiveness > Lower-body emphasis (rotational kicks)**.

**Foundation and reviewed evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, supervised by a coach qualified to teach the listed kick/plant actions. Reviewed Class 1, the saved progression/workload and drill records, and athlete_feedback.md. Prior work is **prescribed, not observed**; attendance, loads, response and all other training remain unknown. This is a conditional prescription, not an earned higher level or a consecutive calendar day.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The limiting demand is unloading the front leg while retaining a usable rear support-foot pivot. The other drills supply stance exchange, forward rotational projection, same-leg lateral support, proximal thigh rotation and a grounded forward plant; none adds a contact target. **E2 is today's anchor.** Marker: valid E2 attempts out of four per kicking leg, with no gather step, a coordinated rear support-foot turn and a two-second recovered stance. Observe only within its scheduled attempts; no extra test block. Record actual variant, stance, side, range and attempted/valid reps. A different drill is not a directly comparable force/speed test.

## 2. Explosiveness

**Exactly 6 exercises.** All are single drills, including any natural entry/recoil in a kick; integrated flights and kick actions are separately counted in the audit. Bodyweight unless specified. Brisk/explosive intent means rapid controlled force expression, not rushed recovery or maximal range. Mirror the stated working side. For every kick: foot below hip height, knee never snapped into lockout, active recoil, no pad/contact, jumping/spinning kick or unplanned extension. For every turn, feet and knees follow the pelvis; no twisting a fixed knee. Finish each attempt still for 2 s unless the row specifies linked contacts.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split-stance scissor hop to stance freeze** | **1 × 2 per starting lead leg = 4 attempts.** From a shallow split stance, make one low jump that exchanges front/rear feet; land in the mirrored split stance and hold 2 s. No rotation or second bounce. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, then **60 s** before the next entry/section. | Practice transferring which leg supports the next kick without a long ground shuffle. Keep the jump low and both knees soft; shorten the stance exchange if the landing crosses the feet or requires another hop. |
| 2 | **Lead-leg low roundhouse without a gather step** — primary anchor | **2 × 2 per kicking leg = 8 attempts.** Start with the kicking leg in front. Shift onto the rear support leg, allow its foot to pivot, then execute one low roundhouse with a soft terminal knee, active recoil and a return to the starting stance. Do not shuffle the rear foot forward first. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, **90 s between rounds**, then **90 s** before the next entry/section. | Directly train front-leg release while the rear leg supplies the rotational base. Use a lower chamber/shorter extension instead if the athlete needs an extra gather or leans away; do not speed up a trapped rear-foot pivot. |
| 3 | **Rotational Broad Jump to Stick** | **1 × 2 per turn direction = 4 attempts.** Use a two-foot dip and jump a short distance forward while turning no more than 45 degrees in the air. Land on two feet already aligned with the finish; hold 2 s. Flight fits within 2 m, with 2 m clear beyond. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, then **90 s** before the next entry/section. | Express leg force forward while organizing the pelvis for a changed facing direction. Shorten travel or turn if rotation continues after landing; do not chase maximal distance. |
| 4 | **Lateral support-leg hop to same-leg stick** | **1 × 2 per stance leg = 4 attempts.** Hop a short distance sideways from one foot and land on that same foot, maintaining the original facing direction. Free knee stays comfortably bent; hold 2 s. Keep displacement within 1 m. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, then **90 s** before the next entry/section. | Keep body mass over the same supporting leg when the opposite leg is unavailable for a catch step. Use a smaller hop instead for pelvic drop or a rescue touchdown; do not add rotation. |
| 5 | **Pre-chambered bent-knee roundhouse whip** | **1 × 2 per moving leg = 4 attempts.** Begin with the free thigh already lifted below hip height and knee folded. Briskly sweep the thigh through a small roundhouse arc as the support foot turns; keep the knee folded throughout, then return the foot to stance. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, then **90 s** before the next entry/section. | Isolate proximal thigh/pelvis acceleration without the initial lifting or knee-extension task. Lower the starting chamber or reduce arc for trunk lean; the standing knee must turn with the foot, not remain trapped. |
| 6 | **Forward plant-and-open to kick stance** | **1 × 2 per receiving leg = 4 attempts.** Take one brisk short forward step onto the receiving leg and open the feet/pelvis into a shallow split kick stance, no more than 45 degrees from the initial facing. Keep the step and finish inside 2 m; no kick or run-up. Rest **20 s between attempts**, **60 s between sides/directions**, then **90 s** before the next entry/section. | Accept forward arrival onto a leg before freeing the other thigh for a kick. Shorten the step if the athlete overstrides, stiffens the knee or drags a planted foot through the turn. |

**Rest counting:** The between-attempt interval applies within one side-bout; the stated side-switch or round interval replaces it at that boundary. Do not add both intervals to the same gap. A one-rep side-bout has no within-side gap.

**Readiness and stop rules:** Lead-leg release must be possible without hopping or a forced lean. The new same-leg hop uses small amplitude and no turn; if it cannot be controlled, use instead a very small same-leg hop with a light trailing-toe touch at the finish, and record the assisted landing. First scheduled attempts may use the stated smaller-range replacement; they still count. After a miss, extend recovery and apply the row's correction to remaining attempts. Repeated loss of control means stop that slot and log omitted work; pain, joint pinching, dizziness or an uncontrolled landing stops the task immediately. If only slow rehearsal is possible, record technique-only work rather than explosive completion. Never force fourteen completed slots for an unready athlete.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience)

**Exactly 2 exercises.** Light bodyweight strengthening, approximately **RPE 3–4/10**, with at least five clean repetitions' reserve or comparable ease. These should not tire the legs before primary strength.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Standing Hip Airplane Kick Prep** — hand-supported, small-range strengthening execution | **1 × 4 per stance leg = 8 reps.** Light fingertip support on a stable bench; shallow single-leg hinge. Slowly open the pelvis a small amount over the stance hip and return to square: **3 s out, 3 s return** is one rep. Foot stays planted and knee tracks toes. Rest **30 s between sides**, then **30 s**. | Apply low-load eccentric/concentric control around the support hip, limiting pelvic spill during the kick. Hand support removes balance as the limiting task. Shrink the arc or use light trailing-toe support instead if knee alignment or foot pressure changes; no forced end-range rotation. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill** — standing, both feet supported | **1 × 3 holds × 5 s per target foot = 6 holds / 30 foot-seconds.** Both feet remain supported; gently draw the target forefoot toward the heel without moving either contact or curling toes. Relax **5 s between holds**, allow **15 s to switch feet**, then **60 s** before Section 4. | Strengthen low-level arch control for a stable forefoot base as the support heel releases during the pivot. This is active foot-muscle work, not a calf raise. Reduce effort or **use the seated version instead**, same holds, if toes claw or the ankle rolls. |

**Today's light-strength jobs:** The supported airplane controls the rear support hip as weight shifts back; short-foot holds keep that foot organized before its heel releases. Retaining these two low-load strength tasks is intentional: preserve controllable hip/foot capacity while the explosive tasks change. They must remain easy.

## 4. Strength (Primary)

**Exactly 6 exercises.** All working sets should be challenging with **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**. Approximately 2 s lower, 1 s controlled lift and 1 s reset per rep; P4 uses 2 s outward foot motion/2 s return. No maximal tests, technical failure, timed circuits or unstable loaded pivots. Retain a reproducible stance/range for each recurring lift; select actual load from that athlete's current execution, not a written earlier number.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dumbbell Split Squat** — stationary, both feet on floor | **2 × 6 per leg/side = 24 working reps.** Dumbbells at sides; front foot flat, rear forefoot supported. Descend through a repeatable comfortable range and drive up through the lead leg. Rest **90 s between sides**, **120 s between rounds**, then **120 s**. | Retain lead-leg push capacity for the support exchange; the planted lift stays square rather than imitating a kick. Reduce load/depth if pelvis rotates, front heel lifts or rear-leg push dominates. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift** — dumbbell, fingertip support | **1 × 6 per leg/side = 12 working reps.** One hand on a stable bench; hold the dumbbell in the other, weight near the stance leg. Soft knee, hips back, pelvis square; finish tall. Rest **90 s between sides**, then **120 s**. | Load the hip hinge that maintains the rear support leg while the front thigh accelerates. Stop descent before the pelvis opens or back rounds; reduce load/range. If hand support is unsuitable, use instead a kickstand RDL at the same side dose. |
| 3 | **Loaded Cossack Squat** — goblet dumbbell, qualified depth | **1 × 6 per leg/side = 12 working reps.** Wide stance; sit toward the working hip while the opposite leg lengthens, then push back to center. Complete one side before switching. Rest **90 s between sides**, then **120 s**. | Supply lateral hip/adductor force to keep the support pelvis organized during lead-leg release. Keep the loaded foot supported and knee aligned; reduce depth/load for groin pulling or pelvic collapse. Deep range is not mandatory. |
| 4 | **Seated band hip internal rotation** — proposed strength exercise | **1 × 8 per leg/side = 16 working reps.** Sit on a stable bench, hips/knees near 90°, thighs supported. A loop around the lower shins resists the working foot moving outward, lightly clear of the floor, while that knee stays in place; the other foot is firmly planted. **2 s outward rotation, 2 s controlled return.** Rest **60 s between sides**, then **60 s**. | Challenge controlled hip-turn torque without adding another whole-body spinning task. Use modest rotation, enough resistance for 2–3 clean RIR, and a quiet knee/pelvis. If a secure loop cannot be fitted, use instead self-resisted seated hip internal rotation: opposite hand resisting the outside of the lower shin, same reps/tempo. Log manual resistance as less reproducible. |
| 5 | **Standing Cable or Band Hip Flexion** — band-only strength execution | **1 × 8 per leg/side = 16 working reps.** Stable upright stance; loop at the forefeet secured by the support foot, optional fingertip bench support. Raise the bent knee to a comfortable chamber and lower without swinging. Rest **60 s between sides**, then **60 s**. | Load the active lift of the front-leg chamber, independently of its rapid arc. Reduce tension/height if the pelvis tips or foot slips. If the loop cannot be secured, use instead self-resisted seated bent-knee hip flexion, hands resisting the distal thigh, same reps/tempo. Log manual resistance as less reproducible. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise** — bilateral, bent knees | **2 × 8 bilateral = 16 working reps.** Stable bench, feet on floor and knees near 90°. Secure dumbbells on the distal thighs above the knees; raise heels without rolling outward and lower fully with a brief floor pause. Rest **90 s between sets**. | Maintain bent-knee ankle drive during the short support shift and pivot. Reduce load for bouncing or shortened heel travel. If bench fit is unsuitable, use instead supported standing bent-knee calf raises with a secured dumbbell, same bilateral dose; log the changed position. |

**Lighter load-preparation sets, additional to working sets:** P1: 1 × 4/side; P2: 1 × 3/side; P3: 1 × 3/side; P4: 1 × 4/side; P5: 1 × 3/side; P6: 1 × 5 bilateral (**39 reps total**). Use the same listed movement with bodyweight or lighter resistance. P1–P3: 30 s between preparation sides, 60 s before working work; P4–P5: 20 s between preparation sides, 45 s before working work; P6: 60 s before working work. Extra small ramps are allowed only to qualify that exercise's load and must be logged with their time. This is not a replacement preparation sequence. If manual resistance is used, preserve range/tempo and a credible reserve; do not claim a precise load progression from it.

**Strength progression decision:** Retain Class 1 set allocation [2,1,1,1,1,2], 96 working reps and the same load-preparation dose. New front-leg timing, not heavier strength work, is the change. A small load increase on a recurring lift is optional only with reported completed clean work at the target reserve, acceptable recovery and current readiness. Missing feedback means hold the loading intent and use the current qualified load; no automatic heavier weights. Reduce load/range for technique loss; stop for pain. Do not increase load, range and sets together.

**Workload:** 12 planned flight/landings (**8 bilateral, 4 unilateral**); **16 grounded explosive efforts**; **8 kick actions** (kick actions are a task count, not extra attempts to add). Twelve landings: four scissor exchanges, four bilateral rotational broad jumps and four same-leg hops. The remaining sixteen efforts stay grounded; eight of those are kicks. Scissor landings are bilateral split-stance events, not two jumps. Primary strength: **8 rounds / 14 separate working bouts / 96 reps**, plus 39 lighter preparation reps. Stabilization: 8 slow cycles plus six 5 s foot holds. Failed attempts still add exposure. Unintended hops, landing corrections and actual entry/return steps are logged separately; no equivalent-contact safety score is used.

**Delivery and timing:** Approximately **78–85 minutes outside Access & Prepare 1**, with prompt equipment access. The audit models actual prescribed resets, rests, both sides, preparation sets and 13 minutes for instruction/setup/recording. Group queues, extra coaching, ramps and longer recovery are additional. Full session duration is unresolved; do not assume a 60- or 90-minute total booking fits. Each moving drill stays inside a 2 m work segment plus 2 m clear recovery in one 10 m lane; same-leg hops use at most 1 m. Stage other athletes outside the sweep/landing footprint. Confirm lane width, swept-leg clearance, waiting/return areas, bench fit, secure band loops and staffing. Run each athlete's four sections in order; equipment rotation is not permission to do strength before explosiveness. No throwing wall, pad, cable machine, external band anchor, rack or device capability is presumed.

**Recovery and placement:** Review actual recent/upcoming kicking, sport, gym, other focus classes and separately programmed tumbling. Around 48–72 hours before another comparably demanding lower-body exposure is an initial consideration, adjusted for real workload and recovery. These are numbered classes, not twelve consecutive training days. A different focus name does not remove overlapping adductor, calf or hip loading.

It lowers grounded drill volume while retaining twelve flight events and strength exposure. No improvement or greater tolerance is assumed. New drills supply the stated learning job, not evidence of improved capacity. Later classes remain conditional on their own prerequisites.
