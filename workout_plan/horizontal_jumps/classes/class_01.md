# Horizontal Jumps — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Project the body forward from a standing two-foot takeoff with a coordinated dip and arm swing, then own the landing. Maximize the distance that can be finished under control.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique and qualified supervision. This starts a curriculum, not necessarily the athlete's training history. Actual recent work, readiness, lifting loads and response are unknown; confirm suitability on the day.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The limiting demand is directing a coordinated hip–knee–ankle push into forward travel without losing the landing. E2 is the primary performance anchor. The other explosive jobs are a small forward ankle rebound, force from stillness, each leg's takeoff, linking two horizontal contacts, and externally loaded forward projection. Light control work supports knee/pelvic and foot position; strength supplies bilateral extension, a loaded hinge, each lead leg's force, terminal hip extension, bent-knee plantarflexion and knee-flexor control. The important constraints are fresh anchor practice, the existing 10 m lanes and the cumulative lower-body load.

**Quality marker within E2:** Record valid attempts out of the **six prescribed attempts**. Valid means one natural-arm countermovement, simultaneous two-foot takeoff, forward travel and a two-foot landing held for **2 seconds** without a corrective step or backward hand touch. Record what failed as well as what succeeded. If a reliable distance-measuring method is actually available, record takeoff line to the rear edge of the nearer heel for each valid attempt; otherwise record quality only. Keep start, shoes, surface, arm policy and measurement method constant. Do not add test attempts or compare different jump variants as the same test.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use fast, purposeful efforts with full resets. E2 receives the greatest allocation of demanding isolated jump practice. Athlete demonstrations/rehearsals count within the written dose; coach demonstrations add no athlete reps.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Low-amplitude forward-traveling pogos** — proposed addition | **1 × 8 jumps**, bilateral, bodyweight; travel no more than **2 m total**. Small, quick contacts rather than maximum distance. **60 s before E2.** | Rehearse ankle-driven rebound while the body travels forward, preparing the contact demand used in E5 without tiring the main jump. Keep knees soft, feet beneath the body and travel increments small. Reduce travel if contacts become deep squats or loud slaps; stop for lost rhythm or discomfort. |
| 2 | **Broad Jump to Stick — natural arm swing (primary anchor)** | **3 × 2 attempts = 6 jumps.** High takeoff intent within controlled landing capability. **30–45 s between attempts; 120 s between sets and before E3.** | Integrate forward projection: dip once, send the arms forward/up with leg extension, then bring both feet into the landing and hold for 2 s. Avoid reaching the feet far ahead of an unsupported torso. Reduce distance if the athlete cannot hold the finish; retain the same arm/start protocol. |
| 3 | **Paused Broad Jump to Stick — hands on hips** — proposed addition | **2 × 2 = 4 jumps.** Hold a comfortable quarter-to-half squat motionless for **2 s**, then push forward rapidly with no new dip. **30 s between attempts; 120 s between sets; 90 s before E4.** | Produce forward force from stillness, separating the leg push from a countermovement and arm contribution. Hands remain on hips; land on two feet and hold 2 s. Shorten distance/start depth if needed. A re-dip invalidates the static-start task; do not make up the attempt. |
| 4 | **Single-leg Broad Jump to Two-foot Landing** — proposed addition | **1 × 2 per takeoff leg = 4 jumps**. Brisk takeoff over a controlled, submaximal distance. Alternate **L–R–L–R**; **30–45 s between attempts**, at least 60 s between attempts on the same leg; **90 s before E5.** | Make each leg supply forward propulsion while retaining the simpler two-foot landing. Keep the free foot off the floor at takeoff, use natural arms, land with both feet and hold 2 s. Reduce travel if pelvis/knee control deteriorates; do not chase equal distances or add weaker-side reps. |
| 5 | **Repeated Broad Jump — two linked jumps** | **2 × 1 sequence of 2 jumps = 4 jumps/landings**. Fast push, modest travel; **120 s between sequences; 90 s before E6.** | Receive forward momentum and use the intermediate bilateral contact to project again. Link without an intentional pause, allow the knee/hip bend the task needs, then stick the second landing for 2 s. Do not chase the shortest possible contact. Use only with demonstrated linked-jump competence; see replacement below. |
| 6 | **Forward Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — light, bilateral** | **2 × 3 releases = 6 throws**. **30 s between releases** including safe retrieval; **90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Accelerate a small external load forward through rapid hip/knee extension and a two-hand scoop. Start facing forward, ball low in front; use one shallow dip, drive and release forward/slightly upward without twisting, stepping or jumping. Choose a load that stays fast without back arching or an arm-only heave. Do not catch the return. |

**Space and exercise replacements:** Reserve one 10 m lane at a time; no crossing traffic or simultaneous retrieval. Start jumps near the beginning. For E2–E4, keep travel/landing within the first 6 m and the remaining 4 m clear; for E5, both jumps and the final hold must fit within those 6 m. These are proposed operating boundaries, not measured athlete capability. Verify width, footing, stopping/fall clearance and the waiting/return position. Return only after the active athlete has stopped and the lane is released. Scale supporting travel to the available envelope; do not sprint between efforts.

If forward pogo travel is poorly controlled, **use Ankle Pogo in Place instead of E1**, 1 × 8 with the same rest; this preserves the ankle-rebound job but removes travel. If E5 linking is unfamiliar or unsuitable today, **use a countermovement Broad Jump to Stick with hands fixed on hips instead of E5**, 2 × 2, 30 s between attempts, 120 s between sets and 90 s before E6. This proposed replacement isolates countermovement leg drive without arms; it differs from E3's motionless start. Keep travel modest. It retains four landings but removes intermediate rebound contacts; log which exercise was used.

E6 requires verified containment of the entire flight, bounce/roll and retrieval inside an exclusive throwing area; the lanes alone do not prove this. If that cannot be provided, **use a Two-hand Kettlebell Swing instead of E6: 2 × 3 complete swings**, light and fast, 90 s between sets and before Section 3. Use a familiar two-hand hinge swing to comfortable lower-chest height, feet planted, arms guiding the bell and no overhead finish; stop after three swings and park it under control. Use only with demonstrated swing and handling competence and a clear swing envelope. This proposed replacement supplies rapid hip-driven implement acceleration with no planned landing, but less direct arm/forward-projection practice than the toss. It replaces six throws with six swings. If neither version is suitable, record the unperformed slot and resolve it before a future full delivery; do not invent extra jumps.

**Quality/recovery rule:** After a clear decline, add 30–60 s before the next scheduled attempt. Reduce intent/travel when needed; end that exercise if quality still fails to recover. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness or an unsafe surface/space. Invalid attempts still count as exposure. The fourteen prescribed slots never obligate an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Light, controlled strengthening: leave at least **5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or comparable ease on holds. No burning, shaking or pre-fatigue target.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Step-Down to Hover — low forward heel-hover execution** | **1 × 4 per leg = 8 reps**, bodyweight or a light dumbbell. Lower **3 s**, hover the free heel just above the floor, return **1 s**. **30 s between legs; 45 s before S2.** | Lightly strengthen stance-leg knee/hip control as the body lowers toward the position used to absorb a forward landing. Keep the whole stance foot supported, pelvis level and knee tracking with the foot; do not push from the free heel. Reduce height/load for knee collapse or pelvic drop. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill — standing, one target foot at a time** | **1 × 4 holds of 5 s per foot = 8 holds/40 foot-seconds.** Both feet remain on the floor; shift only enough weight to control the target arch. **5 s relaxed between holds; 30 s between feet; 60 s before primary preparation sets.** | Strengthen active foot-arch control while maintaining heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact for the takeoff push and landing. Shorten the target foot gently without clawing toes, rolling the ankle or lifting the forefoot. Reduce effort if the toes grip or the foot cramps. |

If no suitably low step is available, **use a hand-supported single-leg shallow squat instead of S1**, 1 × 4 per leg with the same tempo/rest. Keep the free foot clear and support stable; proposed replacement, not additional work. For S2, use seated one-target-foot doming instead of standing if needed, preserving the same hold/side counts and rests. Neither exercise is a balance test.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Select working load or, for P6, lever length so the prescribed work is **challenging with approximately 2–3 technically sound reps/cycles in reserve**. Use observed control plus the athlete's report; RIR is an estimate. Lower under control and apply purposeful force on the return without jumping. No maximum-load testing, forced reps or grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Goblet Squat — dumbbell** | **3 × 6 working reps.** **3 s down**, purposeful stand. **120 s between working sets and before P2 preparation.** | Main bilateral strength investment: challenge combined knee/hip extension while keeping the trunk and whole foot organized. Use a repeatable comfortable depth; the lift develops force capacity rather than copying the jump angle. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee collapse or trunk loss. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **2 × 6 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 120 s between working sets and before P3 preparation.** | Strengthen hip extension from a flexed-hip position with posterior-chain tension, complementing the squat's larger knee excursion. Keep the bells close and knees softly bent; stop the descent before the back rounds or pelvis tucks. Reduce load/range rather than reaching for the floor. |
| 3 | **Split Squat — stationary, two dumbbells** | **1 × 6 per lead leg = 12 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 60 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Give each lead leg a challenging force contribution for E4 using a stable base. Keep both feet planted with rear heel raised and enough stance width to control the pelvis. One working set per leg limits overlap with the main squat. Reduce load if balance replaces force production. |
| 4 | **Hip Thrust — dumbbell across pelvis, upper back on bench** | **1 × 8 working reps.** **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. 90 s before P5 preparation.** | Challenge hip extension near the finish with less trunk-holding demand than the RDL. Secure the load with the hands; finish with ribs and pelvis aligned, not lumbar arching. One challenging set supplies this distinct range emphasis without another large hinge workout. |
| 5 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **2 × 10 working reps**, both legs together. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. 90 s between working sets and before P6 preparation.** | Strengthen ankle plantarflexion with the knee flexed, supporting force transmission through the foot during the dip-to-forward-drive transition and linked contact. Keep knee angle steady, loads secured above the knees and heels returning quietly to the floor. Reduce load for shortened heel travel or bouncing. |
| 6 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 full out-and-back cycles**, bodyweight at a challenging controlled lever. Each cycle: **2 small outward steps per foot, then 2 inward per foot**; approximately **1 s per step**. | Load the knee flexors as the heels move away and return while the pelvis stays lifted, complementing P2's hip-hinge emphasis. Start in a floor bridge; keep a slight knee bend at the furthest point and ribs down. Choose step lengths that leave 2–3 controlled cycles in reserve. Shorten the lever for pelvic sag/cramping; stop if control cannot be restored. |

**Preparation sets — additional workload for these same exercises, not working sets:** P1: **1 × 5 light, then 1 × 3 moderate**, rest 60 s between and 120 s before working. P2: **1 × 4 light**, then 60 s. P3: **1 × 3 light per leg**, 15 s to switch legs, then 60 s. P4: **1 × 4 light**, then 60 s. P5: **1 × 5 light**, then 45 s. P6: **1 short-lever out-and-back cycle**, then 60 s. Keep all preparation easy. Log any additional ramp sets and time required.

If a goblet load is limited by holding capacity before the legs are challenged, **use Double Front-Rack Squat — dumbbells instead of P1**, retaining its working/preparation doses and rests, only with competent rack handling. If the bench is unsuitable, **use Dumbbell Glute Bridge on the floor instead of P4**, with the same sets/reps/rest; record the different support/range. A suitable sandbag may replace the pelvic dumbbell. Actual load ranges, setup fit and secure support must be checked. Do not work to failure to compensate for insufficient equipment.

If the technical limit arrives early, stop the set and lower the next load/lever demand. Extend recovery when needed. Missing or reduced work belongs in the actual log; do not make it up in a finisher.
