## Selection decisions and revisions

| Consequential choice | Decision, reason and resulting change |
|---|---|
| Main jump placement | Put E2 immediately after just eight low pogos. The paused and unilateral support work follows it, so the highest-priority integrated task is not performed after several tiring drills. Six fresh attempts provide the repeatable anchor. |
| Paused vs another natural-arm broad jump | Use hands-on-hips, motionless-start E3 to train force from stillness. A second dose of the identical natural-arm task would duplicate E2 without earning another slot. |
| Each leg's propulsion | Choose single-leg takeoff to two-foot landing, not a same-leg landing hop or long alternating bounds. This isolates propulsion with only two takeoffs per side and avoids silently adding unilateral landing demand. Suitability still requires observed technique. |
| Linked jump dose | Limit E5 to two sequences of two modest jumps: only two intermediate rebound contacts. Triple broad jumps, long bounds and depth-to-broad jumps add contacts, travel or eccentric demand that Class 1 does not need. Link only when competence is established; otherwise the explicit countermovement/no-arms replacement has a separate useful job. |
| External power exercise | A forward scoop toss closely matches forward leg-to-arm projection and adds no planned landing. Throw containment is unconfirmed, so a fully dosed swing replacement is provided; this sacrifices some task similarity but retains rapid hip-driven external-load acceleration within a verified swing envelope. No catch or throw-return drill is hidden in E6. |
| Light strengthening | Use low step-down control and submaximal target-foot doming instead of a generic abdominal/balance pairing. Eight dynamic reps and forty target-foot hold-seconds limit fatigue before primary work. No mobility-only slot is counted as strength. |
| Squat/hinge selection | Use a goblet squat and two-DB RDL as strength anchors; no rack is assumed. The squat supplies bilateral knee/hip extension, while the RDL uses more hip flexion with limited knee excursion. Three squat sets and two RDL sets receive the main strength investment. |
| Remaining compound overlap | Reduce split squat to one challenging set per leg and hip thrust to one set. These retain unilateral contribution and terminal hip-extension jobs without four high-volume compound lifts after jumping. |
| Sixth strength role | Choose walkout knee-flexor work instead of a second calf exercise or another hinge. RDL loads a hip hinge; hip thrust emphasizes hip extension near the top; walkout holds the pelvis while knee angle changes. This is purposeful overlap, but still cumulative hamstring/glute work; keep walkouts to one challenging set and monitor recovery. |
| Class-to-class progression | Do not prescribe an automatic volume/load increase after a written class. Class 2's job is better repeatability; later changes require actual execution and recovery evidence. The map is reassessed, not twelve completed tables. |

## Workload and recovery findings

The default class contains **26 bilateral landing events / 52 landing-foot contacts**, differentiated as 8 low traveling pogos, 6 natural-arm broad jumps, 4 static-start broad jumps, 4 unilateral takeoffs to two feet and 4 linked broad jumps. Unilateral takeoffs total 2 per leg; there are no unilateral landings. Six throws are recorded separately. No sprint, approach run, depth drop, finisher or tumbling block is added.

Primary strength contains **10 working rounds / 11 bouts**: 8 compound bouts, 2 calf bouts and 1 walkout bout. There are 70 conventional working reps and 4 eight-step walkout cycles. Seven preparation rounds/eight bouts add 27 conventional reps and one walkout cycle. Stabilization adds 8 dynamic reps and 8 five-second holds, with low effort. All sides and secondary grounded walkout steps are explicit in the [ledger](progression_ledger.md).

The principal cumulative demands are quadriceps/glute work from jumping plus squat/split squat, hamstring work from RDL/thrust/walkout, and foot/calf work from pogos, jumping, doming and seated raises. Their distinct roles do not erase overlap. Reducing support volume, retaining full rest and making no unilateral landing/depth-drop progression is the current response to that overlap. No arbitrary contact count certifies this class as safe or optimal.

Primary sets require approximately 2–3 technical RIR; stabilization remains easy. P6 uses lever selection rather than a token bodyweight dose. Unknown equipment load ranges may limit useful challenge; P1/P4 have implement/support replacements, and unsuitable loads must be resolved rather than concealed with fatigue or unstable exercise complexity. If performance deteriorates despite rest, actual completion must be reduced/stopped and recorded.

The rough 48–72-hour recovery consideration is contextual. Actual other training, soreness, readiness, sleep/recovery report and schedule govern delivery. The neighboring Vertical Jumps prescriptions share many tissues and lifts; writing those classes does not mean they were performed or recovered from. Access & Prepare 1 may add meaningful impacts/strength and must be included when its content is available.

## Timing calculation

The reference model is **one athlete with a qualified coach**, familiar exercise skills, equipment made available serially and no queue. It includes every listed within-set reset, between-set rest, side switch, exit rest, prescribed load-preparation set and a modest demonstration/handling allowance. Movement time is an estimate, not a cadence imposed on ballistic takeoff. Extra resets, failed setup, coaching needs or added ramp sets extend the clock.

Explosiveness: **1,777 s / 29.6 min**. Stabilization: **307 s / 5.1 min**. Primary including preparation: **2,168 s / 36.1 min**. Add **180 s** for section reconfiguration, water and final recording beyond the listed row allowances: **4,432 s / 73.9 min** after Access & Prepare 1. Choosing 45 rather than 30 s between E2/E4 attempts adds 90 s. Thus **70–80 minutes is a planning envelope around the calculated 74–75 minutes**, not a guaranteed deadline or permission to compress rest.

Ball retrieval must fit the release-recovery interval; retrieve only after it settles and the lane is released. If retrieval takes longer, extend the interval. No athlete retrieval is counted as active rest while someone else throws through the same area. The E5 no-arms replacement adds about 74 s (four separate held efforts and their within-set resets replace two linked sequences); it remains within the upper estimate absent other delays. Replacements and additional ramp sets can change the actual timing.

**Group model, conditional:** For three athletes sharing one movement lane and one coached lifting station, rotate A–B–C through the same exercise, with individually suitable loads. Finish each section before moving on; do not send other athletes into heavy unsupervised stations. Example E2: if a jump/clearance turn needs 10 s, the first attempts can start A 0/B 10/C 20, the second A 60/B 70/C 80. Next set can start A 190/B 200/C 210, preserving each athlete's minimum 120 s after the preceding attempt. This only works if measured turnaround, supervision and readiness support it. Record recovery per athlete; waiting that already supplies recovery is not added again as another timer.

Across primary work, two additional athletes add roughly 14 minutes of execution alone (before extra handling and excluding time that truly fits existing rest). Much explosive waiting can supply recovery; load changes, preparation and technique checks still consume time. Reserve approximately **85–105 minutes after preparation** for this small shared-station example and validate the actual rotation before booking. It is not a confirmed group size, a promise for a larger class or proof that three lanes can run concurrently under one coach.

No booked duration, coach count or equipment quantities were supplied. Complete class time is **actual Access & Prepare 1 duration + actual Sections 2–4 duration**, presently unresolved. The current prescription cannot fit a 60-minute total booking. If a time limit is later supplied, revise doses/flow while retaining meaningful 6/2/6 work or explicitly report the remaining conflict. Do not silently drop a slot or rush recovery.
