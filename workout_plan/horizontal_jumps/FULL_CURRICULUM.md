# Horizontal Jumps — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Project the body forward from a standing two-foot takeoff with a coordinated dip and arm swing, then own the landing. Maximize the distance that can be finished under control.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique and qualified supervision. This starts a curriculum, not necessarily the athlete's training history. Actual recent work, readiness, lifting loads and response are unknown; confirm suitability on the day.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The limiting demand is directing a coordinated hip–knee–ankle push into forward travel without losing the landing. E2 is the primary performance anchor. The other explosive jobs are a small forward ankle rebound, force from stillness, each leg's takeoff, linking two horizontal contacts, and externally loaded forward projection. Light control work supports knee/pelvic and foot position; strength supplies bilateral extension, a loaded hinge, each lead leg's force, terminal hip extension, bent-knee plantarflexion and knee-flexor control. The important constraints are fresh anchor practice, the existing 10 m lanes and the cumulative lower-body load.

**Quality marker within E2:** Record valid attempts out of the **six prescribed attempts**. Valid means one natural-arm countermovement, simultaneous two-foot takeoff, forward travel and a two-foot landing held for **2 seconds** without a corrective step or backward hand touch. Record what failed as well as what succeeded. If a reliable distance-measuring method is actually available, record takeoff line to the rear edge of the nearer heel for each valid attempt; otherwise record quality only. Keep start, shoes, surface, arm policy and measurement method constant. Do not add test attempts or compare different jump variants as the same test.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use fast, purposeful efforts with full resets. E2 receives the greatest allocation of demanding isolated jump practice. Athlete demonstrations/rehearsals count within the written dose; coach demonstrations add no athlete reps.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Low-amplitude forward-traveling pogos** — proposed addition | **1 × 8 jumps**, bilateral, bodyweight; travel no more than **2 m total**. | 2 | **Broad Jump to Stick — natural arm swing (primary anchor)** | **3 × 2 attempts = 6 jumps.** High takeoff intent within controlled landing capability. **30–45 s between attempts; 120 s between sets and before E3.** | Integrate forward projection: dip once, send the arms forward/up with leg extension, then bring both feet into the landing and hold for 2 s. | 3 | **Paused Broad Jump to Stick — hands on hips** — proposed addition | **2 × 2 = 4 jumps.** Hold a comfortable quarter-to-half squat motionless for **2 s**, then push forward rapidly with no new dip. **30 s between attempts; 120 s between sets; 90 s before E4.** | Produce forward force from stillness, separating the leg push from a countermovement and arm contribution. | 4 | **Single-leg Broad Jump to Two-foot Landing** — proposed addition | **1 × 2 per takeoff leg = 4 jumps**. Brisk takeoff over a controlled, submaximal distance. | 5 | **Repeated Broad Jump — two linked jumps** | **2 × 1 sequence of 2 jumps = 4 jumps/landings**. | 6 | **Forward Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — light, bilateral** | **2 × 3 releases = 6 throws**. **30 s between releases** including safe retrieval; **90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Accelerate a small external load forward through rapid hip/knee extension and a two-hand scoop. **Space and exercise replacements:** Reserve one 10 m lane at a time; no crossing traffic or simultaneous retrieval. Start jumps near the beginning. For E2–E4, keep travel/landing within the first 6 m and the remaining 4 m clear; for E5, both jumps and the final hold must fit within those 6 m. These are proposed operating boundaries, not measured athlete capability. Verify width, footing, stopping/fall clearance and the waiting/return position. Return only after the active athlete has stopped and the lane is released. Scale supporting travel to the available envelope; do not sprint between efforts.

If forward pogo travel is poorly controlled, **use Ankle Pogo in Place instead of E1**, 1 × 8 with the same rest; this preserves the ankle-rebound job but removes travel. If E5 linking is unfamiliar or unsuitable today, **use a countermovement Broad Jump to Stick with hands fixed on hips instead of E5**, 2 × 2, 30 s between attempts, 120 s between sets and 90 s before E6. This proposed replacement isolates countermovement leg drive without arms; it differs from E3's motionless start. Keep travel modest. It retains four landings but removes intermediate rebound contacts; log which exercise was used.

E6 requires verified containment of the entire flight, bounce/roll and retrieval inside an exclusive throwing area; the lanes alone do not prove this. If that cannot be provided, **use a Two-hand Kettlebell Swing instead of E6: 2 × 3 complete swings**, light and fast, 90 s between sets and before Section 3. Use a familiar two-hand hinge swing to comfortable lower-chest height, feet planted, arms guiding the bell and no overhead finish; stop after three swings and park it under control. Use only with demonstrated swing and handling competence and a clear swing envelope. This proposed replacement supplies rapid hip-driven implement acceleration with no planned landing, but less direct arm/forward-projection practice than the toss. It replaces six throws with six swings. If neither version is suitable, record the unperformed slot and resolve it before a future full delivery; do not invent extra jumps.

**Quality/recovery rule:** After a clear decline, add 30–60 s before the next scheduled attempt. Reduce intent/travel when needed; end that exercise if quality still fails to recover. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness or an unsafe surface/space. Invalid attempts still count as exposure. The fourteen prescribed slots never obligate an athlete to finish unsafe work.
