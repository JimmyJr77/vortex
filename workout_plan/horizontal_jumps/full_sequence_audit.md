## Architecture and scope

- All twelve workouts map to **Jumps > Horizontal**. The [curriculum](curriculum.md) preserves every category and subcategory in the supplied seven-category framework without adding strength or stability as performance categories.
- Each class has four training sections in the required order: existing Access & Prepare 1, Explosiveness, Strength (Stabilization/Resilience), and Strength (Primary). Every class has exactly **6 + 2 + 6 nonzero exercise entries**, totaling **168** entries.
- Access & Prepare 1 is referenced only. Its supplied content and duration remain unavailable; no replacement preparation, finisher, conditioning or tumbling block was added. Preparation sets use already-listed strength lifts and are counted separately.
- Each class contains a daily brief, priority task, observable quality marker, exercise-specific jobs, execution cues, working dose, recovery, entry/regression criteria, workload and timing. Assessment takes place within the listed dose.
- These are complete conditional prescriptions for the stated baseline, not proof that every athlete can immediately perform all twelve sessions. More demanding receiving, linkage, drop or loaded tasks require actual competence and a suitable setup.

## Uniqueness: execution review, not only name matching

The default sequence contains **72 distinct explosive prescriptions and 24 distinct light stabilization/resilience prescriptions**. The [96-drill register](drill_uniqueness.md) lists each assignment, with execution/job detail in its linked class and the [structured register](supporting/drill_uniqueness.json). Class 1's specified replacement drills were reserved when selecting later defaults. Recurring primary-strength lifts are permitted and intentional.

These are not 96 unrelated movement families. Reusing a movement family is necessary for a tightly focused curriculum. A new name, different kilogram load, changed rep count or a slightly farther target was not accepted as a distinct drill. The following close families received explicit comparison:

| Related prescriptions | Material execution difference and supporting job |
|---|---|
| Standing, paused, seated and staggered broad jumps | Natural countermovement/arms, motionless hands-on-hips start, supported seated start and staggered two-foot push change how force is initiated or coordinated. Each has an explicit start condition, not a cosmetic stance label. |
| Backward-step, walking/two-step and lateral-entry jumps | They change the incoming weight transfer and direction that must be organized before a forward push. Entry steps are separately described and counted; approach speed is controlled within the lane. |
| Floor and box receiving tasks | Elevated full-foot receiving changes the reception constraint. Seated versus standing/walking start, unilateral versus bilateral takeoff, and same-leg versus two-foot reception differentiate the box tasks. Box height is not treated as a measure of jump height. |
| Pogo and line-hop family | Bilateral versus unilateral/staggered support, stationary versus traveling versus fore-aft contacts, and hand-supported forward lean change support or contact direction. Low amplitude is retained where prescribed; adding a line alone would not justify a duplicate. |
| Isolated hops, linked hops, alternating bounds and skips | Same-foot reuse, opposite-foot transfer and the step-hop rhythm are different contact tasks. Linked sequences require force acceptance and reuse between contacts; isolated landings finish with a hold. Every contact is counted. |
| Distance bounds, height-biased bounds and hurdle bounds | The first emphasizes forward transfer, the second uses a taller upward impulse, and the third imposes verified obstacle clearance. They are not renamed distances or interchangeable performance tests. Hurdles remain low enough to preserve the intended task. |
| Drop-to-jump versus grounded snap-down | The drop imposes an initial aerial landing; the grounded lowering has no initial flight. Both initial and final landings are counted for drop tasks. Horizontal versus vertical rebound changes the outgoing force direction and receives a different dose. |
| Rear-band and dumbbell broad jumps | Rearward horizontal tension with free arm action differs from carried mass with arms fixed at the sides. They are distinct constraints, not the same weighted drill at two weights. Both remain light, conditional tasks. |
| Grounded swings, hip snap and landmine extension | Parking each swing resets its start; kickstand support shifts the contribution toward one leg. Band tension and guided landmine squat extension use different force applications. Grounded rapid-force tasks are identified honestly and are not counted as jumps. |
| Scoop, chest and overhead releases in different supports | Release path/arm lever, seated or kneeling support, unilateral or split stance, entry movement, received-ball reversal and airborne release change the actual coordination task. Throws provide small-dose arm/trunk or rapid-force support; they do not substitute for direct horizontal jump practice. |
| Short foot, toe pressure and ankle control | Foot doming differs from big-toe pressure without curling. Dynamic dorsiflexion differs from an isometric toe-up hold; inversion and eversion oppose different directions. Their light doses address specific contact-control jobs. |
| Step-down, hip and trunk control family | Forward versus lateral lowering changes the load-transfer plane; adduction, abduction and external rotation have distinct joint actions. Static bridge, long-lever bridge and marching bridge change support/lever behavior. These remain easy strengthening, not fatigue tests. |

Class 12's hop–step–jump is one integrated foot-sequence task: hop to the same foot, step to the opposite foot, then jump to both feet. Its two sequences contain six landings. The stationary single-leg start and modest floor travel are declared training adaptations, not a competition test or three unrelated exercises hidden in one row. The [World Athletics description](https://worldathletics.org/disciplines/jump/triple-jump) supports the foot sequence, not this youth dose.

Range or effort reduction within a drill does not create a new drill. If readiness or a required setup is absent, the coach must revise/defer that slot and record the change before full delivery. The uniqueness preference must not force an unsuitable task. A revised replacement must be checked against the register; omitted work is never recorded as completed.

## Relevance, progression and cumulative demands

The sequence develops standing projection, static initiation, each leg's contribution, arm/base coordination, hip impulse, elevated receiving, alternating contacts, same-leg reuse, short entries, imposed landing response, light resistance and final integration. Complexity does not rise automatically: bilateral receiving and reduced supporting doses interrupt the more demanding unilateral/contact tasks. The priority drill receives the first substantial practice; Class 1 places its priority immediately after eight low pogos.

Recurring strength anchors retain squat, hinge, split-stance, terminal hip-extension, ankle and knee-flexor jobs. Each later class audit traces **previous prescription → current dose → reason → next decision criterion**. For example, the split squat receives more emphasis with single-leg tasks, while Class 10 returns from Class 9's step-up emphasis to modest bilateral work. Goblet/RDL loading is held without evidence; a changed written set allocation is an objective-based prescription, not an athlete-earned gain. One or two challenging sets on supporting lifts constrain cumulative fatigue. The six entries are not six maximal lifts.

Working strength uses roughly **2–3 technically sound reps/cycles in reserve**; control work remains light, roughly **5 or more clean reps in reserve or comparable ease for holds**. Squat/split/jump work shares knee/hip-extensor demand; hinges, thrusts and walkouts share posterior-chain demand; ankle control, jumps and calf raises share lower-leg demand. Different jobs do not make these loads independent. A coach should reduce the actual dose or lever when quality/reserve is not available and log the difference.

No athlete feedback establishes attendance, successful reps, working loads, landing control, symptoms or recovery. Adjacent saved jump, agility and other focus plans were reviewed as potential overlap, not performed weekly work. The [feedback record](athlete_feedback.md) retains these unknowns. No class number grants progression. Different jump protocols cannot establish a matched before/after gain; the user's uniqueness request trades repeated jump-test comparability for varied task practice. Strength recurrence preserves a basis for future like-for-like loading records when actual data is supplied.

## Workload and timing reconciliation

The [workload summary](workload_summary.md) separates bilateral and unilateral landing events, low-amplitude subsets, initial drop landings, throws, other rapid efforts, working rounds/bouts and walkout cycles. Individual audits additionally include preparation, stabilization and side accounting. A bilateral landing is one event and two foot contacts; a unilateral landing is one event and one foot contact. Ordinary entry steps, returns and step-down resets are not relabeled as plyometric landings. Failed attempts and unplanned extra flights must be added to actual records.

The written landing totals range from 8 to 26, with different mixtures of unilateral, low-amplitude, linked and drop demands. These are not equal-intensity units or a safety threshold. Class 10 has six initial drop landings plus their six final jump landings. Class 12's airborne throw contributes four athlete landings and four releases, both included in its totals. Holds sum the actual duration of separately performed bouts; a simultaneous bilateral hold counts elapsed time once. Primary work and load-preparation work remain separate.

Individual reference clocks range from **65.5 to 83.1 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including written resets, side/set rests, strength ramps, handling, transitions and recording. They are arithmetic models, not measured delivery times. A large share is recovery: do not compress the session into conditioning to meet an unknown booking. Group size, staff count, available implement quantities and queues are unconfirmed, so neither group end time nor complete-session duration is verified. The actual preparation may also add substantial workload.

Classes are instructional exposures, not consecutive-day instructions. Approximately 48–72 hours before comparable demanding lower-body work is an initial consideration from the supplied example, adjusted to actual response and the entire sport/gym/tumbling schedule. No actual cumulative total can be inferred from written prescriptions.

## Equipment and practical conditions

The current specification's **three separate 10 m lanes** govern this plan. The older 12 m/eight-tenet reference was not imported. Short entries and contained sequences leave receiving/stopping room; three lanes are never treated as one 30 m runway. Box/bench fit, stable supports/anchors, appropriate light loads, lane width, footing and clear waiting/return routes need on-site confirmation. Throwing requires verified flight/bounce/roll containment and safe retrieval; no throwing wall or measuring-device capability is assumed. These conditions remain visible in each affected workout.

Strength equipment replacements are explicitly one-for-one. Unique power/control tasks may be reduced within their stated constraints; an unavailable essential support or unsafe throw envelope requires a revised slot before delivery. Equipment category availability does not establish suitable dimensions or attachment strength. No new equipment purchase, library publication or software/database edit was made for this curriculum.
