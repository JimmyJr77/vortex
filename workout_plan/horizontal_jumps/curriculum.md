## Programming basis and current decision

The style draws on Overtime Athletes' public progression from simpler power tasks toward unilateral and linked horizontal work. The class uses modest supporting doses and challenging squat/hinge strength around a clear horizontal anchor; it does not copy an elite athlete session wholesale. [OTA horizontal-power example](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/pro-level-plyometrics-training-pt-1/)

Qualified instruction, controlled lifting, individualized progression and recovery are consistent with the NSCA's youth resistance-training guidance. These principles inform the plan; the exact fourteen-exercise session and its transfer to jump distance have not been validated as a whole. Exercise jobs describe intended mechanical support, not guaranteed results. [NSCA position statement, 2009](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf)

Class 1 was revised to prioritize the anchor before supporting jump variants, keep only four linked-jump landings, limit unilateral and terminal hip-extension strength to one working set per prescribed side/position, and use knee-flexor walkouts instead of a second calf exercise. This preserves distinct jobs without six large lower-body lifts. Detailed decisions and timing are in the audit.
