# Force Absorption & Elastic Rebound — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Receive a self-generated jump landing with control, then use that landing to produce a prompt, organized second takeoff. Today establishes the difference between stopping a landing and rebounding from it.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Class 1 starts this curriculum; it does not imply novice ability. Individual readiness, previous completed training and athlete response are unknown. Apply the full prescription only when the coach verifies the relevant execution today.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The main demand is coordinated ankle, knee and hip yielding followed by reversal, without collapsing or deliberately pausing. **E3 Repeated Vertical Jump** is the performance anchor. Its supporting jobs are terminal landing control, small ankle rebounds, forward and lateral force acceptance, and a small unilateral elastic exposure. Strength addresses bilateral braking, hip control, each leg's knee/hip contribution, lateral weight acceptance and plantarflexor strength at two knee positions. Keep jump amplitude controllable and strength work challenging without technical failure.

**Style:** The absorption emphasis, connected jumps and small unilateral doses are inspired by [Overtime Athletes' plyometric programming guide](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/create-plyometric-training-program/). The exact exercises, doses and progression gates below are this curriculum's prescription, not a copied or independently validated OTA session.

**Quality marker inside E3:** Across the three sets, record organized rebound transitions **out of six prescribed opportunities** and controlled final landings **out of three**. An organized transition has continuous landing-to-takeoff action, no extra gathering dip, no corrective hop, no obvious knee/pelvis collapse and no progressive forward travel. The final landing must be held for 2 seconds without a step. Record attempted and unperformed opportunities separately; do not score an unperformed attempt as an observed failure. This is a coaching marker, not a measured contact time, force-absorption score or reactive-strength index.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Perform in order, using bodyweight. E1–E2 are deliberately small exposures before E3. All jump takeoffs are purposeful and quick; height and distance remain subordinate to the specified landing or rebound task. These are recovered practice sets, not a conditioning circuit. Rest durations begin after the previous effort finishes and include no extra exercises.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Countermovement Jump to Stick** | **1 × 3 jumps.** Controlled submaximal height; **20 s between jumps; 60 s before E2.** | Establish the terminal braking pattern before asking for rebounds. Use a smooth dip and arm swing, jump vertically, then let ankles, knees and hips yield together and hold 2 s. Keep pressure centered over both feet. Reduce height if the landing needs a step or the trunk folds; stop if control is not restored. |
| 2 | **Ankle Pogo in Place** | **1 × 8 low jumps**, continuous; **90 s before E3.** | Rehearse ankle-led elastic return with little hip/knee excursion before the larger multi-joint task. Stay tall, knees soft, feet under hips; rebound briskly without chasing height. Let the final landing settle. Reduce amplitude for heavy contacts or knee pumping; stop for persistent loss of rhythm. |
| 3 | **Repeated Vertical Jump — three connected jumps** | **3 × 3 jumps = 9 jumps and 6 rebound transitions.** **120 s between sets and before E4.** | **Primary anchor:** accept landing momentum and reverse promptly through the ankle, knee and hip. Use a natural arm swing and a repeatable moderate height; jump 2 follows landing 1 and jump 3 follows landing 2 without resetting. Land after jump 3 and hold 2 s. Allow necessary joint flexion; do not lock the knees or force pogo mechanics onto a larger jump. Lower amplitude if a second dip, collapse or drift develops. |
| 4 | **Broad Jump to Stick** | **1 × 3 jumps.** Modest forward distance with a brisk two-foot takeoff; **20 s between jumps; 90 s before E5.** | Absorb a forward component of momentum that E1/E3 do not provide. Land on two feet, bring the hips over the base and hold 2 s without shuffling. Reset by walking only after the area is clear. Shorten distance if the athlete falls forward or reaches the feet far ahead of the body. |
| 5 | **Lateral Bound — stick landing** | **1 × 2 landings per leg = 4 bounds.** Alternate receiving legs, fully resetting each time. **20 s between bounds; 90 s before E6.** | Accept lateral momentum on one leg while controlling the pelvis, knee and foot. Push from one leg to the other over a small distance, absorb and hold 2 s; this is a bound, not a side-step drill. Reduce distance for pelvic drop, knee collapse or a corrective hop. |
| 6 | **Single-Leg Pogo in Place** | **1 × 5 low hops per leg = 10 hops.** **60 s between legs; 90 s before Section 3.** | Apply a small ankle rebound dose with the load carried by one support leg, after the main bilateral work. Keep the foot beneath the pelvis and knee softly organized; settle the fifth landing. Reduce hop height if the ankle rolls or the free leg flails. End the set if this remains uncontrolled. |

**Hold, regress or stop:** After a visible quality decline, extend the next scheduled recovery by 30–60 s and use a lower amplitude. If the next scheduled effort still deteriorates, end that exercise. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness, or an unsafe surface/setup. Attempts that occur count toward workload even when technically unsuccessful; do not add make-up repetitions. Log unfinished work rather than forcing the fourteen-slot prescription to completion.

For E3, **use two connected jumps per set instead of three** if the athlete can control one rebound but loses the second: 3 × 2, the same rests, 6 landings and 3 rebound opportunities. This reduces total class landings to 34. If even one rebound is uncontrolled at low amplitude, stop E3 and record the readiness limitation; do not relabel a non-rebounding drill as completed rebound practice.

For E6, **use hand-supported Single-Leg Pogo in Place instead of the unsupported version** when support restores control: retain 1 × 5 per leg and the same rests, using only light fingertips on a stable support verified by the coach. Log it as modified. Do not use a movable bench as a leaning rail.

**Space:** Reserve one clear 10 m lane and work one athlete at a time unless additional independently supervised stations are verified. For E4, a proposed layout starts at 2 m, keeps the modest landing within the next 2 m, and leaves the remainder empty for unexpected travel; adjust the jump downward to fit. E5 may run laterally relative to the athlete along the lane's long axis, with the athlete facing across the lane, so it need not cross adjacent lanes. Confirm lane width, overhead clearance, floor traction and all holding/waiting areas on site. Never join the three lanes into a longer runway. No boxes, hurdles, throws, approaches or force-plate capability are needed for today's jumps.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep these light: **at least 5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or similarly easy holds. They should leave the athlete ready for primary strength. The movements below are active strengthening, not a mobility sequence.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported single-leg hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot = 6 holds / 30 s total.** Bodyweight; **5 s relaxed between holds; 30 s between feet; 45 s before S2.** | Lightly load intrinsic foot control while maintaining heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact, supporting a stable base during landing and rebound. Use hand support, a soft knee and long toes. Avoid toe gripping or rolling to the outside edge. Use a seated Short-Foot Drill instead of standing if cramping or loss of tripod contact persists; retain the per-foot hold dose. |
| 2 | **Side-lying hip adduction** — proposed library addition | **1 × 6 per side = 12 reps.** Bodyweight; **1 s lift, 1 s hold, 2 s lower; 30 s between sides; 60 s before primary preparation sets.** | Provide a small medial-hip strength dose for controlling the receiving leg during E5's lateral landing. Lie on the working side; place the top foot on the floor in front for support and lift the straight bottom leg a small distance without rolling the pelvis. Shorten the lever by slightly bending the working knee if necessary; reduce range or stop for groin discomfort. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

**Loading:** The table gives working sets. Select loads that leave approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** at the prescribed tempo and range. Use athlete feedback plus observed execution; estimated RIR is not a measured test result. Lower under control and rise decisively without jumping. Stop a set at the technical limit, reduce the next load and extend rest as needed. All loads must be manageable to pick up, position and put down with the available supervision.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Goblet Squat** | **2 × 5 working reps.** Dumbbell or kettlebell; **3 s down, about 1 s up. 150 s between working sets; 120 s before P2 preparation.** | Main bilateral knee/hip strength anchor: resist flexion under load while maintaining the foot base and trunk position used to receive a jump. Squat through a controlled, comfortable range; do not force an unusually shallow or deep position. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee collapse or loss of trunk position. |
| 2 | **Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift** | **2 × 6 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 120 s between working sets and before P3 preparation.** | Challenge hip-extensor strength while controlling hip flexion, complementing the squat's larger knee demand. Keep knees softly bent and loads close; move the hips back without rounding the back. Stop the lowering at the athlete's controllable range. |
| 3 | **Dumbbell Split Squat** | **1 × 5 per lead leg = 10 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 75 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Load each leg's knee/hip contribution in a stable sagittal stance, supporting single-leg force acceptance without making balance the limiting factor. Keep both feet planted, rear heel raised and sufficient stance width. Reduce load or use stable hand support if the pelvis rotates or the knee loses its line. |
| 4 | **Goblet lateral lunge** — proposed loaded variant | **1 × 5 per receiving leg = 10 working reps.** **3 s to step and lower, about 1 s to return. 75 s between sides; 120 s before P5 preparation.** | Challenge lateral weight acceptance and return from the loaded hip, directly supporting E5. Step sideways, load the receiving hip/knee over a flat foot and push back to the start; the other leg remains relatively straight. Use a moderate step and comfortable depth, not an end-range groin stretch. Reduce load, step length or depth for rolling feet, pelvic rotation or groin discomfort. |
| 5 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single-leg** | **1 × 8 per leg = 16 working reps.** **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. 60 s between legs; 90 s before P6 preparation.** | Challenge plantarflexor force and controlled lowering with the knee nearly straight, supporting the low-amplitude pogo work. Work from the floor; use one hand on a secure support and hold the load in the other. Keep heel travel vertical without ankle rolling. Bodyweight is acceptable only if it meets the effort target; otherwise load appropriately. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise — bent knees, both legs together** | **2 × 8 working reps.** **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. 90 s between working sets.** | Load plantarflexion with the knees held bent, supporting ankle force control when a landing has more knee flexion than a pogo. Sit on a stable, appropriately sized bench; secure suitable dumbbells on the distal thighs, clear of the kneecaps. Raise and lower heels through consistent range to the floor without bouncing or extending the knees. |

**Preparation sets — separate from working sets:** Use only the already listed lifts, comfortably below working effort and at the same tempo. P1: **2 × 4**, light then moderate, **60 s between, 90 s before work**. P2: **1 × 4 light, then 90 s**. P3 and P4: each **1 × 3 light per side, 30 s between sides, then 90 s before work**. P5: **1 × 4 light per leg, 30 s between legs, then 60 s**. P6: **1 × 4 light, then 60 s**. Add and log further preparation sets only if the athlete needs them to reach a technically appropriate working load; allow the added time.

For P1, if holding one goblet load limits the legs prematurely, **use a double-dumbbell front-rack squat instead of Goblet Squat**, provided the athlete already controls the rack and setup. Keep 2 × 5, the same tempo, preparation and rest; record the changed exercise for future comparisons. For P6, **use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead of the seated version** if a bench/load setup cannot be made secure: 2 × 8 bilateral working, 1 × 4 preparation, the same tempo and rests. Hold a small constant knee bend, use a securely positioned suitable load and ensure calf effort, rather than a tiring squat hold, limits the set. If a replacement cannot meet these requirements, resolve the station before running the class.

**Timing and delivery:** Allow approximately **75–85 minutes for Sections 2–4**, including their specified preparation sets, recovery, demonstrations, loading and recording, for an athlete with prompt equipment access. This is not a verified group timetable. Access & Prepare 1, equipment queues and extra recovery are additional. Its unknown duration means the complete session duration is unresolved. A 60-minute total booking does not fit this prescription; a 90-minute total booking needs the actual preparation and group flow checked before delivery. Do not shorten the prescribed recovery to make the clock fit. See the supporting audit for the time calculation and rotation constraints.

**Scheduling:** Treat this as a demanding lower-body exposure, including the eccentric and calf work. Review recent and upcoming sport, gym, jumping and separate tumbling exposure, sleep, soreness and current symptoms. Approximately 48–72 hours before another comparable lower-body session is an initial scheduling consideration, adjusted to the athlete's full workload and response. The class number is not a day or week assignment. Do not stack this with the separate Vertical Jumps class merely because the focus name differs.

**Progression note:** This is the first prescription for this focus; no completed exposure or improvement is claimed. Retain E3's protocol and the squat/hinge anchors for comparison. Before increasing a demand, require reported completion with the specified technique, appropriate RIR and acceptable recovery, plus readiness at the next exposure. Class 2's intended job is to consolidate the same landing-to-rebound transition. Missing feedback means hold the anchor dose and reassess within the scheduled work; it does not justify automatic volume, height or load increases.

**Audit summary:** **6 explosive / 2 light stabilization / 6 primary-strength entries**, with no added exercise block. Full prescription: **37 landing events** (23 bilateral, 14 single-leg), including **21 landing-to-rebound transitions** of different types; 9 primary-strength set rounds / 12 separately performed working bouts / 74 repetitions; 36 additional load-preparation repetitions. Related jump and calf variants have distinct stopping, rebound, support-leg or knee-position jobs. Supporting lunge volume was limited to one set per side and drop-height work was omitted because rebound readiness and box dimensions are unknown. Equipment fit, athlete history, preparation exposure and complete group timing remain practical assumptions, not verified outcomes.
