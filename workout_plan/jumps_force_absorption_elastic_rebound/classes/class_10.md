# Force Absorption & Elastic Rebound — Class 10 of 12

**Primary effort:** Maintain force acceptance and rebound quality through one additional jump in a short vertical sequence.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Load and complexity follow demonstrated competence and current readiness, not age or the class number alone.

**Review before design:** Class 9 retained 2 × 3 vertical rebounds, with a conditional amplitude option, 28 total landings and 60 strength reps. No actual amplitude or recovery result is recorded. Class 10 therefore fixes amplitude at a known qualified level if one exists and treats the fourth jump as a separate earned sequence-length option; it does not combine an unverified height increase with longer sets. **Prescribed work is not observed work.** No actual completion, load history or recovery report is established in the saved feedback record at authoring; these remain unknown.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The limiting factor is preserving organized reversal as a sequence extends from three jumps to four. E3 is the sole candidate volume/sequence change. E2 is reduced to four low pogos to offset two extra vertical landing events. E1 keeps terminal braking, E4 a separately qualified small forward rebound, E5 lateral stopping and E6 unilateral ankle rhythm. The same light and challenging strength roles support the receiving mechanics without an added strength progression.

**Readiness and route choice:** Use the four-jump route only after actual 2 × 3 execution at the chosen fixed amplitude shows 4/4 organized transitions and 2/2 final holds, acceptable subsequent response and current readiness. A height merely prescribed in Class 9 is not qualified. Without evidence, use two sets of three instead. E4 forward rebounds remain independently gated by actual forward-rebound control and recovery; otherwise use the held-forward replacement. Hold strength load and all distances.

**Style:** OTA-inspired; see the [source and library record](../supporting/sources_and_library.md) for provenance and evidence limits. Exercises, doses and progression gates are specific to this curriculum.

**Quality marker within the listed work:** Listed E3: 6 organized transitions and 2 final holds; note whether the third transition in each set remains as organized as the first two. Hold E3: 4 transitions and 2 final holds. Continuous reversal has no corrective hop, gathering dip, obvious collapse or progressive travel; finish with 2 s control. The 2 × 4 result is not directly comparable to the earlier 3 × 3 protocol despite both having six transitions.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Perform in table order, with bodyweight and purposeful quick takeoffs. Control the indicated amplitude rather than chasing height or distance. Rest is recovery, without other exercise work. An ongoing connected sequence is one exercise, not separate drills; stop and settle its final landing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Countermovement Jump to Stick** | **1 × 2 jumps. 20 s between jumps; 60 s before E2.** | Retain a small bilateral braking reference before single-leg acceptance. Use a controllable vertical jump, coordinated ankle/knee/hip yielding and a 2 s hold. Lower height for stepping or trunk collapse. |
| 2 | **Ankle Pogo in Place** | **1 × 4 low jumps**, continuous. **90 s before E3.** | Keep a short ankle-rhythm entry while allocating two more potential contacts to the primary sequence. Stay tall and centered with soft knees; settle the fourth contact. Lower amplitude or stop for knee pumping or lost rhythm. |
| 3 | **Repeated Vertical Jump — four connected jumps** | **2 × 4 jumps = 8 landings / 6 transitions. 120 s between sets and before E4.** | **Primary anchor:** maintain the same receiving/rebound organization through one additional jump per short sequence. Use a previously qualified moderate height and natural arm swing; keep the fourth jump as organized as the earlier ones and hold its landing 2 s. End the sequence if contact becomes a gathering dip, travel increases or the knee/pelvis collapses; do not chase completion. |
| 4 | **Repeated Broad Jump — two connected jumps** | **2 × 2 jumps = 4 landings / 2 forward rebound transitions. 120 s between sets and before E5.** | Consolidate a separately qualified forward landing-to-takeoff pattern at a small dose. Use modest displacement and natural arm swing, continuous reversal without a gather and a 2 s final stop. Shorten distance or use held broad jumps if reaching or uncontrolled travel appears. |
| 5 | **Lateral Bound — stick landing** | **1 × 2 receiving landings per leg = 4 bounds.** Alternate sides, **20 s between bounds; 90 s before E6.** | Keep lateral single-leg force acceptance without adding lateral return demand while vertical amplitude is the candidate change. Use the familiar small distance and hold each landing 2 s. Shorten for knee collapse, pelvis drop or corrective hops. |
| 6 | **Single-Leg Pogo in Place** | **1 × 3 low hops per leg = 6 hops. 60 s between legs; 90 s before Section 3.** | Keep a minimal but real one-leg ankle rhythm exposure while directional practice shares the session. Stay centered over the foot with a softly organized knee; settle the third hop. Use lower amplitude/support or stop if alignment or rhythm is lost. |

**E3 hold route:** Use **2 × 3 connected vertical jumps instead of 2 × 4**, at the same fixed moderate amplitude and 120 s rests. This produces 26 total class landings rather than 28. If the three-jump sequence also deteriorates after lowering amplitude and extending recovery, stop the entry. **E4 hold replacement:** Use **Broad Jump to Stick instead of Repeated Broad Jump: 2 × 2**, 20 s between jumps, 120 s between sets and before E5, modest distance and 2 s controlled landings. This removes two forward rebound transitions without changing the number of landings.

**Quality and stop rules:** After a visible decline, add 30–60 s to the next scheduled recovery and reduce the relevant amplitude. If the next scheduled effort still deteriorates, stop that exercise. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness or an unsafe setup. Failed attempts count as exposure; unperformed work remains unperformed. Do not add make-up repetitions or an extra test block. The written fourteen slots never oblige an athlete to finish deteriorating work.

**Comparison conditions:** Record the actual variant, amplitude/distance, surface, shoes, arm action and recovery. Compare an anchor only under matching conditions; a new amplitude, sequence length, direction or support condition is a changed task. Qualitative scores do not measure absorbed force, contact time or reactive-strength index. Use devices only when their methods are verified and within existing attempts.

**Support replacement:** For Single-Leg Pogo in Place, use the hand-supported version instead of unsupported hops when light fingertips on a verified fixed support restore control; keep the listed hop count and rests. Reduce amplitude first, and stop if a secure support or controlled hop cannot be achieved. Log the changed support. Related variants are replacements, not additional slots.

**Space and stations:** Use the current specification's three separate 10 m lanes, not a joined runway. A default requirement is one supervised active jump station with all waiting athletes outside the movement area. For forward tasks, start near 2 m, keep all planned landings within the next 4 m, and leave the remaining lane clear; distances are adjustable ceilings for layout, not performance targets. Lateral bounds can travel along a lane's length with the athlete facing across the lane. Confirm lane width, floor traction, overhead clearance and the complete travel/holding area. Shorten displacement if the task cannot fit. No external drop height, throwing space, rack or force-plate capability is required. Equipment quantities, bench fit and secure hand support still need on-site verification.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep these light: **at least 5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or similarly easy holds. They should leave the athlete ready for primary strength. The movements below are active strengthening, not a mobility sequence.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported single-leg hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot = 6 holds / 30 s total.** Bodyweight; **5 s relaxed between holds; 30 s between feet; 45 s before S2.** | Lightly load intrinsic foot control while maintaining heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact, supporting a stable base during landing and rebound. Use hand support, a soft knee and long toes. Avoid toe gripping or rolling to the outside edge. Use a seated Short-Foot Drill instead of standing if cramping or loss of tripod contact persists; retain the per-foot hold dose. |
| 2 | **Side-lying hip adduction** — proposed library addition | **1 × 6 per side = 12 reps.** Bodyweight; **1 s lift, 1 s hold, 2 s lower; 30 s between sides; 60 s before primary preparation sets.** | Provide a small medial-hip strength dose for controlling the receiving leg during E5's lateral landing. Lie on the working side; place the top foot on the floor in front for support and lift the straight bottom leg a small distance without rolling the pelvis. Shorten the lever by slightly bending the working knee if necessary; reduce range or stop for groin discomfort. |


## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

**Today’s strength decision:** Maintain the 60-rep dose, controlled tempos and prior suitable loads. The sequence-length option is the sole upward demand. All six primary lifts remain at approximately 2–3 technical RIR; no extra sets compensate for the smaller pogo entry.

**Load-selection clarification:** Directions to retain loads assume those loads still meet the 2–3 technical RIR target today. If a prior load is clearly too easy, use the class’s hold/consolidation jump route and make the smallest appropriate strength-load adjustment rather than adding a jump progression at the same time. If several lifts need calibration, keep all jump demands at their last qualified level and log that strength-focused adjustment. Reductions for technique or symptoms remain appropriate. Preparation identifies a suitable working load; it is not a maximal test. No load increase is earned by the class number.

**Loading:** The table gives working sets. Select loads that leave approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** at the prescribed tempo and range. Use athlete feedback plus observed execution; estimated RIR is not a measured test result. Lower under control and rise decisively without jumping. Stop a set at the technical limit, reduce the next load and extend rest as needed. All loads must be manageable to pick up, position and put down with the available supervision.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Goblet Squat** | **2 × 5 working reps.** Dumbbell or kettlebell; **3 s down, about 1 s up. 150 s between working sets; 120 s before P2 preparation.** | Main bilateral knee/hip strength anchor: resist flexion under load while maintaining the foot base and trunk position used to receive a jump. Squat through a controlled, comfortable range; do not force an unusually shallow or deep position. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee collapse or loss of trunk position. |
| 2 | **Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift** | **1 × 6 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 120 s before P3 preparation.** | Challenge hip-extensor strength while controlling hip flexion, complementing the squat's larger knee demand. Keep knees softly bent and loads close; move the hips back without rounding the back. Stop the lowering at the athlete's controllable range. |
| 3 | **Dumbbell Split Squat** | **1 × 5 per lead leg = 10 working reps.** **3 s down, about 1 s up. 75 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Load each leg's knee/hip contribution in a stable sagittal stance, supporting single-leg force acceptance without making balance the limiting factor. Keep both feet planted, rear heel raised and sufficient stance width. Reduce load or use stable hand support if the pelvis rotates or the knee loses its line. |
| 4 | **Goblet lateral lunge** — proposed loaded variant | **1 × 5 per receiving leg = 10 working reps.** **3 s to step and lower, about 1 s to return. 75 s between sides; 120 s before P5 preparation.** | Challenge lateral weight acceptance and return from the loaded hip, directly supporting E5. Step sideways, load the receiving hip/knee over a flat foot and push back to the start; the other leg remains relatively straight. Use a moderate step and comfortable depth, not an end-range groin stretch. Reduce load, step length or depth for rolling feet, pelvic rotation or groin discomfort. |
| 5 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single-leg** | **1 × 8 per leg = 16 working reps.** **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. 60 s between legs; 90 s before P6 preparation.** | Challenge plantarflexor force and controlled lowering with the knee nearly straight, supporting the low-amplitude pogo work. Work from the floor; use one hand on a secure support and hold the load in the other. Keep heel travel vertical without ankle rolling. Bodyweight is acceptable only if it meets the effort target; otherwise load appropriately. |
| 6 | **Seated Dumbbell Calf Raise — bent knees, both legs together** | **1 × 8 working reps.** **1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down. Allow at least 60 s to settle and record the final set before leaving the station; no further exercise follows.** | Load plantarflexion with the knees held bent, supporting ankle force control when a landing has more knee flexion than a pogo. Sit on a stable, appropriately sized bench; secure suitable dumbbells on the distal thighs, clear of the kneecaps. Raise and lower heels through consistent range to the floor without bouncing or extending the knees. |

**Preparation sets — separate from working sets:** Use only the already listed lifts, comfortably below working effort and at the same tempo. P1: **2 × 4**, light then moderate, **60 s between, 90 s before work**. P2: **1 × 4 light, then 90 s**. P3 and P4: each **1 × 3 light per side, 30 s between sides, then 90 s before work**. P5: **1 × 4 light per leg, 30 s between legs, then 60 s**. P6: **1 × 4 light, then 60 s**. Add and log further preparation sets only if the athlete needs them to reach a technically appropriate working load; allow the added time.

For P1, if holding one goblet load limits the legs prematurely, **use a double-dumbbell front-rack squat instead of Goblet Squat**, provided the athlete already controls the rack and setup. Keep 2 × 5, the same tempo, preparation and rest; record the changed exercise for future comparisons. For P6, **use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead of the seated version** if a bench/load setup cannot be made secure: 1 × 8 bilateral working, 1 × 4 preparation, the same tempo, preparation rest and final 60 s settling/recording period. Hold a small constant knee bend, use a securely positioned suitable load and ensure calf effort, rather than a tiring squat hold, limits the set. If a replacement cannot meet these requirements, resolve the station before running the class.


**Timing:** Allow approximately **71–82 minutes for Sections 2–4** with prompt equipment access. This includes 54 min 40 s of estimated prescribed movement and recovery, plus 16–26 min for instruction, load handling and recording outside already-counted rest. Access & Prepare 1, queues, extra recovery and any additional preparation sets are additional. Complete class duration remains unresolved. It does not fit a 60-minute total booking; a 90-minute booking needs actual preparation and group-flow verification. Never compress necessary rest to claim a fit.

**Scheduling and delivery:** This is a demanding lower-body exposure, even when contact count is reduced. Review actual sport, gym, jumping, separate tumbling, sleep, symptoms and recovery before scheduling; a different class title does not create recovery. About 48–72 hours before another comparable lower-body exposure is an initial consideration, adjusted to actual response and the full schedule. These class numbers are not dates or consecutive days. Do not stack this with the separate Vertical Jumps plan without reviewing real overlap. No finisher, conditioning circuit or body-control block is added.

**Group timing:** Rotation waiting supplies recovery only if the athlete's actual end-to-next-start interval meets the stated rest. Do not add the same waiting time again, assume three lanes mean three supervised stations, or run challenging lifts simultaneously beyond available supervision. Group size, staffing and equipment queues remain unknown.

**Progression note:** Class 9 → Class 10 replaces two bilateral pogo contacts with two potential multi-joint vertical contacts, keeping the listed total at 28. This is a redistribution to a more demanding type of landing, not equivalent loading. E3 gains one jump per set only with actual evidence; the no-feedback route has 26 landings. Amplitude, distances, rest and strength doses are held.

**Audit summary:** Exact **6 explosive / 2 light stabilization / 6 primary strength** entries. Listed route: **28 landing events** (18 bilateral, 10 single-leg), **15 landing-to-rebound transitions**, and 13 terminal landings. Strength: **7 working rounds / 10 bouts / 60 reps**, plus **36 preparation reps**. The audit rejected adding a third set or turning the sequence into a timed rebound test. Two short four-jump sets provide the desired continuity demand while preserving full recovery and a stop rule for the fourth landing. It also rejected retaining Class 9’s conditional height step automatically. The smaller pogo entry remains real, purposeful work rather than a zero-dose slot. Timing, setup, actual preparation exposure, athlete response and outside workload remain unresolved delivery facts.
