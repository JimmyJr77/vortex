## Stimulus and selection

E3 targets frontal-plane force acceptance and reacceleration of the jump itself, not an agility or reaction block. Its limiting demand is maintaining foot/knee/pelvis organization during a direction reversal. E1/E2 provide small stopping and ankle entries; E4 keeps the bilateral vertical rebound; E5 uses forward stopping instead of another new directional rebound; E6 supplies a small ankle-led single-leg return. The same six strength roles remain direct, with lateral lunge and light medial-hip control particularly relevant to E3.

**Selection findings and revisions:** The audit replaced a possible continuous bounding run with four separately recovered two-bound sequences, balanced by starting leg. This defines every contact and permits the task to fit a lane after on-site verification. It rejected running forward and lateral progression tasks together before response is known. Two calf positions remain useful but their working volume stays at one round each.

**Readiness decision:** Use connected lateral returns only after actual records show at least 2 controlled lateral stop landings per side at this small distance, organized low single-leg pogos at the selected support level, acceptable subsequent ankle/knee/groin response and current readiness. Prior vertical or forward success alone does not qualify a lateral return. Without the relevant evidence, use four discrete lateral bounds to a stop instead of the connected route. Do not increase bound distance or strength load today.
