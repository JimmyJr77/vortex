# Class 1 — design, workload and delivery audit

**Scope:** Audit of the written Class 1 prescription, September 13, 2026. This is not an athlete assessment, confirmation of equipment setup, or validation of training outcomes.

## Selection findings and revisions

| Question | Finding and final decision |
|---|---|
| Is the focus exact? | One combined focus maps to the existing Jumps subcategories Force absorption and Elastic rebound. Directional work serves these demands. No new performance categories or separate body-control work were introduced. |
| Is there a primary task? | E3 has three recovered sets and six actual rebound opportunities in the full prescription. E1/E2 total only 11 landings before it. This gives the main multi-joint task priority while checking terminal landing and ankle rhythm first. |
| Are E1 and E3 duplicates? | E1 dissipates momentum and stops; E3 uses incoming momentum for subsequent takeoffs. Their different intent, continuity and observation criteria justify retaining both. |
| Are E2 and E6 duplicates? | Bilateral ankle rhythm precedes the main task; the smaller per-leg unilateral exposure adds a different support demand after it. They are not merely the same task at two loads. |
| Are all six explosive slots real? | All six include jump/hop/bound flight and quick force expression. A no-flight snap-down was considered, but a jump-to-stick was selected to observe actual landing momentum. No ordinary balance or slow strength drill fills an explosive slot. |
| Why forward and lateral work? | E4 adds forward braking and E5 single-leg lateral acceptance, both absent from stationary bilateral rebounds. They stay low-volume and submaximal; no sprint or agility block is smuggled into the session. |
| Why no drop-jump anchor? | The alternative adds externally imposed incoming momentum and needs a verified box height and established response. Neither is known. Self-generated repeated jumps provide the target reversal task with adjustable amplitude. No external drop is required later merely to appear progressive. |
| Why these six primary exercises? | Squat: bilateral knee/hip braking; RDL: hip-dominant control; split squat: each leg's sagittal contribution; lateral lunge: frontal weight acceptance; standing calf: plantarflexion with a straighter knee; seated calf: plantarflexion with bent knees. These are intended mechanical roles, not six independently proven jump outcomes. |
| Does primary overlap earn its volume? | Squat and RDL receive two working sets. Split squat and lateral lunge have one set per side each, rather than treating both as full compound workouts. A hip thrust was considered but not selected: lateral loaded acceptance fills a more distinct task here than another hip-extension emphasis. |
| Why two calf exercises? | Different knee positions support the ankle demands of low pogos and deeper multi-joint landings. Combined with 18 low pogo landings and E3, this is appreciable ankle/calf work. Standing work is one set per leg; no extra calf isometric or finisher is added. Monitor actual response before adding dose. |
| Do stabilization exercises pre-fatigue? | Two small light prescriptions target the foot contact base and medial hip. At least five technical RIR/easy holds limits fatigue before P1. The adduction lever/range is reduced if the exercise cannot remain light. No generic trunk slot is used. |
| Is progress earned? | No completed exposure is known. Class 2 is a consolidation intention with stable anchors; future amplitude, contact count and load changes remain conditional on actual execution and recovery. |

## Workload reconciliation

- **Explosive:** E1 3 + E2 8 + E3 9 + E4 3 + E5 4 + E6 10 = **37 landing events**. Bilateral 23; single-leg 14 (7 each side). Low ankle work = 18 landings; moderate repeated vertical = 9; controlled vertical/forward/lateral terminal work = 10. Different variants are not equivalent impact loads.
- **Rebounds:** E2 7 + E3 6 + E6 8 = **21 transitions**. Terminal landings = 16. E3's two-jump replacement reduces totals to 34 events and 18 transitions; it is not extra work.
- **Primary working:** 10 + 12 + 10 + 10 + 16 + 16 = **74 reps**, **9 rounds / 12 separately performed bouts**. Preparation adds 36 reps / 7 rounds / 10 bouts.
- **Light strengthening:** 30 s of foot holds across both feet and 12 side-lying adduction reps across both sides, 2 rounds / 4 side-specific bouts. No fatigue test.
- **Other output:** 0 throws, 0 sprints, 0 external drops, 0 loaded jumps. These are true zeros in the prescription. Actual outside exposures and Access & Prepare 1 remain unknown, not zero.
- **Joint/tissue overlap:** Knee/hip extensors work in landings, squat and both lunge patterns; posterior hip work includes RDL and lateral acceptance; ankle/calf work includes three rebound variants and two calf lifts. These are reasons to keep support volume small and rest substantial. A numerical contact total alone cannot establish suitability.

## Group-delivery constraints

- The confirmed lane count does not establish a group size, coach-to-athlete ratio or number of suitably loaded stations. Use one supervised active jump station as the default flow requirement; waiting athletes must remain out of the movement/landing area. Convert lanes to strength stations only after jump work finishes and loads can be placed clear of traffic.
- Rotation waiting can supply rest if the actual end-to-next-start interval meets the prescription. Example only: three athletes using E3 could start at 0/20/40 s, then 150/170/190 s, then 300/320/340 s. With 6 s of work, each receives 144 s between their sets. The final athlete ends around 346 s, then needs the prescribed recovery before E4. This illustrates why the group block exceeds the individual estimate; it does not confirm three-athlete staffing or justify adding 120 s again to each already adequate wait.
- For strength, the coach must see each athlete's lifting and setup. Do not assume all six exercises operate simultaneously or that one coach can supervise multiple challenging lifts at once. Equipment sharing must preserve each athlete's preparation and rest.
- Dumbbell/KB quantities, suitable loads, bench height, secure support and floor/overhead clearance must be checked on site. The class gives replacement routes for an unsuitable goblet hold, seated calf station, E6 support need and S1 standing difficulty. A replacement must retain a real job and be logged.
- The supplied specification says 10 m lanes; the older facility note says 12 m and mentions 60/90-minute bookings. This plan uses 10 m. It does not claim fit into an inherited booking: 60 minutes total is insufficient; 90 minutes total remains conditional on the preparation and actual flow. If a fixed booking is later established, revise the whole dosage/flow while preserving 6/2/6 and meaningful recovery before delivery.
