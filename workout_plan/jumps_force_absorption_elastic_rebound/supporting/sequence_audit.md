# Completed twelve-class sequence audit

**Result:** All **12 classes** are finalized as written, conditional prescriptions. Each has **6 explosive exercises, 2 light stabilization/resilience exercises and 6 challenging primary-strength exercises**, following the existing Access & Prepare 1 reference. This verifies the curriculum documents, not athlete attendance, adaptation, suitability or on-site delivery.

The user's continuation explicitly authorized completion through Class 12. Classes 2–12 were developed, audited and saved one at a time, with the ledger, map and state updated after each. Before each class, the preceding class, relevant earlier anchors, current ledger, feedback and unresolved issues were reviewed. Each class has its own review and structured workload record. Earlier read hashes identify the files at the time of design; later clarifications in this final audit are recorded below, and `verification.json` identifies the final file versions.

## Continuity and progression findings

| Classes | Finding and rationale |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Establish and repeat the same terminal-landing, ankle and three-jump vertical anchors. The second exposure does not add contacts merely because it has a new number. |
| 3 | Offer one modest self-generated amplitude change only with actual quality/recovery evidence; retain all counts and strength doses. The familiar-height route remains complete. |
| 4 | Consolidate: reduce total landings and overlapping RDL/seated-calf volume, retain meaningful challenging sets, and keep directional stopping practice. |
| 5 | Prioritize receiving-leg control. The extra lateral-stop set is gated; a nearly unchanged total contact count is explicitly not treated as unchanged single-leg load. |
| 6–7 | Introduce forward and lateral rebound continuity separately. Held landings and vertical rebounds establish distinct prerequisites; success in one direction is not assumed to transfer to the other. |
| 8 | Consolidate at a lower listed contact count. Forward and lateral tasks each have an independent hold route; combining directions is not assumed to be easy simply because the count is lower. |
| 9 | Address incoming momentum with the existing self-generated jump. An external drop is not forced into the map when dimensions and athlete response are unknown. Keep the two-set dose fixed. |
| 10 | Conditionally extend the vertical sequence by one jump per set at a fixed qualified amplitude. Fewer entry pogos offset the two added landing events, but their different loading is acknowledged. |
| 11 | Revisit qualified lateral work without new distance, height or reactive cues. Full, smaller-connected and discrete-stop routes distinguish what is actually known. |
| 12 | Restore the Class 1 first-five exercise order, full-route doses and rests for possible early-anchor comparisons. Reduce later unilateral-pogo and strength support volume. If actual baseline conditions/results are absent, report a current observation only. |

No progression requires twelve consecutive days or weeks, and no exercise needs random variation to justify its continued presence. The same six primary patterns retain direct jobs: bilateral knee/hip control, hip-dominant control, sagittal receiving-leg strength, lateral acceptance, and plantarflexion with straighter and bent knees. Actual load selection and technical quality provide challenge; fixed class numbers do not assign kilograms.

## Relevance, fatigue and delivery review

- Each explosive entry includes actual jump/hop/bound flight and rapid takeoff intent. Held jumps dissipate momentum; connected jumps use the landing for another takeoff. Bilateral/unilateral ankle work, multi-joint rebounds and directional landings have distinct jobs and are not treated as equivalent contacts.
- Primary performance tasks occur after small entry doses. Directional tasks move earlier when they are the day's priority; the reviews record changed comparison context when the familiar vertical anchor moves later.
- The two light entries strengthen the foot contact base and medial-hip control. Their effort remains at least five technical RIR or similarly easy holds, with lever/support adjustments. They are not pre-fatigue tests or generic mobility fillers.
- All six primary entries remain challenging at approximately two to three technical RIR. From Class 4 onward, lower set volume limits overlap between repeated landings and knee/hip/calf loading. No upper-body filler, conditioning circuit, finisher or separate tumbling work was added.
- Recovery is per exercise, with full resets or within-sequence continuity clearly specified. The time calculations include preparation, side changes, rests and transitions once, plus instruction/load-handling time outside already-counted recovery. Some held-jump replacements take longer than connected pairs; their additional time is included in the upper allowance.
- Timings are for prompt equipment access, not a confirmed group timetable. Access & Prepare 1, extra recovery, extra preparation and queues are additional. The full session is unresolved; the prescribed work does not fit a 60-minute total booking, and a 90-minute booking requires actual preparation and group-flow verification.
- The current specification's separate 10 m lanes govern this curriculum despite the older 12 m note. No continuous runway is assumed. Floor, ceiling, lane width, queues, secure supports, suitable loads, bench fit, group size and staffing must be verified on site. These are disclosed delivery facts, not evidence the written curriculum is incomplete.
- Actual sport, gym, other jumping and separate tumbling loads are unknown. Contextual recovery guidance is a planning consideration, not a guarantee that a time interval makes an athlete ready.

## Corrections and clarifications made in the final audit

1. **Reconciled load retention with challenging strength.** A retained weight must still meet the stated RIR. If it becomes too easy, hold jump demands and calibrate the smallest appropriate strength-load change, rather than simultaneously adding a jump progression. No extra sets or repetitions were added. This resolves a possible conflict between “hold loads” and “challenging primary strength.”
2. **Preserved the comparison entry in Class 12.** The original three vertical stops and eight low pogos precede the full original E3 protocol. Smaller later support doses cannot be mistaken for an identical preparation context. Actual Access & Prepare 1 and baseline conditions still must match before interpreting comparisons.
3. **Kept the calf-station replacement timing explicit.** In Classes 4–12, the standing bent-knee replacement retains the final 60-second settling/recording period included in the time calculation.
4. **Clarified lane verification language.** A configured movement can fit a lane after on-site verification; no actual floor layout was represented as inspected.
5. **Reconciled all route arithmetic and state.** Counts, both-side work, rebound versus final contacts, primary/preparation work and longer hold-route times were checked across all twelve classes. Historical class-by-class handoffs remain in the ledger; current state now records twelve completed documents and no pending class.

These changes preserve the exact framework and 6/2/6 architecture. No unrelated application code, database records or other curriculum files were changed.

## Remaining evidence limits and handoff

The written curriculum covers the requested performance focus. Actual athlete completion, tolerance and improvement are **unproven**. If forward or lateral rebound gates are never met, those directional rebound outcomes remain untrained on the hold routes and must not be marked achieved. Qualitative observations do not quantify absorbed force, tendon properties, contact time or reactive-strength index. The cited sources inform principles and style, not proof that the exact full program produces a particular outcome.

The handoff is to select a class and its route from real readiness and exposure records, verify the actual preparation/time/setup, deliver only the suitable prescription, and enter dated observations in `athlete_feedback.md`. Future revisions should follow that evidence. Completing the twelve documents does not authorize an automatic harder block.
