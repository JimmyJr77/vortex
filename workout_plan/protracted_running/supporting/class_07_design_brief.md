# Class 7 stimulus and choice review

The demand is preserving control through the transition between landing and the next push. E1 offers a repeatable externally paced-by-rope bilateral ankle task, with no prescribed running cadence. The remaining tasks distinguish same-leg forward linkage, same-leg lateral reversal, shared vertical/horizontal linkage and opposite-leg lateral transfer. This is brief elastic practice, not endurance conditioning.

Primary result: Link a few elastic contacts without losing foot placement or pelvic control, keeping every effort recovered.

Anchor/marker: E1 — Record clean rope-clearance landings in each six-hop set: one rope turn per small two-foot jump, stable height/position and no rhythm loss. A caught rope or extra hop is recorded as an attempt/fault, never erased with more reps. This task's timing is not a running-cadence prescription.

Prior records: Classes 1–6 are saved prescriptions with no actual response. Class 6 had 26 landings including ten unilateral events and seven primary rounds. Class 7's very short linked tasks total 29 landings including twelve unilateral events; the increased coupling, not that small numeric change, is the main new demand. Primary work stays at seven rounds with reduced hinge/calf sets compared with early classes. Actual response remains unknown.

Choice comparison and novelty: All six default tasks add a genuine coupling constraint: rope synchronization, same-leg forward double contact, same-leg lateral reversal, bilateral vertical linkage, bilateral horizontal linkage and opposite-leg lateral linkage. These are related progressions of earlier isolated/contact tasks, not new exercise families created by renaming. Reusing known isolated mechanics as replacements is intentional and is not counted as additional work or novelty.

Dosage/audit decisions: Rejected longer hopping ladders and fatigue-based repeatability tests. Limited all non-rope tasks to one short sequence per stated side and primary strength to seven rounds. Because linked contacts can be harder despite a modest event count, every linkage has a fully dosed isolated replacement. The rope task remains bilateral low contact practice, not a disguised run. Contact totals and internal transitions were separately audited.

Conditional progression: Linked execution is conditional on current controlled receiving and rebound intent at the listed small amplitude; saved earlier classes do not establish that ability. If isolated routes are used, log them and hold linkage for future instruction. Increase no height, distance, load or contact count automatically. Class 8 should emphasize isolated forward weight acceptance to balance this coupling emphasis.
