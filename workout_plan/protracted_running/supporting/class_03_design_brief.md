# Class 3 stimulus and choice review

The limiting demand is stance-leg force with the pelvis stacked over the foot. E1 provides small lateral single-leg push/acceptance practice; E2 separates bilateral takeoff from unilateral receiving, E3 gives a lower-balance frontal push, E4 adds rapid supported elevation, E5 makes hip drive asymmetric, and E6 accelerates the free thigh without trunk lean. Light hip rotation/adduction work supports the same stance objective.

Primary result: Produce and receive force over one stance leg while keeping the pelvis controlled.

Anchor/marker: E1 — Within the three scheduled lateral hops per leg, count landings held for 2 s with the knee aligned to the foot and pelvis controlled, without an extra step. Keep the small distance the same on each attempt; this is not a test of maximum lateral distance.

Prior records: Class 2 prescribed 31 two-foot landing events and no single-leg landings, with 9 primary rounds. Class 1 included 14 single-leg events. Neither was reported performed. Class 3 limits its unilateral receiving dose to 10 events and retains all strength roles while changing the bilateral RDL to a supported single-leg hinge with the same 12 total working reps. Actual response remains unknown.

Choice comparison and novelty: All six default explosive tasks have new mechanical identities relative to Classes 1–2: lateral same-leg acceptance, two-to-one receiving, shared lateral projection, supported no-flight elevation, kickstand ballistic hip loading and unilateral grounded thigh acceleration. The two hinge styles and hip-flexion tasks overlap by tissues but differ in support, speed and load; dose remains modest. No new drill is counted solely because its load changed.

Dosage/audit decisions: Rejected depth-drop and long bounds for an unknown-response cohort; only 16 total landings remain while unilateral receiving is introduced. Preserved 12 hinge working reps when changing support/laterality. Supine hip flexion stays a strength task while the standing band drive is light and fast; their combined dose is explicit. Added nonflight replacements so equipment or skill gaps do not trigger extra impact.

Conditional progression: This is a conditional shift in stance demand, not evidence that Class 2 was tolerated. Require control of the nominated receiving leg within the listed low efforts; otherwise use the stated simpler landing/support and record changed contacts. Keep familiar strength anchors at selected technical reserve. Class 4 will emphasize knee-flexed ankle force while consolidating overall demand.
