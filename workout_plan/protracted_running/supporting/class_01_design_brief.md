# Class 1 stimulus and selection brief

Written September 13, 2026, before the finalized exercise tables. This is a programming rationale, not an athlete observation.

**Performance result sought:** Establish gym support for repeatable distance-running stance: a responsive ankle, sufficient leg-force reserve, and a controlled pelvis. Running economy is an intended transfer outcome, not a measured result of this class.

**Movement demand:** Repeated weight acceptance and push-off on one leg, with rapid ankle force expression and coordinated hip/knee force. Keep this first exposure short enough that six strength roles remain productive.

**Anchor:** Stationary Bilateral Ankle Pogo, with repeatability observed within its prescribed sets. Single-leg pogos provide a smaller unilateral exposure; neither is a test of running pace, endurance or economy.

**Quality marker:** Count clean pogo landings within each eight-hop set: centred contacts, similar low height/rhythm, and no progressive deep knee bend or trunk drift. Record faults and stopped attempts. No make-up reps or separate test.

**Jobs required before selection:**

| Slots | Required jobs |
|---|---|
| E1–E6 | Repeated ankle spring; unilateral ankle spring; short forward single-leg force acceptance after a quick push; rapid hip/knee extension from stillness; asymmetric leg drive; ballistic hip extension without another landing. |
| S1–S2 | Active arch control; low-fatigue hip-abductor strengthening for pelvic support. |
| P1–P6 | Unilateral knee/hip force; loaded hip hinge; bent-knee plantarflexor force; straight-knee plantarflexor force; hamstring knee-flexion/long-lever control; resisted hip flexion with a stable pelvis. |

**Consequential choices:** Stationary pogos retain a repeatable spring task without running or a cadence drill. Short same-leg forward hops earn a small dose for horizontal weight acceptance; maximal bounds and long traveling series would add landing demand unrelated to today's repeatability priority. A paused squat jump adds force from stillness, while a fixed-stance split jump requires unequal leg contributions; these receive only three efforts per stance, not equal volume to the ankle anchor. A kettlebell swing adds rapid hip extension with planted feet, avoiding another impact exercise or an unrelated upper-body throw.

The floor split squat is preferred to a rear-foot-elevated or step-up main lift because it permits meaningful leg loading without assuming suitable box/bench height or making balance the limiter. A bilateral dumbbell RDL supplies loadable hip extension without stacking another balance-demanding primary lift. Bent- and straight-knee calf work share tissues but have different knee-position demands; the standing version gets only one set per leg. The walkout loads hamstrings through changing knee angle, distinct from the RDL's hip hinge. Supine resisted hip flexion supplies swing-leg force without turning a band march into running or a second stabilization drill.

**Constraints:** Ages 12–14 with established relevant technique; actual readiness unknown. No running, sprinting, skipping, conditioning, tumbling or added preparation sequence. Exactly 6/2/6. No rack, cable station, treadmill, sliding discs or throwing wall assumed. Bands, dumbbells, kettlebells and benches are listed; their size, form and quantities still require matching to the cohort. Access & Prepare 1 content/time is unavailable. The supplied 10 m lanes govern this plan despite an older 12 m facility reference.

**Dose revision to carry into the class:** Use two working sets only for the three main force roles; one working set for each remaining primary role. Keep single-leg pogo exposure to four hops per side and each isolated jump task to three efforts per stance. Use light stabilization work for one set per side. Protect recovery and record the session estimate separately from the unknown existing preparation.
