## Primary research and official specialist/style sources

1. [Overtime Athletes — Create a Plyometric Training Program](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/create-plyometric-training-program/). Style inspiration: low connected ankle work, single-leg control and purposeful hip/knee/ankle force tasks. This curriculum uses short counted sets and selected lifts around one objective. It does not copy a proprietary program, import running drills or adopt a mandatory phase schedule.
2. [Støren et al., 2008 — Maximal strength training improves running economy in distance runners](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18460997/?dopt=Abstract). A small randomized study of trained runners reported improved running economy after supplementary heavy strength training alongside normal endurance training. It supports strength as a possible complement to running. The study's four-repetition-maximum squat protocol is not prescribed to this youth cohort; neither its dose nor its effect size validates this class.
3. [Spurrs, Murphy & Watsford, 2003 — The effect of plyometric training on distance running performance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12627298/). Seventeen male runners were assigned to normal running or added plyometrics over six weeks; the added-training group improved running economy and 3 km performance. This is evidence for a possible supplemental method, not for gym-only endurance training, this age-specific prescription or a guarantee of benefit from individual pogo/hop choices.
4. [Faigenbaum et al., 2009 — NSCA Youth Resistance Training position statement](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf). Official specialist guidance supports qualified instruction, technique-based loading, individual progression, short controlled power sets, appropriate recovery and workload logs. The current 2–3 RIR target, supporting one-set doses and detailed rests are coaching decisions for the supplied baseline, not quoted study thresholds. This older statement is not represented as newly published guidance.

No source here demonstrates that side-lying hip abduction, short-foot work, isolated hip flexion or this specific combination independently improves distance-running performance. Their inclusion is a mechanical programming inference. The two calf positions create different knee-position demands; neither is claimed to isolate one muscle completely. Quiet landing observation does not measure tendon stiffness, force or loading rate. Higher gym jump scores would not establish improved endurance or pacing control.
