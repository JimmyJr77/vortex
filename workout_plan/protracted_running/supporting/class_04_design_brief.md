# Class 4 stimulus and choice review

The day's demand is rapid plantarflexor action when the knee is already flexed, a stance-support condition. E1 is the main shallow-knee spring task. Small forward travel and a short obstacle pair add contact placement; seated takeoff supplies knee/hip force from a supported start; two no-flight tasks supply rapid bent-knee ankle and hip extension. Light lateral-ankle control and pelvis control avoid a calf pre-fatigue block.

Primary result: Express ankle force with a flexed knee while retaining a stable trunk and controlled foot platform.

Anchor/marker: E1 — Count five low bounces per set that preserve the declared shallow knee bend without progressively sinking or straightening. Keep height small and repeatable. This is a joint-position/control marker, not proof of soleus isolation or a contact-time target.

Prior records: Class 3 prescribed 16 landings including 10 single-leg events, eight primary rounds and a supported single-leg hinge. No response is known. Class 4 removes single-leg jump receiving and restores the familiar bilateral RDL with the same twelve working hinge reps; the bent-knee calf lift moves first in primary strength without a preset load increase. Actual response remains unknown.

Choice comparison and novelty: All six default drill executions are new, but the seated squat jump and fast calf raise are explicitly related variants of known force patterns rather than unrelated inventions. The seated start removes standing pre-load; the knee-flexed quick raise changes force-speed intent from seated strength. Forward pogo travel differs from fore-aft target reversal. Rejected extra single-leg ankle variants because the day already concentrates plantarflexor load.

Dosage/audit decisions: Kept the main bent-knee spring to ten contacts and total jumps to 23, with no single-leg receiving. A proposed longer hurdle series was reduced to two pairs with explicit contact counts. Used eversion and easy trunk work instead of another calf isometric pre-fatigue task. The rapid seated calf task is only six light reps; its overlap with challenging P1 is explicit and requires calf/Achilles recovery review.

Conditional progression: The knee-flexed emphasis changes organization, not an earned load or volume step. Retain P1's 2 × 8 and recorded knee angle/heel range for a later comparison. Advance resistance alone only with reported control, technical reserve and acceptable subsequent recovery; otherwise hold/reduce. Class 5 should shift the emphasis toward hip drive and hamstring force while reducing ankle concentration.
