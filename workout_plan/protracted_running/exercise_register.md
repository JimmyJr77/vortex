# Exercise and novelty register — complete sequence

The 72 explosive slots use 69 named execution profiles, including related variants, with three deliberate default repeats in Class 12. This is **not a claim of 69 unrelated exercises or movement families**. Vector, support, coupling and joint-action changes are described in each class; a label or load change alone is not evidence of a distinct job. Exact variation counts describe the written defaults only, not substitutions or athlete experience. Strength/control exercises intentionally recur.

A local slug identifies an inspected repository source, not proof of current production approval or athlete eligibility. Where no matching exact binding was verified, **proposed addition/variant** is stated and no canonical identifier is invented. Slugs below were checked in `docs/exercise-difficulty-review.csv`, except the explicitly identified generated box-jump source. See [source/evidence limits](supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md). No exercise database was changed.

## Novelty audit decisions

- Class 12 E1 returns the original first-position 2 × 8 pogo marker for a potentially valid comparison; it is not renamed. E5 repeats Class 6’s split-stance toss; E6 repeats Class 1’s two-hand swing. The latter two keep familiar nonflight hip-force practice around the final receiving variants.
- The forward toss, fast heel rise and held-ball drive appeared earlier as contingencies. Their later default use may not be novel to an athlete who actually performed those options. No such usage is known.
- Low box, raised single-leg receiving, no-switch/switch landings, static/countermovement starts and linked/isolated contacts are **related variants**. Their different receiving, force-initiation or coupling jobs are specified; none is claimed to be a separate anatomical family.
- Speed versions of strength patterns use different load/intent and separate performances. They are not double-counted across sections. Class 11’s six nonflight tasks contain real brief rapid-force work, not zero-dose placeholders or a repackaged warm-up.
- The program prioritizes drill variety under the updated request. It does not claim that this much variety outperforms more repeated practice; a coach may replace a new variant with its familiar stated regression when readiness demands it.
