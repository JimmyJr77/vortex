# Protracted Running — Class 2 of 12

**Primary effort:** Keep ankle return centred while the support position changes slightly, preserving leg-force reserve for repeated stance.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision. Previous classes are prescriptions; actual completion, loads, response and recovery remain unknown. This is a conditional next exposure, not earned overload.

**Scope and style:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. Overtime Athletes-inspired elastic, unilateral and force training, adapted for gym support. No actual running, sprints, skipping, conditioning or tumbling. Gym work does not directly supply or test endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions. [Style/evidence and limits](../supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md).

**Daily stimulus brief:** The demand is accurate low-amplitude foot return, then organized force from split or single-leg support. E1 supplies the repeatability marker. Split-stance spring, supported upward projection, unilateral push, forward hip-driven release and unloaded-by-bodyweight ankle acceleration fill different supporting jobs. Light toe-lift and knee-control work precede the unchanged six strength roles.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Count accurate fore-aft line-hop landings within each six-contact set: return to the two declared positions without progressive drift, excess knee bend or rhythm loss. One crossing is one hop; six hops, not six out-and-back pairs. Compare the two sets only, not with Class 1 stationary pogo scores. Use scheduled attempts only; record failures/stops, not make-up reps. Qualitative control is not a measurement of force, tendon stiffness, running economy or ground-contact time.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 is first and receives two brief sets. E2 changes stance at low amplitude; E3/E4 use isolated, fully reset takeoffs; E5/E6 add rapid force without further impacts. Bodyweight unless a light implement/band is specified. Ballistic loads must permit crisp acceleration without grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Line Pogo Forward-Back** | **2 × 6 hops**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Main accuracy task: produce a quick ankle return while controlling very small fore-aft displacement. Use a floor seam or two positions indicated by cones outside the landing area, less than one foot length apart. Jump across and back with both feet together, low height and soft knees. Shorten travel for drift; end a set if it becomes a squat-jump pattern. |
| 2 | **Split-Stance Pogo** | **1 × 5 hops per lead-leg stance**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Express ankle spring with unequal front/rear leg contributions while keeping the pelvis facing forward. Small floor split stance; both feet leave and return together, no leg switching. Stay tall with soft knees. Reduce split length/amplitude if the rear ankle or pelvis cannot stay controlled. |
| 3 | **Box Jump — low box, step-down reset** | **1 × 3 jumps**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Create rapid knee/hip extension while limiting the fall distance of the landing through a low raised target. Use a stable low box that permits landing without a knee tuck. One dip, jump up, land on both feet and hold 2 s; step down, never jump down. Lower the target for a deep crouched landing. The box height is not a jump-height test. |
| 4 | **Single-Leg Vertical Jump to Two-Foot Landing** | **1 × 3 jumps per takeoff leg**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 15 s between repetitions | Make each leg supply the upward push while using a simpler bilateral finish to control receiving demand. Dip on the takeoff leg, push quickly over a modest height, place both feet underneath and hold 2 s. Lower height if the pelvis rotates or feet reach forward. Do not compare this with a same-leg pogo. |
| 5 | **Forward Medicine Ball Scoop Toss** | **2 × 3 throws**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Accelerate a light implement forward through coordinated leg/hip extension without extra landing demand. Face forward with ball low; one shallow dip, drive and release forward/slightly up. Feet remain planted, no catch, no trunk twist. Reduce ball mass if arms dominate or the back arches; walk to retrieve only after it stops. |
| 6 | **Seated Straight-Knee Band Plantarflexion — fast press** | **1 × 6 presses per side**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Practice quick ankle force expression against light resistance with no additional bodyweight impact. Sit on the floor, target heel grounded and knee softly extended; loop an intact band around forefoot and hold both ends securely away from the face. Press the forefoot away rapidly, return over 2 s. Keep the leg still; use less tension for jerking or toe curling. |

**Quality and recovery:** Every attempted flight counts, even an invalid landing. Never add demonstration/test/make-up repetitions. A sequence contains only the stated linked contacts; walk between setups. Keep the same left/right order and surface within a comparison. Stop the set for visible speed/control loss; lower the stated amplitude/resistance or extend rest 30–60 s before any remaining scheduled attempt. Stop affected work for pain, limping, dizziness or persisting loss of control.

**Exercise-specific equipment/readiness route:** Use low boxes only after verifying height, stability and full-foot room. If E3 has no suitable box, use a low countermovement jump to stick instead, 1 × 3 with the same rest; three two-foot landings remain but are on the floor. E5 requires verified containment of flight, bounce and retrieval within an exclusive 10 m lane; if unavailable, use the familiar two-hand kettlebell swing instead, 2 × 3 with 90 s after sets, replacing six throws with six swings. For E6, if no band can be securely held, use a supported fast straight-knee heel raise instead, 1 × 6 per leg, 60 s after sides, rapid rise/2 s return with no flight; record the different load-bearing condition. All alternatives are replacements, never additions. If no suitable version of a required task is available, record an incomplete/modified delivery; the written fourteen entries do not oblige an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep at least five clean reps available, or equivalent ease for holds; no burning/shaking or pre-fatigue goal.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heel-Supported Toe Raise** | **1 × 10 reps, both feet**; 30 s after each set/side | Light dorsiflexor strengthening for foot clearance and controlled foot return. Hold stable bench support, keep both heels grounded and lift forefeet for 1 s, lower 2 s. Shift less weight onto heels if the shin work burns or the body rocks; no hopping. |
| 2 | **Step-Down to Hover — low forward reach** | **1 × 4 reps per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Lightly strengthen stance-knee/pelvic control as the opposite foot approaches the floor. Use a suitably low stable box; lower 3 s, free heel hovers, rise 1 s without pushing from it. Reduce range for inward knee drift. If no low step fits, use a supported shallow single-leg squat instead with the same dose/tempo, free foot clear. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use challenging resistance/leverage with about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; holds finish with about five clean seconds available. Coach-observed control supports the athlete’s effort report. No fixed weight or percentage increase, maximal test, forced rep or failure set. The table lists working sets.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **2 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain the main stance-leg knee/hip force anchor while the explosive foot-return task changes. Floor split stance, front foot planted, pelvis square; lower 2 s and rise firmly for 1 s. Reduce load/range for collapse or balance loss. Use one DB with stable hand support instead of two unsupported DBs if needed; counts stay the same. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **2 × 6 reps**; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain loadable hip-extension force without adding another balance-demanding lift. Soft knees, hips back, DBs close; lower 3 s and rise in 1 s. Stop the descent before back rounding; reduce load/range if the hinge becomes spinal movement. |
| 3 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **2 × 8 reps bilateral**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain bent-knee ankle force so new spring drills do not also trigger a calf-volume increase. Both legs together, knees near 90°, heels start on floor, hands secure DBs on lower thighs above knees. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load for bounce/lost heel height. If bench fit is unsuitable, use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead, same reps/rest, and log changed knee angle. |
| 4 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg** | **1 × 8 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain the smaller straight-knee calf exposure alongside the main knee-flexed work. Work from floor, near-straight soft knee, one DB and stable hand support. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load or use bodyweight for lost range; use 1 × 8 bilateral instead of per-leg work if even unilateral bodyweight is too demanding, with 1 × 4 bilateral preparation. |
| 5 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 cycles**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain knee-flexor control through changing knee angle after the hip-dominant hinge. Keep a floor bridge while four small alternating heel steps go out and four return; eight steps at about 1 s each = one full cycle. Choose reach so four cycles are challenging with 2–3 whole cycles available. Shorten reach for pelvic sag or cramping; do not add steps or cycles. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Keep a challenging leg-recovery action under a stable pelvis, distinct from the light toe-lift work. Secure loop band around both forefeet; nonworking leg straight with heel grounded. Flex working hip/knee toward thigh-vertical in 2 s, hold 1 s, return 2 s without arching or moving the anchor heel. Reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt. If a secure loop is unavailable, use seated DB hip flexion instead with hands securing the DB above the knee: same dose/rest, small controlled thigh lift; preparation unloaded. |

**Separate load-preparation sets in these same exercises:** Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides: 1 × 6 reps per side, then 30 s; 1 × 3 reps per side, then 90 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells: 1 × 5 reps, then 60 s. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells: 1 × 6 reps, then 45 s. Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg: 1 × 4 reps per side, then 45 s; 30 s to change sides on each preparation round. Glute Bridge Walkout: 1 × 1 cycles, then 45 s. Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion: 1 × 3 reps per side, then 45 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Use the listed working tempo with lighter resistance/shorter lever; for time holds use the stated shorter duration. Log further preparation and add its time. These are not new exercise slots or challenging working sets.
