# Protracted Running — Class 5 of 12

**Primary effort:** Create hip-driven forward force and controlled hamstring contribution without adding a large ankle-rebound dose.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision. Previous classes are prescriptions; actual completion, loads, response and recovery remain unknown. This is a conditional next exposure, not earned overload.

**Scope and style:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. Overtime Athletes-inspired elastic, unilateral and force training, adapted for gym support. No actual running, sprints, skipping, conditioning or tumbling. Gym work does not directly supply or test endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions. [Style/evidence and limits](../supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md).

**Daily stimulus brief:** The target is force from the hip behind the stance leg and active lower-leg recovery. E1 anchors a paused forward push. Split and single-leg takeoffs change leg contribution while preserving two-foot receiving. Offset swing and hinge-dominant release express hip force against light implements; rapid knee flexion addresses leg recovery. The primary hinge and knee-flexor strength roles receive priority while calf work is reduced.

**Quality marker inside E1:** In each two-attempt set, record a true two-second pause before the forward push and a stable two-foot landing held for two seconds. Record re-dips and balance steps. Keep the modest distance/start depth comparable within the task; do not compare it with a countermovement or vertical jump. Use scheduled attempts only; record failures/stops, not make-up reps. Qualitative control is not a measurement of force, tendon stiffness, running economy or ground-contact time.

**Prior-work review:** Class 4 prescribed 23 bilateral landings and nine primary rounds with concentrated ankle work. Actual completion/recovery remain unknown. Class 5 uses only twelve planned landings, reduces bent-knee calf work to one set and temporarily omits the separate straight-knee calf role in favor of a supported hip-extension finish. The [preceding class](class_04.md), map, ledger, feedback and unresolved delivery issues were reviewed before this prescription. No actual athlete data was added.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 comes first with the greatest isolated forward-push allocation. E2/E3 each use only two attempts per takeoff stance. E4–E6 have no flight; full recovery protects fast hip/knee action. Bodyweight unless a light implement/band is specified. Ballistic loads must permit crisp acceleration without grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Paused Broad Jump to Stick** | **2 × 2 jumps**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Main hip/leg force task: push forward from stillness without borrowing a countermovement. Hands on hips; hold a comfortable partial squat for 2 s, push a modest distance forward without re-dipping, land on both feet and hold 2 s. Reduce distance/start depth for lost control. No distance contest. |
| 2 | **Split-Stance Forward Jump to Parallel Landing** | **1 × 2 jumps per lead-leg stance**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Bias forward leg drive from a split stance while simplifying the finish to two parallel feet. Small stationary split stance; both feet push, travel a short distance forward and land with feet parallel beneath the body for 2 s. No leg exchange in flight. Shorten travel if pelvis twists or the back foot supplies nearly all the push. |
| 3 | **Single-Leg Broad Jump to Two-Foot Landing** | **1 × 2 jumps per takeoff leg**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Isolate each leg's forward push without increasing the single-leg landing requirement. Stand on one foot, use a small dip and natural arms, push forward over a short controllable distance and place both feet underneath for a 2 s stick. Reduce distance for pelvic tilt or a reaching heel-first landing. |
| 4 | **One-Arm Kettlebell Swing** | **1 × 4 swings per arm**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last | Express hip extension against offset loading while resisting unwanted trunk rotation during the force transfer. Bilateral feet, light bell in one hand, no hand-to-hand release. Hinge and snap tall; bell floats below chest height. Park before changing arms. Reduce load if grip, shoulder lifting or rotation limits hip speed. |
| 5 | **Hip-Hinge Medicine Ball Forward Toss** | **1 × 3 throws**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Emphasize rapid hip extension through a small fixed knee bend, contrasting with the earlier knee/hip scoop. Light ball low between thighs, knees softly bent and nearly steady. Hinge back, drive hips tall and release forward with both hands; feet stay grounded. No deep squat, back arch or return catch. Keep the flight low and contained. |
| 6 | **Prone Band Leg Curl — fast concentric** | **1 × 4 curls per side**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Accelerate knee flexion for lower-leg recovery while keeping the hip/pelvis still. Prone on a comfortable floor, light band safely attached around the working ankle and a verified low fixed anchor beyond the feet. Flex knee quickly only toward 90°, return over 2 s. Keep thighs/pelvis grounded; reduce tension for hip lift or cramping. Secure attachment must not slip or snap toward the athlete. |

**Quality and recovery:** Every attempted flight counts, even an invalid landing. Never add demonstration/test/make-up repetitions. A sequence contains only the stated linked contacts; walk between setups. Keep the same left/right order and surface within a comparison. Stop the set for visible speed/control loss; lower the stated amplitude/resistance or extend rest 30–60 s before any remaining scheduled attempt. Stop affected work for pain, limping, dizziness or persisting loss of control.

**Exercise-specific equipment/readiness route:** E4 requires prior competent one-arm bell handling; use two-hand swings instead if needed, 1 × 8 total, same light fast intent and 90 s afterward, no flight. E5 requires an exclusive contained throwing/retrieval lane; use a light two-hand hip-hinge ball swing without release instead, 1 × 3 with 20 s resets and 90 s afterward, replacing three throws with three held-ball accelerations. For E6 without a verified safe band/anchor, use unresisted prone fast leg curls instead, same dose/tempo/rest; gravity/body-segment acceleration supplies the small rapid task without claiming equivalent resistance. No unlisted anchor is assumed available. All alternatives are replacements, never additions. If no suitable version of a required task is available, record an incomplete/modified delivery; the written fourteen entries do not oblige an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep at least five clean reps available, or equivalent ease for holds; no burning/shaking or pre-fatigue goal.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quadruped Bent-Knee Hip Extension** | **1 × 5 reps per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Light gluteal strengthening while controlling lumbar/pelvic motion before loaded hip extension. Hands/knees supported, one knee stays bent as that thigh moves backward only to trunk line. 2 s lift, 2 s return; no back arch or pelvic twist. Reduce range if the trunk changes shape; bodyweight only. |
| 2 | **Supine Long-Lever Heel-Dig Isometric** | **1 × 10 seconds, both heels**; 45 s after each set/side | Lightly load the hamstrings in a longer-knee position before the hinge/walkout work. Supine with knees softly bent and heels grounded well away from hips; press heels gently down/back without moving them or lifting the pelvis. Keep effort easy and breathing relaxed. Bring heels closer for cramping or strain; do not turn this into a maximal bridge hold. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use challenging resistance/leverage with about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; holds finish with about five clean seconds available. Coach-observed control supports the athlete’s effort report. No fixed weight or percentage increase, maximal test, forced rep or failure set. The table lists working sets.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **2 × 6 reps**; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Primary loaded hip-extension anchor for the day's hip-driven force emphasis. Soft knees, hips back, DBs close; lower 3 s and rise in 1 s. Stop the descent before back rounding; reduce load/range if the hinge becomes spinal movement. |
| 2 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **2 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain knee/hip force from each stance leg alongside the hinge emphasis. Floor split stance, front foot planted, pelvis square; lower 2 s and rise firmly for 1 s. Reduce load/range for collapse or balance loss. Use one DB with stable hand support instead of two unsupported DBs if needed; counts stay the same. |
| 3 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 cycles**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Prioritize challenging knee-angle hamstring control, distinct from the hinge and brief isometric preparation. Keep a floor bridge while four small alternating heel steps go out and four return; eight steps at about 1 s each = one full cycle. Choose reach so four cycles are challenging with 2–3 whole cycles available. Shorten reach for pelvic sag or cramping; do not add steps or cycles. |
| 4 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **1 × 8 reps bilateral**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Keep a small knee-flexed ankle-force exposure while reducing plantarflexor concentration. Both legs together, knees near 90°, heels start on floor, hands secure DBs on lower thighs above knees. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load for bounce/lost heel height. If bench fit is unsuitable, use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead, same reps/rest, and log changed knee angle. |
| 5 | **Hip Thrust — dumbbell across pelvis** | **1 × 8 reps**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Add supported hip-extension finish force to complement the RDL's flexed-hip range. Upper back on stable bench, feet planted, hands secure pelvic DB. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s; finish ribs/pelvis aligned. Reduce load for lumbar arching. Use a loaded floor Glute Bridge instead if bench fit/stability fails, with the same dose/rest and changed support logged. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain challenging thigh-recovery strength with a controlled pelvis. Secure loop band around both forefeet; nonworking leg straight with heel grounded. Flex working hip/knee toward thigh-vertical in 2 s, hold 1 s, return 2 s without arching or moving the anchor heel. Reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt. If a secure loop is unavailable, use seated DB hip flexion instead with hands securing the DB above the knee: same dose/rest, small controlled thigh lift; preparation unloaded. |

**Separate load-preparation sets in these same exercises:** Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells: 1 × 5 reps, then 60 s. Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides: 1 × 6 reps per side, then 30 s; 1 × 3 reps per side, then 90 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Glute Bridge Walkout: 1 × 1 cycles, then 45 s. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells: 1 × 6 reps, then 45 s. Hip Thrust — dumbbell across pelvis: 1 × 5 reps, then 45 s. Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion: 1 × 3 reps per side, then 45 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Use the listed working tempo with lighter resistance/shorter lever; for time holds use the stated shorter duration. Log further preparation and add its time. These are not new exercise slots or challenging working sets.

**Prescribed workload:** 12 landing events (12 two-foot, 0 single-leg), 24 individual foot contacts; primary 8 rounds / 11 bouts, 64 reps + 4 cycles; preparation 7 rounds / 10 bouts, 40 reps + 1 cycles. Other explosive work: 8 swings of One-Arm Kettlebell Swing; 3 throws of Hip-Hinge Medicine Ball Forward Toss; 8 curls of Prone Band Leg Curl — fast concentric. E1 four + E2 four + E3 four = twelve two-foot landing events, 24 individual foot contacts. E3's takeoff is unilateral but receiving is bilateral. Other default explosive work: eight offset swings, three hinge-dominant throws and eight fast knee curls. The hamstring hold is ten seconds total with both heels, not ten repetitions. No paired hip-hinge/scoop repetition is counted as two drills. Replacement throws and no-release swings must be logged separately. These totals exclude unknown preparation-sequence and outside workload. A round includes both sides; separately performed sides are separate bouts. Unlike landings/throws/lifts are not equivalent units of stress. [Full per-exercise ledger](../supporting/class_05_record.json).

**Delivery and timing:** Allow about **70–80 min beyond Access & Prepare 1** for one coached athlete or an efficiently equipped small wave, including stated recovery, lift preparation, handling and notes. The full class duration is unresolved. Maintain this exercise order; no timed circuit. Use separate cleared bays within the three separate 10 m lanes; check width, queues and equipment quantities onsite. Rest after the last set may include the next setup once, not twice. Bigger groups, scarce implements or extra instruction extend the clock. Check any 60/90-minute booking against actual preparation and group flow rather than borrowing another program’s timetable. **Scheduling:** Review actual recent/upcoming running, races, sport, gym and tumbling outside this class. Roughly 48–72 h from another comparably demanding lower-body exposure is an initial planning reference, not automatic clearance or a weekly calendar. These short sets can still load calf/Achilles, knee extensors and hamstrings substantially. Move or reduce a session when actual recovery requires it.

**Progression note:** Retain the RDL and split-squat prescriptions unless actual execution/effort warrants a load adjustment; no automatic increase. New swing/throw/curl conditions require demonstrated control at their small doses. Class 6 should consolidate pelvic control with modest lateral demands, not assume these hip-focused tasks were performed.
