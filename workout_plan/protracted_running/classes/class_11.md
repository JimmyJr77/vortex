# Protracted Running — Class 11 of 12

**Primary effort:** Maintain rapid stance-support force with no planned jump landings, preparing for a controlled final comparison exposure.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision. Previous classes are prescriptions; actual completion, loads, response and recovery remain unknown. This is a conditional next exposure, not earned overload.

**Scope and style:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. Overtime Athletes-inspired elastic, unilateral and force training, adapted for gym support. No actual running, sprints, skipping, conditioning or tumbling. Gym work does not directly supply or test endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions. [Style/evidence and limits](../supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md).

**Daily stimulus brief:** The emphasis is force expression with supported feet and a quiet pelvis, preserving training quality without an impact requirement. E1 anchors quick knee-flexed ankle force. Long-lever hip drive, split-base held-implement drive, resisted hip extension, dorsiflexion and hip abduction cover the distinct support/recovery actions. Every rapid task is brief and light; familiar light foot/adductor control precedes six challenging strength roles.

**Quality marker inside E1:** For each leg, compare the two sets of three heel rises: similar heel height, steady small knee bend, no ankle roll and a visibly crisp rise. Forefoot stays grounded. This is execution consistency, not measured power, tendon stiffness or clearance for more impact. Use scheduled attempts only; record failures/stops, not make-up reps. Qualitative control is not a measurement of force, tendon stiffness, running economy or ground-contact time.

Class 11 uses zero planned flight and retains six primary rounds as a deliberate support option; this is not a claim the athlete needs a taper or is sore. Any actual competition schedule still governs placement. The [preceding class](class_10.md), map, ledger, feedback and unresolved delivery issues were reviewed before this prescription. ## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 is the main rapid ankle-force observation. E2/E3 express hip force while supported; E4–E6 accelerate individual leg/foot actions against light resistance without turning them into running drills or a continuous circuit. Bodyweight unless a light implement/band is specified. Ballistic loads must permit crisp acceleration without grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Supported Single-Leg Bent-Knee Calf Rise — fast concentric** | **2 × 3 heel rises per leg**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last | Main no-impact task: express ankle force quickly under a stable flexed-knee single-leg support position. Stable hand support, small knee bend, working forefoot grounded. Rise quickly and lower over 2 s while holding approximately the same knee angle. Bodyweight or a very light DB only if speed/control remain. Use more hand unloading or a small kickstand foot instead if bodyweight is too demanding. |
| 2 | **Long-Lever Heel-Bridge Drive — fast concentric** | **1 × 4 hip drives**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last | Express hip extension with a more open knee angle so hamstrings contribute without a jumping demand. Supine, heels grounded farther from hips than a normal bridge, knees still softly bent. Drive hips up a small amount quickly, return over 2 s; feet/shoulders never launch. Bring heels closer for cramping or back arch. This changes lever/joint position, not a new hip-extension family. |
| 3 | **Split-Stance Held-Ball Hip Drive — no release** | **1 × 3 drives per lead-leg stance**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 5 s between repetitions | Accelerate a light external mass using the legs/hips while the split base controls pelvic rotation. Light ball held low close to body, short split stance; shallow knee/hip bend then drive tall quickly while carrying the ball only to waist/lower-chest height. No release, jump, overhead lift or arm-only heave. Return over 2 s; reduce load/stance offset for twisting. |
| 4 | **Supported Band Hip Extension — fast back drive** | **1 × 4 back drives per side**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Accelerate the thigh backward through a small hip-extension range without substituting lumbar movement. Light band at working ankle to a verified low anchor in front, stable hand support. Stance foot planted; drive the working thigh slightly behind the body with knee soft, return over 2 s. Keep pelvis facing forward. Reduce band/range for back arch or pelvic rotation. |
| 5 | **Seated Band Dorsiflexion — fast toe-up** | **1 × 4 toe lifts per side**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Accelerate forefoot lift against light resistance for the foot-clearance component of leg recovery. Sit with heel supported, light band around forefoot to a verified low anchor ahead. Pull toes/forefoot toward shin quickly, return over 2 s; knee/heel stay still. Reduce tension for toe curling or whole-leg pulling. No forced end-range stretch. |
| 6 | **Supported Band Hip Abduction — quick lateral lift** | **1 × 4 lateral lifts per side**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Express short-range lateral hip force while keeping the stance pelvis controlled. Suitable light loop around ankles, stable hand support. Stance foot planted; move the other straight-but-soft leg a small distance sideways quickly, return over 2 s and reset. No side step, torso lean or pelvis hike. Use less tension/range for compensation. |

**Quality and recovery:** Every attempted flight counts, even an invalid landing. Never add demonstration/test/make-up repetitions. A sequence contains only the stated linked contacts; walk between setups. Keep the same left/right order and surface within a comparison. Stop the set for visible speed/control loss; lower the stated amplitude/resistance or extend rest 30–60 s before any remaining scheduled attempt. Stop affected work for pain, limping, dizziness or persisting loss of control.

**Exercise-specific equipment/readiness route:** Band anchors/attachments in E4/E5 must be verified secure; the supplied inventory does not confirm them. Use unresisted supported hip-extension drives instead of E4 if the anchor is unavailable, same count/tempo/rest. Use heel-supported fast forefoot lifts against gravity instead of E5, same count/tempo/rest, if no safe anchored band fits. E6 may be unresisted instead if no suitable loop exists. All remain brief rapid-force practice but changed resistance is logged, not claimed equivalent. Choose stable hand-support heights and a comfortable floor surface for E2; no treadmill, sled, cable station or running lane traversal is needed. All alternatives are replacements, never additions. If no suitable version of a required task is available, record an incomplete/modified delivery; the written fourteen entries do not oblige an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep at least five clean reps available, or equivalent ease for holds; no burning/shaking or pre-fatigue goal.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill — bilateral supported stance** | **1 × 3 five-second holds per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Maintain familiar light active-arch support while limiting unnecessary novelty before the final exposure. Both feet supported, one target arch gently shortens while toes stay long and heel remains grounded; relax between holds. Use seated execution instead if standing causes toe grip. Do not increase hold force to create fatigue. |
| 2 | **Side-Lying Hip Adduction** | **1 × 5 reps per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Retain easy adductor force for pelvic support, complementing the rapid abductor task. Top foot supported on floor in front, bottom leg straight; lift bottom thigh 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s without rolling. Bodyweight only, smaller range if effort becomes strenuous. Stop for groin discomfort or cramping. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use challenging resistance/leverage with about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; holds finish with about five clean seconds available. Coach-observed control supports the athlete’s effort report. No fixed weight or percentage increase, maximal test, forced rep or failure set. The table lists working sets.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain one challenging stance-leg knee/hip force set per leg. Floor split stance, front foot planted, pelvis square; lower 2 s and rise firmly for 1 s. Reduce load/range for collapse or balance loss. Use one DB with stable hand support instead of two unsupported DBs if needed; counts stay the same. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6 reps**; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain a small loadable hip-hinge force exposure. Soft knees, hips back, DBs close; lower 3 s and rise in 1 s. Stop the descent before back rounding; reduce load/range if the hinge becomes spinal movement. |
| 3 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **1 × 8 reps bilateral**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain controlled knee-flexed ankle strength separately from E1's fast light task. Both legs together, knees near 90°, heels start on floor, hands secure DBs on lower thighs above knees. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load for bounce/lost heel height. If bench fit is unsuitable, use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead, same reps/rest, and log changed knee angle. |
| 4 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg** | **1 × 8 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain the established straight-knee calf strength exposure without flight. Work from floor, near-straight soft knee, one DB and stable hand support. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load or use bodyweight for lost range; use 1 × 8 bilateral instead of per-leg work if even unilateral bodyweight is too demanding, with 1 × 4 bilateral preparation. |
| 5 | **Dumbbell Glute Bridge — floor** | **1 × 8 reps**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Use a supported hip-extension finish to retain challenging force work without a long walkout demand. Hands secure a DB across pelvis. From knees bent and feet planted, rise 1 s, hold aligned ribs/pelvis 1 s, lower 2 s. Finish through hip extension, not back arching. Reduce load for lumbar extension or unstable implement. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain challenging supported hip-flexor force for leg recovery. Secure loop band around both forefeet; nonworking leg straight with heel grounded. Flex working hip/knee toward thigh-vertical in 2 s, hold 1 s, return 2 s without arching or moving the anchor heel. Reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt. If a secure loop is unavailable, use seated DB hip flexion instead with hands securing the DB above the knee: same dose/rest, small controlled thigh lift; preparation unloaded. |

**Separate load-preparation sets in these same exercises:** Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides: 1 × 6 reps per side, then 30 s; 1 × 3 reps per side, then 90 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells: 1 × 5 reps, then 60 s. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells: 1 × 6 reps, then 45 s. Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg: 1 × 4 reps per side, then 45 s; 30 s to change sides on each preparation round. Dumbbell Glute Bridge — floor: 1 × 5 reps, then 45 s. Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion: 1 × 3 reps per side, then 45 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Use the listed working tempo with lighter resistance/shorter lever; for time holds use the stated shorter duration. Log further preparation and add its time. These are not new exercise slots or challenging working sets.
