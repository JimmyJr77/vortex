# Protracted Running — Class 7 of 12

**Primary effort:** Link a few elastic contacts without losing foot placement or pelvic control, keeping every effort recovered.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision. Previous classes are prescriptions; actual completion, loads, response and recovery remain unknown. This is a conditional next exposure, not earned overload.

**Scope and style:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. Overtime Athletes-inspired elastic, unilateral and force training, adapted for gym support. No actual running, sprints, skipping, conditioning or tumbling. Gym work does not directly supply or test endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions. [Style/evidence and limits](../supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md).

**Daily stimulus brief:** The demand is preserving control through the transition between landing and the next push. E1 offers a repeatable externally paced-by-rope bilateral ankle task, with no prescribed running cadence. The remaining tasks distinguish same-leg forward linkage, same-leg lateral reversal, shared vertical/horizontal linkage and opposite-leg lateral transfer. This is brief elastic practice, not endurance conditioning.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record clean rope-clearance landings in each six-hop set: one rope turn per small two-foot jump, stable height/position and no rhythm loss. A caught rope or extra hop is recorded as an attempt/fault, never erased with more reps. This task's timing is not a running-cadence prescription. Use scheduled attempts only; record failures/stops, not make-up reps. Qualitative control is not a measurement of force, tendon stiffness, running economy or ground-contact time.

**Prior-work review:** Classes 1–6 are saved prescriptions with no actual response. Class 6 had 26 landings including ten unilateral events and seven primary rounds. Class 7's very short linked tasks total 29 landings including twelve unilateral events; the increased coupling, not that small numeric change, is the main new demand. Primary work stays at seven rounds with reduced hinge/calf sets compared with early classes. The [preceding class](class_06.md), map, ledger, feedback and unresolved delivery issues were reviewed before this prescription. No actual athlete data was added.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 receives two short sets. Every other row is only one brief set or sequence per stated side. Use links only with current demonstrated control; the isolated replacements below preserve purposeful work without implying earned advancement. Bodyweight unless a light implement/band is specified. Ballistic loads must permit crisp acceleration without grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Jump Rope Bounce — two-foot single turns** | **2 × 6 jumps**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last | Main repeatability task: maintain small ankle contacts while coordinating one external rope cycle per jump. Use a fitted rope in a clear bay, both feet together, low flight and soft knees. Single turns only, no double-unders or alternating-foot jogging. Stop for catches or extra hops; use stationary bilateral pogos instead if the rope prevents a clean six-hop practice dose. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Forward Double Hop** | **1 × 1 two-hop sequences per leg**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last | Use one same-leg landing to initiate a second short forward push while preserving pelvic position. Two small forward hops on the same foot, at most two foot lengths total; link the first contact without a deliberate pause, hold the second landing 2 s. No starter bounce or extra third hop. Shorten travel if the intermediate contact collapses. |
| 3 | **Single-Leg Lateral Out-and-Back Hop Pair** | **1 × 1 two-hop sequences per leg**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last | Control a small same-leg lateral reversal while staying stacked over the foot. Hop outward a very small distance then return on the same foot in one linked pair; hold the final landing 2 s. Toes/pelvis face forward. Reduce travel for ankle rolling or knee drift; no crossover. |
| 4 | **Repeated Vertical Jump — three low connected jumps** | **1 × 1 three-jump sequences**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last | Link shared hip/knee support and push across several low vertical contacts, distinct from ankle-dominant rope work. Three modest two-foot countermovement jumps in place, natural arms, no intentional pause until a 2 s final stick. Allow an appropriate knee/hip bend; do not force a rigid contact or chase height. |
| 5 | **Repeated Broad Jump — two linked jumps** | **1 × 1 two-jump sequences**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last | Receive a modest shared forward push and redirect into a second, keeping the feet underneath the body. Two short bilateral forward jumps, no starter bounce, link once and stick the second for 2 s. Fit both within 2 m with clear room beyond. Shorten distance for heel reaching or excessive trunk dive. |
| 6 | **Skater Bound Continuous — four short transfers** | **1 × 1 four-bound sequences**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last | Link brief opposite-leg support transfers without turning them into a long lateral conditioning drill. Start left, bound right–left–right–left over a small lateral gap, then hold the final landing 2 s. Each foot receives twice. No extra return bound, long reaches or increasing speed after form changes. |

**Quality and recovery:** Every attempted flight counts, even an invalid landing. Never add demonstration/test/make-up repetitions. A sequence contains only the stated linked contacts; walk between setups. Keep the same left/right order and surface within a comparison. Stop the set for visible speed/control loss; lower the stated amplitude/resistance or extend rest 30–60 s before any remaining scheduled attempt. Stop affected work for pain, limping, dizziness or persisting loss of control.

**Exercise-specific equipment/readiness route:** Use a suitable rope only if six clean low hops are feasible; the E1 stationary-pogo replacement keeps 12 two-foot landings. For E2 use two isolated same-leg forward hops instead of the linked pair, 1 × 2 per leg, 20 s between hops and 120 s after each side. For E3 use isolated outward and return hops instead of its linked pair, same four total landings and rest. For E4 use three isolated low countermovement jumps instead, 20 s between reps and 120 s afterward. E5 may use two isolated short broad jumps instead, same reset/rest. E6 may use four isolated skater bounds instead, two per receiving leg, 20 s between attempts and 120 s afterward. These replace the corresponding linked task, preserve event counts and add roughly 3–5 minutes if all isolated routes are needed; record that elastic linkage was not delivered. All alternatives are replacements, never additions. If no suitable version of a required task is available, record an incomplete/modified delivery; the written fourteen entries do not oblige an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep at least five clean reps available, or equivalent ease for holds; no burning/shaking or pre-fatigue goal.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tibialis ISO Toe-Up Hold** | **1 × 10 seconds, both feet**; 30 s after each set/side | Light dorsiflexor loading for controlled forefoot clearance between elastic contacts. Stable hand support, both heels grounded; hold toes/forefeet raised gently for 10 s without rocking backward. Keep effort easy; sit with heels grounded instead if standing position is difficult. |
| 2 | **Side-Lying Clamshell** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Light hip external-rotator strengthening for knee/pelvis alignment through linked receiving contacts. Lie on side, hips/knees modestly bent, heels together; open top knee 2 s and close 2 s without rolling pelvis. Bodyweight only. Reduce range for trunk rotation; no band burn before primary lifting. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use challenging resistance/leverage with about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; holds finish with about five clean seconds available. Coach-observed control supports the athlete’s effort report. No fixed weight or percentage increase, maximal test, forced rep or failure set. The table lists working sets.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **2 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain the two-set stance-force anchor while elastic tasks change. Floor split stance, front foot planted, pelvis square; lower 2 s and rise firmly for 1 s. Reduce load/range for collapse or balance loss. Use one DB with stable hand support instead of two unsupported DBs if needed; counts stay the same. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6 reps**; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Keep a smaller hip-extension force dose after the linked-contact block. Soft knees, hips back, DBs close; lower 3 s and rise in 1 s. Stop the descent before back rounding; reduce load/range if the hinge becomes spinal movement. |
| 3 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **1 × 8 reps bilateral**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain one controlled bent-knee ankle-force set alongside the linked impact exposure. Both legs together, knees near 90°, heels start on floor, hands secure DBs on lower thighs above knees. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load for bounce/lost heel height. If bench fit is unsuitable, use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead, same reps/rest, and log changed knee angle. |
| 4 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg** | **1 × 8 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Keep one straight-knee calf set per leg with no additional bounce or eccentric finisher. Work from floor, near-straight soft knee, one DB and stable hand support. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load or use bodyweight for lost range; use 1 × 8 bilateral instead of per-leg work if even unilateral bodyweight is too demanding, with 1 × 4 bilateral preparation. |
| 5 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 cycles**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Restore a familiar knee-flexor role at its established small cycle dose. Keep a floor bridge while four small alternating heel steps go out and four return; eight steps at about 1 s each = one full cycle. Choose reach so four cycles are challenging with 2–3 whole cycles available. Shorten reach for pelvic sag or cramping; do not add steps or cycles. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Maintain resisted leg recovery under a quiet pelvis without more impacts. Secure loop band around both forefeet; nonworking leg straight with heel grounded. Flex working hip/knee toward thigh-vertical in 2 s, hold 1 s, return 2 s without arching or moving the anchor heel. Reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt. If a secure loop is unavailable, use seated DB hip flexion instead with hands securing the DB above the knee: same dose/rest, small controlled thigh lift; preparation unloaded. |

**Separate load-preparation sets in these same exercises:** Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides: 1 × 6 reps per side, then 30 s; 1 × 3 reps per side, then 90 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells: 1 × 5 reps, then 60 s. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells: 1 × 6 reps, then 45 s. Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg: 1 × 4 reps per side, then 45 s; 30 s to change sides on each preparation round. Glute Bridge Walkout: 1 × 1 cycles, then 45 s. Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion: 1 × 3 reps per side, then 45 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Use the listed working tempo with lighter resistance/shorter lever; for time holds use the stated shorter duration. Log further preparation and add its time. These are not new exercise slots or challenging working sets.
