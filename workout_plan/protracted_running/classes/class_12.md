# Protracted Running — Class 12 of 12

**Primary effort:** Revisit the original ankle-spring marker, apply controlled stance-force tasks and establish an honest end-of-curriculum gym record.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique and qualified supervision. Previous classes are prescriptions; actual completion, loads, response and recovery remain unknown. This is a conditional next exposure, not earned overload.

**Scope and style:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. Overtime Athletes-inspired elastic, unilateral and force training, adapted for gym support. No actual running, sprints, skipping, conditioning or tumbling. Gym work does not directly supply or test endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions. [Style/evidence and limits](../supporting/source_and_exercise_register.md).

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 repeats the original marker under its original conditions before fatigue. Three new low-dose receiving constraints examine shared-to-split, lateral single-leg push to shared receiving and force from a paused single-leg start. Two familiar planted-foot hip-drive tasks close the explosive work without more landings. Familiar light control and six one-round strength roles support interpretation without manufacturing a final workload peak.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Repeat Class 1's two sets of eight stationary bilateral ankle pogos: hands on hips, low height, soft knees, same surface/shoes where recorded, 60 s after each set. Count clean centred landings with stable rhythm and no progressive deep knee bend/trunk drift. Compare with Class 1 only if actual compatible observations exist; otherwise record a new baseline and compare the two current sets only. Use scheduled attempts only; record failures/stops, not make-up reps. Qualitative control is not a measurement of force, tendon stiffness, running economy or ground-contact time.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

E1 is unchanged from the initial marker and stays first. E2–E4 have only two attempts per stated stance/side and full resets. E5/E6 deliberately return known hip-force tasks with feet planted; they are not presented as unique new drills. Bodyweight unless a light implement/band is specified. Ballistic loads must permit crisp acceleration without grinding.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stationary Bilateral Ankle Pogo** | **2 × 8 hops**; 60 s after each set/side, including the last | Repeat the original ankle-spring marker under matched conditions to distinguish a real recorded change from a different task. Hands on hips, small centred two-foot bounces, knees soft and ankles free. Record clean contacts within each set; stop for drift, progressive knee bend or rhythm loss. Lower amplitude for control and label changed conditions rather than forcing a comparison. |
| 2 | **Two-Foot Forward Jump to Split-Stance Stick** | **1 × 2 jumps per receiving lead-leg stance**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Receive a small shared forward push in a controlled split base, assigning a clear lead/trail support role. Parallel two-foot start, modest forward jump, place feet in a short split stance together and hold 2 s with pelvis forward. No long split, twist or second jump. Shorten distance/stance if front knee or rear ankle alignment is lost. |
| 3 | **Single-Leg Lateral Jump to Two-Foot Landing** | **1 × 2 jumps per takeoff leg**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Express a short lateral stance-leg push while both legs share the receiving load. Start on one foot, push a small distance outward, land on both feet parallel beneath the body and hold 2 s. Face forward with no crossover or turn. Reduce distance for reaching or trunk lean. |
| 4 | **Paused Single-Leg Squat Jump to Two-Foot Landing** | **1 × 2 jumps per takeoff leg**; 120 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Produce a controlled one-leg push from a still partial squat, separating force initiation from countermovement assistance. Hold a small single-leg knee/hip bend for 2 s, push quickly to a modest height without another dip, receive on both feet and hold 2 s. Use a shallower start for control; no maximum-height target. An unstable pause is a fault, not a reason for extra attempts. |
| 5 | **Split-Stance Medicine Ball Forward Scoop Toss** | **1 × 3 throws per lead-leg stance**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last; 20 s between repetitions | Retain a familiar split-base forward hip-force expression after the receiving tasks, without another landing demand. Use Class 6's short split stance and light ball; shallow dip and fast forward scoop with both feet planted and pelvis facing ahead. No rotation, catch or step. Reduce load for an arm-only heave/back arch; retrieve by walking after the ball stops. |
| 6 | **Two-Hand Kettlebell Swing** | **2 × 5 swings**; 90 s after each set/side, including the last | Revisit the original planted-foot ballistic hip-drive skill with a familiar complete swing cycle. Two hands, stable parallel feet, light-to-moderate bell chosen for crisp speed. Hinge and snap tall; bell floats no higher than chest, no arm lifting/back arch. Park after five. Use actual prior load only if recorded and appropriate; it is not a mandated increase. |

**Quality and recovery:** Every attempted flight counts, even an invalid landing. Never add demonstration/test/make-up repetitions. A sequence contains only the stated linked contacts; walk between setups. Keep the same left/right order and surface within a comparison. Stop the set for visible speed/control loss; lower the stated amplitude/resistance or extend rest 30–60 s before any remaining scheduled attempt. Stop affected work for pain, limping, dizziness or persisting loss of control.

**Exercise-specific equipment/readiness route:** E1 requires the original surface/hand/footwear conditions only for a true comparison; if those are unavailable, record changed conditions. E2 may use a parallel two-foot landing instead when split receiving is unsuitable, same four events/rest. E4 may use a light rear-toe kickstand paused takeoff instead when a single-leg pause cannot be controlled, same 1 × 2 per lead side and two-foot receiving; record the altered takeoff. E5 without safe contained throwing space uses Class 11's split-stance held-ball hip drive instead, same six total force efforts, no throws. E6 without competent swing/handling uses a light fast floor hip bridge instead, 2 × 5 with 90 s rest and a 2 s lowering phase, replacing ten swings with ten nonflight hip drives. Do not add a comparison test outside these rows. All alternatives are replacements, never additions. If no suitable version of a required task is available, record an incomplete/modified delivery; the written fourteen entries do not oblige an athlete to finish unsafe work.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep at least five clean reps available, or equivalent ease for holds; no burning/shaking or pre-fatigue goal.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill** | **1 × 4 five-second holds per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Retain the original light arch-control role for a stable contact and lifting platform. Both feet supported, target one arch at a time, long toes and heel grounded. Relax fully between holds. Use seated execution instead for cramping or toe gripping; do not increase effort to make the final class harder. |
| 2 | **Side-Lying Hip Abduction** | **1 × 8 reps per side**; 30 s after each set/side | Retain the original low-fatigue hip-abductor support role for pelvic control. Pelvis stacked, bottom knee bent, top leg straight and slightly behind trunk; lift 2 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce range for rolling or hip hike; bodyweight only and at least five clean reps available. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use challenging resistance/leverage with about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**; holds finish with about five clean seconds available. Coach-observed control supports the athlete’s effort report. No fixed weight or percentage increase, maximal test, forced rep or failure set. The table lists working sets.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Record a familiar challenging stance-force set on each leg under the original exercise geometry. Floor split stance, front foot planted, pelvis square; lower 2 s and rise firmly for 1 s. Reduce load/range for collapse or balance loss. Use one DB with stable hand support instead of two unsupported DBs if needed; counts stay the same. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **1 × 6 reps**; 120 s after each complete round, including the last | Record a familiar controlled hip-hinge strength set without a final volume peak. Soft knees, hips back, DBs close; lower 3 s and rise in 1 s. Stop the descent before back rounding; reduce load/range if the hinge becomes spinal movement. |
| 3 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **1 × 8 reps bilateral**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Record knee-flexed ankle force with the original seated angle/heel range where actual setup is known. Both legs together, knees near 90°, heels start on floor, hands secure DBs on lower thighs above knees. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load for bounce/lost heel height. If bench fit is unsuitable, use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead, same reps/rest, and log changed knee angle. |
| 4 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg** | **1 × 8 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain the original one-set straight-knee ankle-force role on each leg. Work from floor, near-straight soft knee, one DB and stable hand support. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s. Reduce load or use bodyweight for lost range; use 1 × 8 bilateral instead of per-leg work if even unilateral bodyweight is too demanding, with 1 × 4 bilateral preparation. |
| 5 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 cycles**; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Return to the original controlled knee-flexor cycle task using a recorded lever only if available. Keep a floor bridge while four small alternating heel steps go out and four return; eight steps at about 1 s each = one full cycle. Choose reach so four cycles are challenging with 2–3 whole cycles available. Shorten reach for pelvic sag or cramping; do not add steps or cycles. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 reps per side**; 60 s between sides; 90 s after each complete round, including the last | Retain the original resisted leg-recovery role with a controlled pelvis. Secure loop band around both forefeet; nonworking leg straight with heel grounded. Flex working hip/knee toward thigh-vertical in 2 s, hold 1 s, return 2 s without arching or moving the anchor heel. Reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt. If a secure loop is unavailable, use seated DB hip flexion instead with hands securing the DB above the knee: same dose/rest, small controlled thigh lift; preparation unloaded. |

**Separate load-preparation sets in these same exercises:** Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides: 1 × 6 reps per side, then 30 s; 1 × 3 reps per side, then 90 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells: 1 × 5 reps, then 60 s. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells: 1 × 6 reps, then 45 s. Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg: 1 × 4 reps per side, then 45 s; 30 s to change sides on each preparation round. Glute Bridge Walkout: 1 × 1 cycles, then 45 s. Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion: 1 × 3 reps per side, then 45 s; 20 s to change sides on each preparation round. Use the listed working tempo with lighter resistance/shorter lever; for time holds use the stated shorter duration. Log further preparation and add its time. These are not new exercise slots or challenging working sets.
