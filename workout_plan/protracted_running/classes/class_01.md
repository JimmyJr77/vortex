# Protracted Running — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Build repeatable ankle spring and leg-force reserve for controlled single-leg support during distance running.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting and landing technique, coached by a qualified adult. This begins a curriculum, not necessarily the athlete's training history. Actual readiness, previous workload and recovery are unknown; match the prescription to demonstrated competence today.

**Focus:** Protracted running → Endurance; sustained pace; pacing control. This is complementary gym strength and explosive training. It contains no running and does not directly train or test aerobic endurance, sustained running pace or pacing decisions.

**Style:** Overtime Athletes-inspired low-level elastic work, single-leg control and purposeful force training, adapted to this running-support objective. These are original class doses, not a copied OTA program. [Style reference](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/create-plyometric-training-program/)

**Quality marker:** Within E1's two scheduled eight-hop sets, record clean landings out of attempts: centred contacts, similar low height/rhythm, and no progressive deep knee bend or trunk drift. Record the reason for any stop. These observations do not measure running economy or ground-contact time. Keep surface, shoes, hand position and intent consistent for later comparison.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

**E1 is the primary repeatability anchor.** Quick force expression matters; maximum height, distance and exhaustion do not. Start each set recovered. The modest supporting doses preserve quality for the later strength work.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stationary Bilateral Ankle Pogo** | **2 × 8 hops**, bodyweight; **60 s after each set** | Main repeated ankle-spring practice. Hands on hips; make small two-foot bounces in place, with soft knees and ankles moving freely. Record the quality marker. End the set if rhythm/position deteriorates; use lower amplitude instead of chasing height. |
| 2 | **Single-Leg Pogo in Place** | **1 × 4 hops per leg**; **90 s between legs and after the second leg** | Apply spring-like ankle force while one leg supports the pelvis. Hands on hips; stay over the foot and finish balanced. Reduce amplitude for drift; if needed, use light fingertip support on a stable bench **instead of** unsupported execution, with the same dose. Do not change this to alternating jogging. |
| 3 | **Single-Leg Forward Hop to Stick** | **1 × 3 per leg**; **12–15 s between reps**, **90 s between legs and afterward** | Quick single-leg push followed by controlled forward weight acceptance. Take off and land on the **same leg** over a short, self-selected distance, no more than one of the athlete's foot lengths today. Hold 2 s, then walk back and reset. Use a shorter distance instead of a farther unstable landing; stop for repeated extra balance steps. |
| 4 | **Squat Jump — paused, hands on hips** | **1 × 3**; **12–15 s between reps**, **90 s afterward** | Express hip/knee extension quickly from stillness, complementing the ankle-dominant anchor. Hold a comfortable quarter-to-half squat for 2 s, jump vertically without another dip, then absorb and hold the two-foot landing for 2 s. Lower jump height for a hard landing; repeated re-dipping ends the task. |
| 5 | **Split-Squat Jump to Stick — no leg switch** | **1 × 3 per lead leg**; **12–15 s between reps**, **90 s between sides and afterward** | Produce quick force from an asymmetric stance while keeping the pelvis facing forward. From a comfortable floor split stance, dip shallowly, jump a small height and land in the **same stance**, holding 2 s. Both feet participate: this is not a single-leg landing. Use a shallower dip/lower flight instead of switching legs or losing alignment. |
| 6 | **Two-hand Kettlebell Swing** | **2 × 5 swings**; light-to-moderate bell selected for crisp speed; **90 s after each set** | Rapid hip extension against an external load, with **no additional jump landing**. Hinge, snap hips tall and let the bell float to no higher than chest height; feet stay planted. Park under control after rep five. Reduce the bell load for arm lifting, back extension or slowing; stop if the hinge cannot stay controlled. |

The first planned efforts are part of the dose, including any failed efforts; do not add athlete demonstration, practice or make-up jumps. E1/E2 are connected low hops; E3–E5 have full individual resets. E6's setup hike and final park belong to the set, not extra swings. Rest after the last set may contain the next demonstration/setup; **do not count that interval twice**. Extend recovery if needed.

For an athlete whose basic lifting experience does not include a competent swing, **use a light medicine-ball forward scoop toss instead of E6**, 2 × 3 throws with 90 s after each set, feet planted, no jump or catch. Use only if the ball, throwing/retrieval area and coach control are suitable within one 10 m lane; release toward clear floor, retrieve by walking after all balls stop. If neither option is feasible, record that athlete's session as modified/incomplete instead of inventing a sixth task or forcing the count.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep resistance light and leave **at least five clean repetitions available**. The purpose is targeted strengthening with minimal fatigue before primary work.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Short-Foot Drill** | **1 × 4 holds per target foot**, **5 s each**, 3 s release; **30 s between feet and afterward** | Strengthen active arch control for a stable contact platform. Stand on both feet; gently draw the target forefoot toward its heel without curling toes or raising the heel. Only one foot is targeted at a time. Use seated execution **instead of** standing if toe gripping or cramping appears. |
| 2 | **Side-lying Hip Abduction** | **1 × 8 per side**, bodyweight; 2 s up, 1 s pause, 2 s down; **30 s between sides and afterward** | Light hip-abductor strengthening for pelvic support in single-leg stance. Bottom knee bent, top leg straight and slightly behind the trunk; raise only while the pelvis stays stacked. Reduce the range for rolling/hiking; no band or burnout today. |

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

Select challenging loads or leverage that leave about **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve (RIR)** at the stated dose. For P5, a repetition is a whole walkout-and-return cycle. The coach checks range, speed and alignment alongside the athlete's effort report. No maximal testing, grinding or forced reps. The tables prescribe working sets; lighter load-preparation sets follow separately.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split Squat — two dumbbells at sides** | **2 × 6 per leg**; **60 s between legs**, **2 min after both legs**, including the final round | Main unilateral knee/hip force reserve for supporting bodyweight over the stance leg. Stationary floor stance; lower for 2 s, rise firmly without bouncing. Keep the front foot planted and pelvis square. Reduce load/range for knee collapse or rear-leg dominance. If balance limits loading, use one DB and light hand support on a stable bench **instead of** two unsupported DBs; log the change. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — two dumbbells** | **2 × 6**; **2 min after each set** | Load hip extension and the hamstrings through a hinge, supporting stance-phase hip-force capacity. Soft knees, hips back, 3 s down and a controlled strong rise; DBs stay close. Stop the range before the back rounds. Reduce load/range if the hinge becomes a squat or spinal movement. |
| 3 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated dumbbells** | **2 × 8**, both legs together; **90 s after each set** | Main bent-knee plantarflexor strength exposure for the ankle's stance-support role. Sit on a stable bench, knees near 90°, DBs secured by hands on lower thighs above the knees. From the floor, rise for 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s; no bounce. Reduce load for lost heel height or discomfort at the implement contact. |
| 4 | **Standing Dumbbell Calf Raise — supported single leg** | **1 × 8 per leg**; **60 s between legs**, **90 s afterward** | Add plantarflexor force with a nearly straight knee, complementing P3's knee-flexed position. Hold one DB, support the other hand on a stable bench and work from the floor. Rise 1 s, hold 1 s, lower 2 s; do not lock the knee or roll the ankle. Reduce load for lost range; use both legs **instead of** one at 1 × 8 if a unilateral bodyweight set is already too hard. |
| 5 | **Glute Bridge Walkout** | **1 × 4 complete cycles**; **90 s afterward** | Challenge hamstrings as the knee opens and closes while the pelvis stays supported, distinct from P2's loaded hip hinge. From a floor bridge, take four small alternating heel steps out, then four back: **8 steps = 1 rep**. About 1 s per step; keep breathing and hips level. Choose heel reach to make four cycles challenging with 2–3 sound cycles available. Shorten reach for pelvic sag or cramping; no extra cycles. |
| 6 | **Supine Band-resisted Hip Flexion** | **1 × 6 per leg**; 2 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down; **60 s between legs**, **90 s afterward** | Load the hip flexors' leg-recovery action while the trunk/pelvis stay quiet. A suitable closed-loop band surrounds both forefeet: keep the nonworking leg extended with heel grounded, bend/lift the working thigh toward vertical, then return without arching or moving the anchor foot. Choose band tension for 2–3 RIR; reduce tension/range for pelvic tilt or foot slippage. |

**Load-preparation sets, within these same six exercises:** P1: 1 × 6 per leg bodyweight, then 1 × 3 per leg with light DBs; allow 20 s to change legs, 30 s after the first round and 90 s after the second. P2: 1 × 5 light, then 60 s. P3: 1 × 6 light bilateral, then 45 s. P4: 1 × 4 per leg bodyweight, 30 s between legs and 45 s afterward. P5: 1 shortened cycle, then 45 s. P6: 1 × 3 per leg with a lighter band, 20 s between sides and 45 s afterward. Use the working exercise's tempo, with less resistance or shorter walkout reach. Use these sets to establish position and load; they are not extra exercise slots or additional challenging sets. Log any further load-preparation work and allow more time.

**Equipment replacements:** Bench height must allow a supported seated foot position. If P3 seating is unsuitable, **use supported standing bent-knee calf raises instead of P3**, same bilateral 2 × 8 and rest, maintaining a small knee bend throughout; record the changed knee angle/support. If no secure loop band is available for P6, **use seated dumbbell hip flexion instead of P6**: same 1 × 6 per leg, tempo and rest, with the hands securing a DB on the distal thigh above the knee; start foot grounded and lift the thigh slightly without trunk lean. Use 1 × 3 unloaded per leg for preparation. Select a load/range that actually achieves the strength effort; do not balance an unsecured weight on a foot. If suitable resistance cannot be supplied, log the limitation rather than calling an easy set challenging.

**Workload:** The default route has **39 jump/hop landing events: 25 two-foot and 14 single-leg**, equivalent to **64 individual foot contacts** under the ledger's convention, plus **10 planted-foot swings**. These are different demands, not interchangeable contacts. Stabilization: 8 arch holds and 16 hip raises. Primary work: **9 set rounds / 13 separately performed bouts**, totaling 80 ordinary reps plus 4 walkout cycles; preparation adds 43 ordinary reps plus 1 shortened cycle. See the [workload ledger](../progression_ledger.md) for per-side counts and substitutions. Access & Prepare 1 and outside activity are excluded because their loads are unknown.

**Delivery and time:** Allow about **65–75 minutes beyond Access & Prepare 1** under the efficient supervised flow in the [audit](../supporting/class_01_audit.md). Full session time is unresolved. Keep athletes in this section/exercise order; use numbered waves, not a timed station circuit. Work in cleared floor bays within separate 10 m lanes; use a separate empty swing bay while that exercise is active, with waiting athletes outside the bell path. Check lane width, bench stability, queues and implement quantities onsite. Equipment changes can use prescribed recovery; queues must never force shorter recovery. Larger groups, extra instruction or scarce implements can extend the estimate. A 60-minute total booking does not fit this prescription; a 90-minute booking still requires checking preparation and group flow.

**Recovery and stop rules:** Treat this as a demanding lower-body exposure despite the short explosive sets. Review actual running, racing, gym, sport and tumbling loads before placing it. About 48–72 hours from another comparable lower-body session is an initial planning reference, adjusted for the athlete's schedule and recovery, not automatic clearance. No running is assigned here. For pain, limping, dizziness, repeated loss of control or unresolved fatigue, stop the affected work and have the supervising coach reassess. An early stop remains an honest modification; never add substitute volume to make the record look complete.
