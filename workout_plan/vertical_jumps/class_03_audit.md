## Selection and substantive revision

| Decision | Final choice and reason |
|---|---|
| Honor unique-drill preference while preserving the static-start job | Replace the paused floor jump with Seated Box Jump. The supported seat start changes the task; it is not merely a renamed jump or a different load. OTA explicitly uses seated box jumping for static-start power. |
| Avoid another natural-arm CMJ or approach jump | Use stationary Volleyball Block Jump with hands initially high. This trains leg drive with restricted backward arm swing and no approach; it is a different application from the previous full arm-swing CMJ. |
| Add an asymmetric task without demanding an unproven single-leg landing | Use low Split-Squat Jump to Stick, same lead leg, both feet contributing and returning to a split stance. Do not silently switch/scissor the legs. |
| Differentiate target-clearance work from the seated box task | Use only three low bilateral hurdle hops: standing entry, slight forward clearance, floor landing and no rebound. The small obstacle constrains upright projection; it is not a long-distance jump or a second high-box challenge. |
| Fill a single-leg force-expression job without adding another landing | Use six rapid bodyweight Step-Ups, with the lead foot kept on the box and controlled lowering. Explosive intent is in the rise; balance, height and load must not slow it into a strength grind. |
| Add a distinct rapid hip-extension action | Use two sets of four light Kettlebell Swings. This is ballistic hinge work, distinct from the controlled loaded RDL later. It remains light and crisp to limit posterior-chain pre-fatigue. |
| Keep progression where repetition is valuable | Retain the six primary lifts and two light strengthening exercises. Their roles remain direct, and load progression is still conditional on actual readiness/response. No invented prior weights or extra lifting pauses. |
| Handle six unfamiliar formats | Keep impact doses modest, integrate execution checks into the first scheduled attempts, and increase demonstration/setup allowance. Lower contact totals do not prove easier tissue loading or faster delivery. |

The six default explosive formats are distinct from the six defaults in Classes 1–2. See drill_history.md for prescription and replacement history. The earlier two classes retain their original historical content; no claim is made that all three classes have unique drills retrospectively. Regressions may repeat a simpler task when needed, with an explicit reason rather than unsafe novelty.

## Workload and overlap

| Explosive entry | Prescribed exposure | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| E1 Seated Box Jump | 3 × 2 | 6 bilateral takeoffs and elevated bilateral landings; 6 controlled step-down dismounts after standing. |
| E2 Volleyball Block Jump | 2 × 2 | 4 bilateral takeoffs/floor landings, constrained starting arm action. |
| E3 Split-Squat Jump to Stick | 1 × 2 per lead leg | 4 asymmetric two-foot takeoffs/landings: 2 left-lead, 2 right-lead. Not four unilateral jumps. |
| E4 Low Hurdle Hop to Stick | 1 × 3 | 3 bilateral takeoffs/floor landings over one low obstacle, full reset. |
| E5 explosive Step-Up | 1 × 3 per leg | 6 rapid non-flight rises, 6 controlled free-foot returns and 2 end-of-side step-offs; no planned jump landing. |
| E6 Kettlebell Swing | 2 × 4 | 8 ballistic hinge repetitions plus 2 controlled start/park sequences; no practice swings outside the stated eight. |

**Total:** 17 jump landing events: 6 elevated bilateral + 7 parallel-foot bilateral floor + 4 split-stance floor landings. All 17 takeoffs use two feet, including the asymmetric split stance. If counting both feet, there are 34 landing foot impacts, but elevation and asymmetric force distribution remain distinct. Separately account for controlled box dismounts and step-up returns; ordinary repositioning/return steps are not falsely labeled jump repetitions. No throws, sprints, bounds, depth drops or continuous rebound chains are prescribed.

E5 is a rapid force-expression task, not a plyometric contact. E6 is a ballistic hinge, not jump practice. They directly support fast leg/hip extension but are not claimed to substitute for the primary jump skill.

**Strengthening:** S1 ten dynamic reps; S2 six five-second foot holds = 30 aggregate foot-seconds, two stabilization rounds/four bouts. Primary = 10 working set rounds/12 separately performed bouts/86 reps (18 + 12 + 12 + 8 + 16 + 20). Preparation = 7 rounds/9 bouts/39 reps (10 + 5 + 6 + 5 + 8 + 5). Primary targets approximately 2–3 technical RIR; light stabilization retains at least five clean reps' reserve/comparable ease.

The new swings overlap with RDL/hip thrust, and E3/E5 overlap with the lead-leg strength task. Limiting swings to 8, step-ups to 3 per leg, split jumps to 2 per lead and the later split squat/hip thrust to one working round keeps each contribution small. This is a programming judgment, not proof of adequate recovery. Actual symptoms, speed, technique and outside exposure govern reductions.

**Replacement accounting:** E1's seated floor-jump replacement retains six flights but changes six elevated landings to six floor landings and removes six box dismounts. E4's virtual-line replacement retains three floor landings with no physical hurdle. E5's Split-Stance Power Rise retains six non-flight rapid efforts but replaces elevated step-up return/step-off work with six controlled shallow lowerings on the floor. E6's dumbbell version retains eight swings/start-park sequences with a different grip/implement. Light-strength replacements retain their stated dose, with bilateral Short-Foot Drill changing side timing. Log variants and all incomplete/invalid work; do not refill attempts.
