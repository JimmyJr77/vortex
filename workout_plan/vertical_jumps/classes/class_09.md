# Vertical Jumps — Class 9 of 12

**Primary effort:** Express rapid upward force against a modest held load while preserving a controlled takeoff and landing. **Dumbbell Countermovement Jump** is the primary task, using two scheduled bodyweight attempts before up to four lightly loaded attempts. Supporting work separates static-start arm-assisted force, brief ankle rebounds, a tall-start ball preload, a floor-start loaded pull and supported lead-leg extension.

**Foundation and review:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Reviewed Classes 1–8, progression/workload records, relevant earlier loaded-jump replacements, drill history, audits, identities and available feedback. Actual attendance, loads, execution and recovery remain unknown. The earlier “Loaded Squat Jump” replacement used a countermovement; today's primary revisits that execution, not a novel jump. The step-up also recurs for its direct supported-force job. Four other formats introduce meaningful task changes.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record the two bodyweight attempts separately from any loaded attempts, with actual implement mass, arm position and valid landings per set. Progress to the loaded sets only when the coach observes a balanced one-dip takeoff, controlled two-foot landing, secure dumbbell handling and suitable current readiness. A loaded attempt is valid when the bells stay beside the body, the jump remains brisk without a second dip or excessive lean, and the landing finishes under control for 2 s. No jump-height, power or improvement claim is made from different loads or unverified measurements.

**Style reference:** OTA's [vertical-power example](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/my-top-3-exercises-for-increasing-vertical-power/) includes dumbbell jumping and upward ball projection. This class uses isolated, lightly loaded jumps with retained implements; it does not adopt the article's weight-dropping combination, platform drop, fixed weights or maximal demands. The dose and execution checks below are coaching adaptations for this cohort.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |

| 2 | **Paused Squat Jump with Natural Arm Swing** — proposed source variant | **2 × 2 = 4 bodyweight jumps**. | 4 | **Tall-Start Vertical Scoop Toss — no flight** — proposed exact execution | **2 × 2 = 4 throws**. Use the earlier hip-level start if overhead control or the longer ball path disrupts the leg drive. |
| 5 | **Kettlebell Floor Pull — rapid extension, no flight or catch** — proposed addition | **2 × 3 = 6 rapid pulls**, each from a settled bell. Place a light bell between the feet and grip with both hands, arms long, back organized. Push through the floor and accelerate smoothly upward into a brief heel rise, then return the heels and bell under control. This is one continuous pull: no pause to add a separate calf-raise rep, backswing, arm curl, high pull, catch or hop. Use a load/handle height that permits a sound start; use the high-hang replacement if the floor position or bell fit prevents it. |
| 6 | **Step-Up — preplanted lead foot, explosive knee drive, no flight** — deliberately recurring | **1 × 2 per leg = 4 rises**. Brief knee-up hold, then about **3 s controlled free-foot lowering**. **20 s after return/reset between same-leg reps; 60 s between legs; 90 s before Section 3.** | Retain supported lead-leg upward force without adding loaded or unilateral jump landings. Set the whole lead foot on a low stable box and leave it there for both reps on that side. Drive up briskly, lift the free knee, then return its foot to the floor. Step the lead foot off only after its side is finished. Avoid pronounced rear-foot push, pelvic rotation or a slow balance grind. Use secure light hand support or a lower suitable step if that restores rapid controlled drive. |
