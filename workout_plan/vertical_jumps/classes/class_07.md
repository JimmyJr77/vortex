# Vertical Jumps — Class 7 of 12

**Primary effort:** Absorb a self-generated landing and turn it into a prompt, organized second vertical takeoff. **Countermovement Jump Rebound** is the primary drill: one floor jump linked to exactly one rebound. Supporting work addresses leg-led dip reversal, low ankle return under slight lateral displacement, lead-leg lowering-to-drive, rapid hip reversal under a one-hand load and leg-driven upward ball release.

**Foundation and review:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Saved Classes 1–6, the progression/workload ledger, drill history, audits and available athlete feedback were reviewed. Actual completion, loads, technique and recovery remain unknown. Class 6's prescribed line contacts do not establish readiness for larger linked rebounds. Today's first scheduled efforts check the exact task at controlled intent, with simpler replacements when needed.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record valid two-flight bouts out of three, plus actual flights and landings. A valid bout has a controlled first jump, a symmetrical first landing followed by one fluid upward rebound without a pause, extra dip or horizontal drift, and a final two-foot landing held for 2 s. Permit useful ankle/knee/hip flexion; do not chase the shortest possible contact by locking the knees. Record arm policy, first/rebound intent, visible collapse or extra contacts and final control. No contact-time, jump-height or reactive-strength score is claimed without a verified measurement protocol. This is not a matched retest of earlier isolated jumps.

**Style reference:** OTA's [bodyweight vertical-jump workout](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/bodyweight-vertical-jump-workout/) uses connected jumps and ankle work. Here the main exposure is only three two-flight bouts, with individualized intent and complete recovery. Its larger example doses and outcome claims are not adopted.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use this order. E1 has six potential flights across three linked bouts; E2 has four isolated flights and E3 six low contacts. E4–E6 have no intended athlete flight. Bodyweight work stays unloaded; bell and ball loads must preserve crisp movement. For side-based work, start left then right unless an actual prior record supports reversing order; record the order.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Countermovement Jump Rebound — one linked rebound** | **3 × 1 two-flight bout = 6 jumps/landings**, with **one rebound transition per bout**. **120 s between bouts and before E2.** | Main task: accept a self-generated floor landing and redirect force upward. Start stationary on two feet with natural arms, make a controlled vertical CMJ, land on both feet and use one smooth yielding action for the second jump. Land again on both feet, hold 2 s and reset. Keep both flights modest initially, with no obstacle or external drop. End after the first landing if symmetry/control is lost; do not force the rebound to finish a count. Reduce intent or use isolated jumps if pausing, deep collapse, knee loss, drift or an unwanted third jump appears. |
| 2 | **Countermovement Jump — hands on hips, isolated** — proposed arm-restricted variant | **2 × 2 = 4 jumps**. **20 s after landing/reset between reps; 90 s between sets and before E3.** | Practice a prompt leg-led dip-to-drive without arm swing masking the lower-body action. Keep hands at the hips, make one comfortable countermovement with no pause, extend upward and finish with a two-foot floor landing held 2 s. Use controlled flight and full resets. Reduce intent if hands leave the hips, the dip becomes a double pump, or alignment/landing fails. This differs from the earlier paused hands-on-hips Squat Jump and natural-arm isolated CMJ. |
| 3 | **Two-Foot Lateral Line Pogo — low amplitude** | **1 × 6 low jumps**, three crossings each way, continuous. **90 s before E4.** | Retain quick ankle return while resisting sideways collapse under a small lateral displacement. Use a virtual lengthwise line marked by cones outside the travel area. Both feet leave/land together; cross only enough to clear the line, stay tall and keep knee motion small. Count every landing including the final one. No distance target or high lateral bound. Use in-place pogos for lateral rolling, drift, loud contacts or rhythm loss. |
| 4 | **Reverse Lunge to Knee Drive — rapid, no flight** — proposed power execution | **1 × 2 per lead leg = 4 rises**. Lower into the lunge over **about 2 s**, reverse without a bottom pause, drive up briskly, hold the free knee briefly and return its foot to the floor. **20 s after reset between same-lead reps; 60 s between legs; 90 s before E5.** | Redirect controlled lowering into upward lead-leg force without another jump landing. Start standing; keep the lead foot planted while the other foot steps back to a short comfortable lunge. Reverse through the lead leg, bringing the rear leg forward into knee drive. Keep pelvis square and lead heel grounded until the upward finish; rear toes may assist, so this is not pure one-leg power. No knee contact, hop or pronounced rear-foot push. Use light hand support; reduce depth or use the standing replacement if balance, range or a slow grind prevents rapid drive. |
| 5 | **Kettlebell Swing — one hand, chest-height ceiling, no flight** | **1 × 3 per working arm = 6 swings**, continuous within each three-swing bout. **60 s between arms; 90 s before E6.** Park before changing hands; no airborne hand switch. | Accept the returning bell through a controlled hinge, then reverse into rapid hip extension while keeping the trunk square under a one-hand load. Both feet stay planted in a parallel stance. Use a light bell, secure grip and free arm clear of its path; wait for the bell to return before hinging. Drive tall so the bell floats no higher than chest height, without arm lifting, twisting or leaning back. First scheduled swings check grip/trajectory. Reduce load or use two hands for twisting, grip uncertainty, bell contact, slow drive or an unsafe park. |
| 6 | **Medicine-Ball Thruster Throw — no flight, upward release** — proposed exact execution | **2 × 2 = 4 throws**. **20 s after retrieval/reset between throws; 90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Transfer a quick leg-driven dip reversal into an upward ball release from the chest. Start with a light ball held near the clavicles, elbows below it. Use a comfortable shallow squat and immediately extend hips/knees/ankles, then let the arms finish the upward/slightly forward throw clear of the face. Forefeet stay on the floor; heels may rise. Keep trunk stacked and finish balanced. Reduce load/range if the arms press before the legs drive, the back arches or the athlete jumps. No catch of the return, wall target or distance challenge. |
