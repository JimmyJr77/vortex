# Vertical Jumps — Class 11 of 12

**Primary effort:** Produce a repeatable standing vertical jump with coordinated dip, leg extension and natural arm swing. **Countermovement Jump** deliberately returns as the primary performance anchor. Establish usable assessment conditions within its six attempts while reducing unnecessary support volume.

**Foundation and review:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Reviewed Classes 1–10, earlier CMJ and seated/swing/power-rise prescriptions and replacements, workload, drill history, identities, audits and feedback. Actual attendance, loads, execution, measurement capability and recovery remain unknown. Class 10's completion on paper does not establish mastery or fatigue. E1 and E6 revisit earlier work deliberately; E2–E5 have four meaningful new format constraints.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record every attempted jump and a valid/modified/invalid/omitted status with reason. Set 1 is two execution checks/rehearsals; sets 2–3 offer four candidate observations if current quality/readiness allow. Valid means stationary parallel start, one self-selected dip with natural arm swing, upward two-foot takeoff and a controlled two-foot 2 s landing hold without extra hop or step. Reduced-intent attempts remain useful practice but are labeled modified. The first pair is excluded from the candidate summary; do not add attempts to replace failures. See the [assessment protocol](../assessment_protocol.md) for measurement conditions and honest comparisons.

**Style:** [OTA's vertical-jump programming article](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/vertical-jump-training-how-to-program-explosive-bounce-for-serious-athletes/) emphasizes specific jump practice, elastic work and lower-body strength. This class adapts that emphasis to the stated cohort and exact 6/2/6 architecture; its doses are programming decisions rather than a validated whole-session protocol.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Use this order: the primary observation occurs before supporting work. Six E1, three E2 and four E3 jumps give **thirteen bodyweight bilateral floor landings**. E4–E6 have no intended athlete flight. First scheduled work checks suitability within each budget; no extra trials or compensating work.
