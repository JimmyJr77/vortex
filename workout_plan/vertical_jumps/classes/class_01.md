# Vertical Jumps — Class 1 of 12

**Primary effort:** Produce a repeatable, powerful vertical takeoff by coordinating the dip, leg drive and upward arm swing. Standing countermovement jumping is today's priority; the other explosive tasks isolate a contributor or rehearse its use with a small approach.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, supervised by a qualified coach. This is the first written exposure in this curriculum, not an assumption of beginner ability. Individual training history, readiness and other training are unreported. The full prescription is conditional on readiness and demonstrated execution today.

**Style:** Inspired by Overtime Athletes' emphasis on purposeful plyometrics, high-intent jump practice and substantial lower-body strength. Exercise doses and the 6/2/6 arrangement are specific to this curriculum. [Style source](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/vertical-jump-training-how-to-program-explosive-bounce-for-serious-athletes/)

**Quality marker, inside E2:** Record valid attempts out of the nine prescribed countermovement jumps. A valid attempt uses one smooth dip, coordinated arm swing, two-foot takeoff, a two-foot landing in the starting area and a controlled two-second hold without a corrective step. Aim to preserve this pattern while jumping as high as possible. If the available force-plate setup has a verified jump-height method, record each valid height and the method; otherwise record technique only. Do not invent a height or add test attempts. Keep shoes, surface, arm policy, device/method and reset conditions consistent on repeat exposures.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Execute in order. All loads are bodyweight except E6. E2 receives the most high-intent practice. Rest is recovery, not time for additional exercises. Failed attempts count toward the prescribed attempts and workload; do not add replacement attempts.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stationary Bilateral Ankle Pogo** | **1 × 10** low hops. Continuous within the set; **60 s before E2**. | Rehearse quick ankle-driven return from the floor with very little knee/hip excursion. Stay tall with low flight and quiet, centered contacts. This is a small elastic dose, not a height test. Reduce amplitude if contacts become heavy; stop if rhythm or alignment cannot be restored. |
| 2 | **Countermovement Jump — natural arm swing, stick landing** | **3 × 3** maximum-height intent. **20 s between attempts; 120 s between sets and before E3.** | **Primary performance anchor:** integrate the dip, leg extension and arm swing into upward takeoff. Start stationary, use one self-selected dip, jump straight up, then hold the landing 2 s. Lower intent if landing control is lost; log those attempts as modified, not comparable maximum efforts. |
| 3 | **Squat Jump — paused start, hands on hips** | **2 × 2** with maximum upward intent from a comfortable partial squat held **2 s**. **20 s between attempts; 90 s between sets and before E4.** | Express upward force from stillness without relying on a preceding dip or arm swing. Keep hands on hips, avoid a second dip, and stick for 2 s. Use a shallower comfortable start if the pause cannot be held; do not turn it into another countermovement jump. |
| 4 | **Single-leg vertical jump to two-foot landing** — proposed execution variant | **1 × 2 per leg = 4 jumps.** Brisk takeoff at a controlled, submaximal height. **20 s between same-leg attempts; 60 s between legs; 90 s before E5.** | Make each leg produce an upward impulse without adding a single-leg landing demand. Dip on the takeoff leg, swing the arms upward and bring both feet to a controlled 2 s landing. Alternate which leg goes first across future exposures. Reduce height/dip if the pelvis tilts or the knee buckles. This does not measure or diagnose an asymmetry. |
| 5 | **Approach Vertical Jump — two steps, two-foot takeoff** | **2 × 2** at controlled approach speed and a height that permits a stable landing. **20 s between attempts; 90 s between sets and before E6.** | Rehearse directing a small amount of approach momentum upward while timing the arm swing to the two-foot plant. Use two walking-to-brisk steps, not a sprint; jump upward and hold the landing 2 s. Shorten/slow the approach if the athlete travels through the landing zone. |
| 6 | **Light medicine-ball vertical scoop toss** — proposed addition | **2 × 3**. **20 s between throws; 90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Express coordinated leg drive and upward arm action against a small external load without another planned jump landing. From a shallow dip, extend the hips/knees and rise onto the forefeet while scooping up and slightly forward; keep the feet in contact with the floor. Choose a ball light enough for a fast, smooth release without back arching or an arm-only heave. Do not catch the return. |

**Quality rule:** Extend the next rest by 30–60 s after a clear decline in output, posture or landing control. If the next scheduled attempt still deteriorates, reduce intent/complexity; end that exercise if quality remains poor. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness, fear that prevents execution, or unsafe equipment/space. Record incomplete work honestly. The fourteen-slot design never obliges an athlete to complete unsafe repetitions.

**Space and replacements:** E5 uses one reserved 10 m lane: take two steps and plant within the first 3 m, land and hold in the next 2 m, and keep the remaining 5 m clear. These are proposed boundaries to verify against actual lane width and athlete travel. One athlete moves at a time; no crossing traffic. Use a **one-step Approach Vertical Jump instead of** the two-step version if needed to fit the verified zone; retain 2 × 2 and the same rests. If even that does not fit, do not run the approach station until a suitable layout is resolved.

For E6, verify ceiling, ball rebound/roll behavior and the full landing/retrieval area before the class. Keep everyone out of that area and retrieve only once the ball settles. If that cannot be arranged, **use a light dumbbell Loaded Countermovement Jump (earlier label: Loaded Squat Jump) instead of E6: 2 × 2**, one smooth dip, fast two-foot takeoff, dumbbells at the sides, controlled two-foot landing and full reset; 20 s between attempts and 90 s between sets/before Section 3. Use only with demonstrated unloaded jump control and secure light-dumbbell handling. It supplies externally loaded vertical power but removes free arm action and adds **4 loaded jump landings**; record the replacement. No automatic substitution is available if neither execution is suitable.

**Final-sequence clarification:** Choose the E6 throw or loaded-jump replacement before starting E6. If throwing has already begun and becomes unsuitable, end that slot; do not restart with four loaded jumps. Other replacements use only the remaining original attempted budget, including failed attempts. The loaded option uses a smooth dip before takeoff, not a paused squat start; only its label is corrected.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Keep external load light and leave **at least 5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or comparable ease on the holds. These sets should improve control without tiring the legs before the main lifts.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Step-Down — low lateral heel-tap variant** | **1 × 5 per leg = 10 reps.** Bodyweight or a light dumbbell. Lower **3 s**, touch without transferring weight, return **1 s**. **30 s between legs; 45 s before S2.** | Load the stance leg lightly while controlling knee tracking and pelvic position through the flexion used to receive and initiate a jump. Use a stable box low enough to retain level hips and the whole stance foot. Reduce load/range if the free heel pushes off or the knee collapses. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported single-leg hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot = 6 holds.** Bodyweight; **5 s relaxed between holds; 30 s between sides; 60 s before primary preparation sets.** | Strengthen active arch control while preserving heel, big-toe base and little-toe base contact. Use hand support so balance does not dominate; stance knee softly bent, toes long, no ankle rolling. The job is a stable foot contact during the dip and plant, not maximum arch height. |

Use a **hand-supported single-leg shallow squat instead of S1** if no suitably low, stable step exists: 1 × 5 per leg, the same 3 s lowering/1 s return and rests, with the free foot clear; proposed replacement, not extra work. For S2, **use a bilateral standing Short-Foot Drill instead of** the single-leg version if the arch cannot be maintained: 1 × 3 holds of 5 s with both feet working together. Neither exercise should cause cramping or substantial fatigue; reduce effort or end it if control cannot be restored.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

**Working loads:** Select a load that leaves approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** at the stated repetitions. Ask the athlete and confirm through observed execution; RIR is an estimate, not a youth testing score. Use a controlled lowering phase and purposeful upward effort without jumping or bouncing. Increase load only when the current load is demonstrably too easy and technique is stable. No forced repetitions or maximal-load tests.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Double Front-Rack Squat — dumbbells** | **3 × 6 working reps.** Lower about **3 s**, stand decisively. **150 s between working sets and before P2 preparation.** | Main bilateral force exercise: challenge coordinated knee and hip extension while keeping the trunk organized over the feet. Use a repeatable, controlled squat depth appropriate to the athlete, not a forced jump-depth restriction. Reduce load/depth for heel lift, knee collapse or loss of rack/trunk position. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — dumbbells** | **2 × 6 working reps.** Lower **3 s**, stand in about **1 s**. **120 s between working sets and before P3 preparation.** | Load hip extension and posterior-chain tension from a flexed-hip position, complementing the squat's larger knee excursion. Keep dumbbells close, knees softly bent and hips moving back. End the lowering before the back rounds or pelvis tucks; reduce load/range if needed. |
| 3 | **Split Squat — stationary, dumbbells** | **1 × 6 per leg = 12 working reps.** Lower **3 s**, rise in about **1 s**. **75 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Give each lead leg a challenging force contribution through a stable stance, supporting the small unilateral takeoff dose. Both feet stay planted, rear heel lifted; use enough stance width to control the pelvis. Reduce load or use hand support instead of chasing balance. One set per leg limits overlap with the main squat. |
| 4 | **Hip Thrust — sandbag across pelvis, upper back on bench** | **1 × 8 working reps.** About **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down**. **90 s before P5 preparation.** | Challenge hip extension near the top of its range with less trunk-holding demand than the RDL. Finish with ribs and pelvis aligned, not by arching the back. One challenging set supplies this complementary role without a second large hip-extension workout. |
| 5 | **Standing Calf Raise — supported single-leg, dumbbell** | **1 × 8 per leg = 16 working reps.** About **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down**. **60 s between legs; 75 s before P6 preparation.** | Load each ankle's plantarflexion with the knee nearly straight, supporting the late part of upward push-off. Work from the floor; use a secured support and hold the dumbbell in the free hand. Keep heel travel vertical and knee position steady. Choose bodyweight only if already challenging at the effort target; otherwise add load. |
| 6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **2 × 10 working reps**, both legs together. About **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down**, heels returning quietly to the floor without rebound. **90 s between working sets.** | Load plantarflexion while the knees remain bent, complementing P5's straight-knee position and supporting force through the ankle during the dip-to-drive transition. Sit securely, knees approximately over feet, hold the loads in position. Reduce load if range shortens, knees extend, or the athlete bounces. |

**Load-preparation sets, separate from working sets:** For P1 use **2 × 5**, first light and second moderately loaded, neither close to fatigue; rest 60 s between them and 120 s before the first working set. P2: **1 × 5 light**, then 90 s. P3: **1 × 3 light per leg**, 30 s between legs, then 90 s. P4: **1 × 5 light**, then 60 s. P5: **1 × 4 light per leg**, 30 s between legs, then 45 s. P6: **1 × 5 light**, then 60 s. These rehearse only the listed lifts; they are not a replacement preparation sequence. Additional ramp sets, if needed for an athlete's working load, must be logged and added to the time/workload estimate.

**Strength adjustments:** If an athlete reaches the technical limit before the target reps, stop the set and reduce the next load. Extend recovery if technique has not recovered; do not shorten rest to fit the clock. Use a two-kettlebell front rack instead of dumbbells for P1 if that provides an appropriate load and manageable setup. Use a **sandbag floor Glute Bridge instead of P4** if a stable, suitably sized bench cannot be arranged; keep 1 × 8 working, 1 × 5 preparation and the stated rest, and record the changed range/support. A bench/box used for hand support must be stable in the direction of force. Do not prescribe an unstable support or a load the athlete cannot safely pick up, position and put down.

**Scheduling and delivery:** Allow approximately **80–90 minutes for Sections 2–4, associated preparation sets, demonstrations, transitions and recording**, before equipment queues or substantial extra recovery. Access & Prepare 1 is additional and its duration is unknown. Complete class duration remains unresolved. This does not fit a 60-minute total booking. A 90-minute booking is possible only if the actual preparation, athlete flow and chosen recovery all fit; do not assume they do. The supporting audit contains the elapsed-time calculation.

Treat this as a demanding lower-body exposure. Check recent and upcoming sport, gym, jumping and separately programmed tumbling before delivery. Around **48–72 hours** before another comparably demanding lower-body exposure is an initial planning consideration, adjusted to actual readiness and schedule rather than a guarantee of recovery. Hold/reduce the prescription when soreness, recent impact volume or fatigue compromises execution. Do not add tumbling, conditioning or a finisher to this class.
