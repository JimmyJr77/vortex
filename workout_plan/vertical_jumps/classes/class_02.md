# Vertical Jumps — Class 2 of 12

**Primary effort:** Repeat a high-quality vertical takeoff with a consistent dip, upward leg drive and coordinated arm swing. Use the standing countermovement jump to practice one observed technique priority while holding total training demand steady.

**Foundation:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Class 1 is a saved prescription; its actual completion, loads, jump results and recovery are **unknown**. This is a conditional consolidation exposure, not evidence that athletes earned a harder workout. Confirm current readiness and recent sport, gym and tumbling demands before delivery.

**Style:** Retain the Overtime Athletes-inspired combination of focused jump practice, small elastic/unilateral contributions and challenging lower-body strength. The exact arrangement and doses are this curriculum's coaching decisions. [Style source](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/vertical-jump-training-how-to-program-explosive-bounce-for-serious-athletes/)

**Quality marker inside E2:** Record valid jumps **per set of three**, retaining the same Class 1 standard: one smooth dip, natural arm swing coordinated with takeoff, two-foot takeoff, bilateral landing in the starting area, and a controlled two-second hold without a corrective step. The first three scheduled attempts establish today's observation. During the existing rest, select at most one useful cue for the remaining six attempts: “One dip, then up” for a repeated dip; “Jump straight up” for travel; or “Swing up as you push” for a stalled arm action. Select only from an observed need; if the pattern is already consistent, ask the athlete to repeat it. Record the chosen cue and whether the same fault recurs. This is coaching within nine attempts, not extra assessment work or a promise of improvement.

Keep the Class 1 measurement protocol: same natural arm policy, stationary start, footwear/surface and reset conditions where actual records allow. Record height only if the force-plate setup and its calculation method are verified; otherwise record technique alone. Compare heights only between compatible valid maximum-intent attempts. Reduced-intent or changed-protocol attempts remain separately labeled. No replacement test attempts after a miss.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Perform in order. Retain the written Class 1 dose and each variant's distinct job. All are bodyweight except E6. The main jump remains early; supporting jumps use a small dose, and E4/E5 remain controlled rather than maximum-height tests.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stationary Bilateral Ankle Pogo** | **1 × 10**, low and continuous. **60 s before E2.** | Rehearse a quick, centered ankle return from the floor before integrating the larger takeoff action. Keep hips tall, flight low and knee motion small. Lower amplitude or stop if rhythm breaks or contacts become heavy; do not increase height to make Class 2 harder. |
| 2 | **Countermovement Jump — natural arm swing, stick landing** | **3 × 3**, maximum-height intent with controlled execution. **20 s between attempts; 120 s between sets and before E3.** | **Main anchor:** repeat one smooth dip-to-drive action while coordinating the arms. Use the observation/cue process above within these attempts. Jump upward, land in the starting area and hold 2 s. Reduce intent for lost control and record the changed intent; do not chase a height at the expense of the landing. |
| 3 | **Squat Jump — paused start, hands on hips** | **2 × 2**, maximum upward intent from a comfortable partial squat held **2 s**. **20 s between attempts; 90 s between sets and before E4.** | Preserve force production from stillness as a contrasting component of the vertical drive. Keep hands on hips; no second dip. Land on both feet and hold 2 s. Use a shallower comfortable start if needed to hold still. A re-dip counts as an attempted rep with a fault, not a valid paused jump. |
| 4 | **Single-leg vertical jump to two-foot landing** — proposed execution variant | **1 × 2 per leg = 4 jumps**, quick takeoff at controlled submaximal height. **20 s between same-leg attempts; 60 s between legs; 90 s before E5.** | Practice each leg's upward push with the same arm/leg timing focus, while retaining a bilateral landing. Dip on the takeoff leg, swing upward and bring both feet to a 2 s landing hold. Reduce height/dip for pelvic tilt or knee collapse. Record side order; reverse the previous actual order only if it is known, otherwise begin left then right. |
| 5 | **Approach Vertical Jump — two steps, two-foot takeoff** | **2 × 2**, controlled approach speed and controllable height. **20 s between attempts; 90 s between sets and before E6.** | Carry the coordinated arm/leg action into a short two-foot plant. Use two walking-to-brisk steps, jump upward and hold the bilateral landing 2 s. Keep the entry speed modest; slow/shorten the steps if forward travel compromises the landing zone. This remains vertical takeoff practice, not sprint training. |
| 6 | **Light medicine-ball vertical scoop toss** — proposed addition | **2 × 3**. **20 s between throws; 90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Rehearse a fast legs-to-arms extension against light resistance. From one shallow dip, drive upward, rise onto the forefeet and release up/slightly forward; keep feet in contact with the floor. Choose a ball that permits a smooth, fast release without an arm-only heave or back arch. Reduce load if those appear. Do not catch the return. |

**Recovery and stop rule:** After a clear output or control decline, extend the next rest 30–60 s. If the next scheduled attempt still deteriorates, reduce intent/complexity; end the exercise if quality remains poor. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness, disabling fear or unsafe space/equipment. Count failed attempts and any unintended flight in actual exposure. Do not force all prescribed work when the athlete is not ready, and do not fill omitted work with extra jumps later.

**Space:** Use the current curriculum's **10 m lane limit** despite the older facility reference's 12 m description. For E5, reserve one lane: steps/plant within the first 3 m, landing/hold in the next 2 m, remaining 5 m clear. Verify width, fall space and actual travel; one active athlete, no crossing traffic. **Use a one-step Approach Vertical Jump instead of** the two-step version if that is needed to fit: keep 2 × 2 and the same rest. Resolve the station before use if even one step cannot fit.

For E6, verify overhead, landing, rebound/roll and retrieval clearance; keep others out and retrieve only after the ball settles. Extend the 20 s reset if retrieval takes longer. **Use a light dumbbell Loaded Countermovement Jump (earlier label: Loaded Squat Jump) instead of E6** only when throwing clearance is unavailable and unloaded jump control plus secure dumbbell handling are demonstrated: **2 × 2**, light dumbbells at sides, one smooth dip, fast bilateral takeoff, controlled bilateral landing with a 2 s hold; **20 s between attempts, 90 s between sets/before Section 3**. It retains externally loaded vertical power but removes free arm action and replaces six throws with **four loaded jump landings**. Record that change; do not perform both. If neither task is suitable, resolve the exercise before delivery rather than claim a complete session.

**Final-sequence clarification:** Choose the E6 throw or loaded-jump replacement before starting E6. If throwing has already begun and becomes unsuitable, end that slot; do not restart with four loaded jumps. Other replacements use only the remaining original attempted budget, including failed attempts. The loaded option uses a smooth dip before takeoff, not a paused squat start; only its label is corrected.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Light and controlled: leave **at least 5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or comparable ease on holds. Retain the small Class 1 dose so these exercises do not undermine the main strength work.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Step-Down — low lateral heel-tap variant** | **1 × 5 per leg = 10 reps**, bodyweight or light dumbbell. **3 s down, 1 s return. 30 s between legs; 45 s before S2.** | Lightly load the stance leg while holding knee, pelvis and foot alignment through flexion. This supports a consistent plant/dip position. Use a suitably low stable box; touch the free heel without pushing from it. Reduce range/load for hip drop, knee collapse or loss of stance-foot contact. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported single-leg hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot = 6 holds**. Bodyweight; **5 s relaxed between holds, 30 s between sides, 60 s before primary preparation.** | Strengthen active arch control while keeping heel and both forefoot bases in contact. Use hand support, a soft knee and long toes so foot control, rather than balance, is trained. Ease the contraction if toes claw or the ankle rolls; stop for cramping that does not resolve. |

If no suitably low stable step is available, **use a hand-supported single-leg shallow squat instead of S1**: 1 × 5 per leg with the same tempo/rest, free foot clear, light effort; this remains a proposed replacement without a claimed canonical ID. **Use bilateral standing Short-Foot Drill instead of S2** if single-leg arch control is inconsistent: 1 × 3 five-second holds with both feet together, 5 s relaxed between holds, then 60 s before primary preparation. These replace slots; they are not added exercises.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

**Load selection:** Target approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** on working sets, supported by coach observation and athlete report. No fixed increase over Class 1 is prescribed. If a compatible load was actually recorded and the athlete is ready, it can inform today's selection. Without that record, establish the load through the preparation sets below; do not invent a previous weight. Finish each repetition with purposeful upward force and controlled lowering, without grinding, forced reps or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Double Front-Rack Squat — dumbbells** | **3 × 6 working reps**. About **3 s down**, stand decisively. **150 s between working sets and before P2 preparation.** | Maintain the main bilateral knee/hip-extension force base for the vertical drive. Keep the rack and trunk organized over the whole foot through a repeatable, controlled depth. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee collapse or trunk/rack loss. Keep the same useful range where actual prior records exist. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — dumbbells** | **2 × 6 working reps**. **3 s down, about 1 s up. 120 s between working sets and before P3 preparation.** | Challenge hip extension from a flexed-hip position, complementing the squat's larger knee excursion. Keep dumbbells close, knees softly bent and hips traveling back. End the descent before back rounding/pelvic tuck; reduce load or range if hinge control is lost. |
| 3 | **Split Squat — stationary, dumbbells** | **1 × 6 per leg = 12 working reps**. **3 s down, about 1 s up. 75 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Maintain a challenging lead-leg force contribution for each side of takeoff without a second large squat volume. Keep the rear forefoot grounded, heel lifted, and pelvis controlled. Reduce load or use hand support if balance limits leg force. One set per leg remains intentional. |
| 4 | **Hip Thrust — sandbag across pelvis, upper back on bench** | **1 × 8 working reps**. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down. 90 s before P5 preparation.** | Load the terminal part of hip extension with supported trunk geometry, complementing the RDL's flexed-hip emphasis. Stop at aligned ribs/pelvis rather than arching the back. Reduce load for lumbar extension or loss of stable bench contact. |
| 5 | **Standing Calf Raise — supported single-leg, dumbbell** | **1 × 8 per leg = 16 working reps**. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down. 60 s between legs; 75 s before P6 preparation.** | Challenge each ankle's nearly straight-knee plantarflexion for the late upward push. Work from the floor with secure hand support; keep heel travel vertical and knee angle steady. Use bodyweight if it meets the effort target, otherwise add manageable load. Reduce load for shrinking range or knee bending. |
| 6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **2 × 10 working reps**, both legs together. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down**; quiet heel return. **90 s between working sets.** | Maintain ankle-force work with bent knees, complementing P5 and the ankle's contribution through the dip-to-drive action. Keep knees approximately over the feet, hold loads securely and keep the knee angle stable. Reduce load for bounce, knee extension or loss of heel range. |

**Separate load-preparation sets:** P1 **2 × 5**, light then moderate, **60 s between preparation sets and 120 s before working**. P2 **1 × 5 light**, then **90 s**. P3 **1 × 3 light per leg**, **30 s between legs**, then **90 s**. P4 **1 × 5 light**, then **60 s**. P5 **1 × 4 light per leg**, **30 s between legs**, then **45 s**. P6 **1 × 5 light**, then **60 s**. Keep these non-fatiguing; use the working exercise's tempo. Additional ramp sets needed for a particular load must be logged and added to the clock. They are not new exercises or a recreated Access & Prepare sequence.

Stop a working set at the technical limit even if the target reps are not reached, then reduce the next load. Extend recovery when needed. **Use two kettlebells instead of dumbbells in P1** if they provide a manageable rack and appropriate load. **Use a sandbag floor Glute Bridge instead of P4** if bench fit/stability is unsuitable: retain 1 × 5 light preparation, 60 s to work, 1 × 8 working at the same tempo and 90 s before P5 preparation; record the different support/range. All loads must be manageable to pick up, position and put down. Secure hand supports against movement.
