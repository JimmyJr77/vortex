# Vertical Jumps — Class 4 of 12

**Primary effort:** Direct a controlled dip or short entry into decisive upward leg drive. **Box Jump — standing countermovement** is the main performance drill. Supporting work practices redirecting a lateral entry upward, single-leg upward projection, low ankle-return rhythm and rapid leg extension against external load.

**Foundation and review:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Classes 1–3 are saved prescriptions; actual attendance, execution, load and recovery are still unknown. Their files, workload records, drill history and available feedback were reviewed. No new athlete observations were found. New drill competence is not inferred from the earlier prescriptions. Use the first scheduled attempts at controlled intent to establish current execution; stronger intent is appropriate only on later scheduled attempts with demonstrated control. Do not add trial or make-up repetitions.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record valid attempts out of six. A valid attempt uses one coordinated standing dip/arm swing, projects upward without a stutter or extra hop, lands with both whole feet supported, and holds the landing 2 s before standing and stepping down. Record box height, starting gap, dip/arm conditions and faults. Keep the box low enough that clearance comes from upward projection with little knee tuck. Box height is not jump height; this is not a matched retest of Class 3's seated start or the earlier floor CMJ.

**Continuity and distinct drills:** Standing Box Jump restores an active dip while keeping Class 3's elevated target concept. The lateral step-and-gather, isolated height skip, rope-timed bounce, high-hang pull and backward overhead throw each change a relevant task constraint. These are distinct default formats, sometimes within an existing exercise family, rather than six unrelated movement patterns. The main task retains six attempts. No athlete is presumed ready for more demanding impacts merely because this is Class 4.

**Style reference:** OTA includes box jumping and power skipping before strength in its [vertical specialization example](https://my.overtimeathletes.com/index.php?wlmfile=/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Vertical-Jump-Specialization-1-1.pdf). This class adapts those task ideas to small, fully accounted doses and the current facility; it does not copy the example's volume or presume its readiness requirements.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Follow this order, with full resets except the small continuous rope bout. E1–E4 are bodyweight. Strong upward intent must remain compatible with the specified landing; E3 stays modest because its same-leg landing is a new demand. E5–E6 use light, readily controlled loads, not strength-set effort.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Box Jump — standing countermovement, step-down reset** | **3 × 2**. **20 s after returning to the start between attempts; 120 s between sets and before E2.** | Main task: turn one standing dip and coordinated arm swing into upward takeoff to a low stable box. No run-up, repeated dip or preliminary hop. Both feet land fully supported; hold 2 s, stand, step down and return. Choose a gap requiring little forward travel. Lower the box/intent for deep tuck, edge contact or drift; stop for unstable support or loss of control. |
| 2 | **Approach Vertical Jump — lateral step-and-gather** | **1 × 2 per entry direction = 4 jumps**. **20 s between same-direction attempts; 60 s between directions; 90 s before E3.** | Redirect a small sideways entry upward without rotating. From a parallel stance, take one short lateral step, step the trailing foot alongside to a comfortable two-foot takeoff base, then dip/drive upward with arms. No crossing, running or extra shuffle. Land on two feet near the takeoff area and hold 2 s. Use a walking entry over at most 1 m; slow/shorten it for sideways drift or knee/pelvis loss. Start leftward then rightward unless a recorded side order justifies reversing it. |
| 3 | **Power Skip for Height — isolated step-hop cycle** — proposed exact execution | **1 × 2 per takeoff leg = 4 hops**. **20 s after clearing/resetting between same-leg cycles; 60 s between legs; 90 s before E4.** | Express upward force through one leg while the free knee and its opposite arm coordinate the rise. Take one walking step onto the takeoff foot, make one modest upward hop with opposite knee drive, land on that same foot, absorb under control, then place the free foot down and walk clear. No second bounce or continuous skip run. Keep each cycle within 2 m including its exit; no distance chase. Start left then right. Lower flight for pelvic drop or loss of same-leg control; use the replacement below if that landing is unsuitable. |
| 4 | **Jump Rope Bounce — low bilateral single passes** | **1 × 8 attempted jumps**, one rope pass per jump, low and continuous when controlled; **90 s before E5.** | Use rope timing to organize a small, quick ankle return beneath an upright body. Both feet leave/return together, knees soft, wrists turn the rope; jump only enough to clear it. These are low elastic contacts, not eight maximal jumps. Stop and reset after a trip; any attempted jump still consumes the eight-jump budget. End for repeated trips, louder contacts or loss of rhythm. No double-unders or extra bounces to reach eight successful passes. |
| 5 | **Dumbbell High-Hang Pull — no flight or catch** — proposed exact execution | **2 × 3** individual rapid pulls. **20 s after lowering/resetting between reps; 90 s between sets and before E6.** | Accelerate a small external load upward through coordinated knee/hip extension and heel rise. Begin tall, dumbbells beside the thighs, arms long; make a shallow dip/hinge to high-thigh level, then drive tall quickly onto the forefeet. Allow a natural shoulder rise, keep elbows long and weights close; no deliberate high elbow pull, rack catch, jump or backward lean. Lower under control over about 2 s, heels down, reset. Choose a load permitting crisp leg-driven motion; first three reps establish technique conservatively. Reduce load/intent or stop for arm hauling, lost trunk position or uncontrolled lowering. |
| 6 | **Backward Overhead Medicine-Ball Throw — stationary, no flight** | **2 × 2** individual throws. **20 s after retrieval/reset between throws; 90 s between sets and before Section 3.** | Express leg-to-arm extension into a free upward/backward release, complementary to E5's retained load. Face away from the coach-closed landing sector, ball in both hands in front. Use one comfortable dip, drive through hips/knees/ankles and release over the head without turning or throwing by arching the back. Heels may rise; feet remain grounded. Use a light ball and controllable range, not a distance test. Finish balanced, wait until the ball settles and the coach clears retrieval; never catch its return. Reduce intent or use the replacement if range/roll cannot be contained. |

**Current execution and stop rules:** E1–E3's first scheduled attempts check the exact takeoff and landing at controlled intent. E3 requires observable same-leg absorption; established bilateral landing technique does not establish this. E4 begins low and slow enough to control the rope. E5 requires a demonstrable dip/hinge, secure grip and controlled heel return; E6 requires controlled leg-driven release and a verified closed sector. Do not add demonstrations performed by athletes outside the dose. Stop immediately for pain, giving way, dizziness, disabling fear or unsafe space/equipment. After a clear quality decline, extend rest 30–60 s; if the next scheduled effort still deteriorates, reduce or end the task. Record invalid, omitted and incidental work rather than replacing failed attempts.

**Space and equipment:** Use suitable stable low box/step/bench sizes, a rope that fits, manageable dumbbells and a light medicine ball whose behavior is known. Only the inventory categories and three separate 10 m lanes are confirmed. For E2, orient the sideways travel along a lane's length when helpful; usable width still must contain the athlete's takeoff/landing and fall margin. E3 needs at most 2 m per isolated cycle plus a clear reset/waiting route, not a long runway. Keep the rope arc and dumbbell station clear of others. E6 needs a closed inspected landing-and-roll sector behind the thrower and adequate overhead clearance; the mere existence of a 10 m lane does not prove containment. The coach stands outside the flight/roll sector with sight of it and signals release and retrieval separately. Never have another group using that sector. Establish a suitable contained release before delivery; do not test an uncertain boundary with a throw.

**Replacements occupy the original slot:** If a suitable E1 box cannot be arranged, **use a natural-arm floor Countermovement Jump instead of E1**, same 3 × 2 and rests with 2 s bilateral landing holds; six elevated landings become floor landings and six step-downs are removed. This deliberately repeats an earlier drill to preserve appropriate execution. If lateral clearance/control is unsuitable, **use a two-walking-step forward Approach Vertical Jump instead of E2**, same 1 × 2 per starting lead, four attempts, rests and landing holds, within a verified short lane; record the repeated earlier format and eight approach foot placements.

If E3's same-leg landing is unsuitable, **use a single-leg vertical takeoff to two-foot landing instead of E3**, 1 × 2 per takeoff leg, same rests, low controlled flight and 2 s bilateral landing hold; no approach step. This repeats the earlier regression, changes four unilateral landings to bilateral landings and removes the four entry/four exit steps of the skip cycles. If even this takeoff is unsuitable, stop the slot and log it. If rope coordination/fit prevents E4's job, **use eight low stationary bilateral ankle pogos instead of E4**, sharing the original eight-attempt budget with any rope jumps already attempted; no extra contacts. This is a disclosed repeated regression.

If E5 cannot be controlled with manageable dumbbells, reduce load or stop; do not substitute a slow strength grind. If E6's backward sector is unavailable but a safe upward/slightly forward ball landing-and-roll area is verified, **use the earlier non-flight vertical scoop toss instead of E6**, 2 × 2, same rests and controlled recovery/retrieval; keep the leg-driven release and log the repeated format. If neither throw area can be made suitable, resolve the slot before delivery or log it unperformed. Replacements are choices within six slots, never an added circuit.

## 3. Strength (Stabilization/Resilience) — Exactly 2 Exercises

Light and controlled: leave **at least 5 clean repetitions in reserve**, or comparable ease on holds. Retain the small established dose so these exercises do not undermine the main strength work.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Step-Down — low lateral heel-tap variant** | **1 × 5 per leg = 10 reps**, bodyweight or light dumbbell. **3 s down, 1 s return. 30 s between legs; 45 s before S2.** | Lightly load the stance leg while holding knee, pelvis and foot alignment through flexion. This supports a consistent plant/dip position. Use a suitably low stable box; touch the free heel without pushing from it. Reduce range/load for hip drop, knee collapse or loss of stance-foot contact. |
| 2 | **Short-Foot Drill — supported single-leg hold** | **1 × 3 holds of 5 s per foot = 6 holds**. Bodyweight; **5 s relaxed between holds, 30 s between sides, 60 s before primary preparation.** | Strengthen active arch control while keeping heel and both forefoot bases in contact. Use hand support, a soft knee and long toes so foot control, rather than balance, is trained. Ease the contraction if toes claw or the ankle rolls; stop for cramping that does not resolve. |

If no suitably low stable step is available, **use a hand-supported single-leg shallow squat instead of S1**: 1 × 5 per leg with the same tempo/rest, free foot clear, light effort; this remains a proposed replacement without a claimed canonical ID. **Use bilateral standing Short-Foot Drill instead of S2** if single-leg arch control is inconsistent: 1 × 3 five-second holds with both feet together, 5 s relaxed between holds, then 60 s before primary preparation. These replace slots; they are not added exercises.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

**Load selection:** Target approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve** on working sets, supported by coach observation and athlete report. No fixed increase over the earlier written classes is prescribed. If a compatible load was actually recorded and the athlete is ready, it can inform today's selection. Without that record, establish the load through the preparation sets below; do not invent a previous weight. Finish each repetition with purposeful upward force and controlled lowering, without grinding, forced reps or maximal testing.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Double Front-Rack Squat — dumbbells** | **3 × 6 working reps**. About **3 s down**, stand decisively. **150 s between working sets and before P2 preparation.** | Maintain the main bilateral knee/hip-extension force base for the vertical drive. Keep the rack and trunk organized over the whole foot through a repeatable, controlled depth. Reduce load/range for heel lift, knee collapse or trunk/rack loss. Keep the same useful range where actual prior records exist. |
| 2 | **Romanian Deadlift — dumbbells** | **2 × 6 working reps**. **3 s down, about 1 s up. 120 s between working sets and before P3 preparation.** | Challenge hip extension from a flexed-hip position, complementing the squat's larger knee excursion. Keep dumbbells close, knees softly bent and hips traveling back. End the descent before back rounding/pelvic tuck; reduce load or range if hinge control is lost. |
| 3 | **Split Squat — stationary, dumbbells** | **1 × 6 per leg = 12 working reps**. **3 s down, about 1 s up. 75 s between legs; 120 s before P4 preparation.** | Maintain a challenging lead-leg force contribution for each side of takeoff without a second large squat volume. Keep the rear forefoot grounded, heel lifted, and pelvis controlled. Reduce load or use hand support if balance limits leg force. One set per leg remains intentional. |
| 4 | **Hip Thrust — sandbag across pelvis, upper back on bench** | **1 × 8 working reps**. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down. 90 s before P5 preparation.** | Load the terminal part of hip extension with supported trunk geometry, complementing the RDL's flexed-hip emphasis. Stop at aligned ribs/pelvis rather than arching the back. Reduce load for lumbar extension or loss of stable bench contact. |
| 5 | **Standing Calf Raise — supported single-leg, dumbbell** | **1 × 8 per leg = 16 working reps**. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down. 60 s between legs; 75 s before P6 preparation.** | Challenge each ankle's nearly straight-knee plantarflexion for the late upward push. Work from the floor with secure hand support; keep heel travel vertical and knee angle steady. Use bodyweight if it meets the effort target, otherwise add manageable load. Reduce load for shrinking range or knee bending. |
| 6 | **Bent-Knee Soleus Raise — seated, dumbbells on thighs** | **2 × 10 working reps**, both legs together. **1 s up, 1 s hold, 1 s down**; quiet heel return. **90 s between working sets.** | Maintain ankle-force work with bent knees, complementing P5 and the ankle's contribution through the dip-to-drive action. Keep knees approximately over the feet, hold loads securely and keep the knee angle stable. Reduce load for bounce, knee extension or loss of heel range. |

**Separate load-preparation sets:** P1 **2 × 5**, light then moderate, **60 s between preparation sets and 120 s before working**. P2 **1 × 5 light**, then **90 s**. P3 **1 × 3 light per leg**, **30 s between legs**, then **90 s**. P4 **1 × 5 light**, then **60 s**. P5 **1 × 4 light per leg**, **30 s between legs**, then **45 s**. P6 **1 × 5 light**, then **60 s**. Keep these non-fatiguing; use the working exercise's tempo. Additional ramp sets needed for a particular load must be logged and added to the clock. They are not new exercises or a recreated Access & Prepare sequence.

Stop a working set at the technical limit even if the target reps are not reached, then reduce the next load. Extend recovery when needed. **Use two kettlebells instead of dumbbells in P1** if they provide a manageable rack and appropriate load. **Use a sandbag floor Glute Bridge instead of P4** if bench fit/stability is unsuitable: retain 1 × 5 light preparation, 60 s to work, 1 × 8 working at the same tempo and 90 s before P5 preparation; record the different support/range. All loads must be manageable to pick up, position and put down. Secure hand supports against movement.

The estimate includes six box dismounts, isolated skip entry/exit, weight lowering and ball retrieval.
