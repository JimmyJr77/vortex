# Vertical Jumps — Class 6 of 12

**Primary effort:** Convert a short entry into an organized plant and upward drive. **Approach Box Jump — two-step entry, two-foot takeoff** is the primary drill. The supporting jobs are a single-leg entry plant, low ankle return across a small displacement, rapid drive after placing a foot on a step, leg-to-arm vertical load transfer and an upward ball release after gathering the feet.

**Foundation and review:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting and landing technique, under qualified supervision. Classes 1–5, their audits, workload/progression records, drill history and available feedback were reviewed. Actual attendance, execution, loads and recovery remain unknown. Class 5's written one-leg takeoffs and changed strength allocation do not establish tolerance. Today retains that strength prescription while introducing short walking entries; no faster approach, higher box or heavier load is automatic.

**Quality marker inside E1:** Record valid attempts out of six, including starting lead. A valid attempt uses exactly two short walking steps into a two-foot plant, projects upward without an extra hop/stutter, and lands with both whole feet on a low box for a 2 s hold. Record entry start mark, gap, box height, lead, arm action, intent, drift, knee/pelvis control and any added contact. Box height is not jump height, and changed entry/landing protocols are not matched tests of prior standing jumps.

**Style reference:** OTA's [Approach Box Jump](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/approach-box-jump/) provides the approach-to-upward-projection task. This class uses its own two-step, six-attempt prescription and controlled walking entry, rather than importing the article's volume or maximal-height instruction. The other formats preserve specific force-development jobs; necessary repeated regressions are disclosed.
