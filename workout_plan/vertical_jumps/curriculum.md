## Style, evidence and revisions

Use OTA-inspired focused explosive practice, lower-body strength, elastic/unilateral contributions and continuity. Scale the workload and complexity to this cohort; advanced depth drops, overspeed rigging and heavy loaded jumps are not default requirements. [OTA programming article](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/vertical-jump-training-how-to-program-explosive-bounce-for-serious-athletes/) and [OTA exercise examples](https://blog.overtimeathletes.com/5-best-plyometric-exercises-for-vertical-jump/) describe the style; they do not establish this session's dose or athlete readiness.

The [NSCA youth resistance-training position statement](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf) supports qualified instruction, technique-led loading, low-repetition power work and individualized progression/recovery. The 6/2/6 structure and exact doses are coaching decisions under the user's constraint, not a research-validated protocol.

Arm swing changes CMJ mechanics and output in a study of 21 adults; this supports recording a consistent arm policy, not predicting a child's training gain. [Cefai et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11368957/)
