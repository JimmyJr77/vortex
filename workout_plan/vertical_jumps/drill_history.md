# Vertical Jumps — drill history and selection policy

**User preference, received during Class 3 development:** “Try to force unique drills; while strength lifts may recur as needed for progressions.” Apply this to the remainder of the curriculum and to the finalized Class 3 revision. Classes 1–2 remain the historical written prescriptions; their repeated drills are not misrepresented as unique or as performed training.

For each new class, prefer explosive drills not previously prescribed as defaults in this focus. Select a meaningful task difference—starting support, arm constraint, takeoff/landing pattern, obstacle, or force-expression action—not a renamed drill or the same drill at a different load. Every drill still needs a direct job in the day's vertical-jump effort. Do not introduce an advanced impact task solely to obtain novelty. A necessary regression or an explicitly justified comparison anchor may recur; record why. The user asked to try for uniqueness, not to sacrifice task relevance, recovery or competence.

Primary lifts and light stabilization/resilience strengthening may recur when their role remains useful and their previous prescription → current prescription → decision reason → next criterion is recorded. A written prior dose does not establish attendance, competence, recovery or a load to increase.

**Class 3 distinct jobs:** supported-start upward power; vertical leg drive with restricted backward arm swing; asymmetric two-foot force; upright clearance/landing over one low obstacle; fast single-leg extension without flight; rapid hip extension with external load. Similar lower-body muscles do not make these the same task, and different names alone would not justify them.

**Replacements already offered:** Classes 1–2: loaded dumbbell Countermovement Jump (earlier label: Loaded Squat Jump; smooth dip), one-step Approach Vertical Jump, supported shallow single-leg squat, bilateral Short-Foot Drill, two-kettlebell front rack and floor Glute Bridge. Class 3: seated floor-landing Vertical Jump, forward hop over a virtual line between cones, supported Split-Stance Power Rise, two-handed dumbbell swing, plus the retained strengthening replacements. Check these too when deciding whether a future drill is novel for an athlete who actually used a replacement. Access & Prepare 1 content is unknown, so uniqueness against that existing sequence cannot be verified here.
