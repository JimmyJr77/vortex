## Daily stimulus brief

1. **Result:** decisive upward force through each takeoff leg with an organized foot, knee and pelvis.
2. **Demand:** generate upward impulse from one leg, with controlled assisted/staggered support tasks that isolate useful parts of the extension action without demanding new single-leg rebound landings.
3. **Primary task:** Single-Leg Takeoff to Two-Foot Box Landing, three attempts per side. This is a new written elevated-target condition, not proof of mastered single-leg takeoff or a measured progression.
4. **Supporting jobs:** assisted staggered propulsion to a square landing; low asymmetric ankle return; rapid front-leg extension from a lower supported start; rapid hip extension with rear-foot support; leg-to-arm upward projection. Primary strength emphasizes split-stance leg force while preserving bilateral, hip-range and ankle-position contributions. Light work retains knee/pelvis/foot control.
5. **Marker/constraints:** valid E1 attempts out of three per side, recording actual support, dip, arm action, box/gap, faults and intent. No prior actual dose, working weight, recovery or task competence is known. Preparation, equipment dimensions and group delivery remain unresolved.

## Selection and revision findings

| Decision | Why it earns its place / revision made |
|---|---|
| Single-leg elevated target rather than more same-leg skip landings | The main task directly practices one-leg upward force, retaining six main attempts but using two-foot landings. It draws on the earlier floor unilateral takeoff and box-target concepts without assuming either was performed. Six one-leg takeoffs versus the four previously prescribed skips is a changed allocation, not an earned increase. First scheduled attempts and stop/replacement rules remain explicit. |
| Staggered jump to parallel landing rather than another one-leg jump | Rear-foot support permits a lead-leg bias with shared propulsion; organizing the feet into a square landing supplies a distinct coordination job. This differs from Class 3's same-lead split landing. No exact force share or isolated-leg measurement is asserted. Keep only two attempts per lead. |
| Low split-stance pogo rather than unilateral pogo or scissor jumps | A brief four-contact bout per lead uses asymmetric ankle timing while both feet contribute. It is not a second large split-squat-jump dose. No lead switching or high flight is prescribed. Eight low contacts retain the previous rope-bout count while changing its constraint; no tolerance is inferred. |
| Rapid half-kneeling rise rather than half-kneeling box flight | A supported lower start changes front-leg range and initiation. Remove flight and landing-height consequences from this support slot, and make the rise genuinely rapid with controlled return. The generic local exercise is a control task; this rapid execution is explicitly proposed. If it is slow, painful at the knee contact or balance-dominated, use the shallower standing rise rather than mislabel it explosive. |
| Staggered swing rather than unsupported single-leg swing | Rear-foot support retains a controllable hinge while biasing the lead leg; both hands hold the bell. Only three swings per lead limit posterior-chain overlap. The stance variant is a local proposal, not claimed approved. Reduce the stagger/use the bilateral replacement when stability restricts the intended hip speed. |
| Split-stance upward scoop rather than another backward throw | A forward-visible release sector and leg-biased base suit this day's force-transfer job. Four releases limit overlap with E4/E5 and primary work. This changes stance/direction, not merely ball mass. It is a proposed exact execution; no false rotational/forward-toss ID is used. |
| Split Squat prioritized; bilateral squat reduced | Move Split Squat to P1 and change 1 × 6/leg to 2 × 4/leg; reduce Front-Rack Squat from 3 × 6 to 2 × 6 and move it to P2. RDL becomes P3 with its dose unchanged. Shorter split-squat bouts get more emphasis and generous same-leg recovery; no heavier weight is prescribed automatically. |
| Overlap audited rather than hidden by total reps | E2/E3/E4/E5/E6 all use asymmetric support, but their propulsion/landing, ankle, range, hip-speed and free-release jobs differ. Doses are deliberately small. Four additional split-squat reps and two additional unilateral takeoffs remain meaningful changes despite fewer total jumps/primary reps. Actual response can require reduction; no arithmetic proves tissue-load equivalence. |
