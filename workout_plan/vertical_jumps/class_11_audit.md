## Daily stimulus brief

1. **Result:** express an organized standing vertical jump under repeatable conditions suitable for a later observation.
2. **Demand:** coordinate natural arm swing and leg drive while preserving one dip, stationary takeoff and a controlled bilateral landing. Establish the protocol rather than infer a performance deficit.
3. **Primary task:** deliberately recurring natural-arm Countermovement Jump, six attempts: two checks/rehearsals then up to four candidates if current control/readiness allows. Record attempt status regardless of measurement availability.
4. **Support jobs:** static seated leg initiation without arm assistance; low ankle return under an upward-reach posture; still chest-held force into ball release; brief single hip impulses under one-hand load; simple grounded-foot lead-leg force. Strength retains bilateral/individual-leg force, complementary hip ranges and both knee positions for ankle force. Light work supports alignment/contact without pre-fatigue.
5. **Marker/constraints:** valid/attempted and attempted/scheduled counts with conditions/reasons; verified height only if an appropriate actual method exists. No assumed baseline, familiarization, valid candidate, device capability or recovered capacity. No extra tests. Reduce overlapping supporting volume without claiming an established fatigue state or a physiological taper effect.

## Selection, overlap and revisions

The standing natural-arm CMJ was chosen over a new approach/box/loaded task because it directly assesses the intended upward performance with a clear protocol and minimal setup demands. This is deliberate recurrence, not six new drills claimed by renaming. Class 10's paused walking entry and the earlier box/loaded variants are not interchangeable measurements. Even Classes 1–2's same exercise had different attempts/rests and no supplied actual data; no matched baseline is assumed.

Four new supporting formats earn their constraints: E2 removes arm assistance from the earlier natural-arm seated floor jump; E3 retains low ankle rhythm with arms fixed overhead rather than adding directional contacts; E4 pauses a chest-held throw rather than another moving or underhand start; E5 combines one-hand support and one separately parked swing rather than another continuous series. E6 revisits the earlier grounded-foot split-rise replacement for simple leg force, avoiding another new balance or impact task. The exact format changes are relative to checked defaults and offered replacements, not unknown preparation or unreported actual use. No approved exact variant IDs are invented.

E1 and E2 overlap in knee/hip extension, but six active-dip task attempts and three supported static initiations have different starting demands. E3 adds four low ankle contacts, not another full-height jump workout. E4 is a three-rep knee/hip-to-arm release task, whereas four E5 swings emphasize hip impulse under a held returning load. E5 still adds grip/start/park demands and must remain light and controlled. E6 has four supported no-flight rises; P2 remains genuinely challenging slow strength rather than duplicate power work. RDL/one Hip Thrust set preserve different hip ranges. P5/P6 retain straight-/bent-knee ankle roles; fewer jumps do not prove calf readiness.

**Revisions before finalization:** explicitly used two of E1's six attempts for current checks instead of adding a test block; created the recording protocol with no fabricated values or assumed reliable device. Reduced support jumping to three E2 reps, throws to three and loaded hip efforts to four; retained meaningful work in every slot. Kept E3 at two pairs/two links. Reduced P2 by one whole round, preserving all remaining rests, five other strength doses and preparation. Removed a pointless final reseating from E2: two between-rep seated returns and a final exit are counted. Regressions preserve distinct static-versus-active jump jobs and share original budgets. A shorter modeled clock is not proof of actual reduced fatigue.
