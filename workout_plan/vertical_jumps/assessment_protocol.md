# Vertical Jumps — shared Class 11–12 assessment protocol

This recording/interpretation contract applies to **E1 within Classes 11 and 12**. Class 12 retains Class 11's exact six-attempt allocation and recovery. It adds no exercise, preparation sequence or test block. Actual delivery, athlete competence, device capability and compatible observed baseline remain unknown; written protocol consistency does not establish matched real conditions.

## Listed exercise and attempt budget

Natural-arm standing **Countermovement Jump**, bodyweight, **3 × 2 attempts**. Pair 1 is two controlled execution checks/rehearsals. Pairs 2–3 provide four candidate observations with maximum-height intent only if readiness, dip/arm coordination and landing control permit. Rest 30 s after landing/reset between attempts and 120 s between pairs; preserve 120 s before E2. No bonus practice, repeated failures or extra tests. The checks belong to the six explosive attempts, not the 39 strength-preparation reps.

If two checks do not establish usable execution, the remaining attempts may remain practice, with no numerical candidate summary. If execution is unsuitable even at reduced intent, stop. Unknown eligibility is not a passed gate. One successful attempt does not establish mastery or universal readiness.

## Conditions to declare before candidate attempts

- Athlete/cohort, session date and reporting coach; actual readiness/restrictions and recent sport, gym and separate tumbling. Record actual Access & Prepare 1 and its timing/load without modifying it.
- Surface/shoes, stance width and foot orientation, same self-selected dip instruction, natural arm swing, stationary start, no preparatory hop/step, one dip/flight and bilateral 2 s terminal hold. Record the cue and intended effort. Keep candidate conditions consistent; a necessary coaching change is recorded as a protocol change, never suppressed to protect a comparison.
- Choose **execution-only observation** unless a suitable measurement method has actually been verified. Equipment inventory alone does not verify a force plate's footprint, setup, calibration, software, sample/processing method, jump-height calculation or operator competence. Verify those from the installed setup and its instructions, using no additional athlete jumps to establish them. If measurement setup cannot be completed within delivery conditions, use execution-only observation and record why.
- If a suitable method is verified, declare exact device/model, software/algorithm, setup/calibration procedure, units, operator, landing/takeoff validity rules and how attempts are saved. Keep the same method/conditions for any proposed comparison; do not mix flight-time, impulse-based or jump-reach values. This document does not select an unverified calculation or invent a device capability.

## Attempt record and summary

For each numbered slot 1–6, record attempted/omitted, rehearsal or candidate, intended effort, valid/modified/invalid and reason, actual rest, and a measured value only when that method is verified. No numeric results exist yet. Valid execution requires the declared stationary parallel start, one dip with natural arm swing, upward two-foot takeoff, controlled bilateral 2 s landing and no extra hop/step. Measurement validity must also meet the verified device protocol. A modified arm policy, reduced intent or changed setup is useful practice but belongs to a separate protocol, not the original candidate summary.

Always report **valid/attempted** and **attempted/scheduled** with reasons for omissions. Pair 1 never enters the candidate height summary. For the four candidate slots, retain every actual value/status. If measurement and all conditions are suitable, report the arithmetic mean and best of valid candidates with the number included; with zero valid candidates, the summary is unavailable, not zero. A mean of one value may be reported descriptively but does not establish repeatability. Do not add attempts to reach a desired sample size. Execution-only counts describe task success, not jump height, power, force symmetry or training adaptation.

## What a later comparison can and cannot establish

For a Class 11–12 or subsequent comparison, check actual Class 11 delivery and whether a real earlier baseline used compatible exercise, arm/dip/intent, preparation, rest, footwear/surface, measurement/processing and summary rules. Record material differences rather than silently matching them. Class 1–2's nine-attempt prescription with shorter within-set rests is not automatically a matched baseline; neither are box height, loaded jumps, seated jumps or Class 10's paused entry.

Even a numerical difference under compatible conditions is descriptive until the method's reliability/typical error, familiarization and recovery context support interpretation. No universal percentage cutoff, age norm or promised improvement is supplied. A different number of valid candidates limits mean/best comparisons and must be disclosed. Without compatible actual data, Class 12 may produce a first usable observation, not a before/after gain. If arm policy changes to the earlier hands-on-hips CMJ, preserve the original six-attempt budget and record a separate protocol.

The arm-policy distinction is supported by an [adult CMJ study comparing arm conditions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11368957/); it does not predict this cohort's gains. NSCA's [jump-metric framework](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/infographics/framework-for-selecting-metrics-jump-tests/) addresses evaluating whether jump metrics are usable in practice. The exact two-check/four-candidate allocation and descriptive summary rules here are coaching decisions, not a validated reliability study.
