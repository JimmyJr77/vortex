# Class 1 — design, workload and delivery audit

**Review date:** 2026-09-13. **Result:** finalized conditional prescription for the stated baseline. Athlete execution has not occurred in the available record. Delivery still requires the stated readiness, equipment-fit and booking checks. This is an exercise-programming audit, not a claim of scientific optimality or a completed training session.

## Daily stimulus brief, established before the exercise table

1. **Performance result:** repeatable upward takeoff with forceful leg extension and coordinated arms, anchored in the stationary CMJ.
2. **Movement demand:** preserve a useful foot/knee/hip/trunk position while reversing the dip and directing force upward. No athlete-specific limiting factor has been measured; these are task demands, not diagnosed weaknesses.
3. **Anchor:** natural-arm Countermovement Jump, three sets of three with full individual resets and a two-second landing hold. Record valid attempts and only verified device measures.
4. **Supporting jobs:** ankle-driven low rebound; force from stillness; each leg's upward impulse; plant-to-upward conversion from two steps; fast leg/arm extension against light load. Strengthening jobs are knee/pelvis control, active arch control, bilateral squat force, flexed-hip extension, split-stance lead-leg force, terminal hip extension, straight-knee plantarflexion and bent-knee plantarflexion.
5. **Quality and constraints:** preserve the valid CMJ pattern at high intent. No readiness results are supplied; six explosive slots cannot all receive large maximal doses. Keep the lane footprint within the current specification's 10 m, disclose ball/bench/step fit, and allow full rest.

## Consequential selection decisions

| Decision | Selection and rationale | Audit limit / revision |
|---|---|---|
| Main explosive task | Natural-arm stationary CMJ is the exact vertical takeoff anchor. A paused start remains a separate supporting task. | Moved CMJ ahead of paused jumps so the main task receives fresher practice. Ten low pogos are the only preceding prescribed explosive work. |
| Explosive support volume | Allocate 9 maximum-intent CMJs, 4 paused jumps, 4 controlled unilateral takeoffs, 4 controlled approach jumps and 6 light throws. | Reduced the reference example's 16 pogos to 10, paused jumps from 6 to 4, and single-leg takeoffs from 6 to 4. Total jump events fall from 41 in the example to 31 here; this is a workload decision, not a universal threshold. |
| Unilateral action | Single-leg vertical takeoff to bilateral landing separates force production by takeoff leg while limiting landing complexity. | A canonical single-leg hop-to-stick would add a different landing task. Exact proposed execution is named honestly; no live library identity is invented. |
| Approach slot | Four short approaches practice redirecting a small incoming momentum into the same vertical takeoff/arm task. | It is a modest application of today's result, not an acceleration block. No maximum approach or extended runway. A one-step replacement keeps the job if needed. |
| Externally loaded power | Light standing vertical scoop toss keeps a legs-to-arms ballistic job with no planned athlete flight. | OTA shows an advanced drop-to-vertical-toss example. Here the drop is removed; no combination slot or drop contacts are hidden. Throw clearance must be verified, with a separately counted loaded-jump replacement. |
| Light support | Lateral step-down controls the stance knee/pelvis; supported short-foot work loads arch control. | Replaced the reference example's Pallof press because foot contact is the more direct unmet job in this class. Use support and low dose to avoid making either a balance/endurance test. Intended mechanical support is not a proven jump-height gain. |
| Main strength anchor | Double front-rack squat is challenging with a manageable implement and no assumed squat rack. | Three working sets retained. A barbell back squat or trap-bar lift would require unconfirmed equipment/setup and is not necessary to fulfill the job. |
| Hip and unilateral strength overlap | RDL loads hip extension from a flexed position; split squat loads each lead leg; hip thrust adds a supported terminal-extension emphasis. | Preserve RDL at two sets, reduce split squat from two to one set per leg, and hip thrust from two to one set. Each remains challenging; these are not six maximal compound lifts. |
| Ankle overlap | Supported straight-knee calf work and seated bent-knee work load different knee-position contexts of plantarflexion. | One set per leg standing plus two bilateral seated sets. These still overlap with pogos/jumps; symptoms, range loss and actual outside impacts can justify less demand. Neither exercise isolates an entire muscle's contribution to jumping. |

## Group delivery and booking

Use one visibly supervised active jumper/thrower at a time in each verified exclusion zone. Keep the order the same for each athlete. With one available coach, use serial observation; do not assume that three lanes permit three concurrently observed maximum-intent tasks. A partner may rest/observe, but must not perform an additional drill while waiting.

Set up the dumbbell loads and bench/step before the relevant section. A secure front-rack pickup/set-down, suitable bench height, stable hand support and controllable ball landing/roll are actual equipment-fit requirements. A band anchor, squat rack, trap bar, marked jump-reach wall, specific force-plate capability or extra runway was not assumed.

For each shared station, check the actual athlete rotation: the athlete's elapsed time from the last attempt/set end to the next start must meet the prescribed rest. Queue time can supply that recovery; do not add it again as a second rest period. Queue time beyond the required recovery lengthens the class. The 80-minute calculation already reserves all prescribed rest, so add only unabsorbed queue delays. No group-size throughput claim is made without athlete counts, equipment counts and staff availability.

The repository's older 60-minute booking cannot contain this full prescription plus Access & Prepare 1. The older 90-minute booking leaves only about ten minutes at the base estimate, less with rest extensions; preparation and group flow must be established before claiming a fit. If that booking is mandatory, revise the dose and delivery plan while keeping meaningful 6/2/6 work, then rerun this audit. Do not silently adopt the older booking or claim an unresolved fit.
