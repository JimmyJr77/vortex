## Daily stimulus brief

1. **Result:** form an organized two-foot takeoff base after a short entry, then project upward under control.
2. **Demand:** square the sequential foot placements, settle without readjusting, then coordinate dip/arm action and vertical leg force. This is a provisional teaching emphasis, not a diagnosed limitation.
3. **Primary task:** six Gather-and-Hold Vertical Jumps, alternating starting lead, with two walking placements and two seconds tall stillness before the active dip. The pause removes immediate entry momentum; no fast-approach performance claim.
4. **Support jobs:** static asymmetric leg force; brief low ankle return while exchanging lead; still-start loaded leg-to-arm release; leg impulse through a fixed staggered base into retained load; upward supported force with frontal-plane pelvis control. Strength preserves bilateral/individual-leg force, complementary hip ranges and both ankle knee positions. Light work supports alignment/contact without pre-fatigue.
5. **Marker/constraints:** valid E1 attempts by starting lead under declared entry/settle/arm/landing conditions. No actual prior load, tolerance, skill or recovery is known. First scheduled work is the check, not added testing. Hold speed, settle and bodyweight jump loading throughout; readiness/regressions govern delivery.

## Selection, overlap and revision

E1's gathered, tall-settled start is distinct from earlier immediate floor/box approach jumps and stationary CMJs: the walking entry is a constraint on one jump, not a separately dosed braking exercise hidden in a combo. A faster or longer approach was rejected because no actual plant/recovery data justify it. A loaded continuation would assume a Class 9 loading response that is absent. The stationary CMJ is the explicitly repeated fallback.

E2 changes a dynamic same-lead split jump to a motionless lower-body start. E3 changes fixed-lead ankle work to tiny fore/aft exchanges with ankle-dominant contacts; it is not Class 8's larger, isolated lunge-switch task under a new label. E4 changes the moving ball preload to a static low start. E5 changes parallel press support to a fixed short stagger while retaining vertical load transfer. E6 changes the preplanted step's orientation to challenge pelvis control during upward drive. These six exact formats differ from reviewed defaults/replacements; this does not establish uniqueness against unknown Access & Prepare 1 or unreported actual variants. Proposed identity boundaries are recorded, with no invented approved IDs.

**Overlap:** E1–E3 share lower-limb tissues but separate organized active-dip takeoff, static split force and low ankle return. Eight split landings and six split presses make asymmetry/control more prominent, so there are no unilateral landings, high split switches or loaded flights. E2's rapid split takeoff differs from P2's controlled challenging strength; they are separately performed work. E4 and E5 both use leg-to-arm transfer but one releases a light ball from a still low start and one retains a rack load through a shallow moving dip; four/six reps limit overlap. E6 and S1 share frontal-plane control but E6 drives rapidly upward whereas S1 lightly loads slow lowering. RDL plus one Hip Thrust set preserve flexed and terminal hip-force roles. P5/P6 retain distinct knee-angle ankle loading, with actual response to pogos governing suitability. Same contact/strength totals do not prove equal tissue load or recovery needs.

**Revisions before finalization:** kept E1 to walking entry and an explicit tall settle rather than adding a faster approach or testing reps; replaced an initial longer four-switch pogo bout with two bouts of two, preserving four landings and only two links. Kept fourteen flights and bodyweight jump loading. Retained all strength dose/order/rest variables; no automatic overload. Added the parallel paused E2 fallback to prevent duplicate CMJ slots when E1 regresses. Preserved the pull replacement's longer reset, counted per-side and entry/step contacts, and retained teaching time because familiarity is unknown. The numerical clock does not establish full-group feasibility.
