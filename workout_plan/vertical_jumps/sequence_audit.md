## Relevance and coverage within the requested focus

The sequence repeatedly addresses the actual upward takeoff through active-dip, static-start, supported, single-leg, approach and short-rebound tasks. It also practices organized force acceptance and low ankle return to support vertical performance. Horizontal/lateral placement appears only in short entries, low obstacle clearance or small foot-placement tasks with a declared vertical job; this is not a separate horizontal-jump, agility or sport-skill curriculum. No external depth-drop progression, sprint block, endurance finisher or tumbling block is hidden inside it. The other framework categories are preserved as context, not silently programmed.

Non-flight throws, swings, pulls, rapid presses and supported rises are prescribed with rapid leg/hip intent. Their roles are force initiation, hip impulse or transferring leg drive into an upward implement action; sustained lifting, ordinary balance or unrelated upper-body hypertrophy are not mislabeled as explosive practice. Overhead load transfer still requires appropriate shoulder/clearance/grip and remains conditional. It is intended support, not a demonstrated jump-height intervention.

The recurring primary lifts have complementary, explicitly limited jobs: front-rack squat for bilateral knee/hip and trunk force; split squat for individual-leg force in a stable base; RDL for hip extension from flexion; one hip-thrust set for terminal hip extension; supported straight-knee calf work for late push-off; seated bent-knee soleus work for force through the dip/drive. Shared tissue demand is real. Separate hip ranges or knee positions justify roles but do not eliminate overlap. Volume is unequal, with small supporting doses and 2–3 technical repetitions in reserve, rather than six maximal compound lifts.

The light step-down and short-foot work remain ten controlled dynamic reps and six easy holds. They address knee/pelvis/foot alignment and active plantar contact, with at least five clean repetitions in reserve or comparable ease; they should not pre-fatigue the primary work. Both sides are counted and hand support prevents balance difficulty from replacing the intended strengthening job.

## Progression and the unique-drill preference

Classes 1–2 intentionally repeat six defaults and precede the user's uniqueness preference. From Class 3, history checks include both earlier defaults and offered replacements. Most later explosive slots change a meaningful start, arm condition, support, takeoff/landing task, entry, link, stance or implement action. Related families recur; a new title, changed load or lower rep count is not presented as a new drill.

Declared exceptions are retained for a reason: Class 8's simple scoop; Class 9's previously offered smooth-dip loaded jump and earlier preplanted step-up; Class 11's standing CMJ and grounded-foot split-rise replacement; Class 12's exact standing CMJ observation protocol. Necessary regressions also recur and are recorded as such. Class 12 has five new exact support formats, not five new movement families. Uniqueness against the unknown Access & Prepare 1 or unreported actual athlete variants cannot be verified. A constraint that adds difficulty without preserving its job is removed using the stated replacement.

The written progression is conditional and non-monotonic. Class 5 reallocates front-squat/split-squat work from 18/12 to 12/16 reps and prioritizes the split squat: 86 becomes 84 total working reps while separately executed bouts rise from twelve to thirteen. Thus fewer total reps do not automatically mean less demand. Class 8 changes lift priority without increasing dose. Class 11 removes one split-squat round—eight reps/two bouts—giving 76 reps/eleven bouts; Class 12 holds this. No automatic increase in kilograms, height or entry speed follows from a class number. Per-slot traces state previous → current → reason → next execution/recovery criterion.

The specification's basic-skill baseline supports a conditional plan, not proof that every athlete has learned each new constraint. Scheduled initial work supplies checks; no uncounted trials establish readiness. Missing response does not prove tolerance, failure or nonattendance. Coaches retain, regress or omit according to actual control, effort and recovery. Novelty is a user preference accommodated within these limits, not a claim of superior gains.

## Contact, handling, fatigue and timing review

Landing events include all intended flights, not just sets/bouts. Class 7's three two-flight bouts count six landings; low linked transitions are subsets of contact totals. Single-leg takeoff to bilateral landing is distinct from same-leg landing, with Class 4's four skip landings explicitly unilateral. Class 9's full loaded branch would include four loaded and ten bodyweight landings, but the loading decision and actual dose remain unknown. No stage is accepted merely because its contact count is lower than an earlier stage.

Additional handling/movement is tracked by task in each audit: box-jump step-down exits, short approach plants, skip entry/exit steps, free-foot returns and lead-foot step-offs, seated returns, ball retrievals, swing hike/parks and controlled loaded lowerings. For example, Class 8 has six box dismounts, six seated-rise returns and six swing parks; Class 10 has twelve walking-entry plants, four step-up free-foot returns and two leading-foot on/off cycles. Class 11's seated jumps have two between-rep reseating returns plus a final exit; Class 12's four seated rises have four prescribed controlled returns. These do not become extra exercise slots or disappear from the clock. Incidental/failed contacts still require actual logging.

Individual models include execution/holds/returns, specified intra-set and inter-set/side recovery, terminal transitions, strength preparation, explanation/setup/loading and 180 seconds of water/recording. The numerical row sums reconcile to the displayed class bases. Classes 1–2 explicitly include ball retrieval within their 20-second reset allowance and extend it if needed; later throw models separately allow approximately 25 seconds of retrieval before post-reset recovery. These are different declared timing conventions, not observed retrieval speeds. Early step-on/off handling occurs within setup/reset allowances; if actual handling requires more time, extend the model. No assumed time allowance permits rushed descent, retrieval or recovery.

Allowances are approximately 80–90 minutes for Classes 1–2, 85–95 for Classes 3–7 and 11–12, and 90–100 for Classes 8–10, all **outside Access & Prepare 1 and excess queues**. The Class 12 detailed model is 5,136 seconds / 85.6 minutes. Full E6 standing-rise replacement removes eight seconds; it does not authorize bonus work. Class 11's P2 reduction removed 32 seconds of reps plus 90 seconds of side rest and 120 seconds between rounds; all remaining rests remain intact.

Three separate 10 m lanes do not establish a continuous 30 m runway, three supervised stations, lane width or throwing/overhead clearance. Implement types are confirmed; counts, dimensions, load increments, device capability, staff/group size and actual booking are not. Before delivery, inspect the intended exclusion zones, retrieval routes, fitted supports and equipment flow. Waiting can satisfy recovery once when real rotation permits; excess waiting adds time. No verified complete-session fit is claimed. The older repository's 12 m lanes and 60/90-minute bookings are not silently imported. Actual preparation impacts/lifts and all performed sport/gym/separate tumbling must inform total exposure and contextual recovery spacing, not a fixed weekly timetable.
