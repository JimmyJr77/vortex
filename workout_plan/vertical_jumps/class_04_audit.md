## Choice comparisons and substantive revisions

| Slot / issue | Final decision and audit reasoning |
|---|---|
| E1: repeated floor CMJ versus standing box task | Choose standing Box Jump. It combines an active dip with the elevated target previously used from a seat. Canonical Box Jump is distinct from Seated Box Jump, but its countermovement remains related to the earlier floor CMJ. Six attempts preserve the primary allocation; no higher box, deeper dip or measured improvement is prescribed. |
| E2: faster forward approach versus small lateral entry | Choose a walking lateral step-and-gather into a bilateral vertical jump. Changed incoming direction requires containing sideways drift and organizing the takeoff base. This is a declared Approach Vertical Jump variant, not a newly invented canonical identity. No reactive cue, faster runway or agility circuit is added. |
| E3: long power-skip runs versus isolated cycles | Reject long skips and distance targets. Four individual height-directed step-hop cycles keep entry, same-leg landing and exit counts observable within the available space. Same-leg landing is a material new demand, not proven by bilateral landing experience; use controlled first scheduled attempts and the stated bilateral-landing replacement when unsuitable. |
| E4: more high jumps versus rope rhythm | Eight low rope bounces add an external rhythm constraint to familiar ankle-return mechanics. They are related to prior pogos, not a new muscle-loading pattern. Missed passes consume attempted contacts; no extra jumps to achieve a success count. Repeated trips trigger the disclosed pogo replacement or termination. |
| E5: high pull/catch versus simple upward pulling derivative | Remove deliberate high elbow pulling and any catch/flight. A light high-hang dumbbell pull uses the legs to accelerate retained weights upward with long arms. It directly loads the rapid upward extension job and is distinct from Class 3's hinge swing; technical prerequisites and lowering remain explicit. It is a proposed exact execution, not a verified published card. |
| E6: repeat scoop toss versus backward overhead release | Choose the locally documented backward overhead throw, with no athlete flight and four releases. It provides full leg-to-arm extension into a free release rather than another retained load. Require a closed inspected landing/roll sector; a 10 m lane is not evidence of containment. Use the earlier scoop-toss replacement only if its forward area is actually suitable. |
| Strength continuity | Retain all six primary exercises and two light exercises, with unchanged doses, tempos/rest and effort targets. This preserves traceable strength development while explosive constraints change. Load is selected from actual records/current preparation, not presumed prior success. |
| Combined fatigue | Do not add depth drops, overspeed assistance, rebound chains, loaded jump landings or a seventh test. Cap the two external-power supports at six light pulls and four throws. Keep the split squat/hip thrust at one working round and maintain full recovery. Actual fatigue can still require reduction; the arithmetic does not prove tolerance. |

**Novelty judgment:** All six default formats differ from earlier defaults, but several intentionally share movement families. Standing versus seated support, lateral versus forward entry and rope-constrained versus unroped rhythm are meaningful declared constraints, not renamed loads. The user's preference is to try for unique drills; necessary simpler replacements may repeat and must be logged. No claim is made of six biomechanically unrelated actions or verified uniqueness against the unknown Access & Prepare 1.
