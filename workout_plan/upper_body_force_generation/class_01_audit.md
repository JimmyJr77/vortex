# Class 1 — programming and delivery audit

**2026-09-13. Status:** Finalized conditional prescription; athlete suitability and site delivery have not been observed. The document audit is complete; the practical assumptions listed below remain explicit. This is not evidence that the class has been taught.

## Selection and revisions

The day's specific result is rapid horizontal push/pull with a steady trunk, expressed in forward ball release. The stationary chest pass receives nine fresh attempts. A universal three-set allocation across six explosive and six strength exercises was rejected: supporting ballistic movements instead receive small doses, and primary supports receive one challenging set. Main press and row receive two working sets each. This limits repeated shoulder/elbow work while retaining real work in all required slots.

| Consequential choice | Final decision and reason |
|---|---|
| Static versus countermovement chest pass | Retain both with **9 versus 3 attempts**. Static starts train force expression without a preload; a single shallow counter trains preload-to-release timing. Stance/ball-load-only duplicates were rejected. These are related variants with distinct jobs, not interchangeable test results. |
| Ballistics across six entries | Three forward-release tasks, one hand push-off task and two rapid pulls. Overhead slams, scoop throws, rotational throws and lower-body jumps were excluded because their vectors/actions are less direct for today's horizontal arm effort. |

## Delivery and replacement decisions

**Base equipment actually needed:** suitable medicine balls; a secured landmine, suitably light bar and compatible plates if needed; dumbbells; bands; one stable elevated hand support and one usable row support (a suitable bench may serve sequentially); a clear floor and inspected throwing arrangement. Equipment categories are confirmed; individual fit, condition and counts are not. No three-simultaneous-station claim is made.

**Layout:** Reserve one contained throwing corridor or a throw-rated wall lane, with the waiting area behind the thrower and beyond the possible rebound path. The thrower's line, target, rebound/bounce zone and retrieval route must fit the inspected site. Three 10 m lanes are not one 30 m space. Keep all lifting, band and push-up work outside flight/rebound zones; with one coach and one throwing bay, run the explosive exercises sequentially so coaching attention and clearance are not split. Do not operate a row station in a live throwing lane. No catches, run-ups or step-throughs are required.

**Throw replacements:** If the wall/open release area cannot contain throws, use a self-anchored **two-hand Band Speed Chest Press from a pause instead of E1**, 3 × 3; use a **shallow-countermovement Band Speed Chest Press instead of E4**, 1 × 3; use a **forward-facing Single-Arm Band Speed Press instead of E6**, 1 × 2 per arm. These are proposed exact executions, not claimed library variants. Place an intact manufacturer-suitable band across the upper back below the neck; for bilateral work secure it in both hands, and for unilateral work secure the nonworking end against the torso with the other hand. No external anchor or band release. Feet stay planted; press quickly, keep the ribs quiet, return under control. Retain each slot's listed inter-rep, inter-set and side recovery. Reduce resistance if tension cannot be controlled. Unilateral pressing also replaces E6 alone if no ball can be securely controlled by one hand. Never perform both versions.

These replacements are rapid resisted pushing, **not throws**, and cannot establish chest-pass release performance. Record E1 as a different anchor, throw totals as 0 if all three are replaced (12 if only E6 is replaced), and an unresolved free-release practice gap. Do not compare band performance with ball performance. If no valid band fit is available either, the session is not fully deliverable under current conditions; resolve the setup rather than hiding a zero-dose slot. The finalized base prescription remains the ball-release class, conditional on verified containment.

**Other replacements:** E2 can use a light two-hand kettlebell paused row if minimum bar mass or grip prevents a fast pull; E3 can use a higher stable support or a non-flight fast incline press; E5 can use a band secured under both feet in a fixed hinge instead of seated foot anchoring; P2 can use a stable thigh-supported row instead of bench support. All retain their slot's remaining sets/reps and rest, and changes must be logged. A change to seated/standing resistance direction invalidates a like-for-like E5 comparison. P4's hinge must remain technically established; use a supported chest-down version **instead of P4** only if a bench actually permits the arm path and clearance, with the same 1 × 8 and effort. Do not assume an adjustable incline bench.

**Group time model:** Let A be actual Access & Prepare 1 minutes and Q be additional queue, supervision and individual load-finding time beyond the base allowance. Full delivery is approximately **A + 65–75 + Q minutes**. For N athletes sharing one bay, each three-throw turn consumes roughly 45–60 seconds including clearance/reset. Return to a station only after that athlete's required recovery; compute waiting as `max(required recovery, actual waiting)` rather than adding both. This example describes turn occupancy, not a confirmed group size or proof of overall fit. Observe the actual flow before promising a booked duration.

## Workload, fatigue and recovery findings

- Base explosive total: **16 throws** (12 bilateral; 2 left-arm and 2 right-arm), **3 bilateral hand-landing events** (6 individual hand contacts) and **10 bilateral fast-pull cycles**. These are different exposures; their sum of 29 task repetitions is not a joint-load score.
- No lower-body jump/hop/bound landings, sprints, rotational throws or catch repetitions. E4's shallow dip is a preload, not a jump. E3 is an upper-body plyometric landing, not a lower-body contact.
- Primary: **8 set rounds / 10 bouts / 68 task repetitions**, plus **7 preparation rounds / 8 bouts / 37 repetitions**. S1/S2: **2 light sets, 14 bilateral cycles**. Work in Access & Prepare 1 and outside sport remains unknown and must be added from actual reporting.
- Pressing tasks share chest, anterior shoulder, triceps and wrist loading across throws, E3 and primary lifts. Rows/curls share shoulder and elbow-flexor work. Fewer supporting sets, no catch-return sequence, generous resets and reserve-based strength loads reduce unnecessary accumulation; no numerical total proves the dose appropriate for every athlete.
- First-session effort estimates require coach judgment. Check late-session P4–P6 technique and elbow response; if productive strength cannot be maintained, record reductions and reassess the next prescription. Never force the listed dose because it passed a document audit.
- Review recent/upcoming upper-body lifting, ball throwing, contact, climbing/hanging, hand-supported tumbling and games. Tentative nonconsecutive exposure spacing is adjusted to actual recovery and schedule. Persistent symptoms require an individualized decision, not automatic continuation because 48–72 hours elapsed.
