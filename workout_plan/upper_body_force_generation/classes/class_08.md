# Upper-body force generation — Class 8 of 12

**Primary effort:** Transfer force into a strong backward draw while maintaining grip and shoulder control.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 7, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The main continuous power row connects a small hip impulse to arm force without a separate lower-body exercise. Alternating draws, elbow reversal and unilateral downward release provide differentiated pulling jobs. A small kneeling forward pass and shoulder-only pull preserve complementary force directions without new high-volume work.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record valid connected pulls out of 6: hips and elbows contribute smoothly, the bar remains controlled, shoulders do not shrug and there is no twisting or separate deadlift-then-row sequence. If using strict rows instead, record a different task; do not compare bar speed without a verified measurement method. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Light resistance, rapid purposeful effort and controlled resets. Use the least assistance/load needed to preserve speed and position; no generic age-based weight. No failure sets or conditioning circuit.

Light two-hand sleeve grip; start in a short hinge. Extend hips slightly as elbows draw toward lower ribs in one connected pull, then lower/reset under control. No separate deadlift rep, shrug, jump, rotation or bar release. Rest 90 s between sets. Reset 8 s between reps. Use the paused arm-driven landmine row if hip-to-arm timing is not controlled, or a light two-hand kettlebell row if bar fit fails **instead of this slot** if the stated execution is unsuitable; follow any explicitly stated replacement dose; otherwise retain the remaining dose. | 2 | **Band Row — alternating arm draws from a fixed trunk** | **1 × 8** | Transfer backward pulling effort between arms without rocking the torso. Permitted seated foot anchoring; alternate four draws per arm while the other hand remains forward. Each draw returns under control before the next arm begins. Eight total draws, not eight per arm. Reset 3 s between reps. Use four bilateral seated band-row cycles if alternating control is unsuitable; record the changed laterality and task-rep count **instead of this slot** if the stated execution is unsuitable; follow any explicitly stated replacement dose; otherwise retain the remaining dose. Log changed laterality/exposure; stop if control cannot be restored. |
| 3 | **Band Biceps Curl — loaded return into quick reversal** | **1 × 4** | Carry force through the elbow after a controlled lengthening rather than a swinging shoulder start. | 4 | **Medicine Ball Overhead Slam — single arm, no trunk rotation** | **1 × 2 per arm** | Express each shoulder's downward force independently while remaining square. Log changed laterality/exposure; stop if control cannot be restored. |
| 6 | **Band Straight-Arm Pulldown — rapid shoulder extension** | **1 × 3** | Finish with a short shoulder-driven pull that does not add elbow-flexor fatigue. Rated overhead attachment; softly fixed elbows, fast pull from comfortable reach toward thighs, return 2 s with ribs quiet. Reset 5 s between reps. Use a light controlled rapid dumbbell pullover toward vertical if no suitable attachment exists; record the changed force curve **instead of this slot** if the stated execution is unsuitable; follow any explicitly stated replacement dose; otherwise retain the remaining dose. Log changed laterality/exposure; stop if control cannot be restored. |

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Inspect landmine arc, grip and plate clearance; nobody stands in its sweep. Use separate contained slam and forward-pass zones sequentially. Overhead and face-height band attachment points must be rated and permitted; they are not confirmed by band ownership. All ball and band replacements are slot-specific and change actual exposure counts. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

Light, controlled strengthening; keep at least **5 technically sound repetitions in reserve**. These sets should not tire the athlete before primary lifting.

## 4. Strength (Primary) — Exactly 6 Exercises

These are **working sets**, each finishing with approximately **2–3 technically sound repetitions in reserve**. Select challenging loads/assistance individually; a small set count does not mean a light effort. No forced reps, maximum testing, unstable supports or timed rotation. Work in order.

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Specific job in this workout |
|---|---|---|---|

Use a thigh-supported dumbbell row with the same arm path if a bench does not fit **instead of this slot** if the stated execution is unsuitable; follow any explicitly stated replacement dose; otherwise retain the remaining dose. **Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 5 per arm; P2: 1 × 5; P3: 1 × 5; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **35 repetitions, 6 rounds / 7 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 13 rapid pull bilateral; 8 alternating arm pull; 4 throw unilateral; 3 throw bilateral. Primary work totals **66 task repetitions in 8 rounds / 10 bouts**; resilience totals **20 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **59–68 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

**Scheduling:** Review recent/upcoming throwing, contact sport, lifting and hand-supported tumbling. Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness.
