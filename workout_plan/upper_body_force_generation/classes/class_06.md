# Upper-body force generation — Class 6 of 12

**Primary effort:** Consolidate clean arm-force onset and finishing positions at reduced volume.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 5, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Midpoint consolidation preserves rapid intent while removing catches, linked hand contacts and extra primary sets. E1 adds a simple cue-to-force timing condition; a small scapular finish task replaces hand-flight work. Familiar low-volume pulls and unilateral release are deliberately retained rather than introducing unrelated drills.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record how many of four cued releases start without an anticipatory dip or twist and remain on the declared forward line. Record any self-initiated replacement; no timing-gate reaction measurement is assumed. For E6, qualitative comparison is allowed only if earlier actual setup and attempts were recorded. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Use only the inspected forward-release bay, landmine/floor supports and free-rope lane; there is no overhead band anchor requirement. Keep rope collection and spectators away from ball travel. One coach runs high-attention work sequentially. Familiar equipment is still inspected; previously written setup is not proof of availability. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

**Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 6 then 1 × 3; P2: 1 × 5 per arm; P3: 1 × 3; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **37 repetitions, 7 rounds / 8 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 4 throw bilateral; 3 rapid pull bilateral; 4 rapid scapular press bilateral; 4 alternating rope pull; 3 rapid press bilateral; 4 throw unilateral. Primary work totals **42 task repetitions in 6 rounds / 7 bouts**; resilience totals **12 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **52–61 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

**Scheduling:** Review recent/upcoming throwing, contact sport, lifting and hand-supported tumbling. Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness. Body control remains separate.

**Progression note:** Class 5 prescribed 13 throws, four hand landings, six catches and eight primary working rounds. This consolidation prescribes eight throws, zero hand landings/catches and six primary working rounds, with all six lifts still challenging at 2–3 technical RIR. Floor Press and Row decrease from 2 × 6 to 1 × 6 (per arm for Row); the remaining lifts use 1 × 6. No automatic load increase. The four exact drill repeats are explicit low-complexity learning/comparison exceptions, not unique-drill claims. After actual recovery and clean completion, a later class may restore an earlier set dose or introduce a side-specific demand, but not both by default.

This class intentionally prioritizes consolidation over novelty while preserving the owner's preference in the rest of the sequence. No tolerance or midpoint improvement is inferred.
