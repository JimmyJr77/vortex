# Upper-body force generation — Class 4 of 12

**Primary effort:** Express a fast forward press from a settled start and control the finish.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 3, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The paused-start plyometric push-up is primary. Small supporting doses distinguish elbow extension, receive-then-set force production, external-load acceleration and independent arm timing; one familiar pull maintains the requested pulling component. Shoulder and elbow overlap is limited by keeping supports to one short set.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record valid hand-flight repetitions out of 6: the pause is visibly still, both hands unload together, trunk remains aligned and the athlete returns softly to the same support. A non-flight replacement uses a separate quality record with zero hand-landing events. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Use one inspected hand support with room for both hand receptions. Keep the coach-fed pass separate from other activity; the coach controls both feed and retrieval so the receiving athlete is never surprised. Ball size, rebound/flight containment and receiving competence are unconfirmed. Keep the band and floor-press stations outside the ball corridor. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

**Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 6 then 1 × 3; P2: 1 × 5 per arm; P3: 1 × 3; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **37 repetitions, 7 rounds / 8 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 6 hand landing bilateral; 8 rapid press bilateral; 3 throw bilateral; 4 rapid press unilateral; 3 rapid pull bilateral. Primary work totals **60 task repetitions in 8 rounds / 9 bouts**; resilience totals **16 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **59–68 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness.
