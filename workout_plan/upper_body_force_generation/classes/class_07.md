# Upper-body force generation — Class 7 of 12

**Primary effort:** Produce independent arm force while keeping the trunk facing forward.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 6, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The main projection task changes from two-hand receiving to one-arm release. Supported unilateral speed row and floor press isolate the arm's contribution; a modest vertical pull and dip-assisted landmine drive cover complementary pull/press demands. Two bilateral releases integrate the sides without a separate test block.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record successful receive-set-single-arm releases out of three attempts per arm. Ball remains controlled, the assisting hand clears before release and shoulders stay forward. Record arm, ball and replacement. Do not infer a weak side without actual observations. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Coach controls every feed in a protected lane; only a ball manageable by one arm is permitted. Use the same inspection/clearance rules for the bilateral release. Keep the supported-row and floor-press stations outside ball paths; inspect minimum landmine load and the overhead band attachment. No one-arm hanging or unsupported ballistic dumbbell catch is required. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

**Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 5 then 1 × 3 per arm; P2: 1 × 5 per arm; P3: 1 × 5 per arm; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **51 repetitions, 7 rounds / 11 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 6 throw unilateral; 10 rapid pull unilateral; 10 rapid press unilateral; 2 throw bilateral. Primary work totals **64 task repetitions in 7 rounds / 11 bouts**; resilience totals **20 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **66–75 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

**Scheduling:** Review recent/upcoming throwing, contact sport, lifting and hand-supported tumbling. Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness. Body control remains separate.

**Progression note:** Class 6 used one set of six for each primary lift. Here the main press changes to single-arm Floor Press 2 × 5 per arm, with one set of each supporting lift, including Row 1 × 6 per arm and strict Landmine Press 1 × 6 per arm. Seven rounds expand to eleven actual bouts because three lifts are unilateral; 64 task reps must not be mistaken for 64 bilateral cycles. No automatic load escalation: choose loads afresh for unilateral control, and retain a one-set version if readiness does not support the second press set. Before future advancement require both sides to demonstrate the stated control/reserve and reported recovery.
