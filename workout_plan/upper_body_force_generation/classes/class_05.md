# Upper-body force generation — Class 5 of 12

**Primary effort:** Convert a small preload or received load into a straight-ahead upper-body release.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 4, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** E1 practices one receive-to-return action at a time. Other jobs connect leg preload to a guided arm press, arm draw to unilateral projection, loaded reach to pulling, one brief hand-contact reversal, and a higher forward release path. These are conditional skill exposures; first-attempt control decides the suitable version.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record clean receive-and-return actions out of 6 attempts: the ball is received softly in front of the chest and returned on line without a second dip, trunk twist or rushed fumble. Record if the catch-set or self-start replacement is used; it is a different task. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Light resistance, rapid purposeful effort and controlled resets. Use the least assistance/load needed to preserve speed and position; no generic age-based weight. No failure sets or conditioning circuit.

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Use a coach-controlled feed lane with no parallel throwing. Overhead release requires its own confirmed clearance; a 10 m lane does not prove containment. The band/bar bay and hand-flight support must sit outside all ball paths. No assumed partner skill, ball specification or staff count. Pair-contact push-ups require an immovable support and visibly controlled reception. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

Rest **45 seconds between exercises** and **60 seconds after S2** before strength preparation. Use lighter resistance or shorter comfortable range if control fades. Stop for symptoms rather than completing a nominal count.

**Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 6 then 1 × 3; P2: 1 × 5 per arm; P3: 1 × 3; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **37 repetitions, 7 rounds / 8 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 9 throw bilateral; 3 rapid press bilateral; 4 throw unilateral; 4 rapid pull bilateral; 4 hand landing bilateral. Primary work totals **64 task repetitions in 8 rounds / 10 bouts**; resilience totals **20 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **62–71 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

**Scheduling:** Review recent/upcoming throwing, contact sport, lifting and hand-supported tumbling. Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness. Body control remains separate.

**Progression note:** Class 4 included six paused hand landings and three catch-set passes. This class prescribes at most four hand landings and six gentle feed-return catches, with conservative same-slot versions when the prerequisites are not demonstrated. Class 1/2 Floor Press and Row doses return at 2 × 6 and 2 × 6 per arm, while Push-Up and elbow-extension support drop to 1 × 6 each. Eight working rounds remain; pressing working cycles per arm drop from Class 4's 32 to 24 to make room for receiving/reversal exposure. No load increase is earned by the sequence. Advance only after actual smooth reception, forward line, technical reserve and satisfactory recovery.
