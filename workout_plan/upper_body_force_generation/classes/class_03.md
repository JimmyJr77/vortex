# Upper-body force generation — Class 3 of 12

**Primary effort:** Initiate and complete a rapid downward or backward arm draw without trunk heaving.

**Baseline and current evidence:** Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting technique and qualified supervision. Reviewed Class 2, saved workload/progression, drill use and available feedback before this prescription. No athlete completion, symptoms, load tolerance or improvement has been reported. This is a conditional planned class, not the next calendar day. New tasks require demonstrated control in the first prescribed attempts; no separate readiness test is added.

**Daily stimulus brief:** The main task is a tall-kneeling slam driven by the arms. Supporting pulls separate upper-arm drive, reversal into a row, elbow-flexion acceleration and initial scapular control. One small familiar forward-release dose retains the requested projection component without competing with the pulling emphasis.

**Quality marker, within E1:** In E1 record how many of the 6 slams stay arm-driven: hips remain tall, ball reaches a comfortable overhead position, release is in front of the knees and no trunk fold substitutes for the pull. Record ball type/mass; do not compare slam distance with chest-pass performance. Record valid attempts and actual work, including misses; an invalid attempt consumes a rep. No bonus test attempts. Do not compare measurements from different drills as the same test.

Light resistance, rapid purposeful effort and controlled resets. Use the least assistance/load needed to preserve speed and position; no generic age-based weight. No failure sets or conditioning circuit.

**Recovery and quality:** Rest **90 seconds between exercises**, including after E6 before Section 3; extend to 2–3 minutes if control, attention or speed has not returned. Returns are controlled, not explosive, unless the row explicitly describes a linked action. Stop the set for pain, jerking, unwanted rotation, hard/asymmetric hand reception or clear loss of speed; reduce load/complexity for remaining work only if control returns. Otherwise stop and record the shortfall. Do not make up missed reps or count a non-flight press as a hand landing.

**Delivery:** Run the slam station separately from the forward-release corridor. Both need inspected surfaces and clear ball travel; a slam is not permission to use a rebounding ball beneath the face. Place the overhead-band/bar station outside both ball zones. Unsupported bands or improvised bar supports are not acceptable substitutes. Ball releases require an inspected throw-rated wall with a protected rebound area, or a clear open corridor containing flight, bounce and roll. No rebound catches unless explicitly prescribed in a row. Retrieve stopped balls on the coach's signal; nobody waits downrange. Three separate 10 m lanes do not confirm throwing containment or extra run-off. Equipment quantities, ball mass/size, anchor ratings, bar minimum load and support dimensions remain unconfirmed. Every replacement occupies its original slot only; a resisted press replacing a throw removes that release exposure and cannot supply the same assessment.

**Load-preparation sets of these same listed lifts:** P1: 1 × 5 per arm; P2: 1 × 5; P3: 1 × 5; P4: 1 × 5; P5: 1 × 5; P6: 1 × 5. Use very light resistance; where two preparation sets are listed, the second is intermediate. Keep at least 5 technical reps in reserve. Rest 60 seconds between preparation rounds, 30 seconds between arms within a preparation round, then 90 seconds before P1/P2 working sets or 60 seconds before P3–P6 work. Preparation totals **35 repetitions, 6 rounds / 7 bouts**. Extra load-finding reps add actual workload and time; never add an exercise.

**Strength transitions:** Rest **2 minutes after P1, P2 and P3**, and **90 seconds after P4 and P5**, before preparing the next lift. Increase load only when an available prescribed set can remain at the stated range, tempo and reserve; if the next increment is too large, hold the load. All symptom, speed and technique stops take precedence over the planned count.

**Workload and timing:** 9 throw bilateral; 6 rapid pull unilateral; 8 rapid pull bilateral; 4 rapid scapular pull bilateral. Primary work totals **66 task repetitions in 8 rounds / 10 bouts**; resilience totals **18 task repetitions**. Bilateral cycles count once; unilateral doses count both arms. These exposure types are not equivalent stress units. No lower-body jumping, sprinting or rotational throw work is added. Estimate **60–69 minutes after Access & Prepare 1**, including listed work, resets, recovery, preparation and instruction; additional group queues or extra coaching add time. Complete session length is unresolved. Match the actual booking rather than compressing recovery.

**Scheduling:** Review recent/upcoming throwing, contact sport, lifting and hand-supported tumbling. Plan the next demanding upper-body exposure on a nonconsecutive occasion, commonly 48–72 hours later initially, then adjust to actual recovery and schedule. Elapsed time is not proof of readiness. Body control remains separate.

**Progression note:** Class 2 used 2 × 6 Floor Press and 1 × 8 Band Lat Pulldown. Here the press falls to 1 × 6 and the pulldown becomes 2 × 6, retaining eight total primary set rounds while giving the main pulling task more practice; total primary task reps change from 68 to 66. One-Arm Row remains 2 × 6 per arm. This planned redistribution is not evidence of improved capacity. Use familiar manageable loads; hold or reduce the pulldown if actual readiness or first-set technique does not support the extra set. Before later load advancement require reported controlled completion, stated reserve and recovery.
