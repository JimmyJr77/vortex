## Baseline and fixed class contract

Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique, individualized loading and qualified supervision. Established basic technique does not prove readiness for every ballistic variation. Check the first prescribed attempts and regress within the same slot where needed. Use the existing **Access & Prepare 1**, unchanged, followed by exactly **6 explosive exercises, 2 light stabilization/resilience exercises and 6 challenging primary-strength exercises**. No additional training blocks. Skill strengthening and resilience are supports, not new categories.

Give every slot a distinct mechanical job before choosing the movement. Prioritize unique drills across classes and distinct tasks within each class. Strength lifts may recur when their role and progression justify it. Apply the drill-selection policy below before selecting the next class. Primary working loads normally leave 2–3 technically sound reps available; supporting sets can be few while still challenging. Pause and reset explosive repetitions to preserve speed. The plan is inspired by public Overtime Athletes material on ballistics, push/pull strength and purposeful row progressions, with dose and delivery adapted to this cohort and specification. It is not an official OTA program or a validated whole-session protocol.

## Drill uniqueness — current owner preference

The owner clarified: **“Try to force unique drills; while strength lifts may recur as needed for progressions.”** This clarification takes precedence over the supplemental spec's general encouragement to repeat drills and over earlier roadmap instructions to preserve a chest-pass drill in subsequent classes.

- **Explosive drills:** Make a previously unused, directly relevant drill execution the default for every slot in each new class. Check the full prescribed history in `drill_usage.md`, including near-duplicates. A rapid lift placed in Explosiveness counts as a drill for this rule; its name does not create an exemption.
- **Meaningful uniqueness:** Changing only a name, load, set/rep count, grip, support height, stance, tempo or implement is not sufficient. A related variant needs a different force-expression or coordination problem and a distinct daily job. Prefer a different task when an equally relevant, feasible choice exists. Do not routinely fill multiple slots with the same movement family.
- **Strength recurrence:** Primary lifts and targeted light stabilization/resilience strengthening may recur when needed. Record the prior prescription, current dose, reason to retain it and next progression criterion; do not rotate useful lifts merely to make the list look new. Floor Press and One-Arm Row remain possible recurring strength anchors.
- **Exceptions:** Treat drill repetition as an exception requiring a specific written reason: athlete learning/readiness, a necessary comparable reassessment, or an equipment constraint for which no equally direct feasible new task was found. State which alternative was considered and why repetition better serves the athlete. A generic desire for an anchor is insufficient. Do not promise 72 unrelated drills or insert filler to avoid acknowledging a justified repeat.
- **Assessment and load:** Use the day's listed drill for its own quality marker. Different drills are not directly comparable power tests. No automatic repeat of E1 and no extra assessment exercise. A new drill does not establish earned progression or justify increasing workload, complexity or loading; judge its demands anew against actual feedback.

This is a strong selection preference, not evidence of athlete response. Maintain the exact seven-category framework, existing preparation reference, 6/2/6 counts, direct relevance, dosage and recovery. The subsequent completion instruction permits all remaining classes to be finalized sequentially. Class 1's prescribed exercises and doses remain unchanged in this preference revision; its related drill families are explicitly recorded for future avoidance rather than relabeled as wholly unique.
