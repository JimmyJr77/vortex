## Continuity and demand decisions

Classes 1–2 establish forward release and arm force against a quiet trunk. Classes 3–4 redistribute work toward pulling and still-start pushing. Class 5 introduces a small receiving/reversal demand while reducing two pressing support doses. This is a teaching plan with conditional prerequisites, not a claim that the earlier classes were tolerated.

Class 6 deliberately consolidates: six primary working rounds, eight throws, no catches or hand landings. The reduced complexity and dose explain four familiar drill exceptions. Class 7 adds independent-arm control; seven primary rounds become eleven actual bouts, so the row count alone understates the delivery burden. Class 8 returns to the earlier pulling prescription without calling it a proven successful dose.

Class 9 introduces controlled plant-to-arm timing; its 14 movement steps are not sprint distance or jump contacts. Classes 10–11 retain strength continuity while focusing on force completion and a small integrated receive–plant–release dose. No actual weak side or limiting factor was invented. Class 12 restores the original strength prescription and repeats the original E1 within its existing slot. Compare E1 only with actual matched ball, stance, target, timing and preparation records; without them, it establishes current quality only. Other final tasks have different preceding work and do not establish controlled before/after improvement.

There is no linear workload escalation. Primary working rounds by class are **8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 6, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8**. Challenging support work remains intentionally small to protect the main effort. Every class still needs a check of late-session technique and reserve; if the full prescribed dose cannot be performed productively, actual reductions must be recorded and the next prescription revised.

## Uniqueness judgment

The user's preference was applied to rapid lifts as well as throws. A change in implement, stance, name or load alone is not treated as a new family. Partial-start, cue-timing, receiving and alternating tasks are explicitly classified as related when appropriate. Repeats remain concentrated around simple learning/consolidation and closing references, with other exceptions documented per slot. This is a deliberate limit on novelty: the plan does not claim that 72 unrelated, equally relevant drills were available or that the chosen list is scientifically optimal. If athlete/site information later supports a better unused task with the same direct job, revise that slot and its workload record.

## Final revisions made

- Clarified that an explicit replacement dose takes precedence over the base dose, including changed laterality or contact counts. Replaced Class 3 E2's bilateral fallback with a dose-matched unilateral supported speed row and disclosed the vertical-pull gap.
- Clarified Class 4 E5: one arm completes its short pressing bout while the other holds only during each pulse; both release between resets, then sides change. The opposing support exposure is eight seconds per arm, within the movement-time estimate.
- Corrected Class 5 E5's between-repetition pattern to **0 / 15 / 0 seconds** for two linked pairs. The total rest is unchanged; the former uniform-gap wording did not describe the intended task. Separate-repetition regression may take longer and must be logged.
- Corrected structured unilateral primary-strength labels; both-side repetition totals were already counted. Added explicit missing zero-event fields and unknown timing/load fields to later workload summaries.
- Refined the floor press's job to its reproducible floor-limited range. Corrected the Class 1 approximate-time rounding label without changing its 65–75-minute planning estimate.
- Reconciled current completion headers and handoff state while retaining clearly labeled original roadmap and sequential design history. Added the full strength trace, aggregate exposure record and combined coach manual.
