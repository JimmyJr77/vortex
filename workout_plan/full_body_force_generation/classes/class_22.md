# Full Body Force Generation — Class 22 of 36

**Stage 8, lesson A: Hip-to-release and shoulder pull**

**Primary effort:** Transmit hip force into a release or draw, with a controlled ankle and shoulder finish.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting/landing technique, individually qualified ballistic/kicking skill and supervision. All athlete execution, load and recovery remain unreported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Open with Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — held hinge to forefoot finish as the controlled lower-body force anchor. Transmit hip force into a release or draw, with a controlled ankle and shoulder finish.

**Quality marker:** Main observation: Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — held hinge to forefoot finish. Within its listed attempts, record the declared starting position, side, movement/receiving control and a stable finish. Also record the partner task in the first connection under its own criteria; do not treat unlike drills as equivalent force tests. A miss consumes the attempt; no additional testing slots or retries are added.

**Connections:**

- **E1 + E2 — Hip-to-implement force transfer:** Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — held hinge to forefoot finish and Landmine Power Row — continuous hip-to-arm drive: Organize the hip impulse so it continues into the forward scoop or arm draw, finishing tall without an added lumbar arch.
- **E3 + E4 — Controlled ankle and shoulder finish:** Seated Bent-Knee Calf Raise — fast light rise and Medicine Ball Overhead Slam — single arm, no trunk rotation: Keep the support base organized while the ankle rises or shoulder drives the ball downward, preserving each task's declared line.
- **E5 + E6 — Hip initiation into hand force:** Forward step-in low roundhouse and Tall-kneeling diagonal medicine-ball side slam: Constrained-base upper diagonal slam and lower forward step-in kick separate trunk/hand expression from stance-leg arrival.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Six separate explosive tasks: two upper force, two lower force, one upper rotation and one lower rotation. The opener is today’s main quality observation; every other task has a specific supporting job. Use bodyweight/light resistance and rapid intent with controlled execution, preserving the written reset and side/set recovery. Extend rest to 2–3 minutes when needed. A miss consumes the attempt; no bonus test or make-up reps.

Stop for pain, unsafe implement path, trapped support knee, uncontrolled reception, lost alignment or clear slowing. Regress only remaining work when control returns; otherwise log the stopped slot. Source alternative doses are capped by the remaining combined dose. No new flight is added by the rotational selections.
