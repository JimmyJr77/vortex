# Full Body Force Generation — Class 12 of 36

**Stage 4, lesson C: Knee-to-ankle drive through the press finish**

**Primary effort:** Initiate controlled hip internal rotation and produce a lead-hand rotational pushing release. Grounded knee-to-ankle drive and linear arm presses supply supporting initiation and finishing force.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting/landing technique, individually qualified ballistic/kicking skill and supervision. All athlete execution, load and recovery remain unreported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Open with Seated light-band hip-rotation acceleration as the grounded rotational force anchor. Give the hip internal rotators a small rapid force-expression task without the balance or whole-kick demand.

**Quality marker:** Main observation: Seated light-band hip-rotation acceleration. Within its listed attempts, record the declared starting position, side, movement/receiving control and a stable finish. Also record the partner task in the first connection under its own criteria; do not treat unlike drills as equivalent force tests. A miss consumes the attempt; no additional testing slots or retries are added.

**Connections:**

- **E1 + E2 — Turn and control the finish:** Seated light-band hip-rotation acceleration and Lead-hand rotational medicine-ball push throw: Lead-hand rotational projection plus repeat seated hip torque avoids inventing a third rotating lower task; Class 4 pure chamber/recoil drills are not themselves rotation.
- **E3 + E4 — Extension through a controlled boundary:** Squat-to-Calf Rise — rapid extension, feet stay grounded and Floor Press — light dumbbells, fast concentric from floor contact: Carry force through the intended knee/ankle or arm extension, retaining the floor boundary and avoiding a back arch to finish.
- **E5 + E6 — Independent contribution with a steady trunk:** Reverse Lunge — quick front-leg return, no flight and Band Chest Press — one arm presses while opposite arm holds: Keep the support pressure and trunk organized while the front leg returns or one arm presses, preserving control of the opposite support or arm.
