# Full Body Force Generation — Class 2 of 36

**Stage 1, lesson B: Grounded hip drive and fast traction**

**Primary effort:** Drive the hips sharply while expressing a quick arm pull and controlled hand push-off.

**Baseline:** Ages 12–14 with established lifting/landing technique, individually qualified ballistic/kicking skill and supervision. All athlete execution, load and recovery remain unreported.

**Daily stimulus brief:** Open with Two-Hand Landmine Bent-Over Row — fast pull from a pause as the controlled upper-body force anchor. Drive the hips sharply while expressing a quick arm pull and controlled hand push-off.

**Quality marker:** Main observation: Two-Hand Landmine Bent-Over Row — fast pull from a pause. Within its listed attempts, record the declared starting position, side, movement/receiving control and a stable finish. Also record the partner task in the first connection under its own criteria; do not treat unlike drills as equivalent force tests. A miss consumes the attempt; no additional testing slots or retries are added.

**Connections:**

- **E1 + E2 — Force from a parked load:** Two-Hand Landmine Bent-Over Row — fast pull from a pause and Kettlebell Swing — dead-stop two-hand repetitions: Organize the held starting load before accelerating; preserve the row's still trunk and the swing's deliberate hip drive as separate ways of producing force.
- **E3 + E4 — Impulse and controlled reception:** Plyo Push-Up — hands elevated and Medicine Ball Scoop Toss — hip-driven forward release: Direct a decisive push through the support before the body or ball moves away, then control the body's finish; only the push-up includes a hand reception.
- **E5 + E6 — Supported rotational force:** Standing low roundhouse shadow kick and Half-Kneeling Rotational Med Ball Throw — floor-target adaptation: Reduce upper leg propulsion in the throw while directly expressing the lower hip-led kick.

## 2. Explosiveness — Exactly 6 Exercises

Six separate explosive tasks: two upper force, two lower force, one upper rotation and one lower rotation. The opener is today’s main quality observation; every other task has a specific supporting job. Use bodyweight/light resistance and rapid intent with controlled execution, preserving the written reset and side/set recovery. Extend rest to 2–3 minutes when needed. A miss consumes the attempt; no bonus test or make-up reps.

Stop for pain, unsafe implement path, trapped support knee, uncontrolled reception, lost alignment or clear slowing. Regress only remaining work when control returns; otherwise log the stopped slot. Source alternative doses are capped by the remaining combined dose. No new flight is added by the rotational selections.

No forced reps, failure/max testing or breathless circuit. Same-lift preparation is separate from working sets; extra load finding adds actual work/time.

The original force-course doses and preparation stay with their source exercises. Added rotational support uses the clearly stated smaller combined dose and its own easy preparation. A new class or changed position in the sequence does not justify a load increase. Judge late-session technical reserve and record reductions rather than forcing nominal completion.
