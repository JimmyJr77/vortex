# Full Body Force Generation — 36-class curriculum

**User request:** Combine all twelve Upper Body Force Generation classes and all twelve Lower Body Force Generation classes, with upper- and lower-body rotational power in every class. This new grouped program belongs to Athleticism Accelerator. The request authorizes all 36 classes; develop, audit and save each in order before moving on. No calendar frequency or 36-week schedule is imposed by a 36-class request.

**Current user direction:** Use the existing exercises from the upper-force, lower-force and rotational programs. Build each class through purposeful selection, grouping and order: place the main explosive effort first, alternate complementary upper and lower work where useful, then use the existing resilience and strength exercises to support that effort. Do not invent drills, hybrid movements or renamed variations to force uniqueness. Repeating an existing exercise is appropriate when its job warrants it. Reordering alone is not a reason to change its prescription.

**Composition:** Twelve stages of three integrated classes. Each class: existing Access & Prepare 1; six explosive tasks (2 upper-force,2 lower-force,1 upper rotation,1 lower rotation); two light supports (1 upper, 1 lower); six primary-strength tasks (2 upper-force,2 lower-force,1 upper-rotation support,1 lower-rotation support). Across each stage, use all six explosive and all six primary entries plus both light entries from each matching force source class. The third light slot per region is a justified support repeat. Rotation additions are selected and dosed for the combined class, with original source prescriptions kept in provenance. Coverage is 336 original force entries plus 72 rotational explosive, 72 rotational primary and 24 light repeats = 504 entries.

**Focus:** Upper-body force generation > Pushing/Pulling/Straight-line projection; Jumps > Vertical/Horizontal; Rotational explosiveness > Upper-body emphasis/Lower-body emphasis. Limited source acceleration remains within the existing Sprinting subcategory. The program title is not an eighth category. Strength and resilience support force transfer; body control remains separate.

## Baseline, selection and delivery

Ages 12–14 with established basic lifting/landing technique, individualized loading and qualified supervision. Kicking, turning, receiving and ballistic competence must be checked within the prescribed first attempts; age or written source completion does not establish it. Primary work remains challenging at about 2–3 technical reps/cycles in reserve; all light supports retain at least 5. Source classes, logs and all four feedback records were reviewed; no actual athlete attendance, loads, symptoms, output or recovery is supplied.

Existing Access & Prepare 1 stays unchanged and its workload/duration remains unknown. Each class keeps four ordered sections and exactly 6/2/6 nonzero entries. Replacements occupy the same slot and change actual recorded exposure; simultaneous substitutions must not duplicate another slot. Stop/record an incomplete modified delivery when distinct appropriate replacements cannot be retained. No bonus assessments, finishers, circuits, impact kicking or tumbling are inserted.

Use the adopted inventory and three separate 10 m lanes. Full start/entry/action/landing/stopping and swept-leg/ball paths must fit verified space. Counts, loads, support fit, device functions, ceiling/throwing-wall suitability, staff and group flow are unconfirmed. Source lower Class 3 starts retain 1 m stance, 2 m acceleration and up to 7 m slowing. Throwing requires complete flight/bounce/roll containment. Keep no-contact kicks below hip height, permit the support heel to release and use active recoil with a soft knee. A grounded step or knee lift with no meaningful hip turn is not automatically rotational power.

The six explosive tasks are separately delivered with full recovery. A connection is a shared coaching cue, not a simultaneous jump/kick/throw or a superset. Recompute combined time from selected work, reset/side/set rest, same-lift preparation, transitions and instruction; never reuse or add whole source-class durations. Unknown preparation, queues, extra load-finding and rest extend the booking. Schedule by actual broader sport/strength/tumbling load and recovery, initially often 48–72 h between comparably demanding exposures. Do not stack the standalone source workouts on top of these classes or double up missed classes.

**Exercise reuse:** Existing exercises supply the variety. Use the saved source executions and necessary repeats; exercise novelty is not a design target. Give each selection a clear job through its place in the class and progression.

Each stage has a main initiation lesson, a complementary force/receiving lesson and a rotational application lesson. The map below was provisional during sequential authoring. All 36 class files are now saved and fully audited; each stage preserves complete original force coverage.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 | Initiate from a settled base | Stationary upper push/pull and leg drive connect to a freely turning support base. |

| 3 | 7–9 | Independent limbs and a controlled turn | Preserve per-side dosing as support transfers between limb and trunk. |
| 4 | 10–12 | Upward push, recoil and finish | Link knee-led projection and upper pressing to active rotational braking. |
| 5 | 13–15 | Preload and reacceleration | Separate coupled hand and leg contacts across the three lessons; keep rotational additions grounded. |
| 6 | 16–18 | Consolidate every region | Use source reduced doses and smaller added rotational doses; no assumed recovery. |
| 7 | 19–21 | Independent stance and controlled transfer | Single-leg takeoffs retain bilateral reception; qualify handoff and support before release. |
| 8 | 22–24 | Hip-to-ankle and diagonal force | Sequence the grounded push through the upper limb and a controlled diagonal turn. |
| 9 | 25–27 | Controlled entry into force | Walking entries and short task travel stay within verified space; distinguish rotation from mere stepping. |
| 10 | 28–30 | Force through the finish | Stable support, controlled limb endpoints and modest low kicks replace a volume peak. |
